# Donor Restricted Gift Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs restricted gifts, designated gifts, donor preferences, donor conditions, and donor communications for the Church Support Foundation.

The Foundation must steward gifts faithfully while protecting doctrine, mission, governance, legal compliance, and recipient dignity.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A donor's generosity should be received with gratitude, but the Foundation's mission must not be sold by restriction.

The Foundation may accept restricted gifts only when the restriction is lawful, mission-aligned, administratively feasible, and approved by proper authority.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- restricted gifts;
- designated gifts;
- donor-advised recommendations where lawful;
- endowments;
- named funds;
- scholarship funds;
- church support funds;
- capacity-building funds;
- emergency funds;
- international gifts;
- gifts of property;
- gifts with conditions;
- donor communications affecting use of funds.

---

# 4. Definitions

## 4.1 Restricted Gift

A gift legally or formally limited to a particular purpose, program, recipient category, time period, or use.

## 4.2 Designated Gift

A gift where the donor expresses a preference or designation, subject to acceptance and control by the Foundation.

## 4.3 Donor Preference

A non-binding donor expression of interest.

## 4.4 Donor Condition

A requirement attached to a gift that must be reviewed before acceptance.

---

# 5. Acceptable Restrictions

Restrictions may be accepted when they:

- align with the Society's Statement of Faith;
- support approved Foundation purposes;
- comply with law;
- do not create improper private benefit;
- do not control church authority;
- are administratively feasible;
- do not distort mission priorities;
- can be reported and tracked;
- are approved by proper authority.

---

# 6. Unacceptable Restrictions

The Foundation should not accept restrictions that:

- contradict doctrine;
- require unlawful discrimination;
- give donors control over grant decisions;
- create private benefit;
- direct funds to relatives or insiders improperly;
- control church preaching, discipline, sacraments, or governance;
- bypass conflict policy;
- create administrative burden beyond capacity;
- are vague or impossible to administer;
- bind future boards unwisely;
- damage mission alignment.

A gift that cannot be governed should not be accepted merely because it is large.

---

# 7. Gift Acceptance Authority

Gift acceptance authority must be defined.

Higher approval may be required for:

- large gifts;
- property gifts;
- endowments;
- gifts with complex restrictions;
- gifts involving specific churches or students;
- gifts from conflicted donors;
- gifts with naming rights;
- gifts with international implications;
- gifts that affect reserved powers.

---

# 8. Donor Control

Donors may not control grant decisions unless a lawful structure explicitly permits donor advice and governance approves it.

Even where donor advice is permitted, the Foundation must retain final control and discretion.

Donors should not become invisible committee members.

---

# 9. Specific Recipient Gifts

Gifts intended for a specific person, church, student, or ministry require careful review.

The Foundation should confirm:

- legal permissibility;
- charitable purpose;
- mission fit;
- conflict risks;
- private benefit concerns;
- recipient eligibility;
- reporting requirements;
- whether the Foundation retains discretion.

---

# 10. Named Funds

Named funds may be created when approved.

A named fund agreement should define:

- name;
- purpose;
- restrictions;
- governance authority;
- reporting;
- duration;
- amendment process;
- unused funds;
- donor communication;
- public recognition if any.

---

# 11. Endowments

Endowments require special review.

Review should consider:

- legal structure;
- investment policy;
- spending policy;
- donor restrictions;
- administrative cost;
- long-term mission fit;
- board authority;
- amendment or cy-près provisions where applicable;
- reporting.

The Foundation should not create endowments faster than it creates governance.

---

# 12. Property Gifts

Property gifts require due diligence.

Review should consider:

- title;
- valuation;
- restrictions;
- environmental concerns;
- maintenance burden;
- tax implications;
- liquidity;
- mission fit;
- legal obligations;
- sale restrictions;
- use restrictions.

---

# 13. Tracking Restricted Funds

Restricted funds must be tracked separately in accounting records.

Tracking should include:

- donor;
- amount;
- restriction;
- date received;
- approved use;
- balance;
- disbursements;
- reporting obligations;
- unused funds;
- release or amendment terms.

---

# 14. Donor Reporting

Donor reports should be truthful and appropriate.

Reports may include:

- funds received;
- funds used;
- general outcomes;
- remaining balance;
- ministry summary;
- prayer themes.

Reports must protect recipient privacy and should not give donors improper control.

---

# 15. Changing Restrictions

A restriction may be changed only according to law, gift agreement, donor consent where possible, court process where required, or approved legal procedure.

Changes must be documented.

---

# 16. Unused Restricted Funds

If restricted funds cannot be used as intended, the Foundation should review:

- alternate permitted use;
- donor communication;
- legal requirements;
- board authority;
- mission alignment;
- return of funds where required or appropriate.

---

# 17. Conflicts of Interest

Donor restrictions may create conflicts.

Examples include:

- donor wants to support family member;
- donor wants to influence church;
- donor is connected to applicant;
- donor has business relationship with Foundation;
- donor offers gift with personal benefit expectation.

Conflicts must be disclosed and managed.

---

# 18. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during Foundation launch and at least every three years once gift operations stabilize.
