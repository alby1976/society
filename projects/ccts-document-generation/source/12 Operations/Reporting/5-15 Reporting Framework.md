# 5-15 Reporting Framework

## 1. Purpose

The 5-15 Report is the Society's primary short-form reporting tool.

A 5-15 Report is designed to take approximately fifteen minutes to prepare and approximately five minutes to read. It gives leaders enough information to understand what happened, what needs attention, what decisions are required, and what should be prayed for.

## 2. Governing Principle

A 5-15 Report should be short enough to complete and useful enough to matter.

The Society shall use 5-15 reports to reduce mental load, reduce unnecessary meetings, identify problems early, preserve institutional memory, and support prayerful leadership.

The report should not become a performance theatre. It should tell the truth in a usable format.

## 3. Meaning of 5-15

**5** means five minutes to read.

**15** means fifteen minutes to prepare.

These numbers are guidelines, not a stopwatch religion. The goal is concise reporting.

## 4. Why the Society Uses 5-15 Reports

The Society uses 5-15 reports because they make communication regular, reduce meeting burden, help neurodivergent leaders externalize memory, reveal risks early, help supervisors support workers, preserve ministry history, identify prayer needs, create source material for monthly and annual reports, support mission alignment review, and make leadership transitions easier.

## 5. Standard Sections

A general 5-15 report should include:

1. Basic Information
2. Praise and Thanksgiving
3. Major Accomplishments
4. Current Priorities
5. Challenges or Risks
6. Decisions or Support Needed
7. Upcoming Work
8. Prayer Requests
9. Additional Comments

Role-specific templates may add additional sections.

## 6. Basic Information

Every 5-15 report should identify name, role, ministry or department, national chapter if applicable, reporting period, date submitted, confidentiality level, and supervisor or reviewer.

## 7. Praise and Thanksgiving

This section records reasons for gratitude, including answered prayer, people helped, conflict resolved, ministry progress, volunteer encouragement, church partnership development, provision received, risk avoided, or lesson learned.

The Society should remember God's mercies, not merely its tasks.

## 8. Major Accomplishments

This section summarizes what was completed during the reporting period. It should focus on meaningful outcomes, not busyness.

Examples include residents supported, ministry event completed, report submitted, policy drafted, church meeting held, grant reviewed, chaplain visit completed, volunteer trained, or risk addressed.

## 9. Current Priorities

This section identifies what the reporter is focused on now. It helps supervisors and leaders understand workload, direction, and alignment.

## 10. Challenges or Risks

This section identifies problems, obstacles, concerns, or risks.

Examples include volunteer shortage, resident conflict, budget pressure, unclear authority, delayed report, church relationship concern, pastoral care concern, safeguarding concern, legal or privacy issue, or burnout risk.

This section should be honest. A report that hides problems is just a newsletter with commitment issues.

## 11. Decisions or Support Needed

This section identifies what the reporter needs from a supervisor, board, committee, chaplain, church partner, or other authority.

Requests should be specific where possible.

## 12. Upcoming Work

This section identifies planned work for the next reporting period. It helps with continuity and follow-up.

## 13. Prayer Requests

Prayer requests may include ministry needs, wisdom, people served, workers, decisions, churches, risks, and thanksgiving.

Sensitive personal details should not be included unless appropriate and authorized.

## 16. Confidentiality

5-15 reports may contain sensitive information. Reporters must avoid unnecessary personal details.

Sensitive matters should be summarized carefully and escalated through proper confidential channels.

A 5-15 report should not become a diary, confession booth, personnel file, or public prayer chain.

## 17. Escalation

A 5-15 report should trigger escalation when it identifies safeguarding concern, legal risk, financial irregularity, serious conflict, theological concern, mission drift, privacy breach, abuse allegation, severe burnout, urgent housing issue, serious chaplaincy concern, or board decision needed.

The reviewer is responsible to notice escalation needs.

## 18. Review Responsibility

Each report should have a designated reviewer.

The reviewer should read promptly, identify action items, respond where needed, escalate concerns, preserve report according to retention policy, and summarize trends for monthly or quarterly reports.

Reports that no one reads are digital compost.

## 19. Integration with Monthly Reports

Weekly 5-15 reports should feed monthly summaries. Monthly summaries should identify repeated themes, unresolved issues, ministry progress, decisions needed, risks, prayer themes, and resource needs.

## 20. Integration with Annual Reports

Annual reports should draw from 5-15 reports, monthly summaries, quarterly reports, financial reports, and mission alignment reviews. This prevents annual reporting from becoming archaeological excavation.
