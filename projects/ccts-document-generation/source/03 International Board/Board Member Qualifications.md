# Board Member Qualifications

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the qualifications expected of members of the International Board of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Society's board members carry responsibility for doctrine, mission, governance, finance, risk, international expansion, national chapter oversight, and institutional memory. Board membership therefore requires more than enthusiasm.

---

# 2. General Standard

A board member should be a person of Christian character, theological seriousness, governance wisdom, and mission alignment.

A board member must be able to steward the Society's mission rather than merely contribute personal opinions, professional status, financial influence, or social connections.

---

# 3. Theological Qualifications

A board member shall affirm the Society's [[Statement of Faith]] according to the level of subscription required by policy.

A board member should:

- respect Scripture as supreme authority;
- support the Society's Reformed identity;
- understand that the Society is not a church;
- support the Society's church-strengthening mission;
- reject mission drift;
- be willing to seek theological counsel when needed.

Different board roles may require different levels of confessional subscription, but no board member should be opposed to the Society's doctrinal commitments.

---

# 4. Church Relationship

A board member should ordinarily be a member in good standing of a faithful local church.

Where local church membership is not immediately possible due to exceptional circumstances, the matter should be reviewed carefully.

A board member should not be under unresolved church discipline or known serious spiritual scandal.

The Society should avoid appointing board members who view the Society as a replacement for the church.

---

# 5. Character Qualifications

A board member should demonstrate:

- honesty;
- humility;
- self-control;
- courage;
- patience;
- teachability;
- prayerfulness;
- fairness;
- confidentiality;
- willingness to repent;
- ability to work with others;
- care for vulnerable people;
- seriousness about stewardship.

Board members need not be flawless. But unrepentant patterns of pride, deceit, manipulation, greed, harshness, instability, or secrecy are disqualifying concerns.

---

# 6. Governance Qualifications

A board member should be willing and able to:

- read board materials;
- understand governing documents;
- attend meetings;
- ask careful questions;
- distinguish governance from operations;
- respect delegated authority;
- record decisions properly;
- preserve confidentiality;
- disclose conflicts;
- participate in evaluation;
- support board decisions once made.

A person who will not read documents should not govern a document-heavy international Society. That is not elitism; that is basic survival.

---

# 7. Skills and Experience

The Board should collectively include skills in:

- theology;
- law;
- finance;
- charity governance;
- international operations;
- church relationships;
- housing;
- cooperative development;
- grants and foundations;
- chaplaincy;
- risk management;
- human resources;
- communications;
- technology;
- institutional memory.

Not every member must have every skill. The Board as a whole should be competent.

---

# 8. International Perspective

Because the Society is international, board members should be able to think beyond one country.

This includes willingness to consider:

- local legal differences;
- cultural differences;
- language and translation issues;
- church contexts;
- chapter capacity;
- international risk;
- cross-border governance.

Board members should avoid treating the founding chapter's customs as automatically universal.

---

# 9. Independence and Conflicts

A board member must disclose actual, potential, and perceived conflicts of interest.

A board member should not use board service for:

- personal profit;
- family advantage;
- business preference;
- political influence;
- control of a ministry;
- protection from accountability.

Conflicts do not always disqualify a person, but hidden conflicts are dangerous.

---

# 10. Time and Capacity

A board member must have sufficient time and capacity to serve.

Board work may include:

- meetings;
- document review;
- committee service;
- prayer;
- crisis decisions;
- reports;
- training;
- annual review;
- national chapter oversight.

A willing but unavailable board member creates governance fog.

---

# 11. Disqualifying Concerns

Concerns that may disqualify a potential or current board member include:

- rejection of the Statement of Faith;
- hostility to local church authority;
- serious unresolved moral scandal;
- financial misconduct;
- refusal to disclose conflicts;
- breach of confidentiality;
- repeated failure to participate;
- divisive conduct;
- abuse of authority;
- unwillingness to be corrected;
- using the Society for personal agenda;
- inability to distinguish board governance from personal control.

---

# 12. Nomination and Review

Board candidates should be reviewed through a process that may include:

1. written application or nomination;
2. statement of faith affirmation;
3. church reference where appropriate;
4. conflict of interest disclosure;
5. skills and experience review;
6. interview;
7. prayerful board deliberation;
8. approval according to bylaws or policy.

The process should be serious without becoming theatrical. The goal is discernment, not paperwork cosplay.

---

# 13. Ongoing Expectations

Board members should continue to meet qualifications during service.

The Board should provide:

- orientation;
- continuing education;
- annual self-evaluation;
- conflict disclosure renewal;
- theological and governance training;
- review of participation.

---

# 14. Removal or Resignation

A board member may resign according to bylaws.

A board member may be removed according to bylaws or policy for failure to meet qualifications, breach of duty, conflict of interest concerns, doctrinal misalignment, misconduct, or persistent non-participation.

Removal should be documented and handled with truth, fairness, and appropriate confidentiality.

---

# 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during the founding period and whenever the Society revises board structure, confessional requirements, membership standards, or international governance requirements.
