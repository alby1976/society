# Grant Reporting and Accountability Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes reporting and accountability requirements for grants made by the Church Support Foundation.

Grant accountability protects recipients, donors, churches, the Foundation, the Society's mission, and the reputation of Christian stewardship.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Accountability should be proportionate to the grant.

Small grants should not require burdensome reporting. Large or high-risk grants should not rely on vague updates and warm feelings.

The Foundation should ask for enough information to steward funds well.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- church support grants;
- capacity-building grants;
- student support grants;
- ministry training grants;
- emergency grants;
- restricted fund grants;
- national chapter grants;
- Society ministry grants;
- recurring grants;
- multi-year grants.

---

# 4. Reporting Levels

Grant reporting may be assigned by risk and amount.

| Level | Description | Reporting Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | small, low-risk grant | short final report |
| Standard | ordinary grant | interim or final report with financial summary |
| Enhanced | larger or higher-risk grant | milestones, receipts, narrative, financial details |
| Intensive | high-risk, multi-year, or sensitive grant | scheduled reports, review meetings, board oversight |

---

# 5. Standard Report Contents

A grant report may include:

- grant title;
- recipient;
- amount received;
- amount spent;
- remaining funds;
- activities completed;
- outcomes;
- challenges;
- changes from original plan;
- financial summary;
- receipts where required;
- prayer requests;
- testimony or narrative where appropriate;
- next steps.

---

# 6. Financial Accountability

Financial reporting should show that funds were used for approved purposes.

Depending on grant level, this may include:

- summary budget;
- receipts;
- invoices;
- payroll summary;
- bank record excerpts;
- reimbursement forms;
- signed certification;
- unused funds statement.

The Foundation should avoid collecting more sensitive financial data than needed.

---

# 7. Outcome Accountability

Outcome reporting should identify whether the grant achieved its intended purpose.

Outcomes may include:

- training completed;
- ministry launched;
- policy adopted;
- church supported;
- student completed term;
- equipment purchased;
- translation completed;
- governance improved;
- capacity increased;
- people served.

---

# 8. Changes to Grant Purpose

Recipients must request approval before materially changing grant use.

Changes may involve:

- budget change;
- timeline change;
- program change;
- recipient change;
- unused funds;
- change of institution;
- change of ministry direction;
- project cancellation.

Material changes should be documented.

---

# 9. Missed Reports

If a recipient misses a report deadline, the Foundation may:

- send reminder;
- pause future payments;
- schedule check-in;
- request simplified report;
- escalate to committee;
- suspend grant;
- decline future grants;
- request repayment where agreement allows.

The response should be proportionate and documented.

---

# 10. Misuse of Funds

If misuse is suspected, the Foundation should review:

- facts;
- grant agreement;
- financial records;
- recipient explanation;
- intent;
- amount;
- legal requirements;
- donor restrictions;
- reporting obligations;
- recovery options.

Responses may include corrective plan, repayment, suspension, future ineligibility, legal advice, or board action.

---

# 11. Unused Funds

Unused funds must be handled according to the grant agreement.

Options may include:

- return to Foundation;
- approved reallocation;
- extension;
- transfer to related approved purpose;
- closure with documented balance.

Unused funds should not disappear into general use without approval.

---

# 12. Multi-Year Grants

Multi-year grants require annual review.

Review should address:

- milestones;
- funds used;
- outcomes;
- sustainability;
- risks;
- changes;
- continued eligibility;
- mission alignment;
- whether future payments should continue.

---

# 13. Emergency Grants

Emergency grants may use simplified reporting, but accountability still applies.

Emergency reporting may include:

- confirmation funds were received;
- brief explanation of use;
- remaining need;
- basic financial confirmation;
- follow-up needs.

---

# 14. Privacy

Grant reports may contain sensitive information.

The Foundation must protect:

- student information;
- church matters;
- financial hardship;
- family circumstances;
- health or pastoral information;
- donor information;
- ministry risks.

Reports should not include unnecessary identifying details.

---

# 15. Donor Reporting

Donor-facing reports should be accurate and appropriately summarized.

Donors should not receive confidential applicant or recipient details unless authorized and appropriate.

Donor reports must not give donors improper control.

---

# 16. Closure

A grant may be closed when:

- final report received;
- financial accountability complete;
- unused funds resolved;
- outcomes documented;
- concerns addressed;
- closure record filed.

Closure should identify lessons learned where useful.

---

# 17. Reporting to Board

The Foundation should provide grant accountability summaries to the appropriate board.

Summaries may include:

- grants approved;
- reports received;
- overdue reports;
- funds disbursed;
- unused funds;
- concerns;
- outcomes;
- risks;
- corrective actions.

---

# 18. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during Foundation launch and at least every three years once grant operations stabilize.
