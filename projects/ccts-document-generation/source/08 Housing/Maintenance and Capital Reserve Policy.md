# Maintenance and Capital Reserve Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for maintenance, repairs, inspections, capital planning, and reserve funding for Society housing.

The Society's housing ministry must provide safe, lawful, dignified, and well-stewarded housing. Maintenance is not a side task. It is a ministry responsibility with legal, financial, and moral weight.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society shall maintain housing as stewardship before God and care for neighbor.

Deferred maintenance may look like savings in the present and become expensive failure in the future. The Society should not confuse neglect with thrift.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Society-owned housing;
- leased housing managed by the Society;
- housing trust property;
- community land trust assets;
- long-term lease housing where the Society has maintenance responsibility;
- shared spaces;
- ministry housing;
- transitional housing;
- worker housing.

Local law and lease terms determine specific responsibilities.

---

# 4. Maintenance Categories

## 4.1 Emergency Maintenance

Immediate issues affecting life, safety, major property damage, essential services, or legal habitability.

## 4.2 Urgent Maintenance

Important issues requiring prompt attention but not immediate emergency response.

## 4.3 Routine Maintenance

Regular repairs, upkeep, and service requests.

## 4.4 Preventive Maintenance

Planned work to reduce future failure.

## 4.5 Capital Maintenance

Major repair or replacement of building systems or components.

---

# 5. Maintenance Responsibilities

The Society shall define maintenance responsibilities in leases, resident policies, and property management contracts.

Responsibilities may include:

- resident duties;
- property manager duties;
- Society duties;
- vendor duties;
- emergency contact process;
- reporting method;
- response targets;
- documentation requirements.

---

# 6. Resident Requests

Residents should have a clear way to submit maintenance requests.

Requests should be logged with:

- date received;
- unit or area;
- issue;
- urgency;
- action assigned;
- vendor if any;
- completion date;
- resident communication;
- cost;
- follow-up.

Residents should not need to chase five different people to report one leaking sink.

---

# 7. Response Priorities

Each housing ministry should define response targets.

Example categories:

| Category | Example | Expected Response |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | major leak, fire risk, no heat in dangerous weather | Immediate / same day |
| Urgent | appliance failure, security issue | Prompt |
| Routine | minor repair | Scheduled |
| Preventive | inspection item | Planned |
| Capital | roof, boiler, major system | Board-approved plan |

Local law may require specific timelines.

---

# 8. Inspections

The Society should conduct lawful and respectful inspections.

Inspection types may include:

- move-in inspection;
- move-out inspection;
- annual inspection;
- safety inspection;
- maintenance inspection;
- capital planning inspection;
- emergency inspection.

Resident privacy and notice requirements must be respected.

---

# 9. Preventive Maintenance Plan

Each housing property should have a preventive maintenance plan.

The plan may include:

- HVAC service;
- plumbing checks;
- roof inspection;
- fire safety systems;
- appliances;
- elevators if applicable;
- pest prevention;
- exterior maintenance;
- gutters and drainage;
- common areas;
- accessibility features.

---

# 10. Capital Reserve

The Society should establish a capital reserve for major repairs and replacements.

Capital reserve planning should consider:

- roof;
- windows;
- heating and cooling;
- plumbing;
- electrical;
- elevators;
- flooring;
- exterior envelope;
- accessibility upgrades;
- appliances;
- safety systems;
- parking and landscaping.

A housing ministry without reserves is often one broken boiler away from crisis.

---

# 11. Reserve Funding

Reserve funding may come from:

- rent or fees;
- designated donations;
- operating surplus;
- grants;
- capital campaign;
- board-approved allocation;
- insurance proceeds where applicable.

Restricted funds must be tracked.

---

# 12. Vendor Management

Vendors should be selected and managed responsibly.

The Society should consider:

- competence;
- licensing if required;
- insurance;
- cost;
- reliability;
- references;
- safety;
- resident interaction;
- conflicts of interest.

Vendor conflicts must be disclosed and managed.

---

# 13. Emergency Repairs

Emergency repair authority should be delegated in writing.

Delegation should define:

- who may authorize emergency spending;
- spending limits;
- reporting requirements;
- after-hours process;
- resident communication;
- documentation;
- board notification threshold.

No one should be unable to stop a flood because the committee meets next Thursday.

---

# 14. Safety and Habitability

The Society shall comply with applicable safety and habitability requirements.

Safety concerns must be escalated promptly.

Unsafe housing conditions are both legal and ministry concerns.

---

# 15. Financial Reporting

Maintenance and reserve reporting should include:

- routine repair costs;
- emergency repair costs;
- deferred maintenance;
- reserve balance;
- major upcoming repairs;
- vendor issues;
- budget variances;
- capital plan status.

---

# 16. Resident Communication

Residents should receive timely communication about:

- scheduled repairs;
- delays;
- access requirements;
- emergency issues;
- temporary service disruption;
- safety instructions;
- completion status.

Communication should be clear and respectful.

---

# 17. Documentation

Maintenance records should be retained securely.

Records may include:

- service requests;
- invoices;
- inspection reports;
- warranties;
- vendor contracts;
- photos where appropriate;
- safety reports;
- reserve plans;
- capital schedules.

---

# 18. Mission Alignment

Maintenance is part of mission alignment.

A housing ministry that speaks of dignity but ignores unsafe or degrading conditions is out of alignment.

Mission review should consider property condition, response times, resident concerns, reserves, and deferred maintenance.

---

# 19. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
