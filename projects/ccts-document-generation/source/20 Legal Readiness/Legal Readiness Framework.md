# Legal Readiness Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society approaches legal readiness before forming entities, recognizing national chapters, launching ministries, receiving funds, hiring people, acquiring property, making grants, operating housing, or communicating publicly in a jurisdiction.

This document is a governance framework. It is not legal advice.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should not treat legal compliance as an obstacle to mission. Legal readiness is one way to protect mission, people, churches, donors, leaders, and public witness.

Good intentions do not remove legal obligations.

---

# 3. Scope

This framework applies to:

- International Society governance;
- National Chapters;
- exploratory regions;
- formation teams;
- affiliated ministries;
- chaplaincy;
- housing;
- worker cooperative activity;
- Church Support Foundation activity;
- grants;
- fundraising;
- employment;
- volunteer management;
- public communications;
- data and privacy;
- property and leases;
- cross-border relationships.

---

# 4. Legal Review Triggers

Legal review should be considered before:

- incorporation;
- charity or nonprofit registration;
- public fundraising;
- donation receipting;
- grant-making;
- employment or contractor engagement;
- volunteer programs involving higher risk;
- housing operations;
- leases or property acquisition;
- worker cooperative launch;
- chaplaincy or spiritual care in regulated settings;
- cross-border fund transfers;
- public launch in a new country;
- privacy-sensitive data collection;
- safeguarding or serious incident response;
- contracts;
- partnership agreements;
- national chapter recognition.

---

# 5. Legal Readiness Areas

Legal readiness may include review of:

- entity formation;
- charitable or nonprofit purposes;
- bylaws and reserved powers;
- board liability;
- tax obligations;
- fundraising rules;
- donation receipting;
- grant-making;
- employment law;
- contractor classification;
- volunteer rules;
- privacy law;
- safeguarding and reporting duties;
- housing and tenancy law;
- property law;
- cooperative or enterprise law;
- insurance;
- contracts;
- intellectual property and brand use;
- public communications;
- cross-border transfers;
- record retention.

---

# 6. Relationship to Mission Alignment

Legal readiness and mission alignment must work together.

A legally possible structure may still be missionally unwise. A missionally desired activity may still require legal restructuring or delay.

Legal review should inform, but not replace, mission, theological, church advisory, financial, and governance review.

---

# 7. Relationship to Reserved Powers

Legal documents should preserve the Society's reserved powers where possible and lawful.

Reserved powers may need legal review in each jurisdiction so that:

- international doctrine is preserved;
- mission standards remain enforceable;
- national chapter recognition is meaningful;
- local adaptations are bounded;
- authority is clear;
- local legal duties are respected.

---

# 8. Legal Counsel

The Society should seek qualified legal counsel where risk, law, jurisdiction, or complexity requires it.

Legal counsel should be briefed clearly using [[Legal Counsel Engagement Brief]].

The Society should avoid asking lawyers vague questions and then treating vague answers as full review.

---

# 9. Legal Issue Register

Material legal issues should be tracked in [[Legal Issue Register]].

Legal issue tracking should include:

- issue;
- jurisdiction;
- related ministry;
- legal counsel if any;
- risk level;
- decision needed;
- due date;
- current status;
- related documents.

---

# 10. Legal Readiness Decision

Before proceeding with a legally significant action, the Society should decide whether the action is:

- Ready;
- Ready with Conditions;
- Not Ready;
- Needs Legal Review;
- Needs Board Decision;
- Needs International Approval;
- Prohibited or Too Risky.

---

# 11. Legal Advice and Confidentiality

Legal advice may be privileged or confidential.

Legal advice should be stored carefully and shared only with authorized persons.

Do not forward legal advice casually.

---

# 12. Local Chapters

National Chapters must comply with local law.

Local Chapters should not assume that international documents are automatically legally effective in their jurisdiction.

Local Chapters should record local legal adaptations in the relevant local adaptation register.

---

# 13. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually and whenever the Society enters a new jurisdiction, launches a high-risk ministry, or receives significant legal advice.
