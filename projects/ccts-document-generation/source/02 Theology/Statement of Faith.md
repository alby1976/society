# Statement of Faith

## Purpose

This Statement of Faith establishes the theological foundation of the Christian Community Transformation Society. It is a governing document, not merely a devotional appendix. The Society's mission, ministries, national chapters, affiliated organizations, grants, housing work, cooperative work, chaplaincy, and reporting systems must remain consistent with this Statement.

The Society exists to support faithful local churches and encourage Christian life throughout the week. It does not replace the church, administer sacraments, exercise church discipline, or assume ecclesiastical authority. Its work is governed by Scripture, shaped by Reformed theology, and ordered for the glory of God.

See also [[Definitions of Key Terms]], [[Mission Statement]], [[Vision Statement]], and [[Core Values]].

## Scripture

The Society believes that the Holy Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments are the written Word of God, inspired by the Holy Spirit, without error in all that they teach, and the supreme authority for faith, life, doctrine, worship, ethics, and governance.

All Society documents, decisions, ministries, and partnerships must be interpreted and evaluated under Scripture. No policy, board action, national chapter practice, or ministry objective may knowingly contradict Scripture.

## God

The Society believes in one living and true God, eternal, infinite, holy, wise, sovereign, gracious, just, and unchanging. God alone is Creator, Sustainer, Redeemer, and Judge.

God exists eternally in three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. These three are one God, the same in substance, equal in power and glory.

## Creation and Providence

God created all things out of nothing by the word of His power and governs all things by His wise providence.

Human work, housing, community life, mercy, education, stewardship, and public service are not outside God's concern. The Society may therefore support practical ministries while recognizing that all creation belongs to God and must be used for His glory.

## Humanity

Human beings are created in the image of God and therefore possess dignity, responsibility, and moral accountability.

The Society rejects every approach to charity that treats people merely as projects, numbers, clients, consumers, or economic units. People are neighbors made in God's image and must be treated with truth, compassion, patience, and respect.

## Sin and Human Need

Humanity fell into sin through Adam. Sin affects every person and every part of human life, including worship, family, work, economics, housing, community, justice, and institutions.

The Society's ministries must therefore avoid naive optimism. Poverty, loneliness, unemployment, housing insecurity, family breakdown, institutional weakness, and social fragmentation require compassion, but they also require truth, accountability, repentance, wisdom, and the Gospel.

## Jesus Christ

The Society believes that Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God, fully God and fully man, conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the virgin Mary, sinless in life, crucified for sinners, risen bodily from the dead, ascended into heaven, and reigning at the right hand of the Father.

Christ is the only Mediator between God and man. The Society's work must never obscure the centrality of Christ, reduce Christianity to moral improvement, or replace Gospel proclamation with social service alone.

## Salvation

Salvation is by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone, to the glory of God alone.

The Society affirms that sinners are justified by faith apart from works, adopted as children of God, sanctified by the Holy Spirit, preserved by God's grace, and called to walk in new obedience.

Good works do not earn salvation. They are the fruit of grace. The Society's service ministries therefore flow from the Gospel; they do not replace it.

## The Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit applies the redemption accomplished by Christ. He regenerates, convicts, sanctifies, comforts, gifts, and preserves believers.

The Society depends on the Holy Spirit for wisdom, holiness, unity, endurance, repentance, and fruitful service. Prayer is therefore not ornamental but necessary.

## The Church

Christ is Head of the Church. The visible church is the ordinary place where believers are gathered, taught, shepherded, disciplined, and nourished through the ministry of the Word, sacraments, and prayer.

The Society is not a church. It supports churches. It must not replace Lord's Day worship, pastoral oversight, elder rule, church discipline, or the sacraments.

Where the Society serves unchurched people, its aim is not to create permanent dependency on the Society but to encourage connection to faithful local churches.

## Worship and the Lord's Day

The Society affirms the priority of Lord's Day worship in the life of God's people. Its purpose is not to replace corporate worship but to encourage Lord's Day worship to shape the whole week.

## Christian Living

Believers are called to live before God in every area of life. Work, money, housing, family, education, mercy, leadership, speech, and institutional service must be brought under Christ's Lordship.

The Society's ministries should equip Christians to serve faithfully in ordinary life and support communities marked by truth, mercy, responsibility, and hope.

## Mercy, Justice, and Stewardship

Christian mercy is grounded in God's mercy to sinners in Christ. It should be compassionate without becoming sentimental, truthful without becoming harsh, and practical without becoming secularized.

The Society's housing, cooperative, foundation, and chaplaincy ministries must be governed by biblical stewardship and accountable governance.

## Last Things

Christ will return bodily and gloriously to judge the living and the dead, renew creation, and bring His people into everlasting joy.

The Society's work is hopeful but not utopian. It serves faithfully now while awaiting the fullness of Christ's kingdom.

## Confessional Identity

The Society is confessionally Reformed. It may recognize specific Reformed confessional standards for board members, theological officers, chaplains, teachers, or ministry leaders according to future policy.

Further detail belongs in [[Confessional Standards]] and [[Confessional Subscription]].

## Governance Authority

This Statement governs international governance, national chapter recognition, ministry approval, chaplaincy standards, church partnerships, grant-making, housing philosophy, worker cooperative formation, leadership development, and mission alignment reviews.

Any affiliated organization or national chapter that rejects this Statement cannot remain in good standing with the Society.

## Review

This Statement should be reviewed carefully before adoption and whenever the Society enters a new country, forms a national chapter, appoints theological officers, or approves a major ministry framework.
