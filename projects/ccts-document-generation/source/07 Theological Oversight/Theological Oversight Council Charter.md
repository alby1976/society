# Theological Oversight Council Charter

## 1. Purpose

This Charter establishes the Theological Oversight Council of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Council exists to assist the Society in preserving doctrinal fidelity, guarding against mission drift, reviewing theological questions, advising on chaplaincy and teaching standards, and supporting consistent application of the Society's [[Statement of Faith]] across national chapters and ministries.

The Council serves the Society's international theological integrity. It is not a church court and does not replace the authority of local churches.

This Charter should be read with [[Statement of Faith]], [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter]], [[Doctrinal Review Process]], [[Seminary Recognition Policy]], and [[Mission Alignment Review]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society's doctrine must not be protected by good intentions alone.

Theological oversight provides a disciplined process for reviewing doctrine, teaching, ministry proposals, chaplaincy standards, church partnership concerns, and mission drift risks before they become structural problems.

The Council exists to help the Society remain confessionally faithful while continuing practical ministry.

---

# 3. Nature of the Council

The Theological Oversight Council is an advisory and review body established under the authority of the International Board.

The Council may have authority to review, recommend, warn, or escalate according to this Charter and approved policy.

The Council does not ordinarily govern the Society unless a specific power is delegated to it by the International Board.

The Council is not:

- a local church;
- a presbytery;
- a classis;
- a consistory;
- a session;
- a synod;
- a replacement for church discipline;
- a substitute for local pastoral care;
- an independent theological court outside Society governance.

---

# 4. Core Mandate

The Council shall assist the Society by:

1. reviewing theological consistency;
2. advising on doctrinal questions;
3. reviewing proposed theological teaching materials where requested;
4. advising on chaplaincy standards;
5. advising on seminary recognition;
6. reviewing mission drift concerns;
7. supporting mission alignment reviews;
8. advising on church partnership concerns;
9. reviewing doctrinal implications of international expansion;
10. helping maintain consistency across translations and local adaptations;
11. advising the International Board when doctrine or mission is at risk.

---

# 5. Relationship to the International Board

The International Board retains governing authority.

The Council advises, reviews, and recommends. Its recommendations should be taken seriously, especially when they concern the Society's doctrine, chaplaincy, theological education, or mission drift.

The International Board should document any decision to reject a material theological recommendation of the Council.

Where governing documents require Council review before a decision, the Board should not bypass the process except in urgent circumstances, and any urgent bypass should be documented.

---

# 6. Relationship to the Church Advisory Fellowship

The Council and the [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]] have related but distinct roles.

The Church Advisory Fellowship provides churchly counsel, pastoral wisdom, local church perspective, and cooperative relationships.

The Council provides focused theological review, doctrinal analysis, and mission drift assessment.

The Council may consult the Church Advisory Fellowship when a matter involves local church relationships, pastoral care, chaplaincy, or ecclesiastical sensitivity.

---

# 7. Composition

The Council should include persons qualified by theological knowledge, confessional seriousness, church commitment, wisdom, and maturity.

Members may include:

- ministers;
- elders;
- theologians;
- seminary professors;
- experienced church leaders;
- chaplains meeting theological standards;
- qualified board-appointed theological advisors.

The Council should seek international perspective as the Society expands, while preserving doctrinal consistency.

---

# 8. Qualifications

Council members should ordinarily:

- affirm the Society's [[Statement of Faith]];
- be confessionally Reformed or substantially aligned with the Society's theological identity;
- belong to or be accountable within a faithful local church where reasonably possible;
- demonstrate maturity, humility, and careful judgment;
- be able to distinguish Society authority from church authority;
- respect confidentiality;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- support the Society's church-supporting mission;
- avoid factionalism and theological hobby-horse governance.

The Council needs theological seriousness, not theological peacocking.

---

# 9. Appointment

Council members should be appointed by the International Board or according to a process approved by the International Board.

The appointment process may include:

1. nomination;
2. review of theological qualifications;
3. review of church standing;
4. conflict disclosure;
5. interview or consultation;
6. recommendation;
7. board appointment;
8. term assignment;
9. orientation.

Terms should balance continuity and renewal.

---

# 10. Officers

The Council may have a chair, secretary, or other officers.

The chair should:

- prepare agendas;
- guide meetings;
- coordinate reviews;
- communicate with the International Board;
- ensure records are kept;
- protect confidentiality;
- identify conflicts;
- ensure recommendations are clear.

---

# 11. Areas Requiring Council Review

Council review may be required for:

- amendments to the [[Statement of Faith]];
- confessional subscription standards;
- chaplaincy qualifications;
- theological teaching materials;
- devotional materials used internationally;
- mission alignment concerns involving doctrine;
- church partnership concerns involving doctrine;
- seminary recognition;
- translation of doctrinal documents;
- national chapter doctrinal disputes;
- ministry proposals involving spiritual formation;
- theological objections raised by churches or members.

---

# 12. Review Outputs

The Council may issue:

- advisory memo;
- theological review report;
- recommendation;
- warning;
- request for revision;
- approval recommendation;
- non-approval recommendation;
- escalation request;
- minority report where appropriate.

Council outputs should distinguish between:

- biblical requirement;
- confessional requirement;
- prudential concern;
- pastoral concern;
- legal or governance concern outside Council expertise.

---

# 13. Meetings

The Council should meet as needed and at least annually during the founding period.

Meetings may include:

- prayer;
- review of requests;
- doctrinal review;
- chaplaincy standards review;
- seminary recognition review;
- mission alignment support;
- national chapter concerns;
- translation review;
- reporting to the International Board.

---

# 14. Confidentiality

Council members may receive sensitive information involving doctrine, church relationships, pastoral care, complaints, personnel, chaplaincy, national chapters, or mission alignment concerns.

Confidentiality must be protected.

Confidentiality must not be used to hide abuse, legal obligations, serious misconduct, or risks requiring escalation.

---

# 15. Conflicts of Interest

Council members must disclose conflicts of interest.

Conflicts may arise when a Council member:

- reviews their own church;
- reviews a seminary where they work;
- reviews a chaplain they supervise;
- reviews a grant applicant connected to them;
- reviews a national chapter they helped form;
- has denominational or personal interests in the outcome.

Conflict management should be documented.

---

# 16. Records

The Council should maintain appropriate records, including:

- agendas;
- attendance;
- matters reviewed;
- recommendations;
- reports;
- minority opinions where appropriate;
- conflicts disclosed;
- decisions referred to the Board;
- follow-up items.

Sensitive theological and pastoral records should be stored securely.

---

# 17. Limits

The Council shall not:

- govern the Society unless expressly delegated authority;
- appoint or remove directors;
- exercise church discipline;
- administer sacraments;
- override local church courts;
- direct national chapter operations outside approved authority;
- speak publicly for the Society without authorization;
- alter the Statement of Faith;
- create binding policy without approval.

---

# 18. Reporting to the Board

The Council should report to the International Board at least annually and whenever material theological concerns arise.

Reports may include:

- matters reviewed;
- recommendations;
- unresolved concerns;
- chaplaincy standard updates;
- seminary recognition recommendations;
- mission alignment concerns;
- doctrinal risks;
- prayer concerns.

---

# 19. Review

This Charter should be reviewed annually during the founding period and at least every three years once the Council is stable.
