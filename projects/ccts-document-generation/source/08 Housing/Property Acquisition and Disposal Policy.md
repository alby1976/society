# Property Acquisition and Disposal Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs the acquisition, acceptance, transfer, sale, encumbrance, disposal, or major restructuring of housing-related property by the Christian Community Transformation Society or its affiliated housing entities.

Property decisions can strengthen or endanger the Society's mission for decades. This policy requires prayerful, legal, financial, governance, and mission review before the Society acquires or disposes of housing property.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society shall not acquire property merely because it is available, donated, cheap, emotionally appealing, or strategically exciting.

The Society shall not dispose of property merely because it is inconvenient, profitable, administratively annoying, or temporarily burdensome.

Property is a stewardship decision, not a shiny object.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- land;
- residential buildings;
- mixed-use property;
- long-term leases;
- ground leases;
- community land trust assets;
- donated property;
- property held through a subsidiary;
- property held by a national chapter;
- major renovations tied to property acquisition;
- sale or transfer of housing property;
- mortgaging or encumbering property;
- changes to property mission restrictions.

---

# 4. Approval Authority

Approval authority must follow [[Reserved Powers Framework]], [[Delegated Authority Framework]], and [[Authority Matrix]].

The International Board may reserve approval over:

- major property acquisition;
- sale of mission-critical property;
- encumbrance of key assets;
- property outside existing national chapter authority;
- property using the Society's international identity;
- cross-border property structures;
- property gifts with significant restrictions;
- long-term lease models affecting international mission.

National chapters may receive delegated authority within approved limits.

---

# 5. Acquisition Review

Before acquiring property, the Society should review:

- mission fit;
- resident need;
- church and community context;
- legal structure;
- purchase price;
- appraised value;
- financing;
- debt obligations;
- operating budget;
- maintenance condition;
- capital needs;
- insurance;
- taxes or municipal charges;
- zoning;
- tenancy status;
- environmental concerns;
- accessibility;
- neighborhood impact;
- governance capacity;
- staffing capacity;
- risk.

---

# 6. Mission Review

Every acquisition must answer:

- How does this property serve the Society's mission?
- Who will be served?
- Why this property?
- Why now?
- What church relationships are relevant?
- What spiritual care or chaplaincy needs may arise?
- What mission drift risks are present?
- What happens if the project fails?

A property without a mission case is not ready.

---

# 7. Financial Review

The financial review should include:

- acquisition cost;
- closing costs;
- renovation costs;
- financing terms;
- debt service;
- projected rent or fees;
- subsidies;
- operating costs;
- maintenance reserve;
- capital reserve;
- vacancy assumptions;
- insurance;
- professional fees;
- tax implications;
- exit risks.

The board should see conservative projections, not only hopeful projections.

---

# 8. Legal Review

Legal counsel should review:

- title;
- liens;
- zoning;
- permitted use;
- lease obligations;
- tenant rights;
- environmental issues;
- charity law;
- tax law;
- financing documents;
- donor restrictions;
- long-term lease structure;
- liability;
- transfer restrictions;
- governance documents.

---

# 9. Property Gifts

Before accepting donated property, the Society should review:

- title and ownership;
- restrictions;
- liens;
- condition;
- environmental risk;
- maintenance burden;
- tax implications;
- donor expectations;
- mission fit;
- ability to use or dispose of property;
- effect on charity obligations.

A donated property may be a gift, a burden, or a gift-shaped burden.

---

# 10. Due Diligence Checklist

A property file should include:

- mission case;
- board approval record;
- legal review;
- financial review;
- inspection report;
- appraisal or valuation;
- title review;
- insurance review;
- risk assessment;
- conflict of interest disclosure;
- maintenance estimate;
- occupancy plan;
- affordability plan;
- reporting plan;
- exit plan.

---

# 11. Conflict of Interest

Property transactions carry high conflict risk.

Conflicts may involve:

- sellers;
- donors;
- board members;
- staff;
- contractors;
- realtors;
- churches;
- family members;
- lenders;
- property managers.

Conflicts must be disclosed, recorded, and managed before decision-making.

---

# 12. Disposal Review

Before selling, transferring, or disposing of property, the Society should review:

- reason for disposal;
- mission impact;
- resident impact;
- donor restrictions;
- legal restrictions;
- market value;
- alternative uses;
- financial impact;
- church relationship impact;
- public communication needs;
- board authority;
- reserved powers;
- use of proceeds.

Property disposal should not be treated as a routine administrative task.

---

# 13. Resident Impact

If disposal affects residents, the Society should consider:

- notice requirements;
- legal rights;
- relocation support;
- communication;
- pastoral or chaplaincy support;
- financial hardship;
- vulnerable residents;
- timing;
- public explanation.

Residents should not learn about major housing changes through rumors.

---

# 14. Use of Proceeds

Proceeds from sale or transfer should be used according to:

- donor restrictions;
- legal requirements;
- board approval;
- mission purpose;
- reserve needs;
- debt repayment;
- replacement housing strategy.

Funds from housing property should not be casually redirected without mission and legal review.

---

# 15. Documentation

All acquisition and disposal decisions must be documented.

Records should include:

- decision authority;
- minutes or resolution;
- due diligence;
- legal advice summary;
- financial analysis;
- conflicts disclosed;
- mission rationale;
- risk assessment;
- approval conditions;
- closing documents.

---

# 16. Mission Alignment Review

Major property decisions should trigger [[Mission Alignment Review]].

Review should occur before acquisition where possible and after implementation once enough information exists.

---

# 17. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing development and whenever the Society considers major housing property action.
