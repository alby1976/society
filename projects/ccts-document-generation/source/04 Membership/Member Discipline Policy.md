# Member Discipline Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the process by which the Christian Community Transformation Society may correct, suspend, restrict, or remove a member when necessary to protect the Society's mission, witness, doctrine, governance, beneficiaries, staff, volunteers, churches, chapters, or affiliated organizations.

This policy concerns organizational discipline only. It is not church discipline and must not be treated as a substitute for the authority of a faithful local church.

This policy should be read with [[Membership Framework]], [[Membership Categories]], [[Membership Covenant]], [[Membership Application and Admission Policy]], and [[Membership Review and Termination Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Member discipline should be truthful, fair, proportionate, documented, and mission-protecting.

The goal is correction where possible and protection where necessary.

Discipline must not be used for personal retaliation, factional control, avoidance of hard conversations, or silencing proper concerns.

---

# 3. Organizational Nature of Discipline

The Society may discipline a member only regarding the person's relationship to the Society.

The Society may:

- issue warnings;
- require correction;
- restrict participation;
- suspend membership rights;
- remove a person from membership;
- restrict volunteer or leadership roles;
- refer matters to a local church where appropriate;
- report matters to civil authorities where required by law.

The Society may not:

- administer church discipline;
- declare a person excommunicated;
- replace pastoral oversight;
- require a church to act;
- use membership discipline to control a local church.

---

# 4. Grounds for Discipline

Grounds for discipline may include:

1. serious breach of the [[Membership Covenant]];
2. public opposition to the Society's doctrine or mission;
3. conduct that materially harms the Society's witness;
4. abuse, harassment, intimidation, or exploitation;
5. financial misconduct;
6. misuse of Society property or funds;
7. breach of confidentiality;
8. undisclosed or unmanaged conflict of interest;
9. false statements in application or reporting;
10. divisive conduct that undermines governance;
11. persistent refusal to follow approved policy;
12. conduct creating significant risk to vulnerable people;
13. misuse of the Society's name;
14. unauthorized representation of the Society;
15. failure to meet membership eligibility requirements;
16. legal conduct affecting the Society's mission or safety;
17. serious mission misalignment.

Minor disagreement, honest questions, or good-faith criticism are not grounds for discipline. A Society that disciplines people for asking hard questions is not protecting governance; it is growing mushrooms in the basement.

---

# 5. Informal Correction

Where appropriate, concerns should first be addressed informally.

Informal correction may include:

- private conversation;
- clarification of expectations;
- pastoral encouragement;
- referral to supervisor or ministry leader;
- written reminder;
- training;
- mediation;
- temporary pause in activity.

Informal correction is not required where the matter is serious, unsafe, urgent, legal, financial, or likely to compromise evidence or protection.

---

# 6. Formal Discipline Process

A formal discipline process should normally include:

1. written concern or complaint;
2. preliminary review;
3. determination of authority;
4. notice to the member where appropriate;
5. opportunity for the member to respond;
6. evidence review;
7. conflict of interest review;
8. decision by authorized body;
9. written outcome;
10. appeal information if applicable;
11. recordkeeping;
12. follow-up or restoration plan where appropriate.

---

# 7. Preliminary Review

A preliminary review should determine:

- whether the concern falls under this policy;
- whether immediate protective action is required;
- who has authority to handle the matter;
- whether legal, safeguarding, pastoral, or employment issues are involved;
- whether the matter should be referred elsewhere;
- whether conflicts of interest exist;
- whether confidentiality protections are required.

---

# 8. Immediate Protective Action

The Society may take immediate temporary action when necessary to protect people, property, records, funds, confidential information, mission, or legal standing.

Temporary action may include:

- temporary suspension of membership rights;
- temporary removal from volunteer duty;
- temporary restriction from events;
- restriction of access to records or systems;
- pause on leadership responsibilities;
- temporary communication restrictions.

Temporary action is not a final finding.

---

# 9. Notice to the Member

Unless notice would create serious risk, compromise safety, violate law, or interfere with an investigation, the member should receive notice of:

- the concern;
- relevant policy;
- process to be followed;
- opportunity to respond;
- possible outcomes;
- confidentiality expectations;
- contact person.

---

# 10. Member Response

The member should have reasonable opportunity to respond.

The response may be written or verbal, depending on the seriousness of the matter and the process chosen.

The Society should consider the response honestly.

---

# 11. Decision-Making Authority

Decision-making authority should follow the [[Authority Matrix]], bylaws, and membership policy.

Possible decision-makers include:

- National Executive Director;
- National Board;
- International Executive Director;
- International Board;
- membership committee;
- authorized discipline panel;
- board committee with delegated authority.

Serious cases involving doctrine, mission alignment, national chapter status, board members, officers, or public risk should be escalated.

---

# 12. Possible Outcomes

Outcomes may include:

- no action;
- informal warning;
- written warning;
- required training;
- apology or correction;
- restriction from specific role;
- suspension of membership rights;
- suspension from volunteer service;
- removal from leadership role;
- removal from membership;
- referral to local church;
- referral to civil authority where required;
- restoration plan;
- monitoring period.

Outcomes should be proportionate to the issue.

---

# 13. Appeal

A member may appeal a formal disciplinary decision if an appeal is allowed by policy or bylaws.

Appeals should be based on:

- procedural unfairness;
- new evidence;
- conflict of interest;
- disproportionate outcome;
- mistake of fact;
- misapplication of policy.

Appeals should not be used merely to repeat disagreement.

---

# 14. Confidentiality

Discipline matters should be handled confidentially.

Information should be shared only with those who need it for governance, safety, legal, pastoral, employment, or reporting reasons.

Confidentiality must not be used to hide wrongdoing, silence victims, conceal abuse, or prevent lawful reporting.

---

# 15. Relationship to Local Churches

Where a matter involves spiritual care, church membership, pastoral concerns, or conduct affecting a member's church life, the Society may encourage the member to involve local church leadership.

The Society may notify a local church only when lawful, appropriate, and consistent with privacy, safety, and policy.

The Society should not attempt to direct church discipline.

---

# 16. Records

Discipline records should be retained according to records retention policy.

Records may include:

- complaint;
- notes;
- evidence;
- notices;
- response;
- decisions;
- appeal;
- outcome;
- restoration plan;
- final closure.

Serious cases should be cross-referenced in the [[Decision Register]] where governance-significant.

---

# 17. Restoration

Where appropriate, the Society should seek restoration.

Restoration may include:

- confession or acknowledgment;
- restitution;
- training;
- supervision;
- probationary period;
- pastoral support;
- reapplication;
- gradual return to service.

Restoration should not be rushed where safety, trust, or serious misconduct is involved.

---

# 18. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually with [[Membership Framework]], [[Membership Covenant]], and [[Membership Review and Termination Policy]].
