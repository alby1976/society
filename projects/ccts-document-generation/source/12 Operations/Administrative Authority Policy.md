# Administrative Authority Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines ordinary administrative authority within the Christian Community Transformation Society.

It clarifies who may act, approve, sign, communicate, spend, record, and escalate on behalf of the Society under delegated authority.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Administrative authority must be clear before action is taken.

The Society should avoid two opposite errors:

- unauthorized people making commitments;
- authorized people being unable to complete ordinary work because authority is unclear.

Order protects both mission and people.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- International Society administration;
- National Chapter administration;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- officers;
- ministry leaders;
- committee chairs;
- project leads;
- chaplains;
- housing managers;
- cooperative managers;
- Foundation directors;
- authorized advisors.

---

# 4. Authority Sources

Administrative authority may come from:

- International Constitution;
- bylaws;
- board resolution;
- role description;
- employment agreement;
- volunteer role description;
- committee charter;
- delegated authority framework;
- Authority Matrix;
- approved budget;
- written policy;
- supervisor instruction within authority.

Informal preference does not create authority.

---

# 5. Administrative Roles

The Society may designate administrative roles such as:

- Board Chair;
- Secretary;
- Treasurer;
- Executive Director;
- Operations Manager;
- Records Officer;
- Ministry Coordinator;
- Finance Officer;
- Chapter Administrator;
- Document Owner;
- Report Reviewer.

Each role should have written duties and limits.

---

# 6. Routine Administrative Authority

Routine administrative authority may include:

- scheduling meetings;
- preparing agendas;
- collecting reports;
- maintaining records;
- updating registries;
- distributing approved documents;
- tracking action items;
- processing approved expenses;
- coordinating approved events;
- communicating approved information.

Routine authority does not include power to create major obligations.

---

# 7. Signing Authority

Signing authority must be defined.

Documents requiring signing authority may include:

- contracts;
- leases;
- bank documents;
- grant agreements;
- employment agreements;
- government filings;
- donor agreements;
- property documents;
- public statements;
- recognition letters.

No person should sign on behalf of the Society without written authority.

---

# 8. Spending Authority

Spending authority must follow approved budgets and limits.

Spending authority should define:

- approval threshold;
- budget line;
- documentation required;
- receipt requirement;
- conflict review;
- emergency exception;
- reporting duty.

Spending because "it was necessary" still needs documentation.

---

# 9. Communication Authority

Communication authority must be clear.

The Society should define who may communicate:

- internally;
- to donors;
- to churches;
- to government agencies;
- to media;
- to grant recipients;
- to residents;
- to workers;
- to volunteers;
- to the public.

Public communications require special care.

---

# 10. Records Authority

Only authorized persons should create, edit, archive, or delete official records.

Records authority should define:

- source of truth;
- document owner;
- version control;
- archive process;
- approval process;
- access permissions;
- deletion restrictions;
- retention rules.

---

# 11. Emergency Administrative Authority

Emergency authority may be granted for urgent situations.

Emergency authority should define:

- who may act;
- what situations qualify;
- spending limit;
- notification requirement;
- documentation requirement;
- follow-up approval;
- legal or safety escalation.

Emergency action should be reported as soon as practical.

---

# 12. Prohibited Administrative Acts

No person may administratively:

- alter doctrine;
- alter mission;
- change reserved powers;
- create unauthorized debt;
- sign unauthorized contracts;
- make unauthorized public statements;
- approve conflicted benefits;
- delete official records improperly;
- promise grants without approval;
- admit residents without authority;
- hire or terminate staff without authority;
- bind a chapter beyond delegated limits.

---

# 13. Conflict of Interest

Administrative decisions may involve conflicts.

A conflicted person should not approve, sign, process, or record a decision in a way that hides the conflict.

Conflicts must be disclosed and managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 14. Delegation

Administrative authority may be delegated if permitted.

Delegation should be:

- written;
- limited;
- time-bound where appropriate;
- clear about scope;
- clear about reporting;
- revocable;
- recorded.

A delegated task does not remove the delegator's responsibility to supervise.

---

# 15. Review and Revocation

Administrative authority may be reviewed, limited, suspended, or revoked for:

- misuse;
- confusion;
- role change;
- conflict of interest;
- failure to report;
- legal concern;
- financial concern;
- breach of confidentiality;
- mission drift;
- board decision.
