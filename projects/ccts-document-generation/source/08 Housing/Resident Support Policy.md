# Resident Support Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for supporting residents in Society-related housing.

Resident support is the practical, relational, spiritual, and referral-based care offered to residents while respecting legal rights, privacy, dignity, personal responsibility, and local church authority.

Resident support is not control. It is not surveillance. It is not a substitute family, church, therapist, lawyer, bank, or government program.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should support residents as neighbors with dignity and responsibility.

Support should help residents move toward stability, community, wise stewardship, and appropriate church connection where possible.

Support should not create unhealthy dependency, coercion, favoritism, or confusion about the Society's role.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- housing managers;
- resident support coordinators;
- chaplains;
- approved volunteers;
- ministry leaders;
- housing committee members;
- national chapter housing personnel;
- external partners providing resident support on behalf of the Society.

---

# 4. Types of Resident Support

Resident support may include:

- move-in orientation;
- maintenance coordination;
- practical problem-solving;
- conflict support;
- community life support;
- church referral;
- chaplaincy referral;
- prayer where welcomed;
- crisis referral;
- financial counseling referral;
- employment or cooperative referral;
- family support referral;
- accessibility support;
- language or cultural support where possible.

The Society should define which supports are available in each housing program.

---

# 5. Limits of Support

Resident support must remain within authorized role and competence.

Society personnel must not provide unqualified:

- legal advice;
- medical advice;
- mental health treatment;
- financial planning;
- immigration advice;
- professional counseling;
- child protection assessment;
- domestic violence intervention;
- emergency response beyond immediate safety steps.

Referral is often the faithful action.

---

# 6. Resident Support Plan

Where appropriate, a resident support plan may be created.

A support plan may include:

- resident goals;
- identified needs;
- referrals;
- agreed follow-up;
- support persons;
- confidentiality level;
- review date;
- risk concerns;
- responsibilities of resident and Society.

Support plans should be collaborative and respectful.

---

# 7. Spiritual Care

Residents may be offered spiritual care according to [[Spiritual Care Policy]].

Spiritual care may include:

- prayer;
- listening;
- Scripture encouragement;
- chaplaincy visit;
- church referral;
- devotional opportunity;
- crisis spiritual care.

Spiritual care should be voluntary unless an approved lawful program requirement provides otherwise.

Housing need must never be used to force spiritual disclosure.

---

# 8. Church Referral

Residents may be referred to faithful local churches according to [[Church Referral Policy]].

The Society should especially consider referral where a resident:

- requests church connection;
- is unchurched;
- has moved to a new area;
- needs pastoral care;
- needs Christian community;
- is experiencing spiritual distress;
- is disconnected from church.

The Society must not replace local church pastoral care.

---

# 9. Resident Check-Ins

Resident check-ins may be used to support stability and community.

Check-ins should be:

- clearly explained;
- respectful;
- not excessive;
- appropriate to program type;
- documented only as needed;
- focused on support and housing stability.

Residents should know whether check-ins are voluntary, program-based, or required by agreement.

---

# 10. Support for Vulnerable Residents

Some residents may require additional care due to vulnerability, disability, age, language barriers, family stress, trauma, poverty, grief, immigration stress, or other needs.

The Society should respond with dignity and appropriate referral.

The Society must avoid paternalism, exploitation, or pressure.

---

# 11. Boundaries

Resident support providers must maintain boundaries.

They should avoid:

- favoritism;
- secrecy;
- financial entanglement;
- personal dependency;
- private unsupervised high-risk care;
- promises beyond authority;
- accepting inappropriate gifts;
- spiritual manipulation;
- dual relationships that compromise fairness.

---

# 12. Confidentiality

Resident support information must be protected.

Only necessary information should be recorded or shared.

Information may be shared when authorized, required by law, necessary for safety, required for safeguarding, needed for proper housing management, or approved for referral.

---

# 13. Documentation

Resident support documentation should be minimal, accurate, respectful, and useful.

Documentation may include:

- date of contact;
- general issue;
- support offered;
- referral made;
- follow-up needed;
- risk level;
- person responsible;
- confidentiality level.

Sensitive details should not be recorded unless necessary.

---

# 14. Escalation

Resident support concerns should be escalated when they involve:

- safety risk;
- abuse or exploitation;
- serious conflict;
- rent or arrears crisis;
- legal threat;
- possible eviction;
- safeguarding issue;
- serious mental health concern;
- mission alignment concern;
- staff or volunteer misconduct;
- privacy breach.

Escalation should follow proper authority and confidentiality rules.

---

# 15. Reporting

Resident support themes should be reported through [[Housing Manager 5-15 Report]] and other housing reports.

Reports should protect identity where possible and focus on themes, risks, decisions needed, and support requirements.

---

# 16. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
