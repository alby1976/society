# Capacity-Building Grants Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs capacity-building grants made by the Church Support Foundation.

Capacity-building grants are grants intended to strengthen the long-term ability of a church, ministry, national chapter, or approved organization to serve faithfully, govern wisely, report accurately, and sustain mission-aligned work.

---

# 2. Definition

A Capacity-Building Grant is a grant that helps an approved recipient increase durable ministry capacity rather than merely pay for a one-time activity.

Capacity may include:

- leadership development;
- governance systems;
- training;
- reporting infrastructure;
- financial controls;
- legal or compliance readiness;
- theological education;
- operational systems;
- volunteer development;
- ministry tools;
- documentation;
- translation;
- technology;
- safeguarding systems;
- church support infrastructure.

---

# 3. Governing Principle

Capacity-building should strengthen faithfulness, not create dependency.

A good capacity-building grant helps a recipient become more capable, accountable, and mission-aligned. It should not create a permanent need for outside rescue.

---

# 4. Eligible Recipients

Eligible recipients may include:

- faithful local churches;
- church-connected ministries;
- national chapters;
- Society ministries;
- approved nonprofits;
- ministry training programs;
- theological education partners;
- pilot cooperatives where mission-aligned;
- housing or chaplaincy ministries needing governance capacity.

Eligibility must be lawful and mission-aligned.

---

# 5. Eligible Uses

Capacity-building grants may support:

- governance training;
- board development;
- policy development;
- reporting systems;
- accounting setup;
- audit or review readiness;
- legal compliance support;
- volunteer training;
- leadership development;
- technology tools;
- translation of core documents;
- safeguarding training;
- ministry planning;
- theological training;
- grant management training;
- operational manuals.

---

# 6. Ineligible Uses

Capacity-building grants should not be used for:

- ordinary recurring expenses without capacity outcome;
- indefinite salary support;
- private benefit;
- personal luxury expenses;
- unclear consulting;
- projects contrary to doctrine;
- political partisan activity;
- unaccountable cash support;
- debt repayment unless specifically approved;
- activities that undermine local church authority.

---

# 7. Application Requirements

Applications should explain:

- current capacity gap;
- proposed capacity-building activity;
- why the activity matters;
- requested amount;
- budget;
- timeline;
- responsible leader;
- expected outcomes;
- reporting plan;
- sustainability plan;
- risks;
- conflicts of interest.

---

# 8. Review Criteria

Applications should be reviewed according to:

- mission fit;
- durable capacity benefit;
- clarity of plan;
- realistic budget;
- recipient accountability;
- leadership readiness;
- doctrinal alignment;
- church-supporting purpose;
- sustainability;
- legal compliance;
- risk;
- ability to report.

---

# 9. Outcomes

Each capacity-building grant should define outcomes.

Outcomes may include:

- board trained;
- policy adopted;
- accounting system implemented;
- reporting template used;
- safeguarding process launched;
- volunteers trained;
- theological training completed;
- legal compliance achieved;
- documentation translated;
- governance structure improved.

Outcomes should be specific enough to review.

---

# 10. Reporting

Recipients should submit reports showing:

- funds used;
- activities completed;
- outcomes achieved;
- challenges;
- lessons learned;
- next steps;
- remaining needs;
- receipts or financial summary where required.

Reports should focus on capacity gained, not only money spent.

---

# 11. Sustainability

Applications should explain how the capacity will be maintained after the grant.

Sustainability may include:

- trained people;
- adopted policies;
- recurring budget;
- local leadership;
- volunteer structure;
- documentation;
- software ownership;
- integration into governance.

A grant that creates a system nobody can maintain is a very expensive binder.

---

# 12. Multiple-Year Support

Multi-year capacity-building support may be approved where appropriate.

Multi-year support should include:

- annual review;
- milestones;
- funding conditions;
- reporting;
- exit or transition plan;
- board approval;
- mission alignment review where needed.

---

# 13. Conflicts of Interest

Capacity-building grants may involve consultants, trainers, churches, or organizations connected to decision-makers.

Conflicts must be disclosed and managed.

---

# 14. Mission Alignment

Capacity-building grants should be reviewed for mission alignment.

Review should ask:

- Does this strengthen faithful ministry?
- Does this preserve church-supporting boundaries?
- Does this increase accountability?
- Does this reduce dependency?
- Does this improve governance?
- Does this protect doctrine and mission?
- Is the recipient capable of using the grant well?

---

# 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during Foundation launch and at least every three years once grant operations stabilize.
