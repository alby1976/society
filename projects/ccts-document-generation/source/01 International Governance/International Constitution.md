# International Constitution

## Article 1 — Name

The name of the international body shall be the **Christian Community Transformation Society**, referred to in this Constitution as the **Society**.

The Society may operate through national chapters, affiliated organizations, ministries, foundations, cooperatives, trusts, and other lawful structures approved under this Constitution and related governance documents.

## Article 2 — Foundational Allegiance

The Society confesses that Jesus Christ is Lord and Head of His Church. The Society exists under His authority and for His glory.

The Holy Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments are the supreme written authority for the Society's faith, doctrine, morals, mission, and governance.

No constitution, bylaw, policy, charter, board decision, national chapter practice, ministry framework, grant, lease, cooperative arrangement, or partnership may knowingly contradict Scripture or the Society's adopted [[Statement of Faith]].

## Article 3 — Theological Identity

The Society is confessionally Reformed and shall preserve that identity through its doctrine, governance, leadership standards, ministry practices, partnerships, and international expansion.

The Society's theological identity is governed by:

- [[Statement of Faith]]
- [[Definitions of Key Terms]]
- [[Mission Statement]]
- [[Vision Statement]]
- [[Core Values]]
- future confessional subscription policies adopted by the International Board

The Society shall not permit any national chapter or affiliated organization to alter the Society's doctrine, mission, or church-supporting identity.

## Article 4 — Nature of the Society

The Society is a Christian charitable and ministry society established to advance the Christian religion through church-supporting ministries, discipleship, mercy, meaningful work, affordable housing, leadership development, chaplaincy, education, grants, institutional stewardship, and community transformation.

The Society is not a church.

The Society shall not:

- administer sacraments;
- exercise church discipline;
- replace lawful pastors, elders, or church courts;
- replace Lord's Day corporate worship;
- establish itself as a parallel ecclesiastical authority;
- sever mercy ministry from the Gospel;
- permit mission drift for financial, cultural, political, or organizational convenience.

## Article 5 — Mission

The mission of the Society is:

> To advance the Christian religion by supporting faithful local churches and developing ministries that demonstrate the Gospel through discipleship, mercy, meaningful work, affordable housing, leadership development, and community transformation under the Lordship of Jesus Christ.

All activities of the Society shall be interpreted and governed in light of this mission.

## Article 6 — Vision

The vision of the Society is:

> Communities transformed by the Gospel as faithful churches equip believers to glorify God in every area of life throughout the week.

The Society seeks to serve across nations and generations while preserving one theological foundation, one mission, one constitutional identity, and one international governance framework.

## Article 7 — Core Values

The Society shall conduct its governance and ministry according to the values set out in [[Core Values]], including:

- Christ-centered faithfulness;
- biblical authority;
- prayerful dependence;
- church-supporting ministry;
- mercy with truth;
- stewardship;
- meaningful work;
- community and hospitality;
- accountability;
- international unity with local wisdom;
- institutional memory;
- excellence without pride.

## Article 8 — International Character

The Society is international by design.

The Society may begin in one country, but its constitutional identity is not limited to that country. Canada may serve as the founding national chapter without becoming the theological, constitutional, or governance center of the Society.

The international center of the Society is its confession of Christ, its submission to Scripture, its adopted doctrine, and its constitutional mission.

## Article 9 — National Chapters

The Society may recognize national chapters to carry out the Society's mission in specific countries.

Each national chapter shall:

1. affirm the Society's [[Statement of Faith]];
2. adopt or submit to this Constitution to the extent permitted by local law;
3. comply with the [[International Governance Framework]];
4. preserve the Society's mission and core values;
5. maintain lawful local governance;
6. submit required reports;
7. respect reserved powers of the International Society;
8. operate only within delegated authority;
9. maintain appropriate relationships with faithful local churches;
10. participate in mission alignment review.

A national chapter may adopt local bylaws required by civil law, provided those bylaws do not contradict this Constitution or the Society's reserved powers.

## Article 10 — Affiliated Organizations

The Society may create, recognize, own, sponsor, or oversee affiliated organizations, including but not limited to:

- housing trusts;
- worker cooperatives;
- church support foundations;
- chaplaincy ministries;
- training institutes;
- charitable corporations;
- local ministry entities;
- regional ministry bodies.

Affiliated organizations exist to serve the Society's mission and shall remain subject to the governance standards, reserved powers, and mission alignment review processes adopted by the Society.

No affiliated organization may become independent in doctrine, mission, or governance in a way that undermines the Society's identity.

## Article 11 — International Board

The Society shall be governed internationally by an International Board.

The International Board is responsible for:

- guarding the Society's doctrine and mission;
- maintaining this Constitution;
- approving international governance frameworks;
- recognizing national chapters;
- exercising reserved powers;
- appointing or recognizing key international officers and councils;
- approving major affiliated structures;
- overseeing international strategy;
- ensuring institutional memory;
- protecting the Society from mission drift;
- receiving reports from national chapters and international officers;
- approving amendments according to this Constitution.

The International Board shall govern as stewards, not owners. Its authority is fiduciary, theological, missional, and constitutional.

## Article 12 — Officers

The Society may appoint officers necessary to carry out its international mission, including an International Executive Director and other officers defined by policy.

Officers serve under delegated authority and are accountable to the International Board or other authority designated by the Society's governance documents.

No officer may exercise reserved powers unless expressly authorized by this Constitution or International Board resolution.

## Article 13 — International Church Advisory Fellowship

The Society may establish an International Church Advisory Fellowship to provide churchly counsel, wisdom, cooperation, and accountability.

The Fellowship is advisory unless specific authority is granted by the International Board.

The Fellowship shall not exercise ecclesiastical authority over churches and shall not replace lawful church courts, sessions, consistories, presbyteries, classes, synods, or assemblies.

## Article 14 — Theological Oversight Council

The Society may establish a Theological Oversight Council to assist in preserving doctrinal fidelity, reviewing theological concerns, advising on mission drift, and supporting doctrinal consistency across national chapters and ministries.

The Council's authority, composition, standards, appointment process, and review procedures shall be defined by charter.

## Article 15 — Stewardship Advisors

The Society may appoint Stewardship Advisors to preserve institutional memory and advise current leaders.

Stewardship Advisors may include founders, former board members, former officers, or other experienced persons.

Stewardship Advisors do not govern unless separately appointed to a governing office. Their ordinary role is advisory, historical, and mentoring.

## Article 16 — Membership

The Society may establish international, national, voting, supporting, founding, honorary, church partner, or other membership categories through bylaws or membership policy.

Membership requirements shall preserve mission alignment and theological integrity.

Membership in the Society shall not be treated as membership in a church.

## Article 17 — Reserved Powers

Certain powers shall be reserved to the International Society and may not be exercised independently by national chapters or affiliated organizations.

Reserved powers include, at minimum:

- amendment of this Constitution;
- alteration of the [[Statement of Faith]];
- alteration of the Society's mission;
- recognition, suspension, or dissolution of national chapters;
- approval of major international affiliated entities;
- approval of international theological standards;
- final determination of mission alignment disputes;
- protection of the Society's name and identity;
- approval of international governance standards;
- approval of dissolution or merger of the International Society.

Additional reserved powers may be defined in [[Reserved Powers Framework]].

## Article 18 — Delegated Authority

The International Board may delegate authority to national chapters, officers, committees, councils, ministries, and affiliated organizations.

Delegated authority must be:

- documented;
- limited;
- reviewable;
- revocable where necessary;
- consistent with this Constitution;
- subject to mission alignment.

Delegation does not transfer ultimate constitutional responsibility from the International Board.

## Article 19 — Local Church Relationships

The Society exists to support faithful local churches.

The Society shall encourage beneficiaries, residents, workers, students, volunteers, and members to participate in faithful local churches where appropriate.

The Society shall not undermine local church leadership or encourage people to substitute Society activities for ordinary church life.

## Article 20 — Ministries

The Society may pursue its mission through ministries approved under Society governance.

Every ministry must:

- serve the mission;
- remain consistent with the Statement of Faith;
- operate under approved authority;
- report according to policy;
- steward resources faithfully;
- protect vulnerable people;
- avoid mission drift;
- undergo review when required.

## Article 21 — Reporting and Institutional Memory

The Society shall maintain reporting systems to support accountability, communication, prayer, institutional memory, and governance review.

The Society may use 5-15 reports, monthly reports, quarterly reports, annual reports, board reports, ministry reports, and mission alignment reviews.

Reports that document major decisions, crises, theological matters, legal issues, financial concerns, or institutional milestones shall be preserved according to records retention policy.

## Article 22 — Decision Register and Document Registry

The Society shall maintain a [[Decision Register]] and [[Document Registry]].

The Decision Register shall record major governance decisions, reasons, alternatives considered, approving authority, and related documents.

The Document Registry shall identify official documents, versions, status, authority level, review dates, dependencies, and related documents.

These registries are part of the Society's institutional memory.

## Article 23 — Financial Stewardship

The Society shall manage all resources as gifts entrusted by God.

The Society shall maintain financial controls, budgeting practices, reserve policies, gift acceptance standards, audit or review practices where appropriate, and transparent reporting.

No national chapter or affiliated organization may use funds in a way that knowingly contradicts the Society's mission, donor restrictions, law, or approved policy.

## Article 24 — Risk and Accountability

The Society shall maintain risk management systems appropriate to its international and charitable work.

Risks include theological, legal, financial, operational, reputational, pastoral, safeguarding, governance, privacy, and mission drift risks.

The Society shall prefer early identification and correction of risks over concealment.

## Article 25 — Amendments

This Constitution may be amended only by the process established by the International Board and any applicable law.

At minimum, constitutional amendment shall require:

1. written proposal;
2. review against Scripture and the Statement of Faith;
3. review of mission impact;
4. review of implications for national chapters;
5. reasonable notice to required governing bodies;
6. approval by the required supermajority;
7. recording in the Decision Register;
8. publication of the amended version;
9. archival preservation of the prior version.

No amendment may remove the Society's submission to Scripture, deny its Statement of Faith, redefine the Society as a church, or sever the Society from its church-supporting mission.

## Article 26 — Dissolution

If the International Society is dissolved, remaining assets shall be distributed only to organizations consistent with the Society's religious and charitable purposes and applicable law.

Dissolution shall require careful theological, legal, financial, and governance review.

The Society's permanent records, where lawful, should be preserved for institutional and historical memory.

## Article 27 — Interpretation

This Constitution shall be interpreted in harmony with Scripture, the Statement of Faith, the Mission Statement, the Vision Statement, Core Values, and Definitions of Key Terms.

Where ambiguity arises, interpretation shall prefer the meaning that best preserves:

- fidelity to Christ;
- submission to Scripture;
- support for faithful local churches;
- international mission unity;
- constitutional continuity;
- accountability;
- protection against mission drift.

## Article 28 — Transitional Provision

Until the full international governance structure is established, the founding board or founding governance body may exercise necessary interim authority, provided such authority is exercised consistently with this Constitution, the Statement of Faith, and the Society's mission.

Temporary founding arrangements should be replaced by regular constitutional governance as soon as reasonably possible.

## Article 29 — Review

This Constitution shall be reviewed annually during the founding period and at least every three years after the Society reaches stable international governance.

Review does not require amendment. Sometimes the best amendment is the one not made. Governance needs wisdom, not fidgeting.
