# 5-15 Report Confidentiality Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how confidentiality is handled in 5-15 reports.

The Society's 5-15 reports may include ministry updates, risks, prayer requests, pastoral themes, personnel concerns, housing issues, financial concerns, church relationship matters, grant information, and other sensitive material.

This policy protects people, preserves trust, supports lawful compliance, and ensures necessary information reaches the right authority.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Share enough to govern, protect, and pray wisely. Do not share more than is needed.

Confidentiality is not secrecy. It is stewardship of information.

Secrecy hides what should be known. Gossip spreads what should be protected. Faithful confidentiality avoids both ditches.

---

# 3. Confidentiality Levels

5-15 reports should be marked with one of the following levels.

| Level | Use |
|---|---|
| Public | Suitable for public summary |
| Internal | Suitable for Society workers and authorized leaders |
| Restricted | Limited to designated supervisors or leaders |
| Confidential | Sensitive personal, pastoral, legal, employment, housing, grant, or financial information |
| Board Confidential | Limited to board or designated governance authority |

The default level for ordinary internal 5-15 reports is Internal.

---

# 4. Public Information

Public information may include:

- general ministry accomplishments;
- public events;
- non-sensitive statistics;
- general thanksgiving;
- public prayer requests;
- approved announcements.

Public information should still be accurate and mission-aligned.

---

# 5. Internal Information

Internal information may include ordinary updates about:

- ministry activity;
- volunteer coordination;
- staff workload;
- upcoming work;
- routine challenges;
- resource needs;
- general prayer requests;
- non-sensitive risks.

Internal information should not include unnecessary personal details.

---

# 6. Restricted Information

Restricted information may include:

- early conflict concerns;
- supervision matters;
- sensitive operational risks;
- unresolved church relationship concerns;
- resident support concerns;
- grant review concerns;
- chaplaincy themes requiring leadership awareness.

Access should be limited to those responsible to act.

---

# 7. Confidential Information

Confidential information may include:

- personal information;
- pastoral care details;
- health information;
- employment concerns;
- discipline matters;
- legal issues;
- safeguarding concerns;
- financial irregularities;
- housing resident concerns;
- donor-sensitive information;
- grant applicant details;
- private church disputes.

Reports should include only necessary confidential information.

---

# 8. Board Confidential Information

Board Confidential information may include:

- executive evaluation;
- serious legal risk;
- major financial risk;
- serious governance conflict;
- national chapter suspension concerns;
- sensitive theological dispute;
- serious safeguarding matter;
- board discipline;
- major property or funding matter.

Board Confidential reports should be stored and shared with strict access controls.

---

# 9. Prayer Requests

Prayer requests should be handled carefully.

A prayer request may be included if:

- the person has consented;
- the information is general enough to protect privacy;
- there is a proper ministry reason;
- the request does not expose sensitive details unnecessarily.

Example:

Preferred: "Pray for wisdom in supporting a resident family facing stress."

Avoid: detailed personal, financial, medical, or pastoral facts unless there is proper authorization and need.

---

# 10. Chaplain Reports

Chaplain reports require special care.

A chaplain's 5-15 report should normally include:

- number or type of visits;
- general themes;
- referrals made;
- non-identifying prayer themes;
- decisions or support needed;
- risks requiring escalation.

A chaplain should avoid unnecessary names, intimate pastoral details, confessional statements, or sensitive family information.

---

# 11. Housing Reports

Housing reports may involve residents and families.

A housing 5-15 report should protect:

- resident identity;
- financial details;
- conflict details;
- health or disability information;
- family circumstances;
- legal issues;
- safety concerns.

Sensitive housing matters should be restricted to authorized persons.

---

# 12. Staff and Volunteer Reports

Reports involving staff or volunteers may include supervision, workload, conflict, burnout, misconduct, training, or role concerns.

Such information should be shared only with supervisors or governance authorities who need it.

---

# 13. Grant and Foundation Reports

Reports involving grants or scholarships may include financial need, church information, student information, ministry plans, or conditions.

Grant information should be handled according to grant policy, privacy policy, and donor restrictions.

---

# 14. When Confidentiality Must Yield

Confidentiality may need to yield when:

- law requires reporting;
- safety is at risk;
- abuse or exploitation is suspected;
- financial misconduct is suspected;
- a board decision is required;
- legal counsel must be consulted;
- a safeguarding procedure applies;
- the Society must protect people or mission.

Confidentiality cannot be used as a blanket to hide fire.

---

# 15. Access Control

Access to confidential reports should be based on role and need.

Possible access groups:

- direct supervisor;
- National Executive Director;
- International Executive Director;
- relevant board committee;
- International Board;
- chaplain supervisor;
- legal counsel;
- safeguarding officer;
- finance officer;
- records officer.

Access should be documented where practical.

---

# 16. Storage

Confidential reports should be stored in approved secure systems.

Storage should prevent accidental sharing through:

- open folders;
- public dashboards;
- unsecured email forwarding;
- shared devices;
- inappropriate file names;
- uncontrolled exports;
- generated public outputs.

Generator outputs should not publish confidential reports to public HTML, public PDFs, or unrestricted vaults.

---

# 17. Redaction and Summaries

Where a report contains sensitive details but a broader audience needs general awareness, the reviewer should create a redacted summary.

A redacted summary may preserve:

- issue category;
- action needed;
- risk level;
- prayer theme;
- decision required.

It should remove unnecessary identifying details.

---

# 18. Breach of Confidentiality

A breach of confidentiality should be reported promptly.

Possible responses include:

- containment;
- notification to affected persons where appropriate;
- supervisor review;
- privacy review;
- legal review;
- discipline;
- training;
- correction of access controls.

Serious breaches should be documented.

---

# 19. Reporter Responsibility

Reporters must:

- mark confidentiality level;
- avoid unnecessary details;
- use approved channels;
- escalate urgent concerns;
- ask for guidance if unsure;
- protect information after submission.

When in doubt, write less detail in the 5-15 and escalate through the proper confidential channel.

---

# 20. Reviewer Responsibility

Reviewers must:

- respect confidentiality markings;
- adjust classification if needed;
- limit distribution;
- escalate serious concerns;
- create redacted summaries when needed;
- store reports properly;
- ensure retention rules are followed.
