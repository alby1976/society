# Church Referral Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society refers people connected to Society ministries to faithful local churches.

The Society exists to support local churches, not replace them. Church referral helps beneficiaries, residents, workers, volunteers, and unchurched persons connect to ordinary church life where appropriate.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should help people move toward faithful local church life, not permanent dependence on Society ministry.

The Society's care may be temporary, supportive, and practical, but the ordinary place for ongoing pastoral care, worship, sacraments, discipline, and discipleship is the local church.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to referrals involving:

- unchurched beneficiaries;
- housing residents;
- worker cooperative participants;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- grant recipients;
- students;
- families;
- persons receiving chaplaincy care;
- persons participating in Society ministries.

---

# 4. When Referral May Be Appropriate

A church referral may be appropriate when a person:

- is unchurched;
- requests church connection;
- needs pastoral care;
- needs discipleship;
- is seeking worship;
- has moved to a new area;
- is disconnected from church;
- needs language-specific church connection;
- needs family support;
- is receiving Society spiritual care but needs ongoing church care;
- wants to speak with a pastor or elder.

---

# 5. Referral Standards

The Society should refer to churches that are suitable according to [[Church Recognition Standards]] or known through trusted church relationships.

Referral should consider:

- doctrinal faithfulness;
- location;
- language;
- accessibility;
- pastoral capacity;
- family needs;
- transportation;
- cultural context;
- safety;
- willingness to receive referrals;
- relationship to Society mission.

---

# 6. Consent

A referral should ordinarily be made with the person's consent.

The Society should not send personal information to a church without proper authorization unless safety, law, or policy requires disclosure.

A person may be offered church options without personal information being shared.

---

# 7. Referral Process

A standard referral process may include:

1. identify need or request;
2. discuss church connection with the person;
3. identify suitable church options;
4. obtain consent if sharing information;
5. contact church if appropriate;
6. provide contact information;
7. offer follow-up support;
8. document referral generally;
9. protect confidentiality.

---

# 8. Referrals for Persons Already in a Church

If a person already belongs to a faithful local church, the Society should encourage them to involve their own church leadership where appropriate.

The Society should avoid creating parallel pastoral care.

If the person reports serious concern about their church, the chaplain or leader should proceed carefully and seek counsel if needed.

---

# 9. Referrals for Unchurched Persons

For unchurched persons, referral should be patient and respectful.

The Society may explain:

- why local church life matters;
- what a church is;
- what to expect;
- available language or cultural options;
- how to contact a church;
- whether someone can accompany them if appropriate.

The Society should avoid coercion.

---

# 10. Housing Context

Housing residents may be invited or referred to local churches, but housing support should not be manipulated to force church participation unless a lawful, clearly defined program has been reviewed and approved.

The Society may maintain a Christian housing mission while respecting legal and ethical boundaries.

---

# 11. Worker Cooperative Context

Workers may be encouraged toward local church connection where appropriate.

Employment or cooperative participation should not be used to pressure church attendance.

Spiritual encouragement should be offered without workplace coercion.

---

# 12. Chaplain Role

The chaplain may assist with church referrals by:

- discussing church connection;
- identifying suitable churches;
- contacting church leaders with consent;
- following up;
- advising ministry leaders;
- documenting referral themes;
- consulting Church Advisory Fellowship when needed.

---

# 13. Church Advisory Role

The [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]] may help identify suitable churches and advise on referral concerns.

National church advisory teams may maintain local referral lists.

Referral lists should be reviewed periodically.

---

# 14. Confidentiality

Referral information should be limited.

Do not share sensitive personal, pastoral, financial, housing, employment, family, medical, or legal information without proper consent or requirement.

General referral may be made without disclosure of private details.

---

# 15. Documentation

Referral documentation should include:

- date;
- general reason;
- church referred to;
- whether consent was given;
- follow-up needed;
- confidentiality level;
- person responsible for follow-up.

Avoid unnecessary sensitive details.

---

# 16. Follow-Up

Follow-up may include:

- asking whether connection was made;
- offering another option if needed;
- checking whether transportation or accessibility was an issue;
- supporting communication;
- involving chaplain if appropriate.

The Society should not monitor church participation in a controlling way.

---

# 17. When Referral Should Be Paused

Referral may be paused when:

- the person does not consent;
- safety concerns exist;
- no suitable church is known;
- the available church relationship is problematic;
- urgent crisis requires other care first;
- the referral could create harm or confusion.

---

# 18. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually with [[Church Partnership Framework]], [[Church Recognition Standards]], and [[Chaplain Policy]].
