# Church Support Foundation Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the Church Support Foundation ministry of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Church Support Foundation exists to steward funds, grants, scholarships, capacity-building support, and other resources for the strengthening of faithful Christian ministry in a manner consistent with the Society's doctrine, mission, church-supporting identity, and governance standards.

The Foundation exists to support faithful churches and church-connected ministry. It does not govern churches, replace churches, or purchase spiritual influence.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Foundation shall support ministry without controlling the church.

Funds are powerful. Money can help mission, distort mission, or quietly buy influence. The Foundation must therefore combine generosity with humility, accountability, transparency, and theological clarity.

---

# 3. Nature of the Foundation

The Foundation may be organized as:

- a ministry program of the Society;
- a restricted fund;
- a charitable foundation;
- a national chapter foundation;
- a grant-making committee;
- an affiliated legal entity under Society oversight;
- another lawful structure approved by the Society.

The legal form may vary by jurisdiction, but the Foundation remains subject to the Society's theology, mission, reserved powers, and mission alignment standards.

---

# 4. Objectives

The Foundation may pursue the following objectives:

1. support faithful local churches in appropriate ways;
2. support ministry training;
3. support theological education where aligned;
4. provide capacity-building grants;
5. support church-connected mercy ministry;
6. support leadership development;
7. help remove practical barriers to ministry service;
8. strengthen institutional faithfulness;
9. encourage accountable stewardship;
10. support mission-aligned national chapters;
11. preserve long-term mission identity.

---

# 5. What the Foundation Is Not

The Foundation is not:

- a church;
- a presbytery, classis, synod, session, consistory, or council;
- a substitute for church discipline;
- a mechanism for controlling pastors or churches;
- a private benefit vehicle;
- an emergency slush fund;
- a donor influence machine;
- a grant program detached from doctrine and mission;
- a way to bypass local church authority.

---

# 6. Eligible Support Areas

The Foundation may support:

- theological education;
- ministry preparation;
- church capacity-building;
- ministry infrastructure;
- training programs;
- language or translation needs;
- church-connected mercy ministry;
- chaplaincy development;
- leadership development;
- documentation and governance support;
- mission-aligned pilot projects;
- emergency ministry support where approved.

Each support area must be governed by policy.

---

# 7. Ineligible Uses

The Foundation should not support:

- activities contrary to the Statement of Faith;
- activities undermining faithful local churches;
- private benefit unrelated to mission;
- political partisan activity;
- unlawful activity;
- speculative investment disguised as ministry;
- grants without accountability;
- projects controlled by conflicts of interest;
- activities that create unhealthy dependence;
- personal expenses not tied to approved grant purposes.

---

# 8. Relationship to Churches

The Foundation may support churches or church-connected ministries where appropriate.

Support must respect church authority.

The Foundation should not use funding to control:

- preaching;
- sacraments;
- church discipline;
- membership decisions;
- pastoral calls;
- elder oversight;
- worship decisions;
- internal church governance.

Funding conditions may address the use of funds, reporting, doctrine, safeguarding, and stewardship, but must not turn the Foundation into a shadow church court.

---

# 9. Relationship to Theological Oversight

The [[Theological Oversight Council Charter|Theological Oversight Council]] may advise on doctrinal suitability of grant categories, theological education, seminary recognition, and ministry training.

The Foundation should seek theological review when grants involve:

- seminary support;
- doctrinal teaching;
- ministry training;
- church leadership development;
- disputed theological matters;
- new ministry models;
- international contexts.

---

# 10. Relationship to Church Advisory Fellowship

The [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]] may advise on church relationships, church needs, referral standards, church recognition, ministry suitability, and local church context.

Church advisors may help prevent the Foundation from solving church problems in ways that accidentally weaken church life.

---

# 11. Governance

Foundation governance must define:

- board or committee authority;
- grant approval thresholds;
- restricted fund rules;
- conflict of interest process;
- donor influence limits;
- application process;
- reporting requirements;
- grant monitoring;
- appeal or reconsideration process;
- legal compliance;
- financial controls;
- mission alignment review.

---

# 12. Grant Oversight

Grants should be governed through written grant policies.

Each grant should define:

- purpose;
- recipient;
- amount;
- term;
- permitted uses;
- prohibited uses;
- reporting requirements;
- payment schedule;
- conditions;
- recovery or suspension terms;
- contact person;
- review date.

A grant without expectations is not generosity. It is future confusion with a receipt.

---

# 13. Donor Restrictions

Donor-restricted gifts must be reviewed before acceptance.

The Society should not accept restrictions that:

- compromise doctrine;
- override governance;
- control churches improperly;
- create unlawful discrimination;
- create private benefit;
- are impossible to administer;
- conflict with charity law;
- distort mission priorities;
- bind future leaders unwisely.

---

# 14. Financial Stewardship

The Foundation shall maintain financial controls including:

- budget;
- fund accounting;
- restricted gift tracking;
- grant approvals;
- payment records;
- reporting;
- audit or review where required;
- reserves where appropriate;
- anti-fraud controls;
- documentation.

---

# 15. Conflicts of Interest

Foundation grants carry conflict risks.

Conflicts may involve:

- board members applying for grants;
- family members receiving support;
- churches connected to decision-makers;
- donors influencing grants;
- staff recommending relatives;
- seminary relationships;
- vendors connected to applicants.

Conflicts must be disclosed, recorded, and managed.

---

# 16. Reporting

The Foundation shall report through:

- [[Foundation Director 5-15 Report]];
- grant summaries;
- financial reports;
- restricted fund reports;
- annual foundation report;
- mission alignment reviews;
- board reports.

Reports must protect private applicant and recipient information.

---

# 17. Mission Alignment Review

The Foundation shall undergo regular Mission Alignment Review.

Review should ask:

- Does grant-making support the Society's mission?
- Are churches respected?
- Are recipients accountable?
- Are donor restrictions healthy?
- Are conflicts managed?
- Are funds stewarded properly?
- Are grants creating dependency?
- Are theological standards preserved?

---

# 18. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually during launch and at least every three years once Foundation operations stabilize.
