# Expense Approval and Reimbursement Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how expenses are approved, documented, reimbursed, paid, reviewed, and reported.

The Society must spend funds in ways that are authorized, mission-aligned, lawful, documented, and financially responsible.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

No expense should be paid merely because it was incurred.

Expenses require authority, purpose, documentation, and review. A receipt is not a mission argument by itself.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- staff expenses;
- volunteer expenses;
- board expenses;
- ministry expenses;
- travel;
- meals;
- supplies;
- software;
- professional fees;
- grant-related expenses;
- housing expenses;
- cooperative expenses;
- Foundation expenses;
- emergency expenses;
- reimbursements;
- direct vendor payments.

---

# 4. Approval Before Spending

Expenses should be approved before spending unless an emergency exception applies.

Approval should confirm:

- purpose;
- budget availability;
- amount;
- account or fund;
- authority;
- documentation required;
- conflict of interest status;
- restricted fund availability if relevant.

---

# 5. Spending Authority

Spending authority must follow the [[Authority Matrix]].

Spending limits should identify:

- who may approve;
- maximum amount;
- budget category;
- whether second approval is required;
- whether board approval is required;
- whether restricted fund approval applies.

---

# 6. Documentation

Expenses should be supported by documentation.

Documentation may include:

- receipt;
- invoice;
- contract;
- quote;
- approval email or form;
- mileage log;
- travel itinerary;
- explanation of purpose;
- grant agreement;
- board approval.

Missing documentation should be explained.

---

# 7. Reimbursement Requests

A reimbursement request should include:

- person requesting reimbursement;
- date of expense;
- amount;
- currency;
- purpose;
- budget category;
- receipt;
- approval;
- payment method;
- conflict disclosure if relevant.

Requests should be submitted within the required time period.

---

# 8. Direct Payments

Direct vendor payments should require:

- approved invoice;
- vendor identity;
- purpose;
- budget or fund;
- approval authority;
- conflict check;
- payment record.

---

# 9. Prohibited Expenses

The Society should not pay for:

- personal expenses unrelated to approved work;
- undocumented expenses without exception approval;
- unlawful expenses;
- expenses outside mission;
- excessive or unreasonable costs;
- political partisan activity;
- gifts or benefits creating improper private benefit;
- expenses that violate donor restrictions;
- expenses approved by a conflicted person without proper review.

---

# 10. Travel and Meals

Travel and meal expenses should be reasonable and pre-approved where possible.

Travel rules should define:

- eligible travel;
- transportation standard;
- accommodation standard;
- meal limits;
- mileage;
- receipts;
- international travel approval;
- travel insurance where needed;
- family travel rules.

The Society should avoid both luxury and false economy.

---

# 11. Credit Cards and Payment Tools

If the Society uses credit cards, debit cards, online payment tools, or expense platforms, the Board should approve controls.

Controls should address:

- authorized users;
- limits;
- receipts;
- monthly review;
- prohibited uses;
- lost card process;
- online subscription review;
- cancellation when a person leaves role.

---

# 12. Subscriptions and Software

Subscriptions should be reviewed regularly.

The Society should track:

- vendor;
- purpose;
- owner;
- cost;
- renewal date;
- data stored;
- cancellation process;
- access control.

Subscriptions are small leaks until they become a financial plumbing problem.

---

# 13. Emergency Expenses

Emergency expenses may be allowed when delay would create harm, legal risk, safety risk, property damage, or mission interruption.

Emergency expense records should include:

- reason for emergency;
- amount;
- person authorizing;
- action taken;
- receipts;
- follow-up approval;
- report to appropriate authority.

---

# 14. Conflicts of Interest

Expenses involving related parties require conflict review.

Examples:

- payment to board member;
- payment to family member;
- vendor connected to staff;
- church connected to decision-maker;
- reimbursement approved by the person receiving it;
- insider discount or benefit.

A person should not approve their own reimbursement.

---

# 15. Restricted Funds

Expenses charged to restricted funds must match the restriction.

Before payment, the reviewer should confirm:

- fund restriction;
- eligible use;
- available balance;
- documentation;
- reporting requirement;
- approval authority.

---

# 16. Review and Audit

Expenses should be reviewed regularly.

Review may include:

- monthly transaction review;
- budget comparison;
- receipt check;
- conflict check;
- restricted fund review;
- board finance report;
- audit or independent review where required.
