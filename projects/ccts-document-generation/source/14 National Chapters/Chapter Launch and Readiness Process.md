# Chapter Launch and Readiness Process

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines the process for launching a National Chapter of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

It provides a staged path from prayerful exploration to formation, provisional recognition, launch, review, and full recognition.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A chapter should not launch faster than it can govern.

Launch requires prayer, doctrine, law, leadership, finances, church relationships, reporting, and institutional memory. Missing foundations do not become less important after launch. They become expensive.

---

# 3. Launch Stages

The chapter launch process contains the following stages:

1. prayer and initial discernment;
2. country or jurisdiction assessment;
3. formation team identification;
4. legal and governance review;
5. church relationship review;
6. ministry need review;
7. financial readiness review;
8. risk review;
9. provisional recognition;
10. limited launch;
11. reporting and evaluation;
12. full recognition or corrective decision.

---

# 4. Stage 1: Prayer and Initial Discernment

Before formal development, the Society should pray and discern.

Questions:

- Why this country?
- Why now?
- What needs are present?
- Are there faithful churches to support?
- Are qualified leaders available?
- What risks are known?
- Is the Society being pulled by mission or excitement?

A written discernment note should be preserved.

---

# 5. Stage 2: Jurisdiction Assessment

The assessment should consider:

- legal form;
- charity or nonprofit registration;
- tax rules;
- foreign control rules;
- banking;
- employment law;
- housing law if relevant;
- cooperative law if relevant;
- grant-making rules;
- data privacy;
- safeguarding requirements;
- religious organization laws;
- political or public risk.

Local legal advice may be needed.

---

# 6. Stage 3: Formation Team

A formation team may be authorized to explore chapter readiness.

The formation team should include people with capacity for:

- doctrine and mission;
- governance;
- church relationships;
- finance;
- legal coordination;
- operations;
- reporting;
- local cultural knowledge.

The team must understand that formation authority is limited.

---

# 7. Stage 4: Doctrine and Mission Readiness

The formation team should confirm:

- acceptance of the Statement of Faith;
- understanding of the Society/church distinction;
- agreement with mission, vision, and values;
- commitment to reserved powers;
- willingness to use Society reporting and document systems;
- commitment to Mission Alignment Review.

---

# 8. Stage 5: Church Relationship Readiness

The formation team should identify:

- faithful local churches;
- possible church advisors;
- denominational or fellowship relationships;
- language needs;
- local theological education context;
- church referral pathways;
- potential concerns.

The chapter should not launch as if churches are optional scenery.

---

# 9. Stage 6: Governance Readiness

Governance readiness includes:

- proposed board;
- qualifications;
- conflict of interest policy;
- delegated authority;
- reserved powers acceptance;
- decision register;
- document registry;
- reporting schedule;
- meeting process;
- policy adoption plan.

---

# 10. Stage 7: Financial Readiness

Financial readiness includes:

- bank account pathway;
- budget;
- accounting system;
- donation handling;
- restricted fund process;
- expense controls;
- reporting;
- audit or review requirements;
- tax filings;
- grant controls if any.

No chapter should launch with money moving faster than accounting.

---

# 11. Stage 8: Ministry Readiness

The chapter should identify which ministries, if any, are ready to launch.

Possible ministries:

- chaplaincy;
- worker cooperative pilot;
- housing exploration;
- church support foundation exploration;
- prayer gatherings;
- reporting and institutional memory;
- church partnerships.

Each ministry should have policy, authority, reporting, and risk review.

---

# 12. Stage 9: Risk Review

The formation team should prepare a risk review.

Risks may include:

- legal uncertainty;
- weak leadership;
- theological confusion;
- church conflict;
- financial weakness;
- privacy concerns;
- safeguarding concerns;
- public reputation;
- mission drift;
- overexpansion;
- lack of reporting capacity.

---

# 13. Stage 10: Provisional Recognition Request

A request for provisional recognition should include:

- formation team report;
- legal review summary;
- proposed governance;
- church relationship summary;
- budget;
- ministry plan;
- risk review;
- local adaptation requests;
- reporting plan;
- requested authority.

---

# 14. Stage 11: Limited Launch

A limited launch should define:

- permitted ministries;
- spending limits;
- public communications;
- reporting requirements;
- leadership authority;
- review date;
- conditions;
- prohibited activities.

Limited launch protects the chapter from carrying more weight than its frame can hold.

---

# 15. Stage 12: Evaluation

After a defined period, the chapter should be evaluated.

Evaluation should consider:

- reports submitted;
- ministry activity;
- financial controls;
- church relationships;
- leadership health;
- risks;
- legal progress;
- mission alignment;
- local adaptation;
- prayerful dependence.

---

# 16. Possible Outcomes

The International Board may decide to:

- grant full recognition;
- extend provisional recognition;
- require corrective action;
- restrict activities;
- pause launch;
- end the formation process;
- restructure the formation team.
