# Worker Participation and Development Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for worker participation, training, skill development, mentorship, feedback, and growth in a Society-affiliated worker cooperative.

The cooperative exists not only to complete tasks but to help workers grow in responsibility, skill, service, stewardship, and meaningful participation.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Workers should be treated as persons to be developed, not merely labour to be used.

The cooperative should provide real work, real expectations, real support, real accountability, and real opportunities to grow.

Development without accountability becomes drift. Accountability without support becomes pressure.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- member-workers;
- probationary members;
- employees;
- trainees;
- apprentices;
- volunteers where lawful;
- supervisors;
- mentors;
- cooperative managers;
- worker representatives.

Local employment, labour, cooperative, and safety laws must be followed.

---

# 4. Worker Status Clarity

Every person working in the cooperative must have a clear legal and operational status.

Possible statuses include:

- employee;
- contractor;
- cooperative member;
- probationary member;
- trainee;
- apprentice;
- volunteer where lawful;
- supervisor;
- mentor.

The cooperative must not blur worker status to avoid legal obligations.

---

# 5. Participation Principles

Worker participation should be:

- meaningful;
- structured;
- lawful;
- proportionate to role;
- connected to responsibility;
- documented where decisions are made;
- respectful of Society oversight;
- protected from domination by a few voices.

Participation does not mean every person decides everything.

---

# 6. Participation Opportunities

Workers may participate through:

- worker meetings;
- feedback sessions;
- safety reporting;
- project review;
- training planning;
- policy feedback;
- cooperative governance;
- representative roles;
- improvement suggestions;
- mission alignment discussions.

The cooperative should encourage worker voice without turning every decision into a town hall marathon.

---

# 7. Training

Training may include:

- job skills;
- safety;
- customer service;
- quality standards;
- financial basics;
- cooperative governance;
- conflict resolution;
- communication;
- workplace conduct;
- privacy;
- mission and values;
- church-supporting boundaries;
- spiritual care availability.

Training should be tracked.

---

# 8. Skill Development Plans

Workers may have development plans.

A development plan may include:

- current skills;
- target skills;
- training needed;
- mentor or supervisor;
- expected practice;
- review date;
- barriers;
- support needed;
- progress notes.

Development plans should be realistic and respectful.

---

# 9. Mentorship

Mentorship may be offered to support worker growth.

Mentors should:

- model good work;
- give practical guidance;
- respect boundaries;
- avoid control;
- protect confidentiality;
- encourage responsibility;
- report serious concerns properly.

Mentorship is not spiritual domination, therapy, or unpaid management disguised as friendship.

---

# 10. Feedback

Workers should receive regular feedback.

Feedback should be:

- specific;
- honest;
- timely;
- respectful;
- connected to expectations;
- documented when needed;
- paired with support where appropriate.

Feedback should not be vague shame.

---

# 11. Worker Meetings

Worker meetings should have:

- agenda;
- chair or facilitator;
- record of decisions;
- attendance;
- action items;
- confidentiality reminders;
- escalation process.

Meetings should not replace formal board decisions where board authority is required.

---

# 12. Conflict Resolution

Worker conflicts should be addressed promptly.

The process may include:

1. direct conversation where safe;
2. supervisor support;
3. mediation;
4. written concern;
5. manager review;
6. board or committee review;
7. corrective action;
8. external advice where needed.

Conflict is not always failure. Ignoring conflict usually is.

---

# 13. Spiritual Care and Chaplaincy

Workers may access chaplaincy and spiritual care.

Spiritual care must be voluntary, confidential, and separate from employment evaluation.

Managers must not pressure workers to disclose spiritual matters.

Workers may be encouraged toward faithful local church connection where appropriate.

---

# 14. Worker Safety

Worker development includes safety.

The cooperative must provide safety training and safe work procedures appropriate to its operations.

Workers should be encouraged to report hazards without fear of retaliation.

---

# 15. Support for Vulnerable Workers

Some workers may need additional support due to disability, poverty, trauma, language barriers, immigration stress, learning differences, limited work history, or other barriers.

Support should be dignifying and lawful.

The cooperative should avoid paternalism and exploitation.

---

# 16. Advancement

The cooperative may define advancement pathways.

Advancement may include:

- skill level progression;
- supervisory responsibilities;
- membership eligibility;
- wage progression;
- governance participation;
- mentorship role;
- project leadership;
- training certification.

Advancement criteria should be clear.

---

# 17. Corrective Development

Where a worker struggles, the cooperative may use corrective development.

Corrective development may include:

- expectations clarification;
- training;
- mentoring;
- written plan;
- performance review;
- safety restriction;
- role adjustment;
- probation extension;
- termination where necessary.

Corrective development must comply with law.

---

# 18. Documentation

Worker development records may include:

- training completed;
- development plans;
- feedback notes;
- meeting participation;
- safety training;
- certifications;
- advancement decisions;
- corrective actions.

Records should be secure and limited to authorized persons.

---

# 19. Reporting

Worker development should be summarized in cooperative reports.

Reports may include:

- training completed;
- worker participation;
- development themes;
- safety concerns;
- conflict themes;
- support needs;
- advancement;
- mission alignment concerns.

Personal details should be protected.

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during cooperative launch and at least every three years once stable.
