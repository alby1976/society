# Core Values

## Purpose

The Core Values describe the character and commitments that should shape the Society's governance, ministries, national chapters, partnerships, and daily operations.

These values do not replace the [[Statement of Faith]]. They express how the Society seeks to live out its faith and mission.

## Christ-Centered Faithfulness

The Society belongs to Jesus Christ. All ministries, assets, plans, and decisions must be ordered under His Lordship.

Mission success is measured by faithfulness, not merely size. Growth must never come at the cost of theological compromise. Every ministry should be evaluated in light of Christ's glory.

## Biblical Authority

Scripture is the supreme authority for the Society's faith, life, ethics, and governance.

Policies must not contradict Scripture. Difficult decisions require biblical wisdom, not merely efficiency. The Society must remain teachable and correctable by God's Word.

## Prayerful Dependence

The Society depends on God for wisdom, provision, repentance, unity, and fruitfulness.

Prayer should accompany every major stage of founding, expansion, review, and renewal. The Society should hold regular days or seasons of prayer. Leaders should resist prayerless activism.

## Church-Supporting Ministry

The Society exists to strengthen faithful local churches, not replace them.

Chaplaincy must not undermine local pastors or elders. Ministries should encourage church connection. National chapters should build healthy relationships with churches.

## Mercy with Truth

The Society serves vulnerable people with compassion while remaining honest about sin, responsibility, repentance, and hope in Christ.

Help should be humane, wise, and accountable. Mercy ministries should avoid creating unnecessary dependency. The Society must not confuse compassion with mission drift.

## Stewardship

All resources belong to God and must be managed faithfully.

Housing, funds, land, staff time, volunteer energy, data, and reputation are stewarded trusts. Financial controls and reporting are acts of integrity. Waste, secrecy, and self-serving leadership are contrary to stewardship.

## Meaningful Work

Work is a God-given calling and a sphere of discipleship, service, dignity, and responsibility.

Worker cooperative development should support people, not exploit them. Employment and training should encourage skill, responsibility, and Christian character. Work should connect to mission rather than become merely commercial.

## Community and Hospitality

The Society seeks to build communities marked by welcome, stability, service, and shared responsibility.

Housing ministry should be more than property management. Community life should encourage peace, mutual service, and church connection. Hospitality should be wise, not naive.

## Accountability

The Society must be accountable to God, Scripture, its governing documents, its members, its boards, its churches, its donors, its beneficiaries, and lawful civil requirements.

Decisions should be recorded. Conflicts of interest should be disclosed. Reports should be timely and truthful. Leaders must accept review.

## International Unity with Local Wisdom

The Society should preserve one doctrine and mission while allowing lawful and wise local implementation.

National chapters may adapt procedures to local law. National chapters may not redefine theology. Translation must preserve meaning.

## Institutional Memory

The Society should remember its decisions, lessons, failures, prayers, milestones, and founding commitments.

The [[Decision Register]] matters. 5-15 reports and annual reports should feed institutional memory. Stewardship Advisors help future leaders avoid repeating old mistakes. Archives are not clutter; they are governance memory with a filing cabinet haircut.

## Excellence without Pride

The Society should pursue excellence because God is worthy, not because the Society seeks prestige.

Documents should be clear. Reporting should be disciplined. Legal and financial matters should be handled carefully. The Society should avoid both sloppiness and vanity.

## Review

These Core Values should be reviewed annually and during every major mission alignment review.
