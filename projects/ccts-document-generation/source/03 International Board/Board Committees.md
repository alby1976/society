# Board Committees

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how the International Board of the Christian Community Transformation Society creates, authorizes, supervises, and reviews board committees.

Board committees exist to help the Board govern faithfully. They do not replace the Board unless a specific authority has been formally delegated.

This policy should be read with [[International Board Charter]], [[Board Responsibilities]], [[Delegated Authority Framework]], [[Authority Matrix]], and [[Board Meeting Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Committees serve the Board. They do not become little boards with matching stationery.

A committee may study, advise, recommend, monitor, investigate, or decide only within the authority granted by its charter, policy, or board resolution.

---

# 3. Types of Committees

The International Board may establish:

## 3.1 Standing Committees

Standing committees continue from year to year and support ongoing governance work.

Examples:

- Governance Committee;
- Finance and Audit Committee;
- Theology and Mission Committee;
- Risk and Safeguarding Committee;
- International Expansion Committee;
- Executive Committee;
- Nominating Committee;
- Document and Policy Committee.

## 3.2 Special Committees

Special committees are created for a specific task or season.

Examples:

- Constitution Review Committee;
- National Chapter Launch Committee;
- Housing Trust Formation Committee;
- Crisis Response Committee;
- Executive Search Committee;
- Founder Transition Committee.

## 3.3 Advisory Committees

Advisory committees provide counsel but do not decide.

They may include external advisors, church representatives, subject experts, former leaders, or ministry practitioners.

## 3.4 Decision-Making Committees

A decision-making committee may act only within authority clearly delegated by the Board.

Decision-making authority must be written and limited.

---

# 4. Committee Charter Requirement

Every committee should have a written charter.

A committee charter shall include:

1. name;
2. purpose;
3. authority level;
4. whether it is standing, special, advisory, or decision-making;
5. membership;
6. chair appointment process;
7. term or review date;
8. reporting requirements;
9. meeting frequency;
10. quorum if applicable;
11. decision-making limits;
12. relationship to the Board;
13. confidentiality expectations;
14. conflict of interest rules;
15. recordkeeping requirements.

A committee without a charter should not exercise authority.

---

# 5. Creation of Committees

The Board may create a committee by resolution.

The resolution should state:

- why the committee is needed;
- what problem or responsibility it addresses;
- whether the committee is temporary or standing;
- who appoints members;
- when the committee reports back;
- whether the committee has decision-making authority.

---

# 6. Committee Membership

Committee members may include:

- board members;
- officers;
- staff;
- national chapter representatives;
- church advisors;
- theological advisors;
- professional advisors;
- ministry leaders;
- former board members;
- other persons approved by the Board.

Unless otherwise stated, only board members may vote on board committee recommendations that require board approval.

Non-board members may provide expertise but do not automatically receive governance authority.

---

# 7. Committee Chairs

Each committee should have a chair appointed by the Board or according to the committee charter.

The chair is responsible to:

- prepare agendas;
- guide meetings;
- ensure records are kept;
- manage follow-up;
- report to the Board;
- keep the committee within its charter;
- identify conflicts of interest;
- escalate matters outside committee authority.

---

# 8. Committee Authority

Committee authority may include one or more of the following:

## 8.1 Study

The committee may study an issue and report findings.

## 8.2 Recommend

The committee may recommend action to the Board.

## 8.3 Monitor

The committee may monitor compliance, risk, reports, or progress.

## 8.4 Investigate

The committee may investigate a defined matter within approved scope.

## 8.5 Decide

The committee may make decisions only if expressly delegated authority to do so.

## 8.6 Implement

The committee may oversee implementation only where the Board or executive authority assigns that role.

---

# 9. Limits on Committee Authority

A committee may not, unless expressly authorized:

- amend the [[Statement of Faith]];
- amend the [[International Constitution]];
- change the Society's mission;
- recognize or dissolve a national chapter;
- approve a major affiliated organization;
- incur major financial obligations;
- hire or terminate the International Executive Director;
- speak publicly for the Society;
- alter reserved powers;
- bind national chapters beyond delegated authority;
- override the Board.

Reserved powers remain reserved.

---

# 10. Recommended Standing Committees

## 10.1 Governance Committee

Responsible for:

- board policy review;
- governance calendar;
- board evaluation;
- document lifecycle;
- bylaw and constitutional review;
- director orientation;
- governance training;
- board succession.

## 10.2 Finance and Audit Committee

Responsible for:

- budget review;
- financial reporting;
- reserve policy review;
- audit or review coordination;
- internal control oversight;
- major financial risk review;
- gift acceptance review when needed.

## 10.3 Theology and Mission Committee

Responsible for:

- mission alignment review support;
- theological concern intake;
- coordination with [[Theological Oversight Council]];
- review of doctrinal implications;
- church-supporting boundary review;
- review of ministry proposals for theological consistency.

## 10.4 Risk and Safeguarding Committee

Responsible for:

- risk register review;
- safeguarding policy oversight;
- privacy and records risk;
- incident report review;
- crisis preparedness;
- legal and reputational risk monitoring.

## 10.5 International Expansion Committee

Responsible for:

- new country assessment;
- national chapter readiness;
- localization review;
- translation concerns;
- chapter launch planning;
- international partnership review.

## 10.6 Nominating Committee

Responsible for:

- board recruitment;
- candidate screening;
- qualifications review;
- conflict disclosure review;
- succession recommendations.

## 10.7 Executive Committee

Responsible for limited action between board meetings when authorized.

The Executive Committee should be used carefully. It must not become the real board while the full Board becomes a ceremonial audience.

---

# 11. Committee Meetings

Committee meetings should be orderly and recorded.

Committee records should include:

- date;
- attendees;
- agenda;
- key discussion points;
- recommendations;
- decisions within delegated authority;
- action items;
- conflicts disclosed;
- materials reviewed;
- date of next report.

Committee minutes or summaries should be submitted to the Board according to the committee charter.

---

# 12. Confidentiality

Committee members must protect confidential information.

Confidential matters may include:

- personnel;
- pastoral concerns;
- legal advice;
- donor information;
- financial risk;
- national chapter concerns;
- safeguarding matters;
- investigations;
- theological disputes;
- executive evaluation.

Confidentiality must not be used to hide misconduct or avoid proper reporting.

---

# 13. Conflicts of Interest

Committee members must disclose conflicts of interest.

A conflicted member may be asked to leave discussion, abstain from recommendations, or refrain from receiving certain materials.

Committee chairs must report significant conflicts to the Board.

---

# 14. Committee Reporting

Each committee should report according to its charter.

Reports should normally include:

- work completed;
- matters requiring board action;
- risks identified;
- recommendations;
- decisions made under delegated authority;
- unresolved issues;
- prayer concerns where appropriate.

The 5-15 format may be used for concise committee reporting.

---

# 15. Annual Committee Review

Each standing committee should be reviewed annually.

The review should consider:

- whether the committee is still needed;
- whether its charter is clear;
- whether it stayed within authority;
- whether reports were useful;
- whether membership was appropriate;
- whether the committee duplicated staff work;
- whether risks were addressed;
- whether the committee should continue, change, or dissolve.

---

# 16. Dissolution of Committees

The Board may dissolve a committee by resolution.

A special committee should dissolve automatically when its purpose is complete unless extended by the Board.

Committee records should be transferred to the proper archive or board record system.
