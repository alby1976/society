# Corrective Action Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society identifies, assigns, tracks, reviews, escalates, and closes corrective actions.

Corrective action is required when a risk, incident, review, report, complaint, audit, or board decision identifies something that must be changed.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Correction should be clear, proportionate, documented, and aimed at restoration of faithfulness where possible.

The Society should avoid two errors:

- ignoring problems because correction is uncomfortable;
- overcorrecting in a way that harms people or mission.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to corrective actions arising from:

- risk review;
- incident reports;
- Mission Alignment Review;
- financial review;
- legal review;
- complaints;
- safeguarding concerns;
- privacy concerns;
- board review;
- reporting failures;
- ministry failures;
- national chapter concerns;
- document review;
- annual reports.

---

# 4. Corrective Action Triggers

Corrective action may be triggered by:

- High or Critical risk;
- repeated Medium risk;
- serious incident;
- missed legal obligation;
- financial irregularity;
- grant misuse;
- reporting failure;
- leadership overload;
- mission drift;
- conflict of interest;
- privacy breach;
- church relationship concern;
- unsafe practice;
- failed audit or review;
- board decision.

---

# 5. Corrective Action Plan

A corrective action plan should include:

- issue;
- source of issue;
- required correction;
- responsible owner;
- approving authority;
- due date;
- risk level;
- resources needed;
- reporting requirement;
- review date;
- escalation if incomplete;
- closure criteria.

# 7. Ownership

Each corrective action must have an owner.

The owner is responsible to:

- understand the issue;
- complete the action;
- request help if needed;
- report progress;
- identify obstacles;
- document completion.

Unassigned correction is usually uncompleted correction.

---

# 8. Due Dates

Corrective actions should have realistic due dates.

Critical risks may require immediate action. Lower risks may allow longer timelines.

Deadlines should not be so vague that they function as permission to ignore the issue.

---

# 9. Review

Corrective actions should be reviewed until closed.

Review should ask:

- Has the action been completed?
- Did it solve the issue?
- Is risk reduced?
- Is further action needed?
- Should the matter be escalated?
- Should policy be revised?
- Should training occur?
- Should the action be closed?

---

# 10. Escalation

Corrective action should be escalated when:

- due date is missed;
- owner cannot complete action;
- risk increases;
- new information emerges;
- corrective action is resisted;
- legal or safety concern arises;
- board decision is needed;
- mission alignment remains compromised.

---

# 11. Closure

A corrective action may be closed when:

- required action is complete;
- evidence is documented;
- reviewer approves closure;
- risk is reduced or accepted;
- follow-up date is set if needed.

Closure should not occur merely because people are tired of the issue.

---

# 12. Corrective Action Options

Corrective action may include:

- policy revision;
- training;
- report submission;
- financial correction;
- legal review;
- leadership support;
- role change;
- ministry pause;
- restricted authority;
- communication correction;
- repayment request where lawful;
- conflict remedy;
- document update;
- external review;
- Mission Alignment Review;
- suspension or closure.

---

# 13. Relationship to Discipline

Corrective action is not always discipline.

Some corrective actions address systems, training, workload, unclear policy, or missing records.

Discipline may be appropriate where misconduct, refusal, dishonesty, abuse, or serious breach occurs.

Do not use discipline to solve a system failure. Do not use system language to hide misconduct.

---

# 14. Institutional Memory

Completed corrective actions should be preserved where important.

Records should identify:

- what happened;
- what correction was made;
- what was learned;
- what should be watched next;
- what documents changed.

Future leaders should not be forced to rediscover old lessons.

---

# 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and after any major corrective action involving doctrine, legal risk, financial misconduct, safeguarding, privacy, chapter suspension, or mission alignment failure.
