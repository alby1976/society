# Mission Alignment Review

## 1. Purpose

Mission Alignment Review is the formal process by which the Christian Community Transformation Society evaluates whether a chapter, ministry, affiliated organization, policy, partnership, grant, housing project, worker cooperative, foundation, chaplaincy activity, or major initiative remains aligned with the Society's doctrine, mission, governance standards, and church-supporting purpose.

This framework protects the Society from mission drift and should be read with [[Statement of Faith]], [[Mission Statement]], [[Core Values]], [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], [[Theological Oversight Council Charter]], and [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter]].

## 2. Governing Principle

Mission alignment must be tested before drift becomes normal.

Organizations rarely abandon their mission all at once. They usually drift through small compromises, attractive funding, vague partnerships, unclear authority, weak reporting, and tired leaders who say, "Just this once."

Mission Alignment Review exists to notice the drift before the map becomes decorative.

## 3. Scope

Mission Alignment Review may apply to international governance, national chapters, regional ministries, local ministries, affiliated organizations, housing trusts, worker cooperatives, church support foundations, chaplaincy ministries, grants and scholarships, church partnerships, international partnerships, policies, frameworks, major programs, property decisions, donor-restricted projects, crisis responses, and public communications.

## 4. Review Triggers

A review may be triggered by scheduled periodic review, launch of a new ministry, creation of an affiliated organization, recognition of a national chapter, serious complaint, theological concern, financial concern, church partner concern, safeguarding or legal concern, major leadership change, major funding change, proposed expansion, unresolved conflict, reporting concern, International Board request, Theological Oversight Council request, Church Advisory Fellowship recommendation, or National Board request.

## 5. Review Standards

Every review should consider alignment with Scripture, [[Statement of Faith]], [[Mission Statement]], [[Vision Statement]], [[Core Values]], [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], applicable reserved powers, delegated authority, church-supporting boundaries, financial stewardship, reporting obligations, risk policies, local law, and prior decisions in the [[Decision Register]].

## 6. Core Review Questions

A review should ask:

1. Does this activity advance the Society's mission?
2. Does it remain faithful to the Statement of Faith?
3. Does it support faithful local churches rather than replace them?
4. Does it preserve the distinction between Society and church?
5. Does it operate under proper authority?
6. Does it use funds and property faithfully?
7. Does it protect vulnerable people?
8. Does it create unhealthy dependency?
9. Does it introduce theological confusion?
10. Does it create unmanaged legal, financial, or reputational risk?
11. Does it have adequate reporting?
12. Does it preserve institutional memory?
13. Does it require correction, suspension, restructuring, or termination?

## 7. Review Levels

### 7.1 Light Review

Used for low-risk ministries, routine programs, or early-stage concepts.

### 7.2 Standard Review

Used for ongoing ministries, partnerships, grants, reports, or chapter activities.

### 7.3 Enhanced Review

Used for high-risk, high-cost, public, theological, legal, international, or vulnerable-person matters.

### 7.4 Emergency Review

Used where serious harm or urgent risk may exist and may result in temporary suspension while facts are reviewed.

## 8. Review Participants

A review may involve the International Board, National Board, International Executive Director, National Executive Director, Theological Oversight Council, Church Advisory Fellowship, Finance Committee, Risk Committee, ministry leader, chaplain, legal counsel, auditor or accountant, external subject expert, affected church partner, or affected national chapter.

Participants should be proportionate to the matter.

## 9. Review Process

A standard review should include:

1. identification of matter reviewed;
2. determination of review level;
3. collection of documents and reports;
4. review against mission and doctrine;
5. review of authority and governance;
6. review of finances and stewardship;
7. review of risks;
8. review of church relationships;
9. review of reporting and institutional memory;
10. recommendations;
11. decision by proper authority;
12. documentation;
13. follow-up schedule.

## 10. Evidence Considered

Reviewers may consider governing documents, board minutes, decisions, budgets, reports, 5-15 summaries, annual reports, ministry materials, grant documents, contracts, church partnership agreements, complaints, interviews, incident reports, legal documents, theological review reports, and financial statements.

## 11. Possible Findings

A review may find that an activity is aligned, aligned with minor recommendations, conditionally aligned, unclear, correction required, supervision required, restructuring required, temporary suspension required, termination recommended, escalation required, doctrinal concern requiring theological review, or legal concern requiring legal review.

## 12. Corrective Action

Corrective action may include document revision, new reporting requirements, training, leadership change, financial controls, mission clarification, church referral process, theological review, suspension of activity, restructuring, termination, restoration plan, or board oversight period.

Corrective action should be documented and assigned to responsible persons.

## 13. Role of Theological Oversight

The [[Theological Oversight Council Charter|Theological Oversight Council]] should be involved when a review involves doctrine, teaching, chaplaincy, spiritual care, confessional standards, church-supporting boundaries, ministry materials, theological complaints, or mission drift with doctrinal implications.

## 14. Role of Church Advisory Fellowship

The [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]] should be consulted when a review involves church partnerships, local church concerns, ministry to unchurched persons, chaplaincy referral pathways, church support grants, church-hosted activities, or confusion between Society and church authority.

## 15. Role of Reports

Reports are central to review. Reviewers should examine weekly or monthly 5-15 reports, annual ministry reports, financial reports, risk reports, incident reports, board reports, and chapter reports.

A ministry that cannot report cannot be reviewed, and a ministry that cannot be reviewed should not be allowed to grow unchecked.

## 16. Documentation

Every standard or enhanced review should produce a written report including the subject reviewed, review date, reviewers, documents examined, findings, risks, recommendations, decision required, authority responsible, follow-up date, and confidentiality level.

Material outcomes should be entered into the [[Decision Register]].

## 17. Frequency

| Area | Minimum Review |
|---|---|
| International Society | Annual |
| National Chapters | Annual during launch, then every 1-3 years |
| Housing Trust | Annual |
| Worker Cooperative | Annual during pilot, then every 1-3 years |
| Church Support Foundation | Annual |
| Chaplaincy | Annual |
| Major Grants | At completion and as required |
| Church Partnerships | Every 1-3 years |
| Low-risk local ministries | Every 1-3 years |
| High-risk ministries | Annual or more often |

## 18. Confidentiality

Reviews may involve confidential information. Confidentiality must protect people and the Society while allowing proper reporting to those with authority.

Sensitive reviews should identify who may access records.

## 19. Appeals or Reconsideration

A ministry, chapter, or affiliated organization may request reconsideration if allowed by policy. Grounds may include procedural error, new information, conflict of interest, factual mistake, disproportionate corrective action, or unclear findings.

The International Board retains final internal authority on international mission alignment unless law requires otherwise.

## 20. Review
