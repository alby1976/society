# Safeguarding Reporting Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure explains how safeguarding concerns should be reported, escalated, documented, and reviewed within the Society.

It supports [[Safeguarding and Protection Framework]] and [[Incident Escalation Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Report concerns promptly to the proper authority. Do not investigate privately, spread details unnecessarily, or ignore concerns because they are uncomfortable.

The goal is safety, truth, lawful response, and protection of people.

---

# 3. When to Report

Report when there is a concern involving:

- abuse or exploitation concern;
- harassment;
- unsafe conduct;
- boundary violation;
- coercion;
- spiritual manipulation;
- serious power imbalance;
- privacy breach connected to vulnerable persons;
- retaliation;
- misconduct by a person in authority;
- resident, worker, student, grant applicant, or volunteer safety concern;
- any concern that local law requires to be reported.

---

# 4. Immediate Danger

If immediate danger exists, contact emergency services or appropriate local authorities according to the jurisdiction.

Do not wait for internal approval when urgent safety action is required.

---

# 5. Who Receives Reports

Reports may be made to:

- immediate supervisor;
- ministry leader;
- Executive Director;
- Board Chair or designated board officer;
- Safeguarding Lead if appointed;
- Privacy Lead if privacy is involved;
- legal or compliance lead where authorized;
- designated national chapter contact.

A report involving a leader should not be routed only to that same leader.

---

# 6. What to Include

A safeguarding report should include:

- date of report;
- person reporting;
- role of person reporting;
- concern category;
- date of concern if known;
- location if relevant;
- persons or roles involved;
- immediate safety concern;
- action already taken;
- whether authorities were contacted;
- documents or evidence if available;
- confidentiality level;
- requested follow-up.

Avoid unnecessary personal detail.

---

# 7. What Not to Do

Do not:

- promise absolute secrecy;
- investigate beyond your role;
- confront a person when unsafe or unauthorized;
- spread details;
- delete messages or records;
- delay urgent reporting;
- pressure a person not to report;
- use spiritual language to silence concern;
- handle a serious issue as ordinary conflict.

---

# 8. Report Intake

The person receiving the report should:

- listen carefully;
- record necessary facts;
- assess immediate safety;
- escalate according to incident level;
- protect confidentiality;
- avoid making promises beyond authority;
- identify required next steps;
- preserve relevant records.

---

# 9. Escalation Levels

Use [[Incident Escalation Policy]].

| Concern | Minimum Escalation |
|---|---|
| Minor boundary concern | Supervisor or ministry leader |
| Repeated boundary concern | Executive or Safeguarding Lead |
| Serious safety concern | Executive and board delegate |
| Legal or required reporting concern | Authorized leader and legal review |
| Senior leader concern | Board Chair or designated board process |
| Immediate danger | Emergency services / local authority first |

---

# 10. Documentation

Safeguarding reports should be documented in a restricted record.

The record should include:

- report;
- escalation;
- decisions;
- actions taken;
- support offered;
- referrals;
- legal or authority contacts where applicable;
- follow-up dates;
- closure decision.

---

# 11. Support

The Society should consider appropriate support for affected persons.

Support may include:

- safety planning;
- referral to appropriate services;
- chaplaincy support where appropriate and not conflicted;
- local church referral where appropriate;
- practical support within policy;
- communication about next steps.

---

# 12. Corrective Action

If a concern reveals policy failure, role failure, training need, or misconduct, create corrective action under [[Corrective Action Policy]].

Corrective action may involve:

- training;
- supervision;
- restriction;
- removal;
- policy revision;
- legal review;
- privacy review;
- Mission Alignment Review.

---

# 13. Closure

A safeguarding matter may be closed only when proper review confirms that required actions are complete or transferred to the proper authority.

Closure should document:

- decision;
- authority;
- actions completed;
- remaining risk;
- follow-up date if needed;
- record location.

---

# 14. Review

This procedure should be reviewed annually and after any serious safeguarding report.
