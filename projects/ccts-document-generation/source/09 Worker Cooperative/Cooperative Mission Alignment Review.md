# Cooperative Mission Alignment Review

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a cooperative-specific Mission Alignment Review process for Society-affiliated worker cooperatives, pilot cooperatives, and cooperative enterprises.

The review exists to ensure that cooperative work remains faithful to the Christian Community Transformation Society's mission, preserves worker dignity, respects the Society/church distinction, avoids exploitation, remains financially honest, and stays under proper governance.

This document applies the general [[Mission Alignment Review]] system to cooperative ministry.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The cooperative must serve people and mission before profit, while still operating with real business discipline.

A cooperative that becomes merely commercial has drifted. A cooperative that ignores financial reality has also drifted. Mission alignment requires both compassion and competence.

---

# 3. Review Frequency

Cooperative Mission Alignment Review should occur:

- before launching a pilot cooperative;
- at the end of a pilot stage;
- annually during launch or growth;
- at least every three years once stable;
- before scaling;
- before major restructuring;
- after serious worker complaints;
- after significant financial concern;
- after safety incidents;
- when mission drift is suspected.

---

# 4. Review Authority

The review may be conducted by:

- cooperative board or steering committee;
- National Board;
- International Board delegate;
- Mission Alignment Review team;
- external reviewer;
- Church Advisory representative where church-related issues are involved;
- Theological Oversight representative where doctrinal or spiritual care concerns arise.

The reviewer should not have an unmanaged conflict of interest.

# 6. Review Questions

## 6.1 Mission

- Does the cooperative clearly serve the Society's mission?
- Who is being served?
- Why is a cooperative the right structure?
- Is the cooperative helping workers grow in skill, responsibility, and dignity?
- Is the cooperative drifting into mere business?
- Is the cooperative being used to serve insiders?

## 6.2 Worker Dignity

- Are workers treated fairly?
- Are worker status and compensation clear?
- Is training meaningful?
- Are vulnerable workers protected from exploitation?
- Are worker voices heard?
- Are workers safe?
- Are workers supported without being controlled?

## 6.3 Christian Identity and Church Boundary

- Is the cooperative's Christian identity clear?
- Is spiritual care voluntary?
- Are workers pressured to disclose spiritual matters?
- Is the cooperative attempting to function as a church?
- Are workers encouraged toward faithful local churches where appropriate?
- Are chaplaincy boundaries respected?

## 6.4 Governance

- Are authority lines clear?
- Are reserved powers respected?
- Are decisions documented?
- Are conflicts of interest disclosed?
- Are reports submitted?
- Are worker participation structures functioning?
- Is management accountable?

## 6.5 Financial Stewardship

- Is the cooperative financially sustainable?
- Is pricing realistic?
- Are workers compensated lawfully?
- Are payroll, taxes, insurance, and reserves handled properly?
- Are related-party payments disclosed?
- Is surplus allocation governed?
- Are financial problems reported early?

## 6.6 Operations and Customers

- Are contracts reviewed?
- Are customers served honestly?
- Are workers protected from abusive or unsafe customers?
- Are work quality and deadlines managed?
- Are customer complaints reviewed?
- Are tools, equipment, and data protected?

## 6.7 Risk and Safety

- Are risks tracked in [[Cooperative Risk Register]]?
- Are safety incidents reported?
- Are high risks escalated?
- Are workers allowed to pause unsafe work?
- Are data and privacy risks addressed?
- Are legal and insurance risks reviewed?

---

# 7. Rating Scale

Each area may be rated as follows:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strong | Clearly aligned and healthy |
| Stable | Generally aligned with ordinary improvements needed |
| Watch | Concerns exist and require monitoring |
| Corrective Action Required | Significant concerns require action |
| Critical | Serious mission, legal, financial, safety, worker, or governance concern |

---

# 8. Review Findings

The review should produce findings in these categories:

1. strengths;
2. concerns;
3. risks;
4. required corrections;
5. recommended improvements;
6. decisions needed;
7. policy revisions needed;
8. follow-up date;
9. responsible persons.

---

# 9. Corrective Action

Corrective actions may include:

- worker support plan;
- compensation correction;
- legal review;
- safety pause;
- contract review;
- customer termination;
- policy revision;
- financial restructuring;
- reserve plan;
- training;
- governance restructuring;
- chaplaincy clarification;
- church referral improvement;
- conflict of interest remedy;
- pause on scaling;
- closure review.

A cooperative review without corrective action is just a meeting with better headings.
