# Church Advisory Fellowship Charter

## 1. Purpose

This Charter establishes the International Church Advisory Fellowship of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Church Advisory Fellowship exists to provide churchly counsel, wisdom, accountability, encouragement, theological perspective, and cooperative support to the Society while preserving the proper distinction between the Society and the local church.

The Fellowship is advisory unless specific authority is granted by the [[International Constitution]], [[International Board]], or approved policy.

This Charter should be read with [[Statement of Faith]], [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], [[Church Partnership Framework]], [[Church Recognition Standards]], and [[Theological Oversight Council Charter]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society supports the local church. It does not become the local church.

The Church Advisory Fellowship helps the Society remain church-supporting rather than church-replacing.

The Fellowship provides counsel from faithful churches and church leaders, but it does not exercise ecclesiastical authority over the Society or over participating churches unless such authority already belongs to those churches in their own lawful ecclesiastical structures.

---

# 3. Nature of the Fellowship

The Fellowship is a structured advisory relationship between the Society and recognized churches, ministers, elders, theologians, or church representatives.

It may be organized internationally and may have national or regional expressions under international standards.

The Fellowship is not:

- a church court;
- a presbytery;
- a classis;
- a synod;
- a consistory;
- a session;
- an ecclesiastical denomination;
- a replacement for local church authority;
- a governing board of the Society.

The Fellowship exists to advise, not to govern, unless a specific advisory recommendation is made binding by board policy or constitutional provision.

---

# 4. Objectives

The Fellowship exists to:

1. help the Society remain faithful to its church-supporting mission;
2. provide counsel on church relationships;
3. support theological and pastoral wisdom;
4. advise on chaplaincy standards;
5. advise on seminary recognition where required;
6. encourage healthy relationships with local churches;
7. identify risks of the Society functioning as a church substitute;
8. advise on ministries involving discipleship, prayer, spiritual care, or church referrals;
9. support international expansion through churchly counsel;
10. assist in mission alignment review where church relationships are involved.

---

# 5. Relationship to the International Board

The International Board may consult the Fellowship on matters involving:

- church partnerships;
- chaplaincy;
- theological education standards;
- spiritual care;
- worship boundaries;
- prayer gatherings;
- ministry to unchurched people;
- relationships with Reformed denominations;
- church-supporting mission integrity;
- international expansion into new church contexts.

The Board is not required to accept every recommendation of the Fellowship unless governing documents make a specific consultation or approval binding.

The Board should document serious disagreement with the Fellowship when the matter is material to doctrine, mission, or church relationships.

---

# 6. Relationship to the Theological Oversight Council

The Church Advisory Fellowship and the [[Theological Oversight Council Charter|Theological Oversight Council]] have related but distinct roles.

The Fellowship provides churchly and pastoral counsel from recognized churches and representatives.

The Theological Oversight Council provides more focused doctrinal review, theological analysis, and mission drift evaluation.

The two bodies may cooperate but should not be confused.

---

# 7. Membership of the Fellowship

The Fellowship may include:

- local churches;
- ministers;
- elders;
- theologians;
- seminary representatives;
- denominational representatives;
- national church advisory teams;
- recognized Reformed church bodies;
- other church representatives approved under policy.

The Society should prioritize churches and leaders who are confessionally Reformed, faithful to Scripture, and supportive of the Society's mission.

---

# 8. Admission Criteria

Participants in the Fellowship should ordinarily:

- affirm or substantially agree with the Society's [[Statement of Faith]];
- belong to or represent faithful local churches;
- respect the Society's church-supporting role;
- support the Society's mission;
- avoid using the Fellowship for denominational competition;
- commit to charitable counsel;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- respect confidentiality.

The Society may adopt more specific [[Church Recognition Standards]].

---

# 9. International and National Expressions

The Fellowship may function at multiple levels:

```text
International Church Advisory Fellowship
        |
        +-- Canada Church Advisory Team
        +-- Hong Kong Church Advisory Team
        +-- Future National Church Advisory Teams
```

National advisory teams may advise national chapters, but they must remain aligned with international standards.

A national advisory team may not redefine the Society's doctrine or mission.

---

# 10. Areas of Counsel

The Fellowship may advise on:

- church partnership standards;
- chaplain qualifications;
- spiritual care boundaries;
- annual day of prayer;
- devotional meetings;
- unchurched beneficiaries;
- referral pathways to local churches;
- ministry materials;
- theological education;
- local church concerns;
- international church context;
- ministry proposals affecting churches;
- complaints from churches;
- mission drift concerns.

---

# 11. Seminary Recognition

Where the Society requires theological education from a seminary recognized by the Fellowship, the Fellowship may advise on whether a seminary is appropriate for Society purposes.

Seminary recognition should consider:

- confessional identity;
- doctrinal faithfulness;
- academic seriousness;
- relationship to churches;
- pastoral formation;
- compatibility with Society chaplaincy and ministry standards.

Recognition of a seminary for Society purposes does not require every participating church to recognize that seminary for ordination.

---

# 12. Meetings

The Fellowship should meet at intervals appropriate to the Society's stage and needs.

Meetings may include:

- prayer;
- ministry updates;
- consultation requests;
- church relationship reports;
- chaplaincy concerns;
- national chapter updates;
- theological concerns;
- mission alignment concerns;
- recommendations to the Board.

Meeting notes should be kept, but sensitive pastoral matters should be recorded carefully and discreetly.

---

# 13. Recommendations

Recommendations from the Fellowship should be:

- written when material;
- clear;
- tied to the issue presented;
- grounded in Scripture and wisdom;
- respectful of board authority;
- mindful of local church authority;
- recorded when significant.

The Fellowship should distinguish between:

- counsel;
- concern;
- warning;
- recommendation;
- request for review;
- referral to Theological Oversight Council.

---

# 14. Confidentiality

Fellowship participants may receive sensitive information.

Confidentiality must be maintained regarding:

- pastoral care;
- chaplaincy concerns;
- member or beneficiary information;
- church disputes;
- national chapter concerns;
- theological review matters;
- personnel issues;
- legal or safeguarding concerns.

Confidentiality must not be used to hide abuse, legal obligations, or serious misconduct.

---

# 15. Conflicts of Interest

Fellowship participants must disclose conflicts of interest.

Conflicts may arise when a participant or their church:

- applies for grants;
- receives Society support;
- refers residents or beneficiaries;
- participates in a housing project;
- has a contractual relationship;
- is involved in a complaint;
- has denominational or institutional interests.

Disclosure protects trust. It is not an accusation.

---

# 16. Limits of Authority

The Fellowship shall not:

- govern the Society;
- direct the International Board;
- appoint or remove directors;
- control Society assets;
- discipline Society members as church members;
- require a local church to act;
- administer sacraments;
- override national chapter legal obligations;
- approve actions reserved to the International Board unless expressly authorized.

---

# 17. Reporting

The Fellowship may submit reports to the International Board or national chapters.

Reports may include:

- recommendations;
- concerns;
- ministry observations;
- church relationship updates;
- chaplaincy advice;
- seminary recognition advice;
- annual summary.

Reports should be concise, clear, and respectful of confidentiality.

---

# 18. Dissolution or Suspension

The International Board may suspend or dissolve the Fellowship or a national advisory team if it no longer serves the Society's mission, becomes inactive, acts outside authority, creates confusion with church authority, or undermines doctrine or governance.

Dissolution should be documented.

---

# 19. Review

This Charter should be reviewed annually during the founding period and at least every three years after stable operation.
