# Cooperative Membership Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for membership in a Society-affiliated worker cooperative.

Cooperative membership must be clear, lawful, mission-aligned, and distinct from membership in the Christian Community Transformation Society or membership in a local church.

This policy should be read with [[Worker Cooperative Framework]], [[Cooperative Governance Policy]], [[Worker Participation and Development Policy]], and [[Cooperative Compensation and Surplus Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Cooperative membership is a work and governance relationship, not a spiritual status.

A cooperative member may share responsibility in the cooperative, but cooperative membership does not make a person a church member, Society member, board member, or theological leader.

The Society should avoid confusing participation in work with standing before God, standing in the church, or standing in Society governance.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- formal worker-members;
- probationary members;
- founding cooperative members;
- trainee members;
- associate members where allowed;
- worker representatives;
- cooperative board members;
- persons transitioning into or out of membership.

Local cooperative law and governing documents may require additional rules.

---

# 4. Membership Categories

A cooperative may define membership categories such as:

## 4.1 Founding Member

A person approved to help launch the cooperative.

## 4.2 Probationary Member

A worker being evaluated for full membership.

## 4.3 Full Worker-Member

A worker with defined voting, participation, and responsibility rights.

## 4.4 Trainee Member

A worker participating in a training pathway with limited rights.

## 4.5 Associate Member

A person connected to the cooperative with limited participation rights where lawful.

## 4.6 Non-Member Worker

A worker who is employed, contracted, or trained by the cooperative without formal membership.

Categories must be legally reviewed.

---

# 5. Eligibility

Eligibility for membership may consider:

- work participation;
- completion of probation;
- skill and reliability;
- commitment to cooperative responsibilities;
- willingness to follow policies;
- ability to work safely;
- mission alignment;
- conflict of interest;
- legal eligibility;
- training completion;
- approval by proper authority.

Eligibility must not be arbitrary or unlawfully discriminatory.

---

# 6. Application Process

A membership application process may include:

1. expression of interest;
2. explanation of cooperative mission;
3. explanation of Society relationship;
4. review of worker status;
5. probationary period where appropriate;
6. training;
7. conflict of interest disclosure;
8. supervisor or member review;
9. board or committee approval;
10. membership agreement;
11. orientation;
12. entry into membership register.

The process should be documented.

---

# 7. Probationary Period

A probationary period may be used before full membership.

The probationary period should define:

- length;
- work expectations;
- training requirements;
- review criteria;
- voting rights if any;
- compensation;
- supervision;
- feedback process;
- decision authority;
- possible outcomes.

Probation should be a real review, not a vague waiting room.

---

# 8. Rights of Members

Depending on law and governing documents, members may have rights such as:

- participation in meetings;
- voting;
- access to approved financial summaries;
- eligibility for worker representative roles;
- training;
- fair review;
- grievance process;
- surplus allocation where lawful and approved;
- information about major cooperative decisions.

Member rights must be clearly written.

---

# 9. Responsibilities of Members

Members may be responsible to:

- work faithfully;
- follow policies;
- attend required meetings;
- participate in governance;
- disclose conflicts;
- protect confidentiality;
- treat others with respect;
- support safety;
- steward property;
- serve customers honestly;
- report risks;
- preserve mission alignment.

Membership is not only a voice. It is also a burden of responsibility.

---

# 10. Voting

Voting rights must be defined in governing documents.

Voting rules should address:

- who may vote;
- one-member-one-vote principle where applicable;
- quorum;
- meeting notice;
- proxy voting if allowed;
- conflict of interest;
- reserved powers;
- matters requiring Society approval;
- records of decisions.

Member voting cannot override Society reserved powers.

---

# 11. Relationship to Society Membership

Cooperative membership is distinct from Society membership.

A cooperative member does not automatically become a Society member.

A Society member does not automatically become a cooperative member.

The two systems may overlap only where policy and law allow.

---

# 12. Relationship to Church Membership

Cooperative membership is distinct from church membership.

The cooperative should encourage faithful local church connection where appropriate, but membership in the cooperative does not replace church membership, pastoral care, sacraments, or discipline.

The cooperative must not claim spiritual authority that belongs to the local church.

---

# 13. Membership Register

The cooperative shall maintain a membership register.

The register should include:

- member name;
- category;
- date admitted;
- voting status;
- training completed;
- role;
- status changes;
- termination or withdrawal date;
- notes required by law.

The register should be secure and accessible only to authorized persons.

---

# 14. Conflicts of Interest

Members must disclose conflicts of interest.

Examples include:

- family relationships;
- contracts with the cooperative;
- customer relationships;
- financial interests;
- employment decisions;
- Society leadership roles;
- housing involvement;
- vendor relationships.

Conflicts must be managed according to [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 15. Discipline or Corrective Action

Membership discipline or corrective action may be needed for:

- misconduct;
- repeated failure to meet responsibilities;
- unsafe conduct;
- confidentiality breach;
- harassment;
- fraud;
- mission contradiction;
- conflict of interest violation;
- refusal of accountability;
- serious work failure.

Corrective action must be fair, documented, and lawful.

---

# 16. Termination of Membership

Membership may end through:

- resignation;
- withdrawal;
- termination for cause;
- loss of eligibility;
- end of employment or work relationship;
- dissolution of cooperative;
- death;
- legal requirement;
- board or member decision according to governing documents.

Termination must respect law, governing documents, and procedural fairness.

---

# 17. Appeals or Review

A member may have access to review or appeal where required by law or policy.

Review should consider whether:

- process was followed;
- evidence was adequate;
- conflict of interest existed;
- decision was fair;
- law or governing documents require correction.

---

# 18. Reporting

Membership changes should be reported in cooperative governance reports.

Reports may include:

- number of members;
- new members;
- probationary members;
- exits;
- training status;
- participation concerns;
- disputes;
- governance risks.

Personal details should be protected.

---

# 19. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during cooperative launch and at least every three years once stable.
