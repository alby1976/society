# Church Recognition Standards

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes standards for recognizing churches, church bodies, ministers, elders, or church representatives for advisory, partnership, referral, or ministry cooperation with the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Recognition does not imply that the Society governs churches. It means that, for Society purposes, the church or representative is considered suitable for the specified relationship.

This document should be read with [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter]], [[Church Partnership Framework]], and [[Statement of Faith]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should cooperate with churches carefully.

The Society is church-supporting, not church-replacing. Recognized churches should help the Society remain faithful to Christ, Scripture, Reformed theology, and local church life.

Recognition must not become a popularity contest, denominational turf war, or rubber stamp.

---

# 3. Recognition Categories

The Society may recognize churches or church representatives for specific purposes, including:

- Church Advisory Fellowship participation;
- partner church relationship;
- referral church relationship;
- chaplaincy support;
- seminary recognition advice;
- grant relationship;
- housing support;
- worker cooperative support;
- prayer and training cooperation;
- national chapter formation.

Recognition should specify the purpose. A church recognized for referrals is not automatically approved for every type of partnership.

---

# 4. Essential Standards

A recognized church should ordinarily:

1. confess the Triune God;
2. affirm the authority of Scripture;
3. proclaim salvation through Jesus Christ;
4. maintain the ordinary marks of a true church;
5. practice lawful church governance;
6. administer Word and sacraments according to its confession;
7. exercise pastoral care and discipline;
8. uphold Christian moral teaching;
9. avoid serious unresolved scandal;
10. respect the Society's church-supporting mission.

For advisory or theological roles, stronger Reformed confessional compatibility should be required.

---

# 5. Reformed Compatibility

Because the Society is confessionally Reformed, churches in advisory or theological roles should ordinarily be confessionally Reformed or substantially compatible with the Society's theological commitments.

Recognized confessional traditions may include churches aligned with:

- Westminster Standards;
- Three Forms of Unity;
- other historically Reformed confessions approved by the Society;
- Reformed Presbyterian or Continental Reformed traditions;
- faithful churches recognized through policy.

The Society may cooperate more broadly in limited practical matters where doctrine is not compromised.

---

# 6. Marks of Church Faithfulness

Recognition should consider whether a church is marked by:

- faithful preaching of the Word;
- proper administration of sacraments;
- church discipline;
- prayer;
- pastoral care;
- accountable leadership;
- moral seriousness;
- care for the vulnerable;
- commitment to discipleship;
- integrity in finances and governance.

No church is perfect. But unresolved serious disorder should not be ignored.

---

# 7. Church Governance

The Society should understand the church's governance structure.

Relevant questions include:

- Who are the church's lawful officers?
- Is there elder oversight?
- Is the church connected to a denomination or federation?
- Is there accountability beyond one leader?
- How are discipline and pastoral care handled?
- Who may speak for the church?
- Are there unresolved governance disputes?

The Society should avoid partnerships where church authority is unclear and risk is high.

---

# 8. Public Scandal or Serious Concern

Recognition may be denied, suspended, or reviewed if a church or representative is involved in:

- serious doctrinal error;
- abuse cover-up;
- financial misconduct;
- authoritarian leadership;
- unresolved public scandal;
- legal crisis;
- exploitation of vulnerable persons;
- divisive conduct;
- hostility to the Society's mission;
- misuse of Society relationship.

Serious concerns should be handled carefully, documented appropriately, and reviewed by authorized persons.

---

# 9. Recognition Process

The recognition process may include:

1. application or nomination;
2. doctrinal review;
3. church governance review;
4. ministry relationship review;
5. reference or denominational information;
6. conflict of interest disclosure;
7. review by Church Advisory Fellowship or authorized body;
8. recommendation;
9. approval;
10. registry entry;
11. review date.

The process should be proportionate to the relationship's significance.

---

# 10. Approval Authority

Approval authority may vary by recognition type.

Possible authorities include:

- International Board;
- National Board;
- Church Advisory Fellowship;
- Theological Oversight Council;
- International Executive Director;
- National Executive Director;
- authorized committee.

Relationships affecting international doctrine, chaplaincy standards, or national chapter formation should receive higher-level review.

---

# 11. Recognition Registry

The Society should maintain a registry of recognized churches and church representatives.

The registry may include:

- name;
- location;
- denomination or federation;
- recognition category;
- approval authority;
- date recognized;
- review date;
- contact person;
- related agreement;
- restrictions or notes;
- status.

The registry should be maintained with appropriate confidentiality.

---

# 12. Review Cycle

Recognition should be reviewed periodically.

Review may be annual, biennial, or tied to renewal of a partnership agreement.

Review should consider whether the relationship remains doctrinally, pastorally, legally, and missionally appropriate.

---

# 13. Suspension or Revocation

Recognition may be suspended or revoked if standards are no longer met or serious concerns arise.

Suspension may be temporary pending review.

Revocation should be documented and communicated appropriately.

---

# 14. Local Context and International Standards

National chapters may recommend churches for local recognition.

International standards remain applicable, especially for advisory, theological, chaplaincy, or cross-border relationships.

A church recognized in one national context is not automatically recognized for all international purposes.

---

# 15. Review

These standards should be reviewed annually during the founding period and whenever the Society enters a new country, forms a national advisory team, or revises church partnership policy.
