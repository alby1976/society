# Grant Application and Review Process

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines the ordinary process for receiving, reviewing, approving, denying, documenting, and closing grant applications submitted to the Church Support Foundation.

The process protects fairness, stewardship, confidentiality, mission alignment, and accountability.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Grant review should be careful enough to protect funds and simple enough to serve ministry.

The Foundation should avoid both careless giving and bureaucratic exhaustion. The goal is wise stewardship, not making applicants climb Mount Paperwork.

---

# 3. Process Overview

The standard grant process includes:

1. inquiry;
2. eligibility screening;
3. application;
4. document collection;
5. conflict review;
6. financial review;
7. theological or church advisory review where needed;
8. grant committee review;
9. decision;
10. grant agreement;
11. payment;
12. reporting;
13. closure;
14. archival record.

---

# 4. Inquiry Stage

An inquiry may be informal or written.

The Foundation may use inquiry to determine:

- basic eligibility;
- grant category;
- mission fit;
- expected amount;
- timing;
- applicant type;
- obvious conflicts;
- whether a full application is appropriate.

A declined inquiry should be recorded briefly.

---

# 5. Eligibility Screening

Eligibility screening should confirm:

- applicant identity;
- eligible category;
- lawful purpose;
- mission fit;
- doctrinal or church connection where required;
- geographic or chapter fit;
- funding availability;
- reporting capacity;
- no obvious prohibited use.

Eligibility screening does not guarantee approval.

---

# 6. Application Requirements

A full application may request:

- applicant information;
- church or organization information;
- project description;
- theological or church connection;
- grant amount requested;
- budget;
- timeline;
- expected outcomes;
- sustainability plan;
- other funding;
- references or endorsements;
- conflicts of interest;
- reporting agreement;
- privacy consent.

Only necessary information should be collected.

---

# 7. Conflict Review

Before substantive review, conflicts must be identified.

The reviewer should ask:

- Is the applicant connected to a decision-maker?
- Is a donor influencing the application?
- Is a reviewer connected to the applicant's church, school, ministry, or family?
- Would any insider receive benefit?
- Is a vendor connected to the grant?

Conflicts must be recorded and managed.

---

# 8. Financial Review

Financial review should consider:

- reasonableness of budget;
- permitted uses;
- other funding;
- restricted funds;
- payment schedule;
- financial controls;
- risk of misuse;
- sustainability;
- reporting ability.

Large or complex grants may require deeper financial review.

---

# 9. Theological Review

Theological review may be required when a grant involves:

- theological education;
- seminary support;
- doctrinal teaching;
- church leadership development;
- chaplaincy training;
- disputed theological matters;
- new ministry model;
- publication or curriculum;
- international doctrinal questions.

The review should be conducted by the appropriate theological authority.

---

# 10. Church Advisory Review

Church advisory review may be required when a grant involves:

- direct church support;
- church relationships;
- local church referrals;
- ministry candidates;
- church capacity-building;
- pastoral or elder training support;
- sensitive church context;
- possible church authority concerns.

The Foundation should respect local church authority.

---

# 11. Grant Committee Review

The grant committee should review:

- application;
- eligibility;
- mission fit;
- budget;
- risks;
- conflicts;
- advisory comments;
- reporting plan;
- funding source;
- recommended decision.

Committee discussion should be recorded.

---

# 12. Decision Options

Possible decisions include:

- approved;
- approved with conditions;
- partially approved;
- deferred;
- referred to another program;
- declined;
- declined without prejudice;
- closed by applicant withdrawal.

Reasons should be documented.

---

# 13. Grant Agreement

Approved grants should use a written agreement.

The agreement should include:

- recipient;
- amount;
- purpose;
- payment schedule;
- permitted uses;
- prohibited uses;
- reporting requirements;
- term;
- conditions;
- recovery or suspension rights;
- confidentiality;
- signatures.

No payment should be made before the required agreement is complete unless emergency authority allows it.

---

# 14. Payment

Payments should be made according to approved schedule and financial controls.

Payments may be:

- lump sum;
- milestone-based;
- reimbursement;
- direct-to-provider;
- matching grant;
- conditional release.

Payment records must be retained.

---

# 15. Monitoring

The Foundation may monitor grants through:

- reports;
- receipts;
- check-ins;
- site or ministry updates;
- church or advisor confirmation;
- financial summary;
- final report.

Monitoring should be proportionate to grant size and risk.

---

# 16. Closure

A grant is closed when:

- funds are used;
- required reports are received;
- unused funds are returned or approved for other use;
- issues are resolved;
- closure record is completed.

Closure records should identify lessons learned.

---

# 17. Denial Communication

Denials should be respectful and clear enough to avoid confusion.

The Foundation may explain:

- ineligibility;
- lack of funds;
- mission mismatch;
- incomplete application;
- risk concern;
- timing;
- policy limitation.

Denial should not disclose confidential committee discussions unnecessarily.

---

# 18. Recordkeeping

The grant file should include:

- inquiry;
- application;
- documents;
- conflict disclosures;
- reviews;
- decision;
- agreement;
- payment records;
- reports;
- correspondence;
- closure record.

---

# 19. Review

This process should be reviewed annually during Foundation launch and at least every three years once grant operations stabilize.
