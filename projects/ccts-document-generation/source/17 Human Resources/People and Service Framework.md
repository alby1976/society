# People and Service Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society understands people serving within the Society.

The Society may involve board members, staff, volunteers, contractors, advisors, chaplains, housing workers, cooperative workers, Foundation reviewers, national chapter leaders, and ministry participants. Each role must be treated with clarity, dignity, accountability, and proper boundaries.

## 2. Governing Principle

People are not tools for the mission. People are neighbors, servants, leaders, and stewards within the mission.

The Society should not build ministry by consuming people. A structure that requires chronic overload, unclear roles, secret work, or unreviewed authority is not healthy governance.

## 3. Scope

This framework applies to board members, staff, volunteers, contractors, ministry leaders, chaplains, advisors, national chapter leaders, housing personnel, worker cooperative personnel, Foundation personnel, committee members, and formation teams.

Local chapters must comply with local employment, volunteer, privacy, safety, and nonprofit law.

## 4. Role Clarity

Every person serving in a continuing role should know:

- role title;
- reporting relationship;
- authority;
- limits;
- expected duties;
- confidentiality level;
- reporting duties;
- term or duration;
- review process;
- conflict of interest requirements;
- whether the role is paid, volunteer, advisory, or contracted.

Confusion about role is one of the fastest ways to create conflict.

## 5. Categories of Service

The Society may use the following categories:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Board Member | A person with governance responsibility |
| Staff Member | A person employed or formally engaged in an operational role |
| Volunteer | A person serving without compensation in an approved role |
| Contractor | A person or entity providing services under contract |
| Advisor | A person giving counsel without direct governance authority unless separately granted |
| Ministry Leader | A person responsible for a program, team, or ministry area |
| Chaplain | A person appointed to provide spiritual care under chaplaincy policy |

## 6. Appointment Principles

Appointments should be mission-aligned, role-specific, documented, lawful, free from unmanaged conflicts, proportionate to capacity, reviewed periodically, connected to reporting, and clear about authority.

No one should be appointed merely because they are available or eager.

## 7. Service Expectations

People serving the Society should:

- act with integrity;
- follow approved policy;
- respect local churches;
- preserve confidentiality;
- disclose conflicts;
- submit required reports;
- stay within authority;
- treat others with dignity;
- seek help when overloaded;
- protect the Society's mission.

## 8. Training and Orientation

The Society should provide orientation appropriate to role.

Orientation may include mission and values, Society/church distinction, role description, authority limits, reporting, confidentiality, conflict of interest, safety and safeguarding where applicable, privacy, records, and relevant ministry policies.

## 9. Leadership Health

The Society shall take leadership health seriously.

Leadership health includes sustainable workload, spiritual responsibilities, family responsibilities where applicable, rest, accountability, support, role fit, and protection from chronic overload.

A ministry that survives only by exhausting faithful people needs correction.

## 10. Reporting

People serving in roles should report according to role and risk.

Reports may include 5-15 reports, ministry reports, board summaries, incident reports, annual reports, risk reports, and corrective action updates.

Reporting is not mistrust. It is how shared work stays truthful.

## 11. Confidentiality

People serving the Society may access sensitive information.

Confidentiality applies to personnel matters, resident matters, worker matters, grant applicants, donors, students, churches, legal matters, safeguarding concerns, and spiritual care.

Confidentiality should be explained before access is granted.

## 12. Conflicts of Interest

All continuing roles require conflict awareness.

Conflicts may involve family relationships, financial benefit, church relationships, donor influence, housing applications, cooperative employment, Foundation grants, contracts, or advisory influence.

Conflicts must be disclosed and managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

## 13. Role Review

Roles should be reviewed regularly.

Review should ask whether the role is still needed, the person is qualified, workload is sustainable, reporting is happening, authority is clear, conflicts are managed, the role is mission-aligned, and support is needed.

## 14. Ending a Role

A role may end through completion of term, resignation, removal, role restructuring, loss of qualification, misconduct, inability to continue, mission or governance concern, or legal requirement.

Role endings should include record handoff, access removal, confidentiality reminder, and transition plan.

## 15. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually and whenever the Society creates a new major role category.
