# Records Management Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society creates, stores, protects, reviews, retains, and archives official records.

Records management protects accountability, continuity, legal compliance, institutional memory, and future leadership.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Records should be accurate, accessible to authorized persons, protected from improper disclosure, and preserved according to their importance.

A record that cannot be found is only slightly better than a record that was never made.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- governance records;
- board records;
- committee records;
- reports;
- financial records;
- legal records;
- ministry records;
- chaplaincy records;
- housing records;
- cooperative records;
- Foundation records;
- national chapter records;
- public communications;
- policies and templates;
- decision records;
- document registry records.

---

# 4. Record Categories

## 4.1 Governance Records

Constitution, bylaws, board minutes, resolutions, policies, charters, registers, decisions.

## 4.2 Financial Records

Budgets, financial statements, receipts, invoices, donor records, grant records, restricted fund records.

## 4.3 Legal Records

Incorporation documents, filings, contracts, leases, legal correspondence, insurance.

## 4.4 Ministry Records

Reports, program records, participation summaries, risk registers, annual reports.

## 4.5 Confidential Care Records

Chaplaincy, spiritual care, resident support, worker support, grant applicant, student support, safeguarding, or sensitive pastoral-adjacent records.

## 4.6 Public Records

Approved public statements, website text, newsletters, published reports, public policies.

---

# 5. Source of Truth

The Society shall define the source of truth for each record type.

For the governance vault:

- Markdown source files are the source of truth;
- generated PDFs, DOCX files, HTML, or dashboards are outputs;
- generated outputs should not be edited as master copies;
- version history should be preserved in source.

---

# 6. Naming and Identification

Official documents should use stable document IDs.

File names should be clear, consistent, and human-readable.

Records should avoid ambiguous names such as:

- final-final;
- new version;
- updated copy;
- board thing;
- policy draft latest.

The computer may tolerate chaos, but future leaders will not.

---

# 7. Document Registry

Official governance documents should be entered in [[Document Registry]].

The registry should record:

- title;
- document ID;
- version;
- status;
- category;
- owner;
- authority;
- review date;
- dependencies;
- related documents.

---

# 8. Decision Register

Significant decisions should be recorded in [[Decision Register]].

Decision records should include:

- decision ID;
- date;
- authority;
- decision;
- rationale;
- related documents;
- conditions;
- responsible person;
- review date.

---

# 9. Access Control

Access should be based on role and need.

Records may be classified as:

- Public;
- Internal;
- Restricted;
- Confidential;
- Board Confidential;
- Legal Privileged where applicable.

Sensitive information should not be broadly accessible merely because it is convenient.

---

# 10. Confidential Records

Confidential records require special protection.

Examples include:

- personnel matters;
- resident information;
- worker information;
- grant applications;
- student support;
- donor restrictions;
- church concerns;
- chaplaincy notes;
- safeguarding concerns;
- legal advice;
- conflict investigations.

Access should be limited and logged where feasible.

---

# 11. Retention

Records should be retained according to legal requirements and Society policy.

Suggested retention categories:

| Record Type | Suggested Retention |
|---|---|
| Constitution, bylaws, board minutes, major decisions | Permanent |
| Annual reports | Permanent |
| Financial records | At least legal minimum, often 7 years or more depending on jurisdiction |
| Grant records | At least legal minimum, often 7 years or more depending on jurisdiction |
| Housing records | According to local law and risk |
| Personnel records | According to local law |
| Incident records | According to local law and risk |
| Drafts | Retain selectively if important to institutional memory |

Local law may require different periods.

---

# 12. Archiving

Records should be archived when no longer actively used but still required for retention or institutional memory.

Archiving should preserve:

- file integrity;
- metadata;
- version;
- access level;
- retention period;
- review date;
- related decision if any.

---

# 13. Deletion

Official records should not be deleted casually.

Deletion may occur only when:

- retention period expired;
- legal holds do not apply;
- record is duplicate or non-substantive;
- deletion is authorized;
- confidentiality and privacy requirements are followed.

Do not delete records to hide mistakes.

---

# 14. Legal Hold

If legal action, investigation, audit, complaint, or serious dispute is likely, relevant records must be preserved.

Legal hold should suspend ordinary deletion.

---

# 15. Backups

Important records should be backed up.

Backup planning should consider:

- location;
- frequency;
- access control;
- encryption where needed;
- restoration test;
- disaster recovery;
- national chapter copies;
- source file preservation.

---

# 16. Version Control

Documents should use version control.

Version changes should record:

- date;
- version number;
- summary of changes;
- author or editor;
- reviewer;
- approval authority;
- superseded document if any.

---

# 17. Records Transfer

When a leader, staff member, volunteer, board member, or ministry leader leaves a role, official records must be transferred.

Exit should include:

- document handoff;
- password or access removal;
- task transfer;
- pending decisions;
- report status;
- archive location;
- confidentiality reminder.

---

# 18. National Chapter Records

National Chapters must maintain records consistent with international standards and local law.

National Chapters should maintain:

- local document registry;
- local decision register;
- annual reports;
- financial reports;
- risk registers;
- local adaptation register;
- ministry reports.

---
