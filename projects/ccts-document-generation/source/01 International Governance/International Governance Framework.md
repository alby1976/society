# International Governance Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework explains how the Christian Community Transformation Society is governed as an international Christian society.

It translates the [[International Constitution]] into a working governance architecture for the International Society, national chapters, affiliated organizations, boards, councils, advisors, ministries, officers, and reporting systems.

This document should be read together with [[Definitions of Key Terms]], [[Statement of Faith]], [[Mission Statement]], [[Vision Statement]], [[Core Values]], [[Governance Architecture]], and [[Governance Principles]].

## 2. Governing Assumption

The Society is international by design.

The Society is not merely a Canadian charity that may someday have international projects. Canada may serve as the founding national chapter, but the Society's doctrine, mission, constitution, and governance system are international from the beginning.

This protects the Society from rebuilding its governance every time it enters a new country.

## 3. Highest Authorities

The Society's authority structure begins with Christ and Scripture.

```text
Jesus Christ
        |
        v
Holy Scripture
        |
        v
Statement of Faith
        |
        v
Mission, Vision, and Core Values
        |
        v
International Constitution
        |
        v
International Governance Framework
        |
        v
Boards, Chapters, Ministries, Policies, and Procedures
```

No lower authority may contradict a higher authority.

## 4. Governance Objectives

The governance system exists to:

1. preserve theological fidelity;
2. protect the Society's mission;
3. support faithful local churches;
4. prevent mission drift;
5. maintain accountability;
6. steward resources wisely;
7. support international expansion;
8. protect national chapters from isolation;
9. preserve institutional memory;
10. enable practical ministry without creating confusion about authority.

## 5. International Society

The International Society is responsible for the shared identity of the whole organization.

It guards:

- the [[Statement of Faith]];
- the [[Mission Statement]];
- the [[Vision Statement]];
- the [[Core Values]];
- the [[International Constitution]];
- international chapter recognition;
- reserved powers;
- international governance standards;
- mission alignment review;
- international institutional memory.

The International Society may act through the International Board, officers, councils, committees, advisors, and policies.

## 6. National Chapters

A national chapter is a country-level expression of the Society.

Each national chapter must:

- comply with local law;
- remain loyal to the Society's doctrine and mission;
- operate under delegated authority;
- respect international reserved powers;
- maintain local governance structures;
- report to the International Society;
- maintain appropriate relationships with faithful local churches;
- participate in mission alignment review.

National chapters may adapt procedures to local law and context, but may not redefine doctrine, mission, or constitutional identity.

## 7. Relationship Between International and National Authority

The International Society holds constitutional and mission-protecting authority.

National chapters hold delegated operational and local legal authority.

The distinction is:

```text
International Society:
Doctrine, mission, constitution, chapter recognition, reserved powers, international standards

National Chapter:
Local incorporation, local operations, local compliance, local ministry implementation
```

This framework allows local wisdom without global fragmentation.

## 8. Reserved Powers

Reserved powers are authorities retained by the International Society.

They may not be exercised independently by national chapters or affiliated organizations.

Reserved powers include matters such as:

- amendment of the International Constitution;
- alteration of the Statement of Faith;
- alteration of mission or vision;
- recognition or suspension of national chapters;
- approval of major international affiliated organizations;
- final determination of mission alignment disputes;
- protection of name, identity, and doctrine.

A separate [[Reserved Powers Framework]] shall define these powers in more detail.

## 9. Delegated Authority

Delegated authority is authority granted by the International Board or other approved authority to national chapters, officers, committees, councils, ministries, or affiliated organizations.

Delegation must be:

- written;
- limited;
- accountable;
- reviewable;
- revocable when necessary;
- consistent with the International Constitution.

A separate [[Delegated Authority Framework]] shall define these delegations in more detail.

## 10. International Board

The International Board governs the Society internationally.

Its primary responsibilities are:

- doctrine protection;
- mission protection;
- constitutional stewardship;
- international strategy;
- recognition of national chapters;
- oversight of reserved powers;
- approval of major policies;
- accountability of international officers;
- oversight of institutional memory;
- reception of major reports.

The International Board should not manage every operational detail. Its role is governance, not micromanagement.

## 11. National Boards

National boards govern national chapters within local law and delegated authority.

National boards are responsible for:

- local legal compliance;
- chapter operations;
- local budgets;
- local ministry oversight;
- local staffing;
- local risk management;
- national reporting;
- implementation of international standards.

A national board may not contradict international doctrine or exceed delegated authority.

## 12. Officers

Officers implement approved governance direction.

The Society may appoint:

- International Executive Director;
- National Executive Directors;
- financial officers;
- secretaries;
- ministry directors;
- chaplains;
- other officers approved by policy.

Officers serve within delegated authority and are accountable to the board or authority that appoints them.

## 13. Advisory and Oversight Bodies

The Society may establish advisory and oversight bodies, including:

- [[Church Advisory Fellowship]];
- [[Theological Oversight Council]];
- [[Stewardship Advisors]];
- board committees;
- ministry councils;
- national advisory teams.

These bodies must have clear charters. Advisory bodies advise. Governing bodies govern. Confusing the two is how organizations grow extra heads.

## 14. Relationship with Local Churches

The Society exists to support faithful local churches.

The Society must not replace:

- Lord's Day worship;
- preaching of the Word;
- sacraments;
- church discipline;
- elder oversight;
- pastoral care of church members.

Society chaplaincy and ministry care should encourage appropriate connection to faithful local churches.

## 15. Affiliated Organizations

Affiliated organizations may include:

- housing trusts;
- worker cooperatives;
- church support foundations;
- training bodies;
- chaplaincy ministries;
- property-holding entities;
- service corporations.

Each affiliated organization must have:

- a clear purpose;
- a governance relationship to the Society;
- a mission alignment requirement;
- reporting obligations;
- financial controls;
- dispute and exit provisions.

## 16. Ministry Approval

New ministries should be approved through a structured process.

The approval process should consider:

1. theological consistency;
2. mission alignment;
3. church relationship impact;
4. legal feasibility;
5. financial sustainability;
6. governance capacity;
7. staffing and volunteer needs;
8. risk;
9. reporting ability;
10. long-term stewardship.

No ministry should be launched merely because it is exciting, fundable, or emotionally compelling.

## 17. Mission Alignment Review

Mission Alignment Review is the Society's formal process for evaluating whether a chapter, ministry, partnership, grant, cooperative, housing project, or affiliated organization remains aligned with the Society's theology and mission.

Reviews may be scheduled, triggered by concern, or required before renewal.

A Mission Alignment Review may recommend:

- continuation;
- correction;
- supervision;
- restructuring;
- suspension;
- termination;
- escalation to the International Board.

## 18. Reporting

The Society's reporting system supports communication, accountability, prayer, decision-making, and institutional memory.

The reporting structure may include:

- 5-15 reports;
- monthly ministry summaries;
- quarterly governance reports;
- annual reports;
- mission alignment reviews;
- financial reports;
- risk reports;
- board reports.

Reporting is not paperwork for its own sake. It is how the Society remembers, learns, corrects, and gives thanks.

## 19. Institutional Memory

The Society shall preserve institutional memory through:

- [[Document Registry]];
- [[Decision Register]];
- changelogs;
- release plans;
- 5-15 report archives;
- annual reports;
- board minutes;
- stewardship advisors;
- historical archives;
- review schedules.

Institutional memory protects future leaders from guessing what past leaders already learned the hard way.

## 20. Document Hierarchy

The Society's document hierarchy is:

```text
1. Scripture
2. Statement of Faith
3. Mission, Vision, and Core Values
4. International Constitution
5. International Governance Framework
6. International Board policies
7. National chapter bylaws and policies
8. Ministry frameworks
9. Procedures
10. Templates and forms
```

Where documents conflict, the higher authority governs unless civil law requires a specific local legal adjustment. Any legal adjustment must be documented and reviewed for mission impact.

## 21. Local Adaptation

Local adaptation is permitted when required by:

- civil law;
- language;
- culture;
- administrative practice;
- ministry context;
- local church relationships.

Local adaptation is not permitted when it would alter:

- doctrine;
- mission;
- constitutional identity;
- reserved powers;
- church-supporting boundaries;
- governance accountability.

## 22. International Expansion

Before entering a new country, the Society should assess:

- theological partnerships;
- church relationships;
- legal feasibility;
- charitable registration options;
- governance capacity;
- financial sustainability;
- language and translation needs;
- cultural risks;
- reporting capacity;
- leadership development.

International expansion should be prayerful, deliberate, and governed. Expansion without governance is not faith; it is organizational caffeine.

## 23. Conflicts and Disputes

Disputes should be handled according to biblical wisdom, governing documents, and applicable law.

Potential disputes include:

- chapter disputes;
- doctrinal concerns;
- mission alignment disagreements;
- board conflicts;
- employment issues;
- property concerns;
- grant disputes;
- inter-chapter disagreements.

The International Board shall have final internal authority over international mission alignment and reserved powers, subject to applicable law.

## 24. Review and Amendment

This framework should be reviewed annually during the founding stage and at least every three years after the Society reaches stable international governance.

Amendments should be recorded in the [[Decision Register]] and reflected in the [[Document Registry]].

## 25. Implementation Sequence

The Society should implement this framework in this order:

1. adopt foundational theology;
2. adopt mission, vision, and values;
3. adopt the International Constitution;
4. adopt this framework;
5. define reserved powers;
6. define delegated authority;
7. charter the International Board;
8. charter advisory and oversight bodies;
9. recognize national chapters;
10. approve ministry frameworks;
11. implement reporting and review systems.
