# Hong Kong Chapter Formation Brief

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a starter framework for exploring and forming a Hong Kong Chapter of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Hong Kong Chapter would be a national or regional expression of the Society's international mission, doctrine, governance standards, reporting system, and church-supporting identity within Hong Kong's legal, cultural, linguistic, and church context.

This document is a governance planning tool. It is not legal advice.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Hong Kong expansion should be prayerful, lawful, church-supporting, culturally attentive, and mission-aligned.

A chapter should not be launched merely because there is opportunity, personal connection, or strategic excitement. Expansion must be carried by qualified leaders, local church relationships, legal clarity, financial controls, risk review, and reporting capacity.

---

# 3. Formation Status

Hong Kong should initially be treated as an exploratory region or formation-stage chapter until the International Board formally authorizes a formation team or provisional chapter.

Exploratory work may include:

- prayer and discernment;
- local church relationship mapping;
- legal and operational review;
- language and translation needs;
- local leadership identification;
- ministry need assessment;
- financial readiness;
- risk review;
- local adaptation register;
- Mission Alignment Review readiness.

---

# 4. Proposed Chapter Identity

Any public name must be approved before use.

Possible working names:

- Christian Community Transformation Society — Hong Kong Chapter;
- CCTS Hong Kong;
- Christian Community Transformation Society Hong Kong;
- another lawful name approved after legal and governance review.

The chapter should not publicly represent itself as recognized until recognition is granted.

---

# 5. Strategic Rationale

Hong Kong may be considered because of:

- significant church and diaspora connections;
- possible cross-cultural ministry opportunities;
- Cantonese and English language context;
- regional connection to Asia;
- potential need for church-supporting mercy, work, housing, and grant structures;
- possible connection to Canadian and Hong Kong families;
- opportunities for theological and practical ministry support.

This rationale must be tested through local counsel and Mission Alignment Review.

---

# 6. Mission and Doctrine

The Hong Kong Chapter shall affirm:

- the Society's Statement of Faith;
- the Society's mission;
- the Society's vision;
- the Society's core values;
- the Society/church distinction;
- international reserved powers;
- church-supporting identity;
- Mission Alignment Review.

The chapter must not alter doctrine, mission, or church boundaries for local convenience.

---

# 7. Language and Translation

Hong Kong formation may require English, Traditional Chinese, and Cantonese-sensitive communication.

Translation work should be governed by [[Chapter Reserved Powers and Local Adaptation Policy]] and tracked in [[Hong Kong Chapter Local Adaptation Register]].

Translation should preserve meaning, not merely vocabulary.

---

# 8. Church Relationships

The Hong Kong formation team should identify faithful local churches and church leaders who can advise on:

- local church context;
- theological education;
- church referral;
- chaplaincy boundaries;
- mercy ministry needs;
- cultural concerns;
- language needs;
- risks of role confusion.

The chapter must not replace local church pastoral care or worship.

---

# 9. Initial Ministry Priorities

Hong Kong should not launch all ministries at once.

Possible staged priorities:

1. prayer and discernment;
2. church relationship review;
3. legal and operational readiness;
4. leadership development;
5. reporting and institutional memory setup;
6. chaplaincy feasibility;
7. worker cooperative feasibility;
8. Foundation grant feasibility;
9. housing feasibility only after strong legal review;
10. annual review and correction.

Housing and grant-making should be approached slowly because they carry significant legal, financial, and governance risk.

---

# 10. Legal and Operational Readiness

Before formation, Hong Kong should review:

- legal form options;
- nonprofit or charitable structure options;
- banking;
- accounting;
- employment obligations;
- privacy and data protection;
- fundraising rules;
- grant-making rules;
- cross-border transfer issues;
- housing law if relevant;
- cooperative or enterprise law if relevant;
- insurance;
- public communications;
- board liability.

Qualified local legal and accounting review is required before legal formation or operations.

---

# 11. Leadership Readiness

The formation team should identify leaders with:

- commitment to the Society's mission;
- ability to uphold doctrine where required;
- church-supporting posture;
- local cultural knowledge;
- bilingual or multilingual capacity where possible;
- reporting discipline;
- legal and governance seriousness;
- financial integrity;
- humility and accountability.

Formation leadership must not claim authority before authority is delegated.

---

# 12. Financial Readiness

Hong Kong formation should include:

- startup budget;
- accounting process;
- donation handling review;
- expense approval process;
- bank account pathway;
- restricted fund controls if applicable;
- reporting calendar;
- audit or review expectations;
- cross-border funding review.

Money should not be received or promised without clear authority and controls.

---

# 13. Risk Considerations

Hong Kong formation should review risks including:

- legal uncertainty;
- public communications risk;
- cross-border governance complexity;
- donor restriction concerns;
- local church relationship concerns;
- translation error;
- leadership capacity;
- financial controls;
- privacy;
- housing and employment law complexity;
- mission drift;
- confusion between Society and church authority.

---

# 14. Recognition Path

The Hong Kong Chapter should proceed through:

1. international discernment;
2. exploratory region designation;
3. local formation team authorization;
4. legal and church relationship review;
5. local adaptation register;
6. provisional recognition request;
7. limited launch if approved;
8. reporting and evaluation;
9. full recognition if ready.

---

# 15. Immediate Deliverables

Before provisional recognition, the formation team should prepare:

- legal readiness memo;
- church relationship summary;
- proposed leadership list;
- conflict of interest disclosures;
- initial budget;
- local adaptation register;
- risk register;
- ministry priority recommendation;
- reporting plan;
- translation plan where needed;
- prayer and discernment record.

---

# 16. Review

This formation brief should be reviewed annually during exploration and whenever Hong Kong's formation or recognition status changes.
