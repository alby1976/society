# Church Partnership Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines how the Christian Community Transformation Society relates to local churches and church bodies.

The Society exists to support faithful local churches and to encourage Christian life throughout the week. Church partnerships help the Society remain accountable, connected, and useful without becoming a church itself.

This framework should be read with [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter]], [[Church Recognition Standards]], [[Statement of Faith]], [[Mission Statement]], and [[International Governance Framework]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society cooperates with churches without replacing them.

The Society may serve alongside churches, receive counsel from churches, support church members, provide ministries that benefit churches, and refer unchurched beneficiaries to churches. But the Society must not assume ecclesiastical authority.

The relationship is cooperation, not confusion.

---

# 3. Purpose of Church Partnerships

Church partnerships may serve the following purposes:

1. prayerful cooperation;
2. referral of beneficiaries to faithful churches;
3. churchly counsel;
4. volunteer coordination;
5. chaplaincy support;
6. housing ministry support;
7. worker cooperative discipleship connection;
8. grant accountability;
9. ministry collaboration;
10. theological and pastoral wisdom;
11. international expansion support;
12. local community service.

---

# 4. Types of Church Relationships

The Society may recognize several types of church relationships.

## 4.1 Advisory Church

A church that participates in counsel through the [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]] or national advisory team.

## 4.2 Partner Church

A church that cooperates with the Society in specific ministry activities.

## 4.3 Referral Church

A church willing to receive referrals of residents, workers, students, beneficiaries, or unchurched persons.

## 4.4 Supporting Church

A church that supports the Society through prayer, volunteers, funding, space, counsel, or encouragement.

## 4.5 Host Church

A church that hosts Society events, prayer gatherings, training, or local ministry activity.

## 4.6 Grant-Recipient Church

A church receiving support from the Society or a Church Support Foundation.

Each relationship type should be documented according to risk and responsibility.

---

# 5. Partnership Standards

Church partners should ordinarily:

- affirm the authority of Scripture;
- be confessionally compatible with the Society;
- support or respect the Society's Statement of Faith;
- maintain lawful church governance;
- support the Society's church-supporting mission;
- avoid undermining the Society's doctrine;
- protect vulnerable people;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- respect confidentiality;
- cooperate honestly.

Specific standards are defined in [[Church Recognition Standards]].

---

# 6. Partnership Agreements

A partnership agreement may be required when the relationship involves:

- shared ministry activity;
- grants;
- housing referrals;
- volunteer coordination;
- use of church facilities;
- chaplaincy cooperation;
- public representation;
- data sharing;
- financial obligations;
- safeguarding responsibilities;
- cross-border partnership.

A partnership agreement should define:

- purpose;
- term;
- responsibilities;
- authority limits;
- confidentiality;
- safeguarding;
- reporting;
- conflict resolution;
- financial arrangements;
- termination.

---

# 7. Relationship to Church Authority

The Society shall respect the authority of local churches over their own members, worship, sacraments, discipline, officers, doctrine, and pastoral care.

The Society may recommend, refer, advise, or cooperate. It may not command a church to act.

Similarly, a partner church may advise and cooperate with the Society but does not govern the Society unless represented through a formal governance role defined by the Society's documents.

---

# 8. Referrals to Churches

The Society may refer beneficiaries, residents, workers, students, volunteers, or unchurched persons to partner or referral churches.

Referral should consider:

- doctrine;
- language;
- location;
- pastoral capacity;
- cultural context;
- safety;
- family needs;
- accessibility;
- transportation;
- local church willingness.

The Society should avoid treating referrals as mechanical. People are not parcels.

---

# 9. Chaplaincy and Church Partnerships

Chaplaincy should be connected to local churches.

A Society chaplain may provide spiritual care temporarily or in defined contexts, especially for unchurched persons, but should seek appropriate connection to local churches where possible.

The chaplain must not contradict local church leadership or present Society spiritual care as a permanent substitute for church life.

---

# 10. Housing and Church Partnerships

Housing ministry may involve church partnerships for:

- resident referrals;
- pastoral support;
- community life;
- volunteer support;
- crisis response;
- prayer;
- practical care;
- discipleship connection.

A housing resident should not be coerced into church participation in a way that violates law or conscience, but the Society may maintain a clearly Christian mission and offer church connection as part of its community vision.

---

# 11. Worker Cooperative and Church Partnerships

Worker cooperative ministry may involve churches through:

- worker referrals;
- mentoring;
- discipleship support;
- pastoral connection;
- prayer;
- conflict support;
- training;
- community support.

The cooperative should not become a church substitute for workers.

---

# 12. Church Support Foundation and Church Partnerships

A Church Support Foundation may support churches through grants, scholarships, capacity-building, leadership development, or ministry support.

Grant relationships require clear standards, accountability, reporting, and conflict of interest disclosure.

---

# 13. International Partnerships

International church partnerships should be reviewed carefully.

The Society should consider:

- doctrine;
- local church context;
- local law;
- denomination or network;
- language;
- safeguarding;
- financial accountability;
- political or social pressure;
- cultural assumptions;
- mission alignment.

International partnerships should not be rushed. Cross-border confusion travels fast and brings luggage.

---

# 14. Conflicts of Interest

Church partnerships may create conflicts of interest.

Examples:

- a board member's church applies for a grant;
- a partner church refers a family member for housing;
- a church leader serves on a committee reviewing their own church;
- a chaplain is asked to intervene in a church dispute;
- a donor directs support to their own congregation.

Conflicts must be disclosed and managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 15. Partnership Review

Church partnerships should be reviewed periodically.

Review may consider:

- mission alignment;
- doctrinal compatibility;
- communication quality;
- reporting;
- safeguarding;
- financial accountability;
- conflicts of interest;
- complaints;
- effectiveness;
- continued suitability.

---

# 16. Suspension or Termination

A church partnership may be suspended or terminated if:

- doctrine is no longer compatible;
- the church opposes the Society's mission;
- serious misconduct arises;
- safeguarding concerns are unresolved;
- financial accountability fails;
- conflict of interest is unmanaged;
- the relationship creates confusion between Society and church authority;
- the partnership no longer serves its purpose.

Termination should be documented.

---

# 17. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually during the founding stage and whenever the Society expands into a new country, establishes chaplaincy, launches housing, or creates a Church Support Foundation.
