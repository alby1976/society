# Housing Complaints and Dispute Resolution Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes a fair, clear, and documented process for handling complaints and disputes in Society-related housing.

Complaints may involve residents, staff, volunteers, property managers, contractors, neighbors, church partners, housing committees, or affiliated organizations.

A complaint process protects residents, staff, property, mission, and trust.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Complaints should be handled with truth, fairness, confidentiality, and appropriate urgency.

The Society should neither ignore complaints nor turn every disagreement into a tribunal. Wisdom is needed.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to complaints involving:

- maintenance;
- rent or fees;
- lease terms;
- staff conduct;
- resident conduct;
- neighbor disputes;
- privacy;
- discrimination;
- safety;
- harassment;
- spiritual care;
- chaplaincy;
- community expectations;
- property management;
- contractor conduct;
- termination or non-renewal concerns.

---

# 4. Rights and Legal Processes

This policy does not remove legal rights.

Where local law provides a statutory complaint, tenancy, housing tribunal, human rights, or court process, those rights remain.

Society internal processes must not be used to prevent lawful reporting or access to legal remedies.

---

# 5. Informal Resolution

Where safe and appropriate, minor concerns may be resolved informally.

Informal resolution may include:

- respectful conversation;
- clarification;
- apology;
- maintenance follow-up;
- written reminder;
- housing manager conversation;
- mediation where appropriate.

Informal resolution should not be required where safety, abuse, harassment, discrimination, power imbalance, or serious misconduct is alleged.

---

# 6. Formal Complaint

A formal complaint should include:

- complainant name, unless anonymous reporting is permitted;
- date;
- issue;
- persons involved;
- location;
- desired resolution;
- urgency;
- supporting documents if any;
- confidentiality concerns.

The Society should provide accessible ways to submit complaints.

---

# 7. Anonymous Complaints

Anonymous complaints may be accepted, but the Society's ability to respond may be limited.

Anonymous complaints involving safety, abuse, exploitation, harassment, privacy breach, fraud, or serious misconduct should still be reviewed.

---

# 8. Complaint Intake

Complaint intake should record:

- date received;
- person receiving complaint;
- category;
- urgency;
- confidentiality level;
- immediate safety concerns;
- assigned reviewer;
- expected next step.

Urgent safety matters must be escalated immediately.

---

# 9. Triage

Complaints should be triaged by seriousness.

| Level | Description | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Low | minor concern or misunderstanding | ordinary follow-up |
| Medium | repeated issue or unresolved problem | assigned review |
| High | safety, harassment, privacy, legal, or serious conflict | urgent review |
| Critical | immediate danger, abuse, severe misconduct | emergency or safeguarding response |

---

# 10. Reviewer Assignment

A complaint should be reviewed by a person with proper authority and no unmanaged conflict of interest.

Possible reviewers:

- housing manager;
- housing committee member;
- National Executive Director;
- property manager;
- safeguarding lead;
- chaplain supervisor;
- board delegate;
- external reviewer.

Conflicts must be disclosed.

---

# 11. Investigation

Investigation may include:

- reviewing documents;
- speaking with parties;
- inspecting property;
- consulting lease terms;
- reviewing policy;
- obtaining legal advice;
- reviewing maintenance records;
- checking prior complaints;
- consulting safeguarding or privacy leads.

Investigation should be proportionate.

---

# 12. Confidentiality

Complaint information should be shared only with those who need to know.

The Society should protect:

- complainant privacy;
- resident privacy;
- staff privacy;
- sensitive allegations;
- pastoral or chaplaincy information;
- legal communications.

Confidentiality cannot be promised absolutely.

---

# 13. Protection from Retaliation

The Society shall not tolerate retaliation against a person who raises a concern in good faith.

Retaliation may include:

- threats;
- eviction pressure;
- harassment;
- loss of support;
- spiritual pressure;
- social exclusion;
- staff hostility;
- discriminatory treatment.

Retaliation concerns should be escalated.

---

# 14. Resolution Options

Resolution may include:

- explanation;
- apology;
- maintenance action;
- policy clarification;
- mediation;
- behavior agreement;
- lease enforcement;
- staff discipline;
- contractor correction;
- transfer request review;
- chaplaincy support;
- referral;
- board review;
- legal process;
- mission alignment review.

---

# 15. Communication of Outcome

The Society should communicate the outcome to the complainant where appropriate.

The response should include:

- issue reviewed;
- action taken where shareable;
- next steps;
- appeal or reconsideration process if any;
- confidentiality limits.

The Society should not disclose private disciplinary details unnecessarily.

---

# 16. Appeals or Reconsideration

Where appropriate, a resident or complainant may request reconsideration.

Reconsideration may be available when:

- policy was not followed;
- new evidence exists;
- conflict of interest affected decision;
- decision was unreasonable;
- legal rights require review.

Appeal processes must comply with law.

---

# 17. Documentation

Complaint records should include:

- complaint;
- intake record;
- triage level;
- reviewer;
- evidence reviewed;
- communications;
- decision;
- actions taken;
- follow-up;
- closure date;
- confidentiality level.

Records must be retained according to policy and law.

---

# 18. Pattern Review

The Society should periodically review complaint patterns.

Patterns may reveal:

- maintenance failure;
- staff training need;
- unclear lease terms;
- community conflict;
- discrimination risk;
- privacy weakness;
- spiritual care boundary issues;
- mission drift;
- governance failure.

Patterns are not just data. They are smoke. Look for the fire.

---

# 19. Reporting

Complaint themes should be included in housing reports without unnecessary personal details.

Serious complaints should be escalated to appropriate leadership.

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
