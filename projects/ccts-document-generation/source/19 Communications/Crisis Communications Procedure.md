# Crisis Communications Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes how the Society handles communications during serious incidents, public controversy, legal matters, safeguarding concerns, privacy breaches, financial concerns, chapter crises, or public reputation risks.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

In a crisis, the Society should communicate with truth, restraint, compassion, lawful care, and proper authority.

The Society should avoid silence that hides truth and speech that creates harm.

---

# 3. When This Procedure Applies

This procedure may apply when there is:

- serious incident;
- safeguarding concern;
- privacy breach;
- legal notice;
- public complaint;
- media inquiry;
- social media controversy;
- financial misconduct allegation;
- grant misuse concern;
- housing incident;
- worker safety issue;
- chaplaincy boundary concern;
- national chapter crisis;
- leadership misconduct concern;
- public misrepresentation of the Society.

---

# 4. Immediate Steps

When a crisis arises:

1. ensure immediate safety;
2. escalate under [[Incident Escalation Policy]];
3. identify authorized decision-maker;
4. preserve records;
5. limit public comment;
6. identify legal, privacy, safeguarding, or board review needed;
7. prepare internal holding statement if needed;
8. prepare external statement only if authorized.

---

# 5. Crisis Communications Lead

The Society should appoint a communications lead for the crisis.

The lead should coordinate:

- message drafting;
- approvals;
- media responses;
- internal updates;
- public statements;
- church communications where appropriate;
- donor communications where appropriate;
- recordkeeping.

The communications lead does not replace legal, safeguarding, privacy, or board authority.

---

# 6. Holding Statement

A holding statement may be used when facts are incomplete.

A holding statement should:

- acknowledge awareness;
- avoid speculation;
- avoid confidential details;
- state that the matter is being reviewed;
- express appropriate care;
- identify whether more information will be provided when appropriate.

---

# 7. Approval

Crisis statements should be approved by the proper authority.

Approval may include:

- Board Chair;
- Executive Director;
- legal counsel;
- safeguarding lead;
- privacy lead;
- National Chapter Board;
- International Board;
- communications lead.

Approval depends on the nature of the crisis.

---

# 8. What Not to Say

Do not:

- speculate;
- blame without facts;
- disclose confidential information;
- identify vulnerable persons unnecessarily;
- promise outcomes before review;
- deny before facts are known;
- admit legal liability without advice;
- attack complainants;
- use spiritual language to minimize harm;
- make unauthorized statements.

---

# 9. Internal Communication

Internal communication should be need-to-know.

Internal updates may include:

- what happened in general terms;
- what action is being taken;
- who is authorized to respond;
- what not to discuss publicly;
- where questions should go;
- confidentiality reminder.

---

# 10. External Communication

External communication may be needed for:

- affected persons;
- churches;
- donors;
- regulators;
- insurers;
- media;
- partners;
- public website or social media.

External communication should be accurate, authorized, and documented.

---

# 11. Social Media

During a crisis:

- pause scheduled posts if needed;
- monitor official channels;
- avoid debate;
- remove unauthorized posts if required;
- preserve relevant records;
- use approved statements only;
- do not disclose confidential details.

---

# 12. Recordkeeping

Crisis communication records should include:

- timeline;
- drafts;
- approvals;
- statements released;
- media inquiries;
- internal updates;
- external notices;
- decisions;
- corrections;
- follow-up.

Records should be stored according to [[Records Management Policy]].

---

# 13. After-Crisis Review

After the crisis, review:

- what happened;
- whether response was timely;
- whether communications were accurate;
- whether confidentiality was protected;
- whether legal obligations were met;
- whether policy changes are needed;
- whether training is needed;
- whether public correction is needed;
- what future leaders should learn.

---

# 14. Review

This procedure should be reviewed annually and after any significant crisis communication event.
