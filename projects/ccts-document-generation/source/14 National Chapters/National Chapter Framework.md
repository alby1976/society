# National Chapter Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes how national chapters of the Christian Community Transformation Society are formed, recognized, governed, supported, reviewed, and corrected.

The Society is international in identity and governance. National chapters are not independent societies using a shared brand. They are local national expressions of one international mission, doctrine, and governance architecture.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A national chapter may adapt to local law and context, but it must not create a separate mission.

Local adaptation is permitted. Local mission drift is not.

The Society should not become a loose franchise of unrelated national charities. It should remain one international society with lawful local embodiment.

---

# 3. Definition of National Chapter

A National Chapter is a legally recognized or provisionally authorized expression of the Society in a particular country or jurisdiction.

A National Chapter may carry out Society ministries such as:

- chaplaincy;
- housing;
- worker cooperative development;
- church support foundation work;
- reporting;
- church relationships;
- prayer gatherings;
- leadership development;
- other mission-aligned ministries approved by the International Board.

---

# 4. International Identity

Every National Chapter shall remain accountable to the Society's:

- Statement of Faith;
- mission;
- vision;
- core values;
- International Constitution;
- International Governance Framework;
- reserved powers;
- reporting framework;
- Mission Alignment Review system;
- institutional memory standards.

A chapter does not own the mission. It receives and implements it.

---

# 5. Possible Chapter Statuses

The Society may recognize chapter development in stages.

## 5.1 Exploratory Region

A country or region being prayerfully assessed.

## 5.2 Formation Team

A local group authorized to explore chapter formation.

## 5.3 Provisional Chapter

A chapter authorized to develop legal, governance, church, and ministry readiness.

## 5.4 Recognized National Chapter

A chapter formally recognized under Society standards.

## 5.5 Reviewed Chapter

A chapter undergoing correction, review, restructuring, or temporary restriction.

## 5.6 Suspended Chapter

A chapter whose authority is paused due to serious concern.

---

# 6. Requirements for Recognition

A National Chapter should ordinarily demonstrate:

- doctrinal alignment;
- mission alignment;
- local legal readiness;
- qualified leadership;
- church advisory connection;
- reporting capacity;
- financial controls;
- conflict of interest policy;
- safeguarding and privacy readiness;
- ability to maintain institutional memory;
- willingness to submit to reserved powers;
- sustainable ministry plan;
- prayerful launch process.

Recognition should not be rushed because there is enthusiasm.

---

# 7. Legal Form

Each chapter must use an appropriate legal form in its jurisdiction.

Possible forms include:

- nonprofit corporation;
- charity;
- society;
- foundation;
- association;
- company limited by guarantee;
- trust;
- cooperative-related entity;
- other lawful structure.

Legal form must not override international doctrine, mission, and reserved powers.

---

# 8. Local Law and International Standards

National Chapters must comply with local law.

Where local law requires a variation from standard international practice, the chapter must document:

- legal reason;
- proposed local adaptation;
- effect on governance;
- risk;
- International Board approval if required;
- review date.

The Society should obey lawful local requirements while preserving its identity.

---

# 9. Chapter Governance

A National Chapter should have a governing board or equivalent authority.

Chapter governance should define:

- board composition;
- qualifications;
- appointment process;
- relationship to International Board;
- reserved powers;
- delegated powers;
- officers;
- committees;
- financial controls;
- reporting duties;
- conflict of interest process;
- document registry;
- decision register;
- review cycle.

---

# 10. Ministry Authority

A National Chapter may only launch ministries for which it has readiness and authority.

Before launching a ministry, the chapter should review:

- legal requirements;
- leadership capacity;
- financial capacity;
- ministry policy readiness;
- reporting capacity;
- risks;
- church relationships;
- mission alignment;
- International Board approval where required.

---

# 11. Church Relationships

A National Chapter should seek appropriate relationships with faithful local churches.

This may include:

- Church Advisory Fellowship participation;
- national church advisory team;
- local church referrals;
- theological counsel;
- ministry partnership;
- prayer partnership.

The chapter must not replace the local church.

---

# 12. Reporting

A National Chapter shall report according to the Society's reporting framework.

Reports may include:

- National Executive Director 5-15 Report;
- National Board Summary;
- ministry reports;
- financial reports;
- risk reports;
- annual ministry summary;
- mission alignment review;
- document registry updates;
- decision register updates.

Reports are not paperwork for its own sake. They are how future leaders avoid guessing.

---

# 13. Financial Stewardship

National Chapters must maintain financial controls appropriate to local law and Society standards.

Financial stewardship should include:

- budget;
- accounting;
- restricted funds;
- bank controls;
- expense approvals;
- audit or review where required;
- donor records;
- grant records;
- reserves;
- reporting;
- tax compliance.

---

# 14. Use of Name and Identity

Use of the Society's name, logos, documents, doctrine, mission language, and public identity is subject to international approval and reserved powers.

A chapter may not alter the Society's identity without authorization.

---

# 15. Mission Alignment Review

Every National Chapter shall undergo Mission Alignment Review.

Review should consider:

- doctrine;
- mission;
- governance;
- church relationships;
- ministries;
- reporting;
- finances;
- risk;
- leadership;
- institutional memory;
- local adaptation.

---

# 16. Correction or Suspension

If a National Chapter drifts from doctrine, mission, governance, or legal compliance, the Society may require:

- corrective action;
- reporting increase;
- leadership review;
- ministry pause;
- financial restriction;
- theological review;
- church advisory review;
- suspension;
- restructuring;
- revocation of recognition.

Correction should be firm, documented, and aimed at restoration where possible.

---

# 17. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually during international formation and whenever a new chapter is launched, suspended, or restructured.
