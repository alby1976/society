# Financial Stewardship Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the financial stewardship principles of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Society's finances must be governed with honesty, accountability, mission alignment, legal compliance, donor integrity, and care for future leaders.

Financial stewardship is not merely bookkeeping. It is one of the ways the Society protects mission from confusion, pressure, private benefit, and drift.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society shall receive, hold, spend, restrict, report, and review funds as a steward of mission.

Money should serve the mission. The mission should not be redesigned around money.

---

# 3. Scope

This framework applies to:

- International Society finances;
- National Chapter finances;
- ministry budgets;
- restricted funds;
- donor-designated gifts;
- grants;
- housing funds;
- worker cooperative finances;
- Church Support Foundation funds;
- chaplaincy funds;
- administrative funds;
- reserve funds;
- emergency funds.

---

# 4. Financial Stewardship Commitments

The Society commits to:

- lawful financial conduct;
- transparent financial reporting to appropriate authorities;
- clear budget authority;
- proper expense approval;
- careful restricted fund tracking;
- conflict of interest controls;
- donor communication integrity;
- appropriate reserves;
- documented decisions;
- regular review;
- protection against private benefit;
- mission alignment in financial decisions.

---

# 5. Authority

Financial authority must be defined through:

- governing documents;
- board resolutions;
- approved budgets;
- delegated authority;
- role descriptions;
- expense policies;
- grant agreements;
- fund restrictions;
- local law.

No person should commit, spend, receive, restrict, or promise funds beyond their authority.

---

# 6. Budgeting

Budgets should be prepared before major activity begins.

Budgets should be approved by the proper authority and should identify:

- expected income;
- expected expenses;
- restricted funds;
- reserves;
- ministry allocations;
- staff or contractor costs;
- legal and compliance costs;
- reporting and audit costs;
- risk contingencies.

A ministry without a budget is often a surprise looking for a treasurer.

---

# 7. Restricted Funds

Restricted funds must be tracked carefully.

A restricted fund exists when a donor, board, legal requirement, grant agreement, or approved purpose limits how money may be used.

Restricted funds must not be treated as general cash simply because the bank account balance looks healthy.

---

# 8. Expense Approval

Expenses must be approved before payment unless an emergency exception applies.

Expense approval should consider:

- budget availability;
- purpose;
- authority;
- documentation;
- conflict of interest;
- restricted fund rules;
- legal requirements;
- reasonableness;
- mission alignment.

---

# 9. Reimbursement

Reimbursements should require:

- approved purpose;
- receipt or documentation;
- timely submission;
- proper account coding;
- reviewer approval;
- conflict review where needed.

The Society should not rely on informal repayment promises.

---

# 10. Financial Reporting

Financial reports should be timely, accurate, and reviewed.

Reports may include:

- budget-to-actual report;
- income and expense statement;
- balance report;
- restricted fund report;
- grant report;
- reserve report;
- cash flow report;
- annual financial summary.

Reports should be clear enough for non-accountants to understand and detailed enough for responsible oversight.

---

# 11. Reserves

The Society should maintain appropriate reserves where possible.

Reserve categories may include:

- operating reserve;
- emergency reserve;
- legal reserve;
- maintenance reserve;
- housing reserve;
- grant reserve;
- payroll reserve;
- restricted fund reserve.

Reserves are not a lack of faith. They are one way to avoid crisis stewardship.

---

# 12. Conflicts of Interest

Financial decisions carry conflict risk.

Conflicts may involve:

- payments to leaders;
- payments to family members;
- related-party contracts;
- grants to connected persons;
- donor-directed benefits;
- housing or cooperative relationships;
- expense approvals by interested persons.

Conflicts must be disclosed and managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 13. Donor Integrity

Donor communication must be truthful.

The Society should not:

- promise tax treatment without proper authority;
- imply unrestricted funds are restricted;
- imply recognition not yet granted;
- overpromise outcomes;
- hide administrative realities;
- accept restrictions that compromise mission;
- allow donors to improperly control grants or ministries.

---

# 14. Legal Compliance

Financial practices must comply with applicable law in each jurisdiction.

This may include:

- charity law;
- nonprofit law;
- tax law;
- employment law;
- grant-making rules;
- donation receipting rules;
- fundraising rules;
- anti-fraud rules;
- privacy law;
- financial reporting obligations.

---

# 15. Financial Risk

Financial risks should be entered into appropriate risk registers.

Financial risks may include:

- cash flow weakness;
- donor concentration;
- restricted fund errors;
- weak controls;
- missing receipts;
- unclear authority;
- grant misuse;
- fraud;
- payroll failure;
- tax noncompliance;
- debt exposure;
- unfunded commitments.

---

# 16. Annual Review

The Society should annually review:

- financial statements;
- budget performance;
- restricted funds;
- reserves;
- donor restrictions;
- grant activity;
- expense approvals;
- financial risks;
- audit or review findings;
- policy compliance.

---

# 17. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually with related finance policies and after any serious financial incident.
