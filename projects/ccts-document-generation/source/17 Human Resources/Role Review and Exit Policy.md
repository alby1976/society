# Role Review and Exit Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Society reviews roles and handles role transitions, resignations, removals, completions, and exits.

A person's departure from a role should not create confusion, lost records, unsafe access, unresolved authority, or institutional memory loss.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Roles should end with the same care used to create them.

A poor exit can undo years of good work if records, access, responsibilities, relationships, and decisions are not transferred well.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- board roles;
- staff roles;
- volunteer roles;
- advisory roles;
- ministry leadership roles;
- chaplaincy roles;
- housing roles;
- cooperative roles;
- Foundation roles;
- national chapter roles;
- committee roles;
- formation team roles.

---

# 4. Routine Role Review

Roles should be reviewed periodically.

Review should ask:

- Is the role still needed?
- Is the person still qualified?
- Is authority clear?
- Is workload sustainable?
- Are reports being submitted?
- Are conflicts disclosed?
- Are records maintained?
- Is training needed?
- Should the role continue, change, pause, or end?

---

# 5. Review Frequency

Recommended review frequency:

| Role Type | Review Frequency |
|---|---|
| Board members | Annual |
| Executive leaders | Annual or more often |
| Ministry leaders | Annual |
| Staff | According to local law and role |
| Volunteers | Annual or role-based |
| Chaplains | Annual |
| Formation team leaders | Quarterly during formation |
| High-risk roles | More frequent |

---

# 6. Exit Types

A role may end through:

- completion of term;
- resignation;
- retirement;
- removal;
- suspension;
- termination;
- role restructuring;
- ministry closure;
- loss of qualification;
- legal requirement;
- death;
- incapacity.

---

# 7. Resignation

A resignation should be documented.

The record should include:

- person;
- role;
- resignation date;
- effective date;
- reason if voluntarily provided;
- transition plan;
- records to transfer;
- access to remove;
- final report required;
- appreciation or acknowledgement where appropriate.

---

# 8. Removal or Suspension

Removal or suspension may occur due to:

- misconduct;
- mission drift;
- breach of confidentiality;
- unmanaged conflict;
- failure to report;
- legal concern;
- safeguarding concern;
- inability to fulfill role;
- persistent workload concern;
- refusal of accountability;
- role no longer needed.

Removal should follow applicable law, bylaws, contracts, and policies.

---

# 9. Exit Handoff

An exit handoff should address:

- documents;
- passwords or access;
- pending tasks;
- reports due;
- financial matters;
- keys or equipment;
- contacts;
- decisions in progress;
- risks;
- confidential records;
- ministry relationships;
- successor briefing.

---

# 10. Access Removal

Access should be removed promptly when a role ends.

Access may include:

- email;
- shared drives;
- financial systems;
- document vaults;
- calendars;
- donor records;
- grant records;
- resident records;
- worker records;
- website access;
- social media;
- keys;
- banking permissions;
- signing authority.

---

# 11. Final Report

A final report may be required for significant roles.

The report may include:

- current responsibilities;
- unfinished tasks;
- major risks;
- key contacts;
- records location;
- decisions pending;
- recommendations;
- lessons learned;
- prayer requests where appropriate.

---

# 12. Confidentiality Reminder

Departing persons should be reminded that confidentiality continues after service ends.

Confidential information must not be retained, copied, shared, or used after role completion unless authorized.

---

# 13. Knowledge Transfer

Knowledge transfer protects institutional memory.

A transition meeting may be held with:

- departing person;
- supervisor;
- successor;
- records officer;
- board representative where appropriate.

The goal is not to extract everything from the person. It is to preserve what future leaders need.

---

# 14. Exit Interview

An exit interview may be used to learn from the transition.

Questions may include:

- What worked well?
- What was unclear?
- What risks remain?
- What support was missing?
- What should be improved?
- What documents need updating?
- What should future leaders know?

---

# 15. Death or Incapacity

If a person dies or becomes incapacitated while holding a role, the Society should respond with compassion, confidentiality, legal compliance, and orderly transfer of authority.

Access, records, and responsibilities should be handled carefully.

---

# 16. Records

Exit records should be stored according to [[Records Management Policy]].

Records may include:

- resignation;
- removal decision;
- final report;
- handoff checklist;
- access removal confirmation;
- exit interview notes;
- appreciation letter;
- corrective action if applicable.
