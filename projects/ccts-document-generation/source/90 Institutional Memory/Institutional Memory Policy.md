# Institutional Memory Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society preserves institutional memory.

Institutional memory includes the decisions, documents, reports, stories, lessons, prayers, failures, corrections, milestones, and founding principles that help the Society remain faithful across generations and countries.

This policy should be read with [[Stewardship Advisors Charter]], [[Founder Advisory Role]], [[Document Registry]], [[Decision Register]], [[Changelog]], [[Release Plan]], and [[5-15 Reporting Framework]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should not require future leaders to guess what past leaders already learned.

Institutional memory is not clutter. It is the Society's long-term governance conscience.

Without memory, every generation reinvents the same wheel, hits the same pothole, and then calls it innovation.

---

# 3. What Institutional Memory Includes

Institutional memory includes:

- founding vision;
- theological rationale;
- mission decisions;
- governance decisions;
- board minutes;
- decision register entries;
- document versions;
- changelog;
- release notes;
- annual reports;
- 5-15 report summaries;
- national chapter launch records;
- mission alignment reviews;
- church partnership history;
- grant history;
- housing ministry history;
- worker cooperative development;
- chaplaincy history;
- crisis records;
- lessons learned;
- founder interviews;
- stewardship advisor reports;
- major prayers and providences;
- legal and structural milestones.

---

# 4. Core Institutional Memory Tools

The Society shall maintain the following tools.

## 4.1 Document Registry

The [[Document Registry]] identifies official documents, status, version, category, review date, authority level, and relationships.

## 4.2 Decision Register

The [[Decision Register]] records major decisions, reasons, alternatives considered, approving authority, date, and related documents.

## 4.3 Changelog

The [[Changelog]] records notable changes to the governance library and major document revisions.

## 4.4 Release Plan

The [[Release Plan]] records planned and completed governance releases.

## 4.5 Review Schedule

The Review Schedule identifies when documents, policies, chapters, and ministries require review.

## 4.6 Stewardship Advisors

[[Stewardship Advisors Charter|Stewardship Advisors]] preserve historical wisdom and provide advisory continuity.

## 4.7 5-15 Reports

5-15 reports preserve operational memory, ministry developments, risks, decisions needed, and prayer concerns.

---

# 5. Decision Memory

Major decisions should not be left only in meeting minutes.

The Decision Register should be used for decisions involving:

- doctrine;
- mission;
- constitution;
- national chapters;
- major ministries;
- reserved powers;
- delegated authority;
- board appointments;
- executive appointments;
- property;
- major finances;
- partnerships;
- disciplinary matters with governance significance;
- mission alignment outcomes;
- crisis response.

A good decision register entry helps future leaders understand not only what happened, but why.

---

# 6. Document Memory

Every official governance document should have:

- document ID;
- title;
- version;
- status;
- type;
- category;
- authority level;
- review cycle;
- related documents;
- dependencies;
- superseded documents;
- changelog entry where material.

Superseded documents should be archived rather than deleted.

Deletion is not the same as memory management. Sometimes it is just amnesia with confidence.

---

# 7. Reporting Memory

Reports should feed institutional memory.

The Society's reporting system should preserve:

- recurring issues;
- ministry growth;
- risks;
- prayer concerns;
- decisions needed;
- resource needs;
- leadership concerns;
- church relationship issues;
- major accomplishments;
- lessons learned.

Individual reports may have limited retention, but summaries, annual reports, governance-significant reports, and major incident reports should be preserved longer or permanently according to retention policy.

---

# 8. Founder Memory

Founder knowledge should be captured intentionally.

Methods may include:

- written founding reflections;
- interview transcripts;
- recorded oral history;
- founding roadmap notes;
- theological rationale documents;
- decision explanations;
- early risk lists;
- prayer journal summaries where appropriate;
- lessons learned.

Founder memory should be preserved without creating founder dependency.

---

# 9. National Chapter Memory

Each national chapter should preserve:

- launch documents;
- recognition decision;
- local bylaws;
- local legal milestones;
- church advisory relationships;
- ministry launches;
- annual reports;
- mission alignment reviews;
- major risks;
- local adaptations;
- translation decisions;
- leadership transitions.

National chapter memory should be reported to the International Society according to policy.

---

# 10. Ministry Memory

Major ministries should preserve:

- founding rationale;
- approval decision;
- governing framework;
- annual reports;
- policies;
- major partnerships;
- major incidents;
- changes in scope;
- mission alignment reviews;
- closure or restructuring decisions.

This applies to housing, worker cooperatives, church support foundations, chaplaincy, and other ministries.

---

# 11. Archives

The Society should maintain an archive for inactive, superseded, historical, or permanent records.

Archive categories may include:

- constitutional archive;
- board archive;
- national chapter archive;
- ministry archive;
- financial archive;
- legal archive;
- theological archive;
- reports archive;
- founder archive;
- historical media.

Archives should be searchable and protected.

---

# 12. Confidentiality

Institutional memory must respect confidentiality.

Sensitive records may involve:

- personal information;
- pastoral care;
- employment;
- legal advice;
- safeguarding;
- member discipline;
- housing residents;
- grant applicants;
- donors;
- financial risk;
- national chapter disputes.

Confidential records should be preserved securely with access controls.

---

# 13. Accessibility

Institutional memory should be accessible to authorized persons who need it.

Access should be based on role and need.

The Society should avoid two opposite failures:

- locking everything away so no one can learn;
- exposing everything so confidentiality is destroyed.

---

# 14. Annual Institutional Memory Review

The Board or delegated committee should conduct an annual institutional memory review.

The review should ask:

- Are major decisions recorded?
- Are documents current?
- Are superseded documents archived?
- Are reports being retained properly?
- Are founder memories captured?
- Are stewardship advisors being consulted?
- Are national chapter records complete?
- Are major lessons being summarized?
- Are there orphaned documents?
- Are there undocumented practices?

---

# 15. Technology and Format

The Society may preserve institutional memory through:

- Markdown source files;
- Obsidian vault;
- generated PDF manuals;
- DOCX manuals;
- HTML site;
- Git repository;
- secure archive storage;
- encrypted records where appropriate;
- document generator;
- registries and dashboards.

The Markdown source repository should be treated as a primary source of truth for governance documents.
