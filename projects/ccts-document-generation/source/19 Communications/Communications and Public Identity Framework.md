# Communications and Public Identity Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society communicates internally, publicly, with churches, with donors, with national chapters, and with people served by its ministries.

Communications must preserve the Society's doctrine, mission, church-supporting identity, legal accuracy, confidentiality, and public trust.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should speak truthfully, humbly, clearly, and within proper authority.

Public communication should not move faster than governance approval. A website, logo, social media post, email, newsletter, or public statement can create expectations, legal exposure, donor confusion, church concerns, and mission drift.

---

# 3. Scope

This framework applies to:

- public statements;
- websites;
- newsletters;
- social media;
- donor communications;
- church communications;
- grant announcements;
- housing communications;
- worker cooperative communications;
- chaplaincy communications;
- national chapter communications;
- recruitment materials;
- crisis communications;
- logo and brand use;
- public reports;
- internal announcements intended for broad distribution.

---

# 4. Communication Commitments

The Society commits to communication that is:

- truthful;
- mission-aligned;
- church-supporting;
- legally cautious;
- respectful;
- confidential where needed;
- accurate about authority and status;
- careful with donor language;
- clear about chapter recognition;
- consistent with approved doctrine;
- sensitive to vulnerable persons;
- accountable to governance review.

---

# 5. Communications Authority

The Society should define who may communicate on behalf of:

- International Society;
- International Board;
- National Chapters;
- formation teams;
- ministries;
- chaplaincy;
- housing;
- worker cooperative;
- Church Support Foundation;
- public relations;
- donors;
- churches.

No person should speak publicly for the Society beyond delegated authority.

---

# 6. Public Identity

Public identity should be consistent with the Society's mission, vision, core values, and doctrine.

Public identity includes:

- name;
- logo;
- slogan or tagline;
- website;
- social media presence;
- public documents;
- donor materials;
- ministry descriptions;
- chapter descriptions;
- public reports.

Public identity should not imply that the Society is a church or replaces the church.

---

# 7. Chapter Status Language

National Chapters and formation teams must communicate their status accurately.

Possible status language:

| Status | Public Description |
|---|---|
| Exploratory Region | Under prayerful and governance review; not yet a recognized chapter |
| Formation Team | Authorized to explore formation; not yet a full chapter |
| Provisional Chapter | Provisionally recognized under conditions |
| Recognized Chapter | Recognized National Chapter under international governance |
| Restricted Chapter | Operating under specific restrictions |
| Suspended Chapter | Not authorized for ordinary operations unless specified |

A formation team must not communicate as if it is fully recognized.

---

# 8. Church Relationship Language

Communications should make clear that the Society supports faithful local churches.

Communications should not:

- imply the Society replaces Lord's Day worship;
- claim church authority;
- undermine local church leadership;
- present Society gatherings as church substitutes;
- suggest chaplaincy replaces pastoral care;
- imply church endorsement without permission.

---

# 9. Donor and Fundraising Language

Donor communications must be truthful and approved.

They should not:

- promise tax treatment without proper basis;
- imply charitable status before it exists;
- create unauthorized restrictions;
- overpromise outcomes;
- hide administrative costs;
- use emotional pressure;
- present donor control as stewardship;
- imply grants are guaranteed.

---

# 10. Privacy and Vulnerable Persons

Communications must protect privacy and dignity.

Do not publish identifying stories, photos, testimonies, prayer requests, housing details, worker information, grant information, or sensitive personal details without proper consent and approval.

Even with consent, the Society should consider power imbalance and vulnerability.

---

# 11. Internal Communications

Internal communication should be clear, respectful, and appropriately confidential.

Internal updates should identify:

- purpose;
- audience;
- action required;
- decision authority;
- confidentiality level;
- follow-up owner;
- record location if official.

---

# 12. Public Reports

Public reports should be approved before release.

Reports should avoid unnecessary personal information and should accurately represent:

- mission;
- finances;
- ministry activity;
- church relationships;
- risk and accountability;
- chapter status;
- future plans.

---

# 13. Crisis Communications

Crisis communications should follow [[Crisis Communications Procedure]].

During serious incidents, communication should be authorized, accurate, compassionate, legally reviewed where needed, and consistent with confidentiality.

---

# 14. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually and after any major public communication issue, crisis, rebrand, website launch, chapter launch, or fundraising campaign.
