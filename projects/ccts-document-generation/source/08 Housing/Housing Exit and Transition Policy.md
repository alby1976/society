# Housing Exit and Transition Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for ending housing arrangements, supporting resident transitions, handling move-outs, managing non-renewals or terminations, and preserving dignity during housing exits.

Housing exits are high-risk moments. They affect people, property, finances, legal rights, community peace, and the Society's witness.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should handle housing exits with truth, lawfulness, documentation, fairness, and compassion.

A resident's departure should not become administrative revenge, emotional avoidance, or spiritual pressure. It should be handled like stewardship before God.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- voluntary move-outs;
- lease expiry;
- non-renewal;
- program completion;
- transfer;
- termination for cause;
- eviction process;
- emergency removal where lawful;
- death of resident;
- property closure;
- relocation due to renovation or disposal;
- transition from supported housing.

Local law and agreement terms govern specific procedures.

---

# 4. Exit Categories

## 4.1 Voluntary Exit

The resident chooses to leave.

## 4.2 Planned Transition

The resident completes a program, changes housing type, or moves to more suitable housing.

## 4.3 Non-Renewal

The Society or resident does not renew an agreement.

## 4.4 Termination for Cause

The arrangement ends due to breach, non-payment, safety risk, or other lawful reason.

## 4.5 Emergency Exit

Immediate safety concerns require urgent action under law.

## 4.6 Property-Driven Exit

The property is sold, renovated, closed, or repurposed.

---

# 5. Legal Compliance

All exits must comply with local law.

The Society must follow required notice, documentation, tribunal, court, tenancy, or statutory processes.

No internal policy may override legal rights.

Legal counsel should be consulted for high-risk exits.

---

# 6. Notice

Notices must be clear, lawful, and documented.

Notice should identify:

- reason;
- effective date;
- legal basis;
- resident rights;
- required actions;
- contact person;
- appeal or complaint options where applicable;
- support or transition resources where appropriate.

---

# 7. Voluntary Move-Out

For voluntary move-outs, the Society should document:

- resident notice;
- move-out date;
- forwarding contact;
- inspection plan;
- rent or fee balance;
- keys and access return;
- deposit process if applicable;
- support needs;
- church or community transition needs if requested.

---

# 8. Transition Support

Where appropriate, transition support may include:

- referral to other housing;
- church referral;
- chaplaincy support;
- practical move-out guidance;
- financial counseling referral;
- community support;
- coordination with family or support persons where authorized;
- accessibility support;
- crisis referral.

The Society is not always able to solve the next housing need, but it should communicate clearly and compassionately.

---

# 9. Program Completion

If housing is tied to a program, program completion criteria must be clear in advance.

Completion may involve:

- timeline;
- goals;
- support review;
- transition plan;
- notice;
- final meeting;
- documentation.

Program housing should not trap people, but neither should it drift indefinitely without review.

---

# 10. Non-Renewal

Non-renewal must be reviewed carefully.

The decision should consider:

- legal rights;
- agreement terms;
- resident history;
- affordability;
- community concerns;
- safety;
- support needs;
- alternatives;
- mission alignment;
- board or committee authority;
- documentation.

The reason should be documented even where not all details can be shared.

---

# 11. Termination for Cause

Termination for cause may be considered for:

- serious lease breach;
- non-payment;
- illegal activity;
- threats or violence;
- severe property damage;
- serious safety risk;
- repeated serious disruption;
- fraud;
- unauthorized occupancy;
- refusal to comply with lawful program terms.

Termination must follow local law and approved authority.

---

# 12. Eviction

Eviction is a serious step.

Before eviction where time permits, the Society should review:

- legal basis;
- documentation;
- alternatives attempted;
- resident vulnerability;
- safety concerns;
- arrears or breach history;
- support offered;
- board or delegated approval;
- legal counsel advice;
- communication plan.

Eviction must never be used as spiritual punishment.

---

# 13. Emergency Situations

Emergency exits may be required where immediate safety is at risk.

Emergency action must comply with law and should involve emergency services, legal counsel, safeguarding response, or senior leadership where appropriate.

Documentation is required.

---

# 14. Move-Out Inspection

Move-out inspection should document:

- property condition;
- keys returned;
- cleaning;
- damage;
- repairs needed;
- resident belongings;
- photos where lawful and appropriate;
- deposit implications;
- forwarding address or contact.

The inspection should be fair and documented using [[Move-Out Inspection Checklist]] where applicable.

---

# 15. Abandoned Property

Abandoned property must be handled according to local law.

The Society should not discard resident belongings casually.

Records should include notice, storage, inventory where required, disposal authority, and dates.

---

# 16. Death of Resident

If a resident dies, the Society should respond with dignity, confidentiality, legal compliance, and compassion.

The Society should coordinate with lawful authorities, emergency contacts, estate representatives, family where appropriate, and chaplaincy if requested.

Personal property, privacy, and communication must be handled carefully.

---

# 17. Resident Records

After exit, resident records should be retained according to policy and local law.

Access should be limited to authorized persons.

Sensitive information should remain confidential.

---

# 18. Reporting

Housing exits should be summarized in reports without unnecessary personal details.

Reports may include:

- number of exits;
- reasons by category;
- transition needs;
- legal risks;
- vacancy impact;
- property condition issues;
- lessons learned;
- mission alignment concerns.

---

# 19. Exit Review

High-risk exits should be reviewed afterward.

Review questions:

- Was the process lawful?
- Was notice proper?
- Were support needs considered?
- Were safety concerns handled?
- Was communication clear?
- Were records complete?
- Was the resident treated with dignity?
- Does policy need revision?

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
