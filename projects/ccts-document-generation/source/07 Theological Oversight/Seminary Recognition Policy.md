# Seminary Recognition Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society recognizes seminaries or theological institutions for Society purposes.

Recognition may be relevant to chaplain qualifications, theological teaching roles, leadership development, grant eligibility, ministry training, or advisory roles.

Seminary recognition by the Society does not equal church ordination approval. Churches and denominations retain their own standards for ordination, licensure, and ecclesiastical office.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should require serious theological training where the role requires serious theological responsibility.

Chaplaincy, teaching, doctrinal review, and spiritual care should not be entrusted merely to enthusiasm, charisma, or the ability to quote half a verse with confidence.

Recognition exists to protect doctrine, beneficiaries, churches, and the Society's mission.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to recognition of seminaries or theological institutions for:

- Society chaplain qualifications;
- theological oversight roles;
- teaching roles;
- leadership development;
- scholarship or grant eligibility;
- ministry training;
- advisory appointments;
- international chapter leadership standards.

Recognition may be general or role-specific.

---

# 4. Recognition Authority

Seminary recognition may be recommended by:

- Theological Oversight Council;
- Church Advisory Fellowship;
- national church advisory teams;
- International Executive Director;
- National Board;
- International Board committee.

Final recognition authority should be defined by the [[Authority Matrix]] or Board policy.

For international use, final approval should ordinarily rest with the International Board or a delegated theological authority.

---

# 5. Recognition Criteria

Recognition should consider:

1. fidelity to Scripture;
2. confessional alignment;
3. theological seriousness;
4. quality of faculty;
5. relationship to faithful churches;
6. doctrinal accountability;
7. curriculum strength;
8. pastoral formation;
9. institutional integrity;
10. reputation among faithful churches;
11. suitability for Society roles;
12. language and cultural context;
13. accreditation or legal recognition where relevant.

---

# 6. Confessional Compatibility

Because the Society is confessionally Reformed, recognized seminaries for chaplaincy and theological leadership should ordinarily be Reformed or substantially compatible with the Society's theological commitments.

A seminary may be recognized for limited purposes even if not suitable for all roles.

Examples:

- recognized for general biblical studies;
- recognized for chaplaincy preparation;
- recognized for Reformed theological training;
- recognized for language or regional training;
- recognized for continuing education only.

Recognition should specify scope.

---

# 7. Recognition Categories

The Society may use categories such as:

## 7.1 Fully Recognized

Suitable for primary theological training for chaplains, theological officers, or teachers.

## 7.2 Provisionally Recognized

Generally suitable but requiring further review, supplemental training, or time-limited recognition.

## 7.3 Limited Recognition

Suitable for specific courses, regions, languages, or roles but not full theological qualification.

## 7.4 Not Recognized

Not suitable for Society purposes due to doctrinal, academic, governance, or integrity concerns.

---

# 8. Review Process

The recognition process may include:

1. nomination or request;
2. collection of institutional information;
3. review of statement of faith;
4. review of confessional commitments;
5. review of curriculum;
6. review of faculty;
7. consultation with churches;
8. review of reputation and concerns;
9. recommendation;
10. approval;
11. registry entry;
12. review date.

---

# 9. Required Information

Information requested may include:

- official name;
- location;
- website;
- statement of faith;
- confessional standards;
- accreditation or legal status;
- denominational relationships;
- curriculum;
- degree programs;
- faculty list;
- governance structure;
- language of instruction;
- field education requirements;
- known concerns;
- references from churches or theologians.

---

# 10. Role-Specific Use

Recognition should be tied to Society roles.

## 10.1 Chaplaincy

For Society chaplains, seminary recognition should consider pastoral care, biblical counseling, Reformed theology, church accountability, and practical ministry formation.

## 10.2 Teaching

For teaching roles, recognition should consider doctrinal precision and instructional competence.

## 10.3 Theological Oversight

For theological oversight roles, recognition should consider advanced theological training and confessional competence.

## 10.4 Grants and Scholarships

For grant eligibility, recognition may determine whether a student or ministry worker's program qualifies for Society support.

---

# 11. Supplemental Requirements

Even if a seminary is recognized, a person may still need:

- local church recommendation;
- additional confessional training;
- chaplaincy training;
- safeguarding training;
- supervised ministry;
- language or cultural training;
- approval by the Society;
- approval by local church or denomination where applicable.

A diploma is not a personality transplant. Character and church accountability still matter.

---

# 12. Recognition Registry

The Society should maintain a Seminary Recognition Registry.

The registry may include:

- institution name;
- country;
- recognition category;
- scope;
- approval authority;
- date approved;
- review date;
- notes;
- limitations;
- related decisions.

The registry should be reviewed regularly.

---

# 13. Review and Revocation

Recognition may be reviewed or revoked if:

- doctrine changes;
- faculty direction changes;
- serious scandal emerges;
- institutional integrity is compromised;
- church partners raise concerns;
- curriculum changes materially;
- the institution no longer serves Society needs;
- recognition was based on inaccurate information.

Revocation should be documented.

---

# 14. International Considerations

International recognition should account for:

- local language;
- local church context;
- regional theological education realities;
- legal status;
- persecution or political pressure;
- availability of Reformed training;
- online education quality;
- cross-cultural pastoral care.

The Society should be careful not to assume that one country's seminary ecosystem is universal.

---

# 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually with [[Theological Oversight Council Charter]], [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter]], and chaplaincy standards.
