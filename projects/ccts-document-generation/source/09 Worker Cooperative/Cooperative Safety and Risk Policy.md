# Cooperative Safety and Risk Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for safety, incident reporting, risk management, and escalation in a Society-affiliated worker cooperative.

The cooperative must protect workers, customers, volunteers, contractors, property, data, finances, and mission.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Worker dignity requires worker safety.

The Society must not create opportunities for work while ignoring risks in the work. Safety is not bureaucracy added to mission; it is part of loving the people doing the mission.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- workers;
- members;
- trainees;
- supervisors;
- managers;
- volunteers where lawful;
- contractors;
- customers where relevant;
- job sites;
- vehicles;
- tools;
- equipment;
- digital work;
- customer data;
- Society property;
- cooperative events.

---

# 4. Safety Responsibilities

The cooperative shall define safety responsibilities.

Responsibilities may include:

- board oversight;
- manager implementation;
- worker compliance;
- training completion;
- hazard reporting;
- incident reporting;
- equipment maintenance;
- customer site review;
- emergency response;
- insurance notification;
- legal compliance.

---

# 5. Hazard Identification

Hazards may include:

- physical injury;
- tools and equipment;
- vehicles;
- customer sites;
- chemicals;
- electricity;
- repetitive strain;
- working alone;
- harassment;
- violence;
- data breach;
- financial fraud;
- unsafe customers;
- overwork;
- spiritual coercion;
- conflict of interest;
- legal noncompliance.

The cooperative should identify hazards before work begins.

---

# 6. Safety Training

Workers must receive safety training appropriate to their role.

Training may include:

- general safety orientation;
- job-specific hazards;
- equipment use;
- emergency procedures;
- incident reporting;
- customer site conduct;
- harassment prevention;
- privacy and data safety;
- working alone;
- lifting or ergonomics;
- vehicle safety;
- spiritual care boundaries.

Training should be documented.

---

# 7. Right to Pause Unsafe Work

Workers should be able to pause unsafe work without retaliation.

The cooperative should define how unsafe work concerns are reported, reviewed, corrected, and documented.

No contract is worth preventable harm.

---

# 8. Incident Reporting

Incidents should be reported promptly.

Incidents may include:

- injury;
- near miss;
- property damage;
- unsafe customer conduct;
- harassment;
- violence or threat;
- vehicle incident;
- equipment failure;
- data breach;
- financial irregularity;
- serious conflict;
- spiritual care boundary concern;
- legal complaint;
- customer complaint involving safety.

---

# 9. Incident Report Contents

An incident report should include:

- date and time;
- location;
- persons involved;
- description;
- immediate action taken;
- injury or damage;
- witnesses;
- photos where appropriate;
- customer involved;
- emergency services if any;
- supervisor notified;
- follow-up required;
- confidentiality level.

Sensitive information should be minimized.

---

# 10. Escalation Levels

| Level | Description | Escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Low | minor issue, no ongoing risk | manager |
| Medium | repeated or significant concern | manager and board delegate |
| High | legal, injury, privacy, serious conflict, unsafe customer | senior leadership |
| Critical | serious harm, immediate danger, major legal exposure | emergency response and board |

---

# 11. Risk Register

The cooperative should maintain a risk register.

Risk categories may include:

- worker safety;
- legal compliance;
- employment classification;
- finances;
- contracts;
- customer relationships;
- data privacy;
- insurance;
- quality control;
- worker conflict;
- spiritual coercion;
- mission drift;
- governance failure;
- related-party transactions.

---

# 12. Customer Site Safety

Before accepting offsite work, the cooperative should consider customer site safety.

Questions include:

- Is the site safe?
- Are workers supervised?
- Are hazards known?
- Is insurance adequate?
- Is the customer respectful?
- Are tools and equipment appropriate?
- Are emergency contacts known?
- Is working alone involved?

---

# 13. Digital and Data Safety

For technology or administrative work, data safety must be addressed.

The cooperative should protect:

- passwords;
- customer data;
- Society data;
- personal information;
- financial information;
- email accounts;
- shared drives;
- devices;
- backups;
- access permissions.

Data breaches must be reported.

---

# 14. Harassment and Violence

The cooperative shall not tolerate harassment, threats, abuse, violence, or exploitation.

Concerns may involve workers, supervisors, customers, contractors, or volunteers.

Concerns should be reported, investigated, and escalated according to policy and law.

---

# 15. Working Alone

Working alone should be reviewed for risk.

The cooperative may require:

- check-in and check-out;
- location awareness;
- emergency contact;
- customer site review;
- two-person work for higher risk tasks;
- manager approval;
- refusal of unsafe work.

---

# 16. Insurance and Legal Compliance

The cooperative should maintain required insurance and comply with applicable occupational health and safety laws.

Insurance coverage should match actual work.

The cooperative should not perform work outside its insurance or legal capacity.

---

# 17. Corrective Action

Corrective actions may include:

- training;
- equipment repair;
- procedure change;
- customer refusal;
- contract revision;
- safety pause;
- staffing change;
- legal review;
- insurance review;
- disciplinary action;
- mission alignment review.

---

# 18. Reporting

Safety and risk should be included in cooperative reports.

Reports may include:

- incidents;
- near misses;
- hazards;
- open risks;
- insurance concerns;
- worker safety themes;
- customer site concerns;
- corrective actions.

---

# 19. Mission Alignment

Safety failures may become mission alignment concerns.

A cooperative that harms workers, hides risks, or pressures vulnerable people into unsafe work is out of alignment with the Society's mission.

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during cooperative launch and at least every three years once stable.
