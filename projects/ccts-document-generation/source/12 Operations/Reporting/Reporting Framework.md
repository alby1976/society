# Reporting Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the reporting system of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Reporting exists to support mission faithfulness, communication, accountability, prayer, governance oversight, risk management, and institutional memory. It is not merely paperwork. It is how the Society remembers what happened, notices what needs attention, gives thanks, makes decisions, and preserves continuity across leaders, ministries, chapters, and generations.

This framework should be read with [[Mission Alignment Review]], [[Governance Review Process]], [[Institutional Memory Policy]], [[5-15 Reporting Framework]], and [[5-15 Report Retention Policy]].

## 2. Governing Principle

The Society should report enough to govern faithfully, but not so much that reporting becomes the ministry.

Good reporting is clear, concise, honest, timely, prayerful, useful for decision-making, proportionate to risk, and connected to institutional memory.

Bad reporting hides problems, wastes time, flatters leaders, or creates document fog. The Society shall avoid all four, especially document fog, which is governance humidity.

## 3. Reporting Objectives

The Society's reporting system exists to support prayer, preserve mission alignment, identify risks early, support board oversight, improve communication, reduce unnecessary meetings, preserve institutional memory, support annual reporting, assist leadership transitions, document ministry progress, identify decisions needed, improve accountability, strengthen national chapter oversight, and help future leaders understand past activity.

## 4. Reporting Levels

### 4.1 Weekly Reporting

Weekly reporting is normally handled through the [[5-15 Reporting Framework]]. It is used by workers, volunteers, ministry leaders, chaplains, managers, and staff to communicate accomplishments, priorities, challenges, decisions needed, and prayer requests.

### 4.2 Monthly Reporting

Monthly reporting summarizes ministry or departmental activity for supervisors, executives, national chapter leaders, or boards. Monthly reports should draw from weekly reports where available.

### 4.3 Quarterly Reporting

Quarterly reporting supports board and governance oversight. Quarterly reports should summarize mission alignment, finances, risks, ministry progress, chapter health, and decisions needed.

### 4.4 Annual Reporting

Annual reporting provides a comprehensive review of the Society's ministry, finances, governance, mission alignment, risks, accomplishments, and future priorities. Annual reports may be used for board review, supporters, churches, donors, members, and public communication where appropriate.

## 5. Reporting Flow

```text
Weekly 5-15 Reports
        |
        v
Monthly Ministry or Department Summaries
        |
        v
Quarterly Governance Reports
        |
        v
Annual Ministry and Governance Reports
        |
        v
Permanent Institutional Memory
```

This flow allows ordinary updates to become long-term organizational memory without requiring leaders to reconstruct the year from panic, memory, and old calendar invites.

## 6. Who Reports

Reporting obligations may apply to volunteers, staff, ministry leaders, department directors, chaplains, housing managers, worker cooperative managers, foundation directors, national executive directors, international executive director, board committees, national boards, international board, affiliated organizations, and special projects.

Reporting requirements should be proportionate to authority, risk, and responsibility.

## 7. Report Content Standards

Reports should normally address accomplishments, current priorities, challenges, risks, decisions or support needed, upcoming work, prayer requests, people served, financial or resource concerns where applicable, mission alignment concerns, and confidentiality level.

Not every report needs every section. The required sections should match the role and report type.

## 8. Confidentiality Levels

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Public | May be shared publicly |
| Internal | For Society personnel and authorized leaders |
| Restricted | Limited to specified leaders or boards |
| Confidential | Sensitive personal, pastoral, legal, employment, or financial information |
| Board Confidential | Limited to board or designated governance authority |

Reports involving pastoral care, discipline, employment, safeguarding, housing residents, grant applicants, legal advice, or sensitive financial matters should be handled carefully.

## 9. Reporting and Prayer

Reports should include prayer requests where appropriate. Prayer requests should be meaningful but should not disclose confidential personal details without permission or proper reason.

Prayer is part of the reporting system because the Society depends on God, not merely management technique.

## 10. Reporting and Mission Alignment

Reports should help identify mission drift. Report reviewers should notice activities disconnected from mission, weak church connection, theological confusion, repeated unresolved risks, dependency patterns, poor financial stewardship, unclear authority, missing reports, volunteer burnout, unsafe practices, and mission language disappearing from ministry practice.

Reporting is one of the earliest warning systems.

## 11. Reporting and Governance

Boards should receive reports that help them govern, not drown.

Board reports should be concise, summarized, and decision-focused. The Board should be able to see what changed, what matters, what risks exist, what decisions are needed, what prayer is requested, and what follow-up is required.

## 12. Reporting and Institutional Memory

Reports feed the institutional memory system.

Reports should be archived according to retention policy and summarized into annual reports where appropriate. Reports that document major decisions, crises, theological concerns, legal risks, financial issues, chapter development, housing milestones, cooperative milestones, foundation grants, or chaplaincy developments may have permanent historical value.

## 13. Reporting and National Chapters

National chapters shall report to the International Society according to international standards.

National chapter reports should include ministry activity, church relationships, finances, risks, legal compliance, staffing, volunteer activity, mission alignment concerns, prayer requests, major decisions, and upcoming needs.

## 14. Reporting and Affiliated Organizations

Affiliated organizations shall report according to their charter or agreement. Reports may include operational status, financial status, governance concerns, mission alignment, risks, people served, legal compliance, decisions needed, and prayer requests.

Affiliated organizations should not become reporting black holes.

## 15. Missed Reports

Missed reports should be followed up according to role and risk. One missed report may require a reminder. Repeated missed reports may indicate overload, confusion, avoidance, poor supervision, role mismatch, hidden problems, or governance failure.

Persistent failure to report should be escalated.

## 16. Report Review

Each report should have a designated reviewer. The reviewer should read promptly, identify action items, respond where needed, escalate concerns, preserve reports according to retention policy, and summarize trends for monthly or quarterly reports.

Reports should not disappear into inbox archaeology.
