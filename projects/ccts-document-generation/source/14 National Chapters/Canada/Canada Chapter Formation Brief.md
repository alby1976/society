# Canada Chapter Formation Brief

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a starter framework for forming the Canada Chapter of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Canada may serve as an early or founding national chapter, but it is not the theological or constitutional center of the Society. The Society's identity is international, and Canada's role is to embody the shared mission faithfully within Canadian law and context.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Canada may be first, but Canada must not become the parent.

The Canada Chapter should model faithful national implementation while remaining accountable to the International Constitution, shared doctrine, shared mission, reserved powers, reporting standards, and Mission Alignment Review.

---

# 3. Formation Status

The Canada Chapter should be treated as a formation-stage or provisional national chapter until formally recognized.

Formation work may include:

- legal assessment;
- charity or nonprofit readiness;
- founding board development;
- church advisory relationships;
- ministry launch planning;
- reporting system setup;
- risk review;
- financial controls;
- local adaptation register;
- Mission Alignment Review readiness.

---

# 4. Proposed Chapter Identity

The Canada Chapter should use a name approved by the International Board.

Possible working names:

- Christian Community Transformation Society — Canada Chapter;
- CCTS Canada;
- Christian Community Transformation Society of Canada;
- another lawful name approved after legal review.

Public use of name should not begin until authority is granted.

---

# 5. Legal Readiness

Canada Chapter formation should include review of:

- federal or provincial incorporation options;
- charity registration strategy if applicable;
- objects or purposes;
- bylaws;
- directors;
- registered office;
- banking;
- records;
- tax filings;
- receipting rules if charitable status applies;
- employment obligations;
- privacy obligations;
- fundraising rules;
- provincial operations;
- insurance.

This document is not legal advice. Qualified Canadian legal review is required.

---

# 6. Mission and Doctrine

The Canada Chapter shall affirm:

- the Society's Statement of Faith;
- the Society's mission;
- the Society's vision;
- the Society's core values;
- the Society/church distinction;
- international reserved powers;
- local church-supporting identity;
- Mission Alignment Review.

The Canada Chapter must not revise doctrine or mission to fit convenience, funding, or local pressure.

---

# 7. Initial Ministry Priorities

The Canada Chapter may consider the following phased priorities:

1. prayer and leadership development;
2. governance and reporting setup;
3. church advisory relationships;
4. worker cooperative pilot;
5. chaplaincy framework implementation;
6. housing feasibility review;
7. Church Support Foundation readiness;
8. national reporting and annual review.

The accepted launch principle is that a pilot cooperative may come before the Foundation because the Foundation requires stronger resources, controls, and grant infrastructure.

---

# 8. Church Relationships

The Canada Chapter should seek counsel and relationships with faithful Reformed and Presbyterian churches where appropriate.

Possible church relationship work includes:

- identifying church advisors;
- confirming church-supporting boundaries;
- creating referral pathways;
- seeking counsel on chaplaincy;
- identifying recognized theological training sources;
- inviting prayer support;
- avoiding competition with local churches.

---

# 9. Governance Requirements

Before formal recognition, Canada should prepare:

- proposed board or formation team;
- board qualifications;
- conflict of interest process;
- decision register;
- document registry;
- reporting schedule;
- finance controls;
- risk register;
- local adaptation register;
- records retention plan;
- ministry launch plan.

---

# 10. Financial Readiness

Canada Chapter financial readiness should include:

- initial budget;
- banking plan;
- expense approval process;
- donation handling rules;
- restricted fund policy;
- accounting system;
- financial reporting schedule;
- audit or review expectations;
- insurance review;
- grant control readiness if Foundation activity begins.

Money should not move faster than governance.

---

# 11. Local Adaptation

Canada-specific adaptations should be documented in [[Canada Chapter Local Adaptation Register]].

Adaptations may relate to:

- Canadian legal form;
- charity language;
- provincial operations;
- bilingual or multilingual needs;
- privacy rules;
- employment standards;
- housing law;
- cooperative law;
- grant-making rules;
- public communications.

Adaptation must not alter doctrine, mission, reserved powers, or church-supporting identity.

---

# 12. Recognition Path

The Canada Chapter should proceed through:

1. formation team authorization;
2. legal and governance review;
3. board readiness;
4. church advisory readiness;
5. financial readiness;
6. ministry priority approval;
7. provisional recognition;
8. limited launch;
9. reporting and evaluation;
10. full recognition if ready.

---

# 13. Immediate Deliverables

The Canada formation team should prepare:

- legal readiness memo;
- proposed bylaws or governance documents;
- board candidate list;
- conflict of interest disclosures;
- initial budget;
- launch priority plan;
- risk register;
- local adaptation register;
- reporting calendar;
- prayer and discernment record.

---

# 14. Review

This formation brief should be reviewed annually during the formation stage and whenever the Canada Chapter's legal status or recognition status changes.
