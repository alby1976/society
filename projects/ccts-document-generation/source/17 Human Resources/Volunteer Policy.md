# Volunteer Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for volunteers serving the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Volunteers are a gift to the Society's mission, but volunteer service still requires clarity, accountability, safety, confidentiality, and care.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Volunteers should be welcomed as servants, not used as free labour without structure.

The Society should give volunteers clear roles, proper support, realistic expectations, and protection from inappropriate responsibility.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- occasional volunteers;
- recurring volunteers;
- ministry volunteers;
- event volunteers;
- administrative volunteers;
- housing volunteers;
- cooperative-related volunteers where lawful;
- Foundation volunteers;
- prayer volunteers;
- volunteer leaders;
- national chapter volunteers.

---

# 4. Volunteer Role Clarity

Each recurring volunteer role should define:

- purpose;
- duties;
- supervisor;
- time expectations;
- authority limits;
- confidentiality level;
- training required;
- reporting requirements;
- safety concerns;
- term or review date.

Occasional event roles may use simplified descriptions.

---

# 5. Volunteer Approval

Volunteers should be approved before serving.

Approval may include:

- application or expression of interest;
- role matching;
- reference where appropriate;
- safeguarding review where required;
- conflict of interest disclosure;
- orientation;
- supervisor assignment.

Higher-risk roles require stronger review.

---

# 6. Volunteer Boundaries

Volunteers must not act beyond their role.

Volunteers may not:

- bind the Society legally;
- approve expenses unless authorized;
- promise grants;
- admit residents;
- provide chaplaincy unless appointed;
- handle confidential records without authorization;
- speak publicly for the Society without approval;
- supervise vulnerable persons without proper safeguards;
- make employment decisions.

---

# 7. Training

Volunteer training may include:

- mission and values;
- Society/church distinction;
- role duties;
- confidentiality;
- safety;
- safeguarding where applicable;
- privacy;
- reporting;
- conflict of interest;
- incident escalation.

Training should be documented for recurring or higher-risk roles.

---

# 8. Confidentiality

Volunteers may encounter sensitive information.

Volunteers must protect confidential information and should access only what is needed for their role.

Sensitive information may include:

- resident information;
- worker information;
- donor information;
- grant applications;
- church concerns;
- prayer requests;
- staff matters;
- legal matters.

---

# 9. Safety

Volunteer roles should be reviewed for safety.

Safety review may include:

- physical risks;
- transportation;
- working alone;
- vulnerable persons;
- property access;
- emergency contacts;
- training;
- supervision;
- incident reporting.

---

# 10. Expenses

Volunteer expenses may be reimbursed only if approved under [[Expense Approval and Reimbursement Policy]].

Volunteers should know in advance whether expenses are reimbursable.

The Society should not assume volunteers can personally subsidize ministry.

---

# 11. Reporting

Volunteers may be asked to report using [[Volunteer 5-15 Report]] or another simple format.

Reports may include:

- service completed;
- concerns;
- support needed;
- safety issues;
- prayer requests;
- follow-up items.

---

# 12. Volunteer Care

Volunteer supervisors should care for volunteers.

This includes:

- clear communication;
- appreciation;
- realistic workload;
- support;
- conflict response;
- training;
- rest;
- respect for family and church responsibilities.

Volunteers should not be guilted into unsustainable service.

---

# 13. Volunteer Misconduct

Volunteer misconduct may include:

- breach of confidentiality;
- unsafe conduct;
- harassment;
- abuse or exploitation;
- refusal to follow role limits;
- financial misconduct;
- public misrepresentation;
- repeated unreliability;
- conflict of interest concealment.

Misconduct should be addressed through appropriate correction, restriction, or removal.

---

# 14. Ending Volunteer Service

Volunteer service may end through:

- completion of role;
- resignation;
- inactivity;
- role change;
- misconduct;
- safety concern;
- loss of qualification;
- supervisor decision;
- ministry closure.

Ending service should include record handoff, access removal, and confidentiality reminder where needed.

---

# 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and whenever volunteer roles expand significantly.
