# International Expansion Assessment Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework provides a standard process for assessing whether the Christian Community Transformation Society should explore, form, or recognize work in a new country or jurisdiction.

It is intended for Hong Kong and future countries.

The framework protects the Society from expanding faster than it can pray, govern, report, fund, staff, and correct.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

International expansion should follow readiness, not momentum.

A new country may present opportunity, but opportunity alone is not authorization. Expansion should be tested through prayer, mission fit, doctrine, local church relationships, law, leadership, finances, risk, and reporting capacity.

---

# 3. Assessment Stages

The assessment process includes:

1. prayer and discernment;
2. mission fit review;
3. local church context review;
4. legal and regulatory review;
5. leadership readiness review;
6. financial readiness review;
7. ministry need review;
8. risk review;
9. local adaptation review;
10. International Board decision.

---

# 4. Stage 1: Prayer and Discernment

The Society should begin by praying for wisdom, humility, restraint, and faithfulness.

Discernment questions:

- Why this country?
- Why now?
- Who is asking?
- What need is being addressed?
- Are faithful churches present?
- Is qualified leadership available?
- What would happen if the Society did not expand there?
- Is this mission or ambition?

A written discernment note should be preserved.

---

# 5. Stage 2: Mission Fit

Mission fit review should ask:

- Does the proposed work support the Society's mission?
- Is there a clear church-supporting purpose?
- Are proposed ministries appropriate to the Society's role?
- Would this expansion strengthen or distract from current obligations?
- What ministries are being proposed first?
- Are those ministries proportionate to capacity?

---

# 6. Stage 3: Local Church Context

Church context review should consider:

- faithful local churches;
- theological alignment;
- denominational or council relationships;
- language needs;
- church referral possibilities;
- pastoral care boundaries;
- local theological education options;
- possible church advisory relationships;
- concerns about replacing or competing with churches.

The Society should not enter a country as if local churches are invisible.

---

# 7. Stage 4: Legal and Regulatory Review

Legal review should consider:

- legal form;
- nonprofit or charitable registration;
- foreign organization restrictions;
- tax obligations;
- fundraising;
- banking;
- employment;
- privacy;
- safeguarding;
- grants;
- housing;
- cooperative enterprise;
- public religious activity;
- cross-border transfers;
- reporting requirements;
- board liability.

Qualified local advice should be obtained before operations begin.

---

# 8. Stage 5: Leadership Readiness

Leadership readiness should consider:

- doctrinal alignment;
- governance competence;
- local credibility;
- church relationships;
- language capacity;
- financial integrity;
- reporting discipline;
- workload sustainability;
- conflict of interest risks;
- willingness to submit to international governance.

A country should not be launched around one overburdened person.

---

# 9. Stage 6: Financial Readiness

Financial readiness should consider:

- startup cost;
- operating budget;
- funding source;
- donor restrictions;
- bank account pathway;
- accounting;
- expense approval;
- audit or review;
- cross-border transfers;
- restricted funds;
- reserve needs;
- financial reporting.

---

# 10. Stage 7: Ministry Need and Sequence

The assessment should identify which ministries, if any, are appropriate first.

Possible ministries:

- prayer gatherings;
- church advisory relationships;
- chaplaincy;
- worker cooperative pilot;
- housing feasibility;
- Church Support Foundation;
- reporting and institutional memory;
- leadership development.

Ministry launch order should be based on readiness and risk.

---

# 11. Stage 8: Risk Review

Risk review should identify:

- legal risk;
- financial risk;
- reputational risk;
- church relationship risk;
- mission drift risk;
- leadership capacity risk;
- donor influence risk;
- privacy risk;
- safeguarding risk;
- cross-border governance risk;
- political or regulatory risk;
- language and translation risk;
- operational risk.

---

# 12. Stage 9: Local Adaptation Review

The assessment should identify likely adaptations.

Adaptations may involve:

- legal structure;
- language;
- board requirements;
- privacy rules;
- employment law;
- fundraising rules;
- grant-making rules;
- housing law;
- cooperative law;
- church advisory structure;
- public communications.

Adaptations affecting reserved powers require approval.

---

# 13. Stage 10: Decision Options

The International Board may decide to:

- decline exploration;
- continue research;
- designate an exploratory region;
- authorize a formation team;
- require additional legal review;
- require church advisory review;
- approve provisional chapter preparation;
- pause expansion;
- redirect to partnership rather than chapter formation.

# 15. Required Outputs

An expansion assessment should produce:

- discernment note;
- mission fit summary;
- church context summary;
- legal readiness memo;
- leadership readiness review;
- financial readiness summary;
- ministry sequence recommendation;
- risk register draft;
- local adaptation list;
- International Board recommendation.

---

# 16. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually and after each major international expansion assessment.
