# Conflict of Interest Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy protects the Christian Community Transformation Society from improper influence, divided loyalties, self-dealing, hidden benefit, and decisions that appear to serve private interests rather than the Society's mission.

Conflicts of interest do not always mean wrongdoing. Hidden conflicts, unmanaged conflicts, and denied conflicts are the real danger.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- International Board members;
- national board members;
- officers;
- committee members;
- council members;
- staff;
- volunteers with decision-making authority;
- chaplains;
- ministry leaders;
- affiliated organization leaders;
- advisors when participating in Society decisions.

National chapters may adopt local conflict policies consistent with this policy and local law.

---

# 3. Definition of Conflict of Interest

A conflict of interest exists when a person's private interests, relationships, duties, loyalties, or financial concerns may improperly influence, or appear to influence, their judgment on behalf of the Society.

Conflicts may be:

- actual;
- potential;
- perceived.

A perceived conflict can still harm trust, even when no misconduct occurs.

---

# 4. Types of Conflicts

## 4.1 Financial Conflict

A person may benefit financially from a decision involving:

- contract;
- employment;
- grant;
- lease;
- purchase;
- sale;
- investment;
- reimbursement;
- consulting;
- professional services;
- affiliated organization.

## 4.2 Relational Conflict

A person has a close relationship with someone affected by a decision.

Examples:

- family member;
- close friend;
- business partner;
- church officer;
- employer;
- employee;
- donor;
- tenant;
- grant applicant.

## 4.3 Organizational Conflict

A person owes duties to another organization whose interests may differ from the Society's interests.

Examples:

- church body;
- nonprofit;
- charity;
- business;
- cooperative;
- housing entity;
- foundation;
- school;
- government body.

## 4.4 Role Conflict

A person holds multiple Society roles that may create confusion.

Example:

- board member and paid contractor;
- national chapter director and grant applicant;
- chaplain and supervisor;
- founder advisor and vendor;
- board chair and executive staff.

## 4.5 Mission Conflict

A person has commitments or objectives that may pull the Society away from its adopted mission, theology, or church-supporting identity.

---

# 5. Duty to Disclose

Covered persons must disclose actual, potential, and perceived conflicts promptly.

Disclosure should occur:

- before appointment;
- annually;
- when a new conflict arises;
- before discussion of an affected matter;
- before accepting a benefit;
- before entering a transaction;
- before participating in a decision.

When in doubt, disclose. Hidden conflicts age badly.

---

# 6. Annual Disclosure

Board members, officers, and key leaders should complete an annual conflict disclosure.

The disclosure should identify:

- employment;
- business interests;
- family relationships relevant to the Society;
- board roles in other organizations;
- financial relationships;
- related-party transactions;
- possible grant or contract interests;
- church or denominational roles that may affect Society decisions.

---

# 7. Meeting Disclosure

At the beginning of each board or committee meeting, the chair should invite disclosure of conflicts related to the agenda.

Disclosures should be recorded in minutes.

---

# 8. Managing Conflicts

Depending on the conflict, management may include:

- disclosure only;
- abstention from vote;
- recusal from discussion;
- leaving the meeting temporarily;
- exclusion from confidential materials;
- independent review;
- competitive process;
- board approval by disinterested members;
- legal review;
- prohibition of the transaction.

The response should be proportionate to the risk.

---

# 9. Related-Party Transactions

A related-party transaction may be permitted only if:

1. the conflict is fully disclosed;
2. the conflicted person does not improperly influence the decision;
3. disinterested decision-makers determine the transaction is fair and mission-aligned;
4. alternatives are considered where appropriate;
5. the decision is documented;
6. the transaction complies with law.

The Society should be especially careful with transactions involving founders, directors, officers, family members, major donors, churches, tenants, contractors, and affiliated organizations.

---

# 10. Employment and Compensation Conflicts

A person should not participate in decisions about their own compensation, employment, contract, evaluation, discipline, or termination.

A person should not improperly influence decisions about compensation or employment of close family members or close associates.

---

# 11. Grants and Benefits

A person involved in grant approval should disclose if they, their family, their church, their ministry, or an organization they lead may benefit.

The Society's grant-making must be transparent, mission-aligned, and free from favoritism.

---

# 12. Housing Conflicts

Housing ministry creates special conflict risks.

Conflicts may arise involving:

- tenant selection;
- lease terms;
- rent relief;
- maintenance contracts;
- property purchases;
- family members seeking housing;
- board members connected to contractors;
- church partners recommending residents.

Housing decisions must be documented and fair.

---

# 13. Cooperative Conflicts

Worker cooperative ministry may create conflicts involving:

- ownership transition;
- patronage;
- wages;
- contracts;
- board roles;
- Society oversight;
- worker membership decisions;
- business opportunities.

These conflicts should be disclosed and managed carefully.

---

# 14. Confidentiality and Conflicts

A conflicted person may be excluded from confidential materials related to the conflict.

Confidentiality must be used to protect the Society and people involved, not to punish disclosure.

---

# 15. Failure to Disclose

Failure to disclose a conflict may result in:

- warning;
- removal from discussion or vote;
- reversal or review of decision;
- removal from committee;
- removal from office;
- termination of contract;
- discipline according to policy;
- legal action if required.

The response should match the seriousness of the failure.

---

# 16. Documentation

Conflict disclosures and management steps should be documented.

Records may include:

- annual disclosure forms;
- meeting minutes;
- recusal notes;
- board resolutions;
- independent review records;
- transaction summaries;
- legal advice summaries where appropriate.

Significant conflict decisions should be entered in the [[Decision Register]].

---

# 17. Culture of Integrity

The Society should cultivate a culture where disclosure is normal and not treated as accusation.

A well-disclosed conflict can often be managed. A hidden conflict becomes a governance raccoon in the walls: noisy, destructive, and discovered too late.

---
