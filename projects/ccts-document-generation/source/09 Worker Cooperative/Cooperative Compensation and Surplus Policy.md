# Cooperative Compensation and Surplus Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for compensation, benefits, reimbursements, profit or surplus allocation, reserves, and financial fairness in a Society-affiliated worker cooperative.

The cooperative must treat workers justly, comply with law, protect mission, and steward money transparently.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Workers should be compensated lawfully and fairly.

Mission language must not be used to excuse exploitation, hidden unpaid labour, unclear promises, or unsafe financial practices.

A cooperative should be generous where possible, lawful always, and honest when resources are limited.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- wages;
- salaries;
- stipends;
- contractor payments;
- trainee payments;
- reimbursements;
- benefits;
- bonuses;
- member distributions;
- patronage dividends where lawful;
- surplus allocation;
- reserves;
- volunteer limits;
- related-party payments.

Local employment, tax, cooperative, charity, nonprofit, and payroll laws must be followed.

---

# 4. Worker Status and Pay

The cooperative must define the legal status of each worker before compensation is set.

Possible worker statuses include:

- employee;
- contractor;
- member-worker;
- trainee;
- apprentice;
- volunteer where lawful;
- supervisor;
- manager.

Misclassification is not stewardship. It is risk with a timesheet.

---

# 5. Compensation Principles

Compensation should be:

- lawful;
- documented;
- approved by proper authority;
- transparent enough for governance;
- fair relative to role and capacity;
- sustainable;
- consistent with mission;
- free from unlawful discrimination;
- protected from favoritism.

---

# 6. Wage or Payment Setting

Compensation decisions may consider:

- local law;
- role;
- skill level;
- training status;
- market comparison;
- cooperative finances;
- worker need;
- mission purpose;
- grant or donor restrictions;
- sustainability;
- board-approved pay bands.

Pay should not be improvised privately.

---

# 7. Volunteer Limits

If volunteers serve the cooperative, their role must be legally reviewed.

The cooperative must not use volunteers to avoid wages where work should lawfully be paid.

Volunteer roles should be:

- clearly defined;
- mission-related;
- lawful;
- non-exploitative;
- supervised;
- documented.

---

# 8. Trainees and Apprentices

Training roles must be clear.

The cooperative should define:

- training purpose;
- duration;
- compensation if any;
- supervision;
- evaluation;
- safety;
- transition pathway;
- legal status;
- expectations.

Training language must not disguise regular unpaid work.

---

# 9. Reimbursements

Reimbursements should be authorized and documented.

The cooperative should define:

- eligible expenses;
- approval process;
- receipts required;
- mileage or travel rules;
- tool or equipment reimbursement;
- deadlines;
- budget limits.

---

# 10. Benefits

Benefits may be provided where feasible and lawful.

Benefits may include:

- health benefits;
- leave;
- training support;
- equipment support;
- transportation support;
- retirement contributions;
- emergency support;
- other approved benefits.

Benefits should be documented and applied fairly.

---

# 11. Surplus Allocation

If the cooperative generates surplus, allocation must follow law, governing documents, Society oversight, and mission.

Surplus may be allocated to:

- operating reserve;
- capital reserve;
- worker distribution where lawful;
- training fund;
- benevolence or hardship fund where lawful;
- debt repayment;
- equipment replacement;
- mission reinvestment;
- Society-approved ministry use.

Surplus should not be distributed before obligations and reserves are considered.

---

# 12. Reserves

The cooperative should maintain appropriate reserves.

Reserves may include:

- operating reserve;
- payroll reserve;
- tax reserve;
- equipment replacement reserve;
- insurance reserve;
- emergency reserve;
- training reserve.

No cooperative should celebrate surplus while forgetting next month's payroll.

---

# 13. Patronage or Member Distributions

If patronage dividends, member distributions, or profit sharing are allowed, the rules must be written.

Rules should define:

- eligibility;
- calculation method;
- timing;
- tax treatment;
- board approval;
- reserve requirements;
- forfeiture or exit rules;
- conflict of interest;
- reporting.

Legal and tax review is required.

---

# 14. Compensation Review

Compensation should be reviewed periodically.

Review should consider:

- legal compliance;
- fairness;
- sustainability;
- worker retention;
- inflation;
- skill development;
- cooperative performance;
- budget;
- mission impact.

---

# 15. Related-Party Payments

Payments to board members, family members, Society leaders, churches, vendors connected to leaders, or related businesses must be disclosed and reviewed.

Related-party payments should be approved only where lawful, fair, documented, and in the cooperative's best interest.

---

# 16. Financial Transparency

The cooperative should provide appropriate financial transparency to members and governing bodies.

Transparency may include:

- budget summary;
- income and expenses;
- reserve status;
- compensation framework;
- surplus allocation;
- major liabilities;
- financial risks.

Transparency must respect privacy and legal requirements.

---

# 17. Prohibited Practices

The cooperative must not:

- withhold lawful wages;
- misclassify workers;
- use spiritual pressure for unpaid work;
- make secret compensation deals;
- distribute surplus while ignoring liabilities;
- allow insiders to extract improper benefit;
- hide financial distress;
- promise ownership or distributions without written authority.

---

# 18. Reporting

Compensation and surplus matters should be reported through financial and cooperative reports.

Reports may include:

- payroll summary;
- contractor payments;
- volunteer use;
- trainee status;
- surplus allocation;
- reserve status;
- compensation concerns;
- related-party payments.

Personal details should be protected.

---

# 19. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during cooperative launch and at least every three years once stable.
