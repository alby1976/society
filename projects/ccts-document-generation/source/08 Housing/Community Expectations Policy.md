# Community Expectations Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes community expectations for Society-related housing.

Community expectations help residents live together with safety, respect, responsibility, hospitality, and peace.

The Society's housing community is shaped by Christian mission, but expectations must be lawful, clear, reasonable, and non-coercive.

This policy should be read with [[Housing Trust Framework]], [[Housing Philosophy]], [[Tenant and Resident Policy]], [[Lease Standards]], and [[Stewardship Housing Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Healthy community requires both welcome and order.

A housing community without expectations becomes chaos. A housing community with harsh expectations becomes control. The Society seeks a better path: truthful, gracious, accountable community life.

Community expectations should protect residents, neighbors, property, staff, volunteers, and the Society's mission.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy may apply to:

- rental housing;
- long-term lease housing;
- community land trust housing;
- transitional housing;
- worker housing;
- ministry housing;
- supported housing;
- shared housing;
- resident programs.

Application depends on local law and the housing agreement.

---

# 4. Legal Status

Community expectations may have different legal effect depending on the housing model.

Some expectations may be:

- lease terms;
- house rules;
- program standards;
- voluntary community guidelines;
- safety requirements;
- ministry expectations;
- orientation material.

The Society must clearly identify which expectations are enforceable lease obligations and which are voluntary community practices.

Legal review is required before enforcement.

---

# 5. Core Expectations

Residents are expected to:

- treat others with respect;
- care for property;
- communicate honestly;
- respect privacy;
- avoid harassment and threats;
- follow safety rules;
- pay rent or fees as agreed;
- report maintenance issues;
- respect quiet enjoyment;
- follow guest rules;
- resolve conflict appropriately;
- comply with lease or occupancy terms.

---

# 6. Christian Identity

The Society's housing ministry has a Christian identity.

Residents should be informed that the Society is Christian and that spiritual care, prayer, and church referral may be offered.

Residents should not be coerced into religious participation.

Where a housing program has specific faith-based requirements, those requirements must be legally reviewed, clearly disclosed, and approved.

---

# 7. Prayer and Devotional Opportunities

The Society may offer prayer, devotional gatherings, Bible studies, or spiritual care opportunities.

Participation should ordinarily be voluntary unless a lawful, approved program structure provides otherwise.

The Society must not present these activities as a replacement for local church worship.

Prayer or devotional invitations should be clear, gracious, and non-coercive.

---

# 8. Local Church Connection

Residents may be encouraged to connect with faithful local churches.

The Society may provide referrals under [[Church Referral Policy]].

Residents should not be treated as members of a Society congregation. There is no Society congregation.

The Society may support church connection without creating pressure, surveillance, or artificial spiritual performance.

---

# 9. Respectful Conduct

Residents must avoid:

- threats;
- harassment;
- abuse;
- intimidation;
- discrimination;
- violence;
- exploitation;
- property damage;
- retaliation;
- serious disruption;
- illegal activity;
- conduct that endangers others.

Serious conduct concerns may require immediate action according to law and policy.

---

# 10. Conflict Resolution

Residents should seek to resolve conflict respectfully.

The process may include:

1. direct respectful conversation where safe;
2. contact with housing manager;
3. mediation where appropriate;
4. written complaint;
5. review by housing authority;
6. corrective action;
7. legal process if necessary.

No resident should be pressured into direct confrontation where safety is a concern.

---

# 11. Guests

Guest policies should be clear.

Guest rules may address:

- overnight guests;
- length of stay;
- safety;
- shared spaces;
- resident responsibility;
- prohibited guests;
- emergency contact;
- local law.

Guest rules must be lawful and reasonable.

Residents should not use guest arrangements to create unauthorized occupancy.

---

# 12. Shared Spaces

Residents using shared spaces should:

- clean after use;
- respect booking or use rules;
- avoid damage;
- respect quiet hours;
- store personal items properly;
- report issues;
- use spaces safely.

Shared spaces are not "nobody's responsibility." They are everybody's stewardship test.

---

# 13. Property Care

Residents should care for units, shared spaces, and property.

Expectations may include:

- reporting damage;
- preventing pests;
- proper waste disposal;
- safe appliance use;
- no unauthorized alterations;
- no unsafe storage;
- respect for landscaping;
- care for keys and access devices.

---

# 14. Safety

Safety expectations may include:

- no tampering with alarms;
- no blocking exits;
- compliance with fire rules;
- reporting hazards;
- safe use of equipment;
- emergency contact procedures;
- no illegal activity;
- no dangerous storage;
- compliance with lawful property rules.

Safety issues should be reported promptly.

---

# 15. Privacy

Residents must respect one another's privacy.

This includes:

- no unauthorized entry;
- no recording without permission where prohibited;
- no gossip about private matters;
- no sharing personal information;
- respect for mail, packages, and belongings.

Housing staff and volunteers must also respect resident privacy.

---

# 16. Quiet Enjoyment

Residents should respect neighbors' reasonable quiet enjoyment.

Expectations may include:

- quiet hours;
- noise limits;
- respectful use of shared spaces;
- attention to children and guests;
- avoidance of disruptive gatherings;
- communication when events are planned.

---

# 17. Care for Vulnerable Residents

Community expectations should protect vulnerable residents.

The Society should avoid rules that expose vulnerable persons to shame, pressure, exploitation, or unnecessary disclosure.

Residents should not be expected to provide spiritual, emotional, financial, or practical support beyond reasonable neighborliness.

---

# 18. Complaints

Residents may make complaints about:

- maintenance;
- safety;
- conduct;
- staff;
- privacy;
- discrimination;
- harassment;
- spiritual care concerns;
- lease concerns;
- community expectations.

Complaints should be handled according to housing policy.

---

# 19. Corrective Action

Violation of community expectations may result in:

- conversation;
- written reminder;
- mediation;
- warning;
- behavior agreement;
- restricted use of shared spaces;
- lease enforcement;
- termination process;
- emergency action.

Corrective action must comply with law and lease terms.

---

# 20. Mission Alignment

Community expectations should be reviewed for mission alignment.

The review should ask:

- Do expectations support dignity and peace?
- Are they lawful?
- Are they clear?
- Are they enforceable?
- Are they coercive?
- Do they preserve Christian identity without manipulation?
- Do they support church connection without replacing the church?
- Are they appropriate to the housing model?

---

# 21. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually with [[Tenant and Resident Policy]], [[Lease Standards]], and [[Stewardship Housing Policy]].
