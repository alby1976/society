# Chaplaincy Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the chaplaincy ministry of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Society's chaplaincy exists to provide spiritual care, prayer, encouragement, biblical counsel within defined limits, crisis support, and referral to faithful local churches. Chaplaincy serves the Society as a whole and is not owned by, limited to, or controlled by any single ministry such as a worker cooperative, housing trust, or foundation.

This framework should be read with [[Chaplain Policy]], [[Chaplain Qualifications]], [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter]], [[Theological Oversight Council Charter]], and [[Seminary Recognition Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society's chaplaincy supports the local church. It does not replace the local church.

The chaplain may provide spiritual care to workers, volunteers, residents, beneficiaries, staff, families, unchurched persons, and ministry participants, but should ordinarily encourage connection to faithful local churches where appropriate.

The chaplain is a bridge, not a substitute church with a smaller parking lot.

---

# 3. Nature of Society Chaplaincy

The chaplain serves the Society as a ministry of spiritual care.

The chaplain may serve across:

- housing ministry;
- worker cooperative ministry;
- church support foundation work;
- staff and volunteer care;
- residents and beneficiaries;
- national chapters;
- prayer gatherings;
- Annual Day of Prayer;
- crisis support;
- church referral processes.

The chaplaincy is Society-wide because spiritual care needs may arise in any ministry.

---

# 4. What the Chaplain May Do

A Society chaplain may:

- pray with people;
- provide spiritual encouragement;
- offer biblical counsel within role limits;
- visit persons connected to Society ministries;
- support workers and volunteers;
- support residents and beneficiaries;
- help unchurched people connect to faithful local churches;
- assist with devotional gatherings where appropriate;
- facilitate or support the Annual Day of Prayer;
- provide a devotional meditation during the Annual Day of Prayer;
- advise leaders on spiritual care themes;
- identify pastoral or church referral needs;
- participate in crisis support;
- submit chaplaincy reports with proper confidentiality;
- advise on spiritual care policies.

---

# 5. What the Chaplain Must Not Do

A Society chaplain must not:

- replace a local pastor;
- replace elders or church officers;
- administer sacraments;
- exercise church discipline;
- contradict lawful local church leadership;
- draw church members away from their churches;
- create permanent dependence on Society spiritual care;
- act outside doctrinal standards;
- provide professional services beyond qualification;
- ignore safeguarding, legal, or privacy obligations;
- use confidential information improperly;
- speak publicly for the Society without authorization.

---

# 6. Relationship to Local Churches

The chaplain should work with a church-supporting posture.

When a person is already connected to a faithful local church, the chaplain should respect that church's pastoral care and leadership.

When a person is unchurched, the chaplain may provide temporary spiritual care while seeking appropriate connection to faithful local churches.

When serious pastoral issues arise, the chaplain should encourage or facilitate referral to local church leadership where appropriate and lawful.

---

# 7. Relationship to Church Advisory Fellowship

The [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]] may advise on:

- chaplaincy boundaries;
- church referral processes;
- seminary recognition;
- devotional practices;
- pastoral concerns;
- Annual Day of Prayer;
- church relationship issues;
- standards for spiritual care.

The chaplain should be willing to receive churchly counsel.

---

# 8. Relationship to Theological Oversight

The [[Theological Oversight Council Charter|Theological Oversight Council]] may advise on:

- doctrinal standards for chaplains;
- spiritual care materials;
- theological concerns;
- devotional teaching;
- chaplaincy training;
- disputed theological questions;
- mission drift concerns.

Chaplaincy materials and teaching should remain consistent with [[Statement of Faith]].

---

# 9. Qualifications

A Society chaplain should meet the standards in [[Chaplain Qualifications]].

At minimum, chaplaincy requires:

- theological maturity;
- alignment with the Society's Statement of Faith;
- theological training from a seminary recognized by the Church Advisory Fellowship or other authorized body;
- spiritual care competence;
- accountability;
- confidentiality;
- respect for local church authority.

---

# 10. Appointment

Chaplain appointment should be approved by the appropriate board or authority.

The appointment process may include:

1. application or nomination;
2. doctrinal review;
3. church reference;
4. seminary or training review;
5. interview;
6. conflict of interest disclosure;
7. safeguarding review;
8. approval;
9. role description;
10. reporting expectations;
11. review schedule.

---

# 11. Reporting

The chaplain shall report according to [[5-15 Reporting Framework]] and the [[Chaplain 5-15 Report]] template.

Chaplain reports must protect confidentiality.

Reports should communicate:

- activity summary;
- general spiritual care themes;
- referrals;
- risks;
- support needed;
- prayer themes;
- decisions required.

Reports should not include unnecessary personal or pastoral details.

---

# 12. Confidentiality

Chaplaincy requires careful confidentiality.

Confidentiality must protect people while allowing proper escalation for:

- safeguarding concerns;
- legal obligations;
- serious risk;
- abuse or exploitation concerns;
- immediate danger;
- board or executive decision needs;
- mission alignment concerns.

The chaplain should never promise absolute confidentiality.

---

# 13. Annual Day of Prayer

The chaplain may facilitate the Society's Annual Day of Prayer if appointed or delegated to do so.

The chaplain may provide a devotional meditation during the Annual Day of Prayer, provided the meditation is consistent with the Society's Statement of Faith and does not contradict local church leadership.

---

# 14. International and National Chaplaincy

As the Society expands, chaplaincy may be organized at multiple levels:

```text
International Chaplaincy Standards
        |
        +-- National Chaplain
        +-- Regional Chaplain
        +-- Ministry Chaplain or Spiritual Care Worker
```

National chaplaincy structures must remain aligned with international doctrine, mission, and church-supporting boundaries.

---

# 15. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually during the founding period and whenever the Society appoints chaplains, launches a national chapter, or creates a major ministry requiring spiritual care.
