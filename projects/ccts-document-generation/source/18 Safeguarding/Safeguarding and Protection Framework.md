# Safeguarding and Protection Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the Society's basic safeguarding commitments for protecting people served by, working in, volunteering with, or connected to the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Safeguarding includes preventing harm, recognizing concerns, reporting promptly, responding appropriately, and learning from incidents.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should never use ministry, authority, charity, housing, work, grants, spiritual care, or institutional reputation to excuse harm or silence concerns.

Protection of people is a governance duty, not a public relations concern.

---

# 3. Scope

This framework applies to:

- board members;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- contractors where applicable;
- chaplains;
- housing workers;
- cooperative workers;
- Foundation grant reviewers;
- national chapter leaders;
- ministry leaders;
- advisors;
- residents;
- workers;
- students;
- grant applicants;
- beneficiaries;
- visitors;
- partner relationships.

Local chapters must comply with local safeguarding, child protection, privacy, employment, housing, charity, and reporting laws.

---

# 4. Safeguarding Commitments

The Society commits to:

- treating people with dignity;
- preventing abuse, exploitation, coercion, and harassment;
- maintaining appropriate boundaries;
- screening and training roles where required;
- reporting serious concerns promptly;
- protecting confidential information;
- avoiding retaliation against good-faith reporters;
- cooperating with lawful authorities where required;
- documenting incidents responsibly;
- reviewing risks and corrective actions;
- supporting local churches without replacing their authority.

---

# 5. Higher-Risk Ministry Contexts

Safeguarding attention is especially important in:

- housing;
- chaplaincy;
- spiritual care;
- worker cooperative settings;
- grant-making;
- student support;
- ministry with minors;
- ministry with vulnerable adults;
- crisis support;
- home visits;
- transportation;
- one-on-one meetings;
- handling personal or financial information.

A ministry is not lower risk merely because it is charitable.

---

# 6. Role-Based Safeguarding

Safeguarding expectations should be built into role descriptions.

Roles may require:

- application or appointment review;
- reference checks;
- background checks where lawful and required;
- training;
- confidentiality agreement;
- supervision;
- reporting duty;
- boundary rules;
- incident escalation knowledge.

---

# 7. Boundaries

People serving the Society must maintain appropriate boundaries.

Boundaries may involve:

- communication;
- transportation;
- private meetings;
- physical contact;
- gifts;
- money;
- spiritual care;
- housing access;
- workplace authority;
- social media;
- personal relationships;
- dependency relationships.

Boundary confusion should be addressed early.

---

# 8. Reporting Concerns

Safeguarding concerns should be reported promptly through [[Safeguarding Reporting Procedure]] and escalated under [[Incident Escalation Policy]].

A person does not need to investigate a concern before reporting it.

Reports should be made in good faith, with necessary facts, and without spreading details unnecessarily.

---

# 9. Confidentiality and Need-to-Know

Safeguarding information should be handled on a need-to-know basis.

Confidentiality must not be used to hide danger, avoid lawful reporting, or prevent proper escalation.

Sensitive information should be shared only with authorized persons or authorities as required.

---

# 10. Retaliation Prohibited

Retaliation against a person who raises a safeguarding concern in good faith is prohibited.

Retaliation may include:

- threats;
- exclusion;
- intimidation;
- role removal without proper reason;
- spiritual pressure;
- public shaming;
- hostile treatment;
- reduced opportunities;
- reputational attacks.

---

# 11. Response

A safeguarding response may include:

- immediate safety action;
- report to appropriate authority where required;
- supervisor or board notification;
- legal advice;
- privacy review;
- role restriction;
- temporary suspension;
- support for affected persons;
- incident report;
- risk register update;
- corrective action plan;
- policy review.

---

# 12. Records

Safeguarding records should be accurate, restrained, confidential, and retained according to law and [[Records Management Policy]].

Records should not include unnecessary personal detail.

Records should not be deleted to avoid embarrassment or liability.

---

# 13. Church Relationship Boundary

When safeguarding concerns involve church relationships, the Society must not hide behind church structures or bypass lawful duties.

The Society should coordinate respectfully where appropriate, but proper reporting, safety, and legal obligations remain.

---

# 14. Training

Safeguarding training should be provided according to role and risk.

Training may include:

- recognizing concerns;
- reporting process;
- confidentiality;
- boundaries;
- privacy;
- incident escalation;
- retaliation prohibition;
- role limits;
- documentation.

---

# 15. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually and after any serious safeguarding concern.
