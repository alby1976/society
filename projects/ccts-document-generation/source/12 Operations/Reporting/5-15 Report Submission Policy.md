# 5-15 Report Submission Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how 5-15 reports are submitted, reviewed, followed up, corrected, escalated, and incorporated into the reporting system of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The 5-15 reporting system exists to support communication, accountability, prayer, supervision, risk awareness, and institutional memory.

This policy should be read with [[Reporting Framework]], [[5-15 Reporting Framework]], [[5-15 Report Confidentiality Policy]], [[5-15 Report Retention Policy]], and [[Report Archive Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A report that is not submitted cannot help anyone.

The Society's reporting discipline should be simple enough to use consistently and serious enough to matter. The purpose is not to create paperwork guilt. The purpose is to keep leaders informed, support workers, notice risks early, and preserve memory.

---

# 3. Who Must Submit Reports

5-15 reports may be required from:

- volunteers;
- staff members;
- ministry leaders;
- department directors;
- chaplains;
- housing managers;
- worker cooperative managers;
- foundation directors;
- national executive directors;
- international executive director;
- board committees;
- project leaders;
- affiliated organization leaders.

A role-specific template may define additional expectations.

---

# 4. Submission Frequency

Typical submission frequency is:

| Role | Default Submission Frequency |
|---|---|
| Volunteer | After service period or weekly if ongoing |
| Staff Member | Weekly |
| Ministry Leader | Weekly |
| Department Director | Weekly or biweekly |
| Chaplain | Weekly with confidentiality safeguards |
| Housing Manager | Weekly |
| Worker Cooperative Manager | Weekly |
| Foundation Director | Weekly or biweekly |
| National Executive Director | Weekly or monthly executive summary |
| International Executive Director | Weekly or monthly executive summary |
| Board Committee | After meeting or monthly |
| National Board | Quarterly summary |
| International Board | Quarterly summary |

Frequency may be adjusted by supervisor, board, or policy according to risk and workload.

---

# 5. Submission Deadline

The default weekly report deadline should be the last working day of the reporting week or the first working day of the following week.

A national chapter or ministry may set a specific day.

Examples:

- Friday by end of day;
- Monday by noon;
- within 48 hours of a ministry event;
- within 7 days after a committee meeting.

Deadlines should be predictable.

---

# 6. Submission Format

Reports should be submitted in the approved template format.

The Society may permit submission through:

- Markdown file;
- form;
- email;
- internal reporting system;
- shared folder;
- generator-ready report file;
- approved database or dashboard.

Where possible, reports should be stored in generator-ready Markdown with YAML frontmatter.

# 8. Required Content

A general 5-15 report should include:

1. Basic Information
2. Praise and Thanksgiving
3. Major Accomplishments
4. Current Priorities
5. Challenges or Risks
6. Decisions or Support Needed
7. Upcoming Work
8. Prayer Requests
9. Additional Comments

Role-specific templates may require additional sections.

---

# 9. Submission Location

Reports should be submitted to the designated reviewer or location.

Recommended structure:

```text
source/
  12 Operations/
    Reports/
      2026/
        06/
          5-15/
            Chaplain/
            Housing/
            Cooperative/
            Foundation/
            National Chapters/
```

For confidential reports, access restrictions must be applied according to [[5-15 Report Confidentiality Policy]].

---

# 10. Reviewer Responsibilities

A reviewer shall:

- read the report promptly;
- identify decisions or support needed;
- identify risks;
- identify prayer requests;
- respond where needed;
- escalate serious concerns;
- ensure the report is stored correctly;
- mark the report reviewed if the system supports it;
- identify items for monthly or quarterly summary.

Reports should not be collected like stamps. They must be read.

---

# 11. Response Expectations

Not every report requires a written response.

A response is required when the report includes:

- decision needed;
- risk;
- request for support;
- safeguarding concern;
- legal concern;
- financial concern;
- theological concern;
- serious conflict;
- urgent prayer concern;
- unclear authority;
- repeated unresolved issue.

The response may be brief.

---

# 12. Escalation

Reports must be escalated when they identify:

- abuse or safeguarding concern;
- immediate safety risk;
- legal issue;
- financial irregularity;
- major conflict;
- serious pastoral concern;
- privacy breach;
- mission drift;
- doctrinal concern;
- board decision needed;
- media or reputational risk;
- national chapter crisis.

Escalation should follow the relevant policy and authority structure.

---

# 13. Late Reports

Late reports should be addressed proportionately.

Possible responses:

- reminder;
- check-in;
- supervisor follow-up;
- workload review;
- training;
- adjustment of reporting frequency;
- escalation for repeated failure;
- role review where necessary.

Repeated missed reports may indicate a problem in supervision, workload, clarity, or accountability.

---

# 14. Corrections and Amendments

If a report contains an error, the reporter should submit a correction.

Corrections should preserve the record where appropriate.

Significant corrections should identify:

- original report;
- correction date;
- corrected item;
- reason for correction;
- reviewer notified.

Reports should not be silently altered when the alteration affects governance memory.

---

# 15. Confidential Submissions

Confidential reports must follow [[5-15 Report Confidentiality Policy]].

A report containing sensitive personal, pastoral, legal, employment, housing, safeguarding, grant, or financial information must be marked appropriately.

The reporter should include only what is necessary.

---

# 16. Reports During Absence

If a person is absent, on leave, ill, traveling, or otherwise unavailable, the supervisor may:

- waive the report;
- request a short update;
- allow delayed submission;
- ask a delegate to report;
- include the update in a supervisor summary.

The goal is continuity, not punishment.

---

# 17. Reports for Volunteers

Volunteer reporting should be simple.

A volunteer may submit a short report after a service period or event.

Volunteer reports may include:

- date served;
- ministry area;
- people served;
- what went well;
- concern or risk;
- support needed;
- prayer request.

---

# 18. Reports for Chaplains

Chaplain reports should protect confidentiality.

A chaplain should report activity and themes without unnecessary personal details.

Sensitive pastoral matters should be handled through restricted channels.

---

# 19. Reports for Boards and Committees

Board and committee 5-15 style summaries should focus on:

- decisions made;
- decisions needed;
- risks;
- documents reviewed;
- action items;
- prayer concerns;
- matters requiring escalation.

These summaries supplement minutes; they do not replace formal minutes where minutes are required.

---

# 20. Integration with Monthly Summaries

Reviewers should use 5-15 reports to prepare monthly summaries.

Monthly summaries should identify:

- repeated themes;
- major accomplishments;
- unresolved risks;
- decisions needed;
- prayer themes;
- reporting gaps;
- mission alignment concerns.
