# Housing Governance Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes governance standards for Society housing ministries, housing trusts, property-holding entities, community land trusts, and housing-related affiliated organizations.

Housing governance must protect mission, residents, property, finances, legal compliance, and the Society's church-supporting identity.

This policy should be read with [[Housing Trust Framework]], [[Housing Philosophy]], [[International Governance Framework]], [[Reserved Powers Framework]], [[Delegated Authority Framework]], and [[Mission Alignment Review]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Housing ministry requires stronger governance before stronger growth.

The Society should not acquire property, admit residents, sign long leases, take on debt, or launch housing programs without clear governance.

Housing problems do not remain theoretical. They come with keys, pipes, invoices, neighbors, and lawyers.

---

# 3. Governance Structure

A housing ministry shall have a defined governance structure.

The structure should identify:

- legal owner;
- beneficial owner if different;
- board oversight;
- management authority;
- property manager;
- resident support role;
- financial authority;
- maintenance authority;
- reporting authority;
- chaplaincy relationship;
- complaint process;
- mission alignment reviewer.

---

# 4. Legal Form

The housing ministry may operate through:

- Society direct ownership;
- national chapter ownership;
- housing trust;
- community land trust;
- nonprofit housing corporation;
- charitable subsidiary;
- property-holding corporation;
- partnership or joint venture;
- other lawful structure.

The legal form must be approved by the appropriate authority and reviewed for mission, tax, charity, liability, and governance implications.

---

# 5. Reserved Powers

The International Society may reserve authority over:

- creation of housing entities using Society identity;
- transfer of mission-critical housing assets;
- major property acquisitions with international significance;
- sale or encumbrance of major housing assets;
- adoption of international housing standards;
- mission alignment review;
- use of Society name in housing projects;
- major housing partnerships;
- cross-border housing models.

Local implementation may be delegated.

---

# 6. Delegated Authority

Housing authority may be delegated to:

- National Board;
- housing board;
- housing committee;
- housing director;
- property manager;
- resident support coordinator;
- chaplain;
- finance officer.

Delegations must be written and should define:

- spending limits;
- lease authority;
- maintenance authority;
- resident selection authority;
- emergency authority;
- reporting duties;
- escalation rules.

---

# 7. Board Oversight

The responsible board shall oversee:

- mission alignment;
- financial performance;
- legal compliance;
- risk;
- resident policy;
- lease policy;
- property stewardship;
- major repairs;
- insurance;
- safeguarding;
- privacy;
- reporting;
- major complaints;
- management performance.

The board should not micromanage maintenance schedules, but it must know whether the roof, budget, and mission are leaking.

---

# 8. Housing Committee

A housing committee may be established.

Its duties may include:

- reviewing housing policy;
- monitoring occupancy;
- reviewing maintenance summaries;
- reviewing resident support themes;
- reviewing risk reports;
- recommending major decisions;
- supporting mission alignment review;
- reviewing affordability standards.

The committee must have a written charter.

---

# 9. Property Management

The Society may use internal or external property management.

Property management responsibilities should include:

- maintenance coordination;
- rent collection;
- lease administration;
- inspections;
- vendor coordination;
- compliance;
- resident communication;
- emergency response;
- reporting.

If an external property manager is used, the contract must preserve mission requirements, privacy, reporting, and resident dignity.

---

# 10. Resident Selection Governance

Resident selection must be governed by written policy.

The policy should address:

- eligibility;
- affordability;
- mission fit where lawful;
- vulnerability;
- church or ministry referral;
- conflicts of interest;
- nondiscrimination requirements;
- documentation;
- review authority;
- appeal or reconsideration where appropriate.

Resident selection must not be arbitrary.

---

# 11. Lease and Agreement Governance

Lease or occupancy agreements must be legally reviewed.

Agreements should identify:

- parties;
- rent or fee;
- term;
- renewal;
- maintenance;
- house rules;
- community expectations;
- privacy;
- spiritual care availability;
- complaint process;
- termination;
- legal rights.

No informal housing arrangement should be allowed to become a legal trap.

---

# 12. Financial Governance

Housing finance shall include:

- operating budget;
- maintenance reserve;
- capital reserve;
- insurance;
- vacancy planning;
- rent policy;
- affordability subsidy policy;
- debt policy;
- financial reporting;
- audit or review where appropriate.

Housing must not depend on wishful budgeting.

---

# 13. Maintenance Governance

Maintenance standards should be documented.

The Society should track:

- routine maintenance;
- urgent repairs;
- inspections;
- capital needs;
- safety issues;
- vendor performance;
- deferred maintenance;
- resident reports.

Deferred maintenance is a silent fundraiser for future disaster.

---

# 14. Risk Governance

Housing risks should be included in a risk register.

Risk categories include:

- resident safety;
- building safety;
- legal compliance;
- lease disputes;
- privacy;
- conflict;
- discrimination claims;
- safeguarding;
- financial shortfall;
- property damage;
- mission drift;
- church relationship confusion;
- vendor risk.

High risks must be escalated.

---

# 15. Reporting

Housing ministry shall submit reports.

Reports may include:

- [[Housing Manager 5-15 Report]];
- monthly housing summary;
- occupancy report;
- maintenance report;
- financial report;
- incident report;
- annual housing report;
- mission alignment review.

---

# 16. Conflicts of Interest

Housing creates significant conflict risks.

Conflicts may involve:

- board members applying for housing;
- family members seeking housing;
- church referrals;
- contractor relationships;
- property purchases;
- rent relief;
- staff housing;
- donor influence.

All conflicts must be disclosed and managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 17. Complaints

The housing ministry must provide a complaint process.

Complaints may involve:

- maintenance;
- neighbor conflict;
- staff conduct;
- discrimination;
- safety;
- privacy;
- lease concerns;
- spiritual care concerns;
- community expectations.

Complaints should be documented, reviewed, and escalated where necessary.

---

# 18. Mission Alignment Review

Housing ministry shall undergo regular Mission Alignment Review.

Review should include:

- resident outcomes;
- church connection;
- chaplaincy role;
- financial sustainability;
- property stewardship;
- legal compliance;
- conflicts;
- risk;
- reporting;
- community life;
- mission clarity.

---

# 19. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing development and at least every three years once housing governance is stable.
