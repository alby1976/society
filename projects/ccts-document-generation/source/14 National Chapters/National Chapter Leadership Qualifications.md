# National Chapter Leadership Qualifications

## 1. Purpose

This document defines qualification standards for leaders serving in a National Chapter of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Leadership qualifications protect the Society's mission, doctrine, people, ministries, finances, and public witness.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

National Chapter leaders must be qualified for the responsibilities they carry.

A person should not be appointed merely because they are available, passionate, generous, influential, related to a founder, or eager to lead.

Leadership should be entrusted to people who can steward mission with humility, competence, accountability, and perseverance.

---

# 3. Scope

This standard applies to:

- National Chapter Board members;
- National Executive Directors;
- senior staff;
- committee chairs;
- ministry directors;
- chapter formation team leaders;
- officers;
- persons representing the chapter publicly;
- persons exercising significant delegated authority.

Specific roles may require additional qualifications.

---

# 4. Core Qualifications

National Chapter leaders should demonstrate:

- commitment to the Society's mission;
- respect for the Statement of Faith where required;
- understanding of the Society/church distinction;
- willingness to follow international governance;
- ability to maintain confidentiality;
- freedom from unmanaged conflicts of interest;
- good judgment;
- reliability;
- humility;
- ability to read and respond to reports;
- willingness to be corrected;
- respect for local churches;
- commitment to lawful conduct.

---

# 5. Mission Alignment

Leaders must understand and support the Society's mission.

They should be able to explain:

- why the Society exists;
- how it supports local churches;
- what it must not do;
- why doctrine matters;
- why reporting matters;
- why reserved powers exist;
- why local adaptation has limits.

---

# 6. Governance Competence

Leaders should understand basic governance responsibilities.

This includes:

- authority limits;
- board responsibilities;
- conflict disclosure;
- financial oversight;
- risk oversight;
- reporting duties;
- decision records;
- document registry;
- confidentiality;
- legal compliance.

A leader who refuses governance should not be entrusted with governance.

---

# 7. Character and Conduct

Leaders should be marked by:

- honesty;
- patience;
- self-control;
- fairness;
- courage;
- compassion;
- teachability;
- discretion;
- diligence;
- respect for others;
- avoidance of manipulation;
- willingness to serve.

Patterns of harshness, secrecy, pride, volatility, greed, manipulation, or refusal of accountability should disqualify or delay leadership.

---

# 8. Church-Supporting Posture

Leaders should respect faithful local churches.

They must not use Society structures to replace, undermine, or control local churches.

They should encourage proper church relationships, church advisory input, and local church referral where appropriate.

---

# 9. Financial Integrity

Leaders with financial authority must demonstrate financial integrity.

They should:

- follow expense policies;
- disclose conflicts;
- avoid self-dealing;
- respect donor restrictions;
- report financial concerns;
- protect restricted funds;
- avoid hidden arrangements;
- support audit or review processes.

---

# 10. Reporting Discipline

Leaders should submit and review required reports.

Failure to report may indicate:

- role overload;
- lack of skill;
- avoidance;
- weak systems;
- mission drift;
- governance resistance.

The Society should support leaders where possible, but repeated refusal to report is a serious concern.

---

# 11. Workload, Prayer, and Family Responsibilities

Leaders should maintain a sustainable workload and proper attention to spiritual and family responsibilities where applicable.

One qualification for leadership is whether the leader is spending appropriate time with God and family.

If a leader's workload prevents this, the first response should ordinarily be workload reduction, support, role clarification, or rest.

If the problem does not improve, removal or suspension from leadership may be recommended until circumstances change.

This is not punishment. It is protection for the person, their household, and the Society.

---

# 12. Disqualifying Concerns

A person may be disqualified or delayed from leadership due to:

- doctrinal opposition to the Society;
- mission opposition;
- unresolved serious conflict of interest;
- financial misconduct;
- breach of confidentiality;
- refusal of accountability;
- abusive or coercive conduct;
- serious legal concern;
- pattern of dishonesty;
- failure to report;
- inability to fulfill role;
- leadership burnout not addressed;
- undermining local churches;
- unresolved safeguarding concern.

---

# 13. Formation Team Leaders

Formation team leaders should be especially careful to avoid premature authority claims.

They should understand:

- formation team authority is limited;
- recognition has not yet been granted;
- public communications must be careful;
- legal commitments require approval;
- fundraising may require approval;
- local enthusiasm does not equal chapter recognition.

---

# 14. Review

Leaders should be reviewed regularly.

Review may include:

- annual self-assessment;
- board review;
- executive review;
- mission alignment review;
- conflict disclosure renewal;
- reporting review;
- workload review;
- spiritual and family health review where appropriate;
- role fit review.

---

# 15. Removal or Suspension

Leaders may be removed or suspended according to law, bylaws, and policy when they no longer meet qualification standards.

Removal should be documented, confidential, fair, and aimed at protecting mission and people.

---

# 16. Review of This Standard

This standard should be reviewed annually during international formation and at least every three years once chapter leadership structures are stable.
