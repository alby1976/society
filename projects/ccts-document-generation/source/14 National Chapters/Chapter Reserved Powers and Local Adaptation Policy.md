# Chapter Reserved Powers and Local Adaptation Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how reserved powers and local adaptations apply to National Chapters of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

It protects one international mission while allowing lawful and wise implementation in different countries.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society shall distinguish between what must remain common and what may be adapted locally.

Not every local difference is mission drift. Not every local preference is contextual wisdom. This policy exists to tell the difference.

---

# 3. Reserved Powers

The International Society may reserve powers over matters including:

- doctrine;
- mission;
- use of name and identity;
- international constitution;
- creation or recognition of chapters;
- amendment of core governance documents;
- dissolution or derecognition of chapters;
- major ministry categories;
- theological standards;
- church advisory structures;
- international reporting standards;
- mission alignment review;
- major cross-border matters;
- major assets or liabilities where applicable.

Reserved powers should be listed in [[Reserved Powers Framework]] and [[Authority Matrix]].

---

# 4. Delegated Local Authority

National Chapters may receive delegated authority over:

- local operations;
- local staff or volunteers;
- ministry implementation;
- local budgeting within limits;
- local church relationships;
- local reporting;
- national compliance;
- local fundraising within policy;
- local communications;
- country-specific policies;
- routine contracts;
- ministry schedules.

Delegation must be written and reviewable.

---

# 5. Local Adaptation

Local adaptation may be permitted for:

- legal structure;
- terminology;
- filing requirements;
- board composition where law requires;
- language;
- currency;
- employment rules;
- privacy and safeguarding requirements;
- housing law;
- cooperative law;
- grant-making rules;
- cultural communication;
- church relationship structures.

Adaptation must preserve mission, doctrine, governance accountability, and reporting.

---

# 6. Adaptation Request

A National Chapter should submit an adaptation request when local practice differs materially from international standards.

The request should include:

- document or policy affected;
- proposed adaptation;
- legal or contextual reason;
- risks;
- effect on mission;
- effect on governance;
- effect on reporting;
- whether legal advice was obtained;
- proposed review date.

---

# 7. Automatic Local Details

Some details may be localized without formal approval if they do not affect doctrine, mission, reserved powers, legal risk, reporting, or governance authority.

Examples:

- local address;
- local spelling;
- local phone format;
- local meeting location;
- local holiday schedule;
- local language version approved for accuracy;
- local administrative contacts.

---

# 8. Adaptations Requiring Approval

Approval is required for adaptations affecting:

- doctrine;
- mission statement;
- governance authority;
- board structure;
- reserved powers;
- grant eligibility;
- membership standards;
- church recognition;
- chaplain qualifications;
- housing eligibility;
- worker cooperative structure;
- financial controls;
- legal liability;
- public identity;
- reporting requirements;
- conflicts of interest;
- safeguarding.

---

# 9. Legal Requirement Conflicts

If local law conflicts with a Society policy, the chapter must notify the International Society.

The response may include:

- local legal adaptation;
- policy revision;
- restricted operation;
- alternative legal form;
- pause on ministry;
- legal opinion;
- International Board decision.

The Society should not force chapters to violate local law. It also should not allow law to become an excuse for avoidable mission drift.

---

# 10. Documentation

All approved adaptations should be documented.

Records should include:

- adaptation request;
- approval decision;
- conditions;
- effective date;
- review date;
- related documents;
- legal notes;
- responsible person.

Adaptations should be tracked in the Document Registry or local adaptation register.

---

# 11. Review

Approved adaptations should be reviewed at least annually during launch and at least every three years once stable.

Review should ask:

- Is the adaptation still needed?
- Does it remain lawful?
- Does it remain mission-aligned?
- Has risk changed?
- Should international policy be updated?
- Should the adaptation be removed?

---

# 12. Translation

Translations of governance documents are local adaptations requiring accuracy control.

Translation process should include:

- source document version;
- translator;
- reviewer;
- theological review where needed;
- legal review where needed;
- approval;
- date;
- revision tracking.

A translated document should not quietly become a different document.

---

# 13. Public Communications

National Chapters may adapt public communications to local language and culture, but must preserve Society identity.

Public communications may not alter doctrine, mission, or reserved commitments without approval.

---

# 14. Mission Alignment Review

Local adaptations should be reviewed through Mission Alignment Review.

Repeated adaptations may indicate that a policy needs revision or that a chapter is drifting from the shared mission.

---

# 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during international formation and whenever a significant local adaptation is approved.
