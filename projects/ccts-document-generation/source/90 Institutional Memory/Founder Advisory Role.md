# Founder Advisory Role

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how founders may continue to serve the Christian Community Transformation Society after the Society moves beyond its founding stage.

The purpose is to preserve founding vision, theological clarity, historical memory, and hard-earned lessons while preventing unhealthy founder dependency or informal control.

This policy should be read with [[Stewardship Advisors Charter]], [[Institutional Memory Policy]], [[International Board Charter]], and [[Succession Planning Framework]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Founders may be honored, heard, and learned from, but the Society must not be governed permanently by founder personality.

The Society's mission must be preserved through Scripture, doctrine, constitution, policies, institutional memory, prayer, and qualified leadership — not merely through one person remembering everything.

Founder memory is valuable. Founder control can become dangerous. The trick is knowing the difference before the organization needs a fire extinguisher.

---

# 3. Nature of the Founder Role

The founder role is historical and advisory unless the founder also holds a formal office.

A founder may serve as:

- board member;
- officer;
- Stewardship Advisor;
- consultant;
- donor;
- volunteer;
- historian;
- mentor;
- ordinary member.

The founder has only the authority attached to the role actually held.

---

# 4. Founder Contributions

Founders may contribute by:

- explaining original vision;
- preserving theological and mission commitments;
- identifying early decisions and reasons;
- mentoring new leaders;
- warning against mission drift;
- assisting with institutional memory;
- participating in anniversary reviews;
- supporting prayer and spiritual continuity;
- advising on new national chapters;
- reviewing governance history.

---

# 5. Limits

A founder shall not, by founder status alone:

- veto board decisions;
- direct staff;
- control budgets;
- approve policies;
- appoint directors;
- remove directors;
- bind the Society;
- override national chapters;
- access confidential records;
- decide disputes;
- speak for the Society without authorization;
- create informal authority outside governance.

Founder status is not a magic key. It is a historical fact.

---

# 6. Transition from Founder-Led to Governance-Led

The Society should intentionally move from founder-led development to governance-led continuity.

This transition should include:

1. written mission documents;
2. adopted Statement of Faith;
3. International Constitution;
4. governance framework;
5. board charter;
6. document registry;
7. decision register;
8. stewardship advisors;
9. reporting system;
10. succession planning;
11. founder interviews;
12. archived founding documents.

This ensures the Society can remain faithful when founders are no longer active.

---

# 7. Founder as Stewardship Advisor

A founder may be appointed as a Stewardship Advisor if qualified and willing to serve within advisory boundaries.

The appointment should define:

- term;
- scope;
- confidentiality;
- expectations;
- limits;
- reporting relationship;
- review date.

The founder should affirm that advisory counsel does not override board governance.

---

# 8. Founder as Board Member

A founder may serve on the Board if elected or appointed according to ordinary governance rules and qualifications.

A founder serving as board member has the same duties and limits as other board members.

The founder should not receive extra voting power unless explicitly provided by governing documents, and such special powers should be avoided or time-limited.

---

# 9. Founder as Staff or Officer

A founder may serve as staff or officer if appointed through proper process.

The founder's executive authority must be defined by job description, board delegation, budget authority, and reporting requirements.

The board should avoid unclear founder-executive arrangements.

The sentence "everyone knows what I mean" is not a governance document.

---

# 10. Founder Conflict of Interest

Founders may have conflicts of interest involving:

- personal reputation;
- prior decisions;
- business interests;
- family involvement;
- intellectual property;
- donations;
- property;
- employment;
- relationships with churches;
- preferred ministries.

Founders must disclose conflicts like any other leader.

---

# 11. Founder Disagreement

A founder may disagree with the Board.

Disagreement should be handled through proper channels:

- advisory memo;
- board consultation;
- stewardship advisor report;
- mission alignment concern;
- appeal process if applicable;
- written historical note.

The founder should not attempt to mobilize informal pressure, donors, members, or national chapters to bypass governance.

---

# 12. Founder Interviews and Historical Records

The Society should preserve founder knowledge through:

- written reflections;
- recorded interviews;
- decision histories;
- founding roadmap notes;
- theological rationale;
- mission drift warnings;
- prayer history;
- early mistakes and lessons learned.

These records should be archived and cross-linked in the institutional memory system.

---

# 13. Founder Succession Questions

The Society should periodically ask:

- What knowledge exists only in the founder's mind?
- What decisions are not yet documented?
- What risks did the founder identify?
- What values are assumed but unwritten?
- What relationships depend on the founder?
- What tasks stop if the founder steps away?
- What documents must be completed for continuity?

If the answer to too many questions is "ask the founder," the Society has homework.

---

# 14. Founder Recognition

The Society may honor founders through:

- historical acknowledgments;
- anniversary events;
- archive collections;
- naming of founding documents;
- prayer and thanksgiving;
- stewardship advisor appointment;
- recorded testimony;
- public recognition where appropriate.

Recognition should encourage gratitude without creating personality culture.

---

# 15. Removal from Advisory Role

A founder may be removed from an advisory role for the same reasons as any advisor, including:

- breach of confidentiality;
- serious misconduct;
- mission misalignment;
- misuse of influence;
- refusal to respect governance;
- unmanaged conflict of interest;
- divisive conduct.

Removal should be handled carefully but not avoided merely because the person is a founder.

---

# 16. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during the founding stage and whenever the founder's role changes materially.
