# Vision Statement

## Vision

The vision of the Christian Community Transformation Society is:

> Communities transformed by the Gospel as faithful churches equip believers to glorify God in every area of life throughout the week.

## Vision Explanation

The Society longs to see communities where Christian worship is not isolated from daily life. Lord's Day worship should shape work, housing, mercy, stewardship, leadership, education, family life, and service.

This vision is international in scope. It may begin in one place, but it is not limited to one nation, culture, or city.

## Long-Term Hope

The Society seeks to help build communities where local churches are strengthened rather than displaced, Christian workers are trained and supported, vulnerable people receive wise and compassionate care, housing becomes a place of stability and stewardship, work becomes meaningful and accountable, resources are used for mission rather than self-preservation, prayer accompanies planning, governance protects theological identity, and future leaders inherit a clear mission.

## Community Transformation

The Society understands transformation as Gospel-shaped renewal, not utopian social engineering.

Transformation includes people reconciled to God through Christ, believers growing in holiness and service, churches strengthened in mission, families and households supported, workers equipped for faithful labor, communities served with mercy and truth, institutions governed with integrity, and resources stewarded for God's glory.

## International Vision

The Society may develop national chapters in places such as Canada, Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia, the United Kingdom, the United States, and other countries as God provides opportunity.

Each chapter should express the same mission in its own setting while remaining under the international Society's doctrine and governance standards.

## Multi-Generational Vision

The Society should be built for faithfulness beyond its founders.

Its governance system should preserve theological clarity, mission continuity, institutional memory, leadership succession, financial stewardship, church relationships, reporting discipline, historical records, and humility before God.

## Relationship to Mission

The mission describes what the Society does. The vision describes what the Society longs to see God produce through faithful service.

The Society serves; God gives the growth.
