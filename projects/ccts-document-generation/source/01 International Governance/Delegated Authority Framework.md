# Delegated Authority Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines how authority is delegated within the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Delegation allows the Society to function across countries, ministries, and affiliated organizations without requiring the International Board to make every operational decision.

Delegated authority must remain accountable, documented, limited, reviewable, and consistent with the [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], and [[Reserved Powers Framework]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Authority may be delegated, but responsibility remains stewarded.

Delegation is not abdication. The International Board may delegate operational authority, but it remains responsible for protecting doctrine, mission, constitution, reserved powers, and international accountability.

---

# 3. Why Delegation Is Necessary

The Society's international structure requires delegation because:

- national chapters must comply with local law;
- ministries require timely operational decisions;
- staff and volunteers need clarity;
- boards should govern rather than micromanage;
- local leaders understand local context;
- international expansion cannot function through constant central approval.

Delegation is an act of trust, but trust with structure. Trust without structure is just optimism wearing a nice hat.

---

# 4. Delegation Requirements

Delegated authority must be:

1. **Written** — authority should be documented in a policy, charter, job description, board resolution, agreement, or approved framework.
2. **Limited** — the scope of authority must be clear.
3. **Accountable** — the delegate must report to an approving authority.
4. **Reviewable** — authority may be reviewed at defined intervals.
5. **Revocable** — authority may be withdrawn when necessary.
6. **Mission-aligned** — delegated actions must remain consistent with doctrine and mission.
7. **Lawful** — delegation must comply with applicable law.
8. **Proportionate** — authority should match the responsibility and risk involved.

---

# 5. Delegating Authorities

Authority may be delegated by:

- International Constitution;
- International Board;
- National Board;
- approved board committee;
- authorized officer;
- ministry charter;
- affiliated organization agreement;
- approved policy.

No person or body may delegate authority they do not possess.

---

# 6. Delegated Recipients

Authority may be delegated to:

- national chapters;
- national boards;
- international officers;
- national executive directors;
- board committees;
- councils;
- ministry leaders;
- chaplains;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- affiliated organizations;
- project teams;
- professional advisors.

The level of delegation should match the recipient's role, competence, accountability, and legal authority.

---

# 7. Non-Delegable Matters

The following matters are not delegable unless the [[International Constitution]] or International Board expressly provides otherwise:

- amendment of the Statement of Faith;
- amendment of the International Constitution;
- alteration of mission or vision;
- recognition or dissolution of national chapters;
- final resolution of international mission alignment disputes;
- approval of major international affiliated organizations;
- dissolution or merger of the International Society;
- transfer of mission-critical international assets;
- change of the Society's theological identity.

These are reserved powers.

---

# 8. Delegation to National Chapters

National chapters may receive authority to:

- incorporate locally;
- adopt local bylaws consistent with international standards;
- manage local operations;
- hire local staff;
- recruit and manage volunteers;
- operate local ministries;
- manage local budgets;
- maintain local church relationships;
- comply with local law;
- produce local reports;
- adapt procedures to local context;
- recommend local partnerships.

National chapters may not use delegated authority to alter doctrine, mission, constitutional identity, or reserved powers.

---

# 9. Delegation to Affiliated Organizations

Affiliated organizations may receive authority to carry out specific mission functions.

Examples:

- a housing trust may manage property and leases;
- a worker cooperative may operate a business enterprise;
- a church support foundation may administer grants;
- a chaplaincy ministry may coordinate spiritual care;
- a training body may deliver approved education.

Each affiliated organization must have a written agreement or charter defining:

- purpose;
- authority;
- limits;
- reporting obligations;
- financial controls;
- mission alignment requirements;
- board or management structure;
- review process;
- dispute process;
- termination or restructuring terms.

---

# 10. Delegation to Officers

Officers may receive authority to implement strategy, manage operations, supervise staff, execute budgets, sign documents, represent the Society, and make decisions within approved limits.

Officer authority should be defined by:

- job description;
- employment or service agreement;
- board policy;
- signing authority policy;
- budget authority policy;
- reporting requirements.

Officers may not create authority for themselves by habit. "We've always done it this way" is not a delegation instrument.

---

# 11. Delegation to Committees

Committees may be advisory or decision-making.

A committee charter must state whether the committee:

- advises only;
- recommends action;
- makes decisions within delegated authority;
- monitors compliance;
- reviews reports;
- conducts investigations;
- approves defined matters.

Committees must report to the board or authority that created them.

---

# 12. Delegation to Ministry Leaders

Ministry leaders may receive authority to manage approved ministries.

Their authority may include:

- planning ministry activities;
- supervising volunteers;
- using approved budgets;
- reporting needs and risks;
- coordinating with churches;
- implementing approved procedures.

Ministry leaders may not launch materially new ministries, create major legal obligations, or change mission without approval.

---

# 13. Delegation to Chaplains

Chaplains may receive authority to provide spiritual care within defined limits.

Chaplaincy authority must respect:

- local church authority;
- confidentiality rules;
- safeguarding rules;
- referral requirements;
- doctrinal standards;
- crisis escalation procedures.

A chaplain does not become a pastor of the Society merely by offering spiritual care.

---

# 14. Delegation Controls

Delegation should be controlled through:

- written charters;
- job descriptions;
- budget limits;
- signing limits;
- reporting schedules;
- review cycles;
- conflict of interest rules;
- mission alignment review;
- supervision;
- audit or financial review;
- board minutes;
- decision register entries.

Controls should be strong enough to protect the mission but not so heavy that faithful work becomes impossible.

---

# 15. Delegation Review

Delegated authority should be reviewed:

- annually for standing delegations;
- at the end of a project;
- when leadership changes;
- when a chapter is reviewed;
- after a major incident;
- when a ministry expands;
- when financial thresholds are reached;
- when mission alignment concerns arise.

---

# 16. Revocation of Delegated Authority

Delegated authority may be revoked when:

- authority is exceeded;
- reports are not submitted;
- mission alignment is compromised;
- law or policy is violated;
- financial controls fail;
- serious risk emerges;
- trust is materially damaged;
- the delegation is no longer needed.

Revocation should be documented and communicated clearly.

---

# 17. Temporary Delegation

Temporary delegation may be used for:

- projects;
- emergencies;
- interim leadership;
- pilot ministries;
- chapter launch work;
- short-term assignments;
- crisis response.

Temporary delegation should include an end date or review date.

---

# 18. Emergency Delegation

Emergency delegation may be necessary when rapid action is required to protect people, assets, legal standing, mission, or reputation.

Emergency action should be:

- limited to the emergency;
- documented promptly;
- reported to the proper authority;
- reviewed after the fact;
- recorded if significant.

---

# 19. Delegation and Reporting

Every significant delegation should have reporting requirements.

Reporting may include:

- 5-15 reports;
- monthly summaries;
- budget reports;
- risk reports;
- incident reports;
- annual reports;
- mission alignment reports.

Reporting turns delegated authority into accountable authority.

---

---

# 21. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually and whenever the Society changes its international governance structure, recognizes a new national chapter, creates a major affiliated organization, or revises reserved powers.
