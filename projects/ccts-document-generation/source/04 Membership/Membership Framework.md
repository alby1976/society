# Membership Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the purpose, structure, principles, and governance logic for membership in the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Membership in the Society is organizational and missional. It is not church membership, does not replace church membership, and does not place the Society in the role of the local church.

This framework should be read with [[Definitions of Key Terms]], [[International Constitution]], [[Mission Statement]], [[Core Values]], [[Membership Categories]], [[Membership Covenant]], and [[Member Discipline Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Membership exists to support the Society's mission, preserve accountability, cultivate prayerful participation, and identify those who share responsibility for the Society's long-term faithfulness.

Membership must not become:

- a substitute for church membership;
- a path to personal control;
- a fundraising list with better stationery;
- a way to blur theological boundaries;
- a mechanism for mission drift.

The Society's members support the mission. They do not redefine it.

---

# 3. Nature of Membership

Membership in the Society is a voluntary organizational relationship governed by the Society's constitution, bylaws, membership policies, and mission.

Members may participate through:

- prayer;
- voting, where applicable;
- service;
- giving;
- counsel;
- local chapter support;
- ministry involvement;
- governance participation;
- institutional memory;
- mission advocacy.

Membership is not a sacramental, ecclesiastical, or pastoral relationship.

---

# 4. Relationship to Local Church Membership

The Society encourages members to belong to faithful local churches where reasonably possible.

The Society shall not present membership in the Society as a replacement for:

- profession of faith in a church;
- church membership;
- Lord's Day worship;
- pastoral oversight;
- elder care;
- church discipline;
- sacraments.

A person may be deeply involved in the Society and still need the ordinary ministry of the local church. The Society is not the bride of Christ; the Church is. That distinction is not decorative. It is load-bearing.

---

# 5. Membership and Doctrine

The Society's membership standards shall preserve theological and mission alignment.

Different membership classes may require different levels of doctrinal affirmation.

For example:

- voting members may need to affirm the Society's Statement of Faith;
- supporting members may need to affirm the mission and agree not to oppose the doctrine;
- board members and theological officers may require fuller confessional subscription;
- church partners may be reviewed under church partnership standards;
- volunteers may be required to respect the Society's mission and conduct policies.

No membership class may be used to undermine the Society's theological identity.

---

# 6. Membership and International Structure

Because the Society is international, membership may exist at multiple levels:

- international membership;
- national chapter membership;
- supporting membership;
- voting membership;
- church partner relationship;
- honorary membership;
- volunteer participation.

National chapters may maintain local membership records and processes under delegated authority, subject to international standards.

Local law may require specific membership structures. Where local law differs from this framework, the national chapter must document the legal requirement and preserve the Society's mission and doctrine as fully as lawful.

---

# 7. Membership Classes

Membership classes shall be defined in [[Membership Categories]].

The Society may establish classes such as:

- Founding Members;
- Voting Members;
- Supporting Members;
- National Chapter Members;
- Church Partner Members or Partners;
- Honorary Members;
- Associate Members;
- Volunteer Participants.

Each class should define:

- eligibility;
- rights;
- responsibilities;
- voting status;
- doctrinal requirements;
- application process;
- review process;
- termination process;
- relationship to national chapters.

---

# 8. Membership Rights

Membership rights may include:

- notice of meetings;
- voting rights if assigned;
- eligibility for certain offices;
- access to reports;
- participation in consultation;
- participation in prayer and service;
- ability to submit concerns;
- ability to recommend ministry opportunities;
- ability to receive official communications.

Rights vary by membership class and applicable law.

No member has a personal ownership interest in Society assets.

---

# 9. Membership Responsibilities

Members should support the Society by:

- praying for the Society;
- upholding its mission;
- respecting its doctrine;
- supporting faithful local churches;
- acting with integrity;
- avoiding conflicts of interest;
- protecting confidential information;
- serving where called and able;
- participating in reporting when required;
- avoiding conduct that harms the Society's witness.

Membership is not meant to be passive spectatorship. At minimum, members carry moral responsibility to care about the Society's faithfulness.

---

# 10. Admission to Membership

The Society should admit members through a clear process.

The process may include:

1. application;
2. affirmation of mission;
3. doctrinal acknowledgment or affirmation according to membership class;
4. church information where appropriate;
5. conflict of interest disclosure where appropriate;
6. review by authorized body;
7. approval;
8. entry into membership registry;
9. orientation.

National chapters may implement the process locally under international standards.

---

# 11. Membership Registry

The Society shall maintain a membership registry or require national chapters to maintain registries.

The registry should include:

- member name;
- membership class;
- national chapter;
- date admitted;
- status;
- voting eligibility if applicable;
- renewal date if applicable;
- relevant acknowledgments;
- resignation or termination date;
- notes required for governance.

Membership records should be protected according to privacy and records policies.

---

# 12. Voting Membership

Voting membership shall be defined by bylaws and membership policy.

Voting rights should be structured to protect the Society's mission and not expose the Society to takeover, factional control, donor capture, or theological drift.

Voting members may participate in:

- election or confirmation of directors where applicable;
- approval of major matters where required by law;
- other decisions defined by bylaws.

Voting authority must be aligned with doctrinal and mission responsibility.

---

# 13. Supporting Membership

Supporting members may participate through prayer, giving, volunteering, advocacy, and encouragement without necessarily holding voting rights.

Supporting membership allows broad participation while preserving governance integrity.

Supporting members should still agree to respect the Society's mission and not publicly oppose its theological identity.

---

# 14. Founding Members

Founding members may be recognized for their role in establishing the Society.

Founding recognition should not create permanent control.

Founders may help preserve institutional memory, but the Society should preserve founding principles through documents, governance, and [[Stewardship Advisors]], not personality dependency.

---

# 15. Church Partners

Church partner relationships should be governed by a separate [[Church Partnership Framework]] or policy.

Church partner participation should respect the authority of local churches while clarifying that the Society remains a separate charitable or ministry organization.

---

# 16. Membership Discipline and Removal

The Society may discipline, suspend, or remove members according to [[Member Discipline Policy]].

Grounds may include:

- mission misalignment;
- serious misconduct;
- public opposition to the Society's doctrine;
- breach of confidentiality;
- conflict of interest abuse;
- financial misconduct;
- conduct damaging to the Society's witness;
- failure to meet membership requirements;
- legal or safeguarding concerns.

This is organizational discipline, not church discipline.

---

# 17. Resignation

A member may resign according to policy.

Resignation should be recorded in the membership registry.

Resignation does not automatically end obligations related to confidentiality, conflicts of interest, funds, property, reports, or unresolved misconduct.

---

# 18. Review and Renewal

Some membership classes may require periodic renewal.

Renewal may include:

- reaffirmation of mission;
- updated contact information;
- conflict disclosure;
- church information where appropriate;
- review of membership class;
- confirmation of continued participation.

Renewal should be simple enough to use and serious enough to matter.

---

# 19. Appeals

A member denied admission, suspended, or removed may have appeal rights as defined by policy.

Appeals should be fair, documented, timely, and handled by persons not improperly conflicted.

Appeals should not become endless procedural fog.

---

# 20. Membership and Reports

Members in governance, leadership, staff, volunteer, or ministry roles may be required to submit reports.

Reports may include:

- 5-15 reports;
- ministry reports;
- conflict disclosures;
- annual affirmations;
- incident reports;
- committee reports.

Reporting obligations should be proportionate to role and authority.

---

# 21. Review

This framework shall be reviewed annually during the founding period and at least every three years after membership structures are stable.
