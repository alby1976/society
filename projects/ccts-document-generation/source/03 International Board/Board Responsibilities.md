# Board Responsibilities

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the core responsibilities of the International Board of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

It supports the [[International Board Charter]] by clarifying what the Board must do, what it must avoid doing, and how it should steward the Society's theology, mission, governance, people, resources, and institutional memory.

---

# 2. Summary of Responsibilities

The International Board is responsible for:

1. theological fidelity;
2. mission protection;
3. constitutional stewardship;
4. governance oversight;
5. international strategy;
6. national chapter recognition and accountability;
7. executive appointment and evaluation;
8. financial stewardship;
9. risk oversight;
10. policy approval;
11. institutional memory;
12. prayerful leadership;
13. accountability to lawful requirements;
14. protection of vulnerable people;
15. long-term succession.

---

# 3. Theological Fidelity

The Board shall guard the Society's theological identity.

This includes:

- preserving the [[Statement of Faith]];
- ensuring the Society remains confessionally Reformed;
- preventing ministries from contradicting doctrine;
- seeking theological review when needed;
- ensuring chaplaincy standards are doctrinally sound;
- reviewing church partnerships for compatibility;
- responding to theological concerns promptly.

The Board should not treat theological oversight as decorative. Theology is load-bearing.

---

# 4. Mission Protection

The Board shall protect the Society's mission from drift.

Mission drift may arise from:

- attractive funding;
- cultural pressure;
- leadership fatigue;
- vague partnerships;
- operational busyness;
- legal complexity;
- property ownership;
- program growth;
- fear of conflict;
- founder dependency;
- weak reporting.

The Board must regularly ask whether the Society's activities still serve the mission described in [[Mission Statement]].

---

# 5. Constitutional Stewardship

The Board shall maintain the [[International Constitution]].

This includes:

- interpreting it faithfully;
- reviewing it periodically;
- recording amendments;
- ensuring national chapter documents remain compatible;
- refusing unconstitutional actions;
- preserving archives of prior versions;
- ensuring constitutional changes follow proper process.

The Constitution should not be treated as a ceremonial document. It governs.

---

# 6. Governance Oversight

The Board shall maintain a coherent governance system.

This includes:

- approving frameworks;
- approving charters;
- approving major policies;
- maintaining the [[Document Registry]];
- maintaining the [[Decision Register]];
- approving review schedules;
- ensuring authority is documented;
- ensuring delegated authority is monitored;
- correcting governance gaps.

The Board should prefer clear governance before crisis. Crisis governance is expensive, rushed, and usually brings snacks no one asked for.

---

# 7. International Strategy

The Board shall approve international strategy.

This includes:

- determining expansion priorities;
- approving entry into new countries;
- reviewing chapter readiness;
- approving international partnerships;
- assessing international risks;
- coordinating cross-border ministry;
- ensuring strategic plans remain mission-aligned.

International growth should be prayerful and governed, not impulsive.

---

# 8. National Chapter Oversight

The Board shall oversee recognition and review of national chapters.

Responsibilities include:

- approving recognition standards;
- reviewing applications;
- approving chapter launch;
- reviewing chapter reports;
- requiring corrective action when needed;
- suspending or restoring recognition;
- ensuring local bylaws remain compatible;
- protecting the Society's name and identity.

National chapters are not franchises in a business sense. They are mission-bearing expressions of one international Society.

---

# 9. Executive Oversight

The Board shall appoint, support, evaluate, and hold accountable the International Executive Director or equivalent senior officer.

This includes:

- approving role description;
- setting expectations;
- providing support;
- reviewing performance;
- approving compensation where applicable;
- reviewing reports;
- addressing concerns;
- planning succession.

The Board should not leave executives unsupported or unchecked.

---

# 10. Financial Stewardship

The Board shall oversee financial stewardship.

This includes:

- approving budgets;
- reviewing financial reports;
- approving major obligations;
- ensuring controls;
- overseeing reserves;
- ensuring donor restrictions are honored;
- approving audit or review processes where appropriate;
- monitoring financial risks;
- ensuring financial decisions serve mission.

The Board should remember that every dollar has a discipleship trail.

---

# 11. Risk Oversight

The Board shall oversee risk.

Risks include:

- theological risk;
- mission drift;
- financial risk;
- legal risk;
- employment risk;
- safeguarding risk;
- privacy risk;
- property risk;
- reputational risk;
- governance risk;
- international expansion risk.

The Board should ensure risk reports are honest, readable, and acted upon.

---

# 12. Policy Approval

The Board shall approve policies according to the authority matrix.

Policies requiring board approval may include:

- conflict of interest;
- financial controls;
- reserves;
- gift acceptance;
- records retention;
- privacy;
- reporting;
- mission alignment review;
- national chapter recognition;
- chaplaincy;
- housing trust;
- worker cooperative;
- church support foundation.

The Board may delegate drafting and implementation, but not responsibility for major governance policy.

---

# 13. Institutional Memory

The Board shall preserve institutional memory.

This includes:

- decisions;
- minutes;
- reports;
- constitutional history;
- policy history;
- founding principles;
- major disputes;
- mission alignment reviews;
- national chapter records;
- annual reports;
- stewardship advisor counsel.

The Board should ensure records are accessible to authorized future leaders. If future leaders cannot find the reasoning, they may repeat the mistake.

---

# 14. Prayerful Leadership

The Board shall lead prayerfully.

Prayer should accompany:

- board meetings;
- major decisions;
- chapter recognition;
- hiring;
- crisis response;
- theological review;
- mission alignment review;
- major property or financial decisions;
- annual planning.

The Board should not confuse prayer with delay or planning with unbelief. Faithful governance uses both prayer and prudence.

---

# 15. Accountability and Compliance

The Board shall ensure the Society complies with lawful requirements in each relevant jurisdiction.

This includes:

- incorporation;
- charity regulation;
- employment law;
- privacy law;
- financial reporting;
- property law;
- contract obligations;
- safeguarding requirements;
- tax obligations;
- local chapter law.

Compliance is not the mission, but failure to comply can harm the mission.

---

# 16. Protection of Vulnerable People

The Board shall ensure that ministries serving vulnerable people have appropriate safeguards.

This includes:

- screening;
- training;
- supervision;
- reporting;
- confidentiality;
- incident response;
- referral pathways;
- clear boundaries;
- escalation procedures.

Mercy ministry without safeguarding is not mercy. It is risk with a hymnbook.

---

# 17. Succession

The Board shall plan for succession.

Succession planning includes:

- board recruitment;
- officer succession;
- founder transition;
- stewardship advisors;
- training future leaders;
- documenting decisions;
- preserving institutional memory;
- avoiding dependency on one personality.

The Society should survive the absence of any one leader.

---

# 18. What the Board Should Not Do

The Board should not:

- micromanage routine operations;
- replace executives in daily administration;
- ignore reports;
- rubber-stamp decisions;
- treat theology as optional;
- use confidentiality to hide problems;
- permit conflicts of interest;
- approve ministries without governance capacity;
- chase funding that compromises mission;
- allow national chapters to drift quietly;
- rely on memory instead of records.

---

# 19. Annual Board Responsibility Review

Each year, the Board should review whether it has fulfilled responsibilities in:

- theology;
- mission;
- constitution;
- governance;
- finance;
- risk;
- reporting;
- executive oversight;
- national chapters;
- institutional memory;
- succession.

The review should produce action items and be recorded.
