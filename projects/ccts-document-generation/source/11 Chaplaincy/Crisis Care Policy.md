# Crisis Care Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs spiritual and practical care during crises involving persons connected to the Christian Community Transformation Society.

A crisis may involve grief, illness, displacement, housing instability, workplace conflict, serious family distress, abuse concerns, legal emergency, financial emergency, ministry failure, national chapter crisis, or other urgent need.

This policy does not replace emergency services, professional counseling, legal counsel, medical care, safeguarding procedures, or local church pastoral care.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

In crisis, the Society should pray, act wisely, protect people, respect proper authority, and avoid pretending to be what it is not.

Crisis care must be compassionate and bounded. Panic is not a policy, and heroics are not governance.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- chaplains;
- ministry leaders;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- housing personnel;
- cooperative leaders;
- foundation leaders;
- national chapter leaders;
- board members responding to crisis.

---

# 4. Crisis Categories

Crisis may include:

- death or grief;
- serious illness;
- hospitalization;
- family breakdown;
- housing emergency;
- eviction risk;
- workplace conflict;
- violence or threat;
- abuse or exploitation concern;
- safeguarding concern;
- legal emergency;
- privacy breach;
- financial emergency;
- mental or emotional distress;
- church relationship crisis;
- chaplaincy concern;
- national chapter crisis;
- ministry shutdown or failure.

---

# 5. Immediate Priorities

In crisis, the first priorities are:

1. immediate safety;
2. emergency services where needed;
3. safeguarding requirements;
4. protection of vulnerable persons;
5. prayer and calm presence;
6. notification of proper authority;
7. confidentiality;
8. documentation;
9. referral to appropriate care;
10. follow-up.

---

# 6. Limits of Society Crisis Care

Society personnel must not act beyond role or competence.

The Society does not replace:

- emergency services;
- physicians;
- mental health professionals;
- lawyers;
- police;
- child protection authorities;
- local church pastors and elders;
- licensed counselors;
- financial professionals.

The Society may support, refer, pray, accompany, and coordinate within its role.

---

# 7. Emergency Services

If immediate safety is at risk, appropriate emergency services should be contacted according to local law and circumstances.

Society personnel should not delay emergency response for internal consultation when urgent danger exists.

---

# 8. Safeguarding Concerns

Any concern involving abuse, exploitation, vulnerable persons, children, serious safety risk, or mandatory reporting must be escalated according to safeguarding policy and local law.

Spiritual care must not be used to avoid required reporting.

---

# 9. Chaplain Role in Crisis

The chaplain may:

- pray;
- listen;
- provide spiritual care;
- support family or affected persons;
- help coordinate church referral;
- advise leaders on pastoral sensitivity;
- assist with grief support;
- support staff and volunteers;
- help identify follow-up needs;
- report concerns appropriately.

The chaplain should not become the sole crisis manager unless specifically authorized and qualified.

---

# 10. Local Church Involvement

Where appropriate and authorized, crisis care should involve the person's faithful local church.

The Society should encourage connection to pastors, elders, or church leaders when the person already belongs to a church.

For unchurched persons, the chaplain may provide temporary care and, where appropriate, help connect them to a faithful local church.

---

# 11. Confidentiality in Crisis

Confidentiality remains important but may be limited in crisis.

Information may be shared when necessary for:

- safety;
- safeguarding;
- legal compliance;
- emergency response;
- proper supervision;
- board or executive decision;
- referral;
- protection of vulnerable persons.

Only necessary information should be shared.

---

# 12. Crisis Documentation

Crisis care should be documented.

Documentation may include:

- date and time;
- persons involved;
- general nature of crisis;
- immediate actions taken;
- referrals made;
- authorities notified;
- chaplain involvement;
- follow-up required;
- confidentiality level;
- person responsible for follow-up.

Avoid unnecessary sensitive details.

---

# 13. Communication

Crisis communication should be clear and limited.

The Society should identify:

- who is responsible for communication;
- who needs to know;
- what may be shared;
- what must remain confidential;
- whether legal or board review is needed;
- whether public communication is required.

No one should freelance crisis announcements.

---

# 14. Follow-Up

After a crisis, the Society should identify follow-up needs.

Follow-up may include:

- pastoral referral;
- chaplain visit;
- professional referral;
- housing support;
- staff support;
- volunteer debrief;
- board report;
- legal review;
- policy review;
- mission alignment review;
- prayer support.

---

# 15. Debrief

A crisis debrief may be appropriate.

Debrief should ask:

- What happened?
- Was anyone harmed?
- Were safety steps taken?
- Were reporting obligations met?
- Was confidentiality handled properly?
- What follow-up is needed?
- What should change?
- Does policy need revision?
- Does this reveal mission alignment or governance risk?

---

# 16. Prayer

Prayer is appropriate in crisis, but prayer must not replace action.

The Society should pray for wisdom, comfort, protection, repentance where needed, and faithful response.

---

# 17. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually with [[Spiritual Care Policy]], [[Hospital and Visitation Ministry Policy]], and safeguarding policies.
