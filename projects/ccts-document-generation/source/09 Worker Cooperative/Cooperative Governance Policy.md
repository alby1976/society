# Cooperative Governance Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes governance standards for worker cooperatives, pilot cooperatives, cooperative enterprises, and affiliated cooperative organizations connected to the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The purpose is to protect workers, mission, finances, legal compliance, and Society oversight.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A cooperative connected to the Society must have both worker participation and accountable governance.

Participation without governance becomes confusion. Governance without participation becomes control. The Society seeks responsible participation under clear authority.

---

# 3. Legal Form

Each cooperative must have a lawful structure.

Possible legal forms include:

- incorporated worker cooperative;
- nonprofit corporation;
- charitable subsidiary where lawful;
- ministry enterprise;
- limited company under Society ownership;
- partnership structure;
- pilot project within the Society;
- other lawful structure approved by the Society.

The legal form must be reviewed for employment, tax, charity, cooperative, liability, and governance implications.

---

# 4. Relationship to Society Oversight

The Society may reserve authority over:

- use of Society name and identity;
- adoption or amendment of mission statements;
- doctrinal standards;
- board appointments where applicable;
- major asset transfers;
- dissolution;
- merger;
- debt above threshold;
- major contracts;
- compensation principles;
- mission alignment review;
- creation of new cooperative branches;
- hiring or removal of senior cooperative leaders;
- legal or regulatory matters.

Reserved powers must be documented.

---

# 5. Cooperative Board or Steering Committee

Each cooperative should have a board, steering committee, or oversight group appropriate to its size and legal form.

Duties may include:

- mission oversight;
- financial oversight;
- worker participation;
- policy approval;
- manager supervision;
- legal compliance;
- risk review;
- conflict of interest review;
- reporting;
- evaluation;
- recommendation to scale, pause, or close.

---

# 6. Worker Participation

Worker participation should be clearly defined.

Participation may include:

- voting rights;
- meetings;
- consultation;
- feedback;
- committees;
- profit allocation input;
- policy review;
- safety reporting;
- training input;
- election of worker representatives.

Participation rights must be consistent with law and governance documents.

---

# 7. Management Authority

Management authority must be written.

The cooperative manager may be responsible for:

- operations;
- scheduling;
- customer relations;
- worker supervision;
- training;
- quality control;
- invoicing;
- purchasing within limits;
- safety;
- reporting;
- conflict resolution;
- implementation of board decisions.

Authority limits must be clear.

---

# 8. Financial Governance

The cooperative must maintain proper financial governance.

This includes:

- budget;
- bookkeeping;
- bank controls;
- invoicing;
- accounts receivable;
- payroll;
- taxes;
- expense approvals;
- reserves;
- financial reporting;
- audit or review where required;
- surplus or profit allocation policy;
- related-party transaction controls.

---

# 9. Worker Compensation

Compensation must comply with applicable law and approved policy.

The cooperative should define:

- wage or compensation structure;
- training compensation;
- volunteer limits;
- profit sharing where applicable;
- member distributions where lawful;
- payroll responsibilities;
- benefits if any;
- expense reimbursement.

Mission language must not be used to justify unlawful or unfair compensation.

---

# 10. Membership Governance

If cooperative membership exists, documents must define:

- eligibility;
- application;
- probation;
- approval authority;
- rights;
- duties;
- voting;
- capital contribution if any;
- surplus allocation if any;
- discipline;
- termination;
- withdrawal;
- transfer restrictions;
- relationship to Society membership.

---

# 11. Conflicts of Interest

Cooperatives carry conflict risk.

Conflicts may involve:

- board members receiving contracts;
- worker-family relationships;
- Society leaders using cooperative labour;
- pricing for insiders;
- customers connected to leaders;
- purchases from related parties;
- employment decisions involving relatives;
- housing and work overlap.

Conflicts must be disclosed, recorded, and managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 12. Contracts and Customers

Customer and contract standards should be approved.

The cooperative should review:

- contract terms;
- pricing;
- liability;
- insurance;
- deliverables;
- worker capacity;
- safety;
- payment terms;
- mission fit;
- reputational risk.

The cooperative should avoid contracts that compromise workers or mission.

---

# 13. Risk Management

The cooperative should maintain a risk register.

Risks may include:

- financial loss;
- legal compliance;
- employment classification;
- worker safety;
- customer dispute;
- quality failure;
- mission drift;
- spiritual coercion;
- dependency;
- conflict of interest;
- privacy;
- insurance;
- reputational harm.

High risks must be escalated.

---

# 14. Reporting

The cooperative shall submit reports according to Society reporting policy.

Reports may include:

- [[Worker Cooperative Manager 5-15 Report]];
- monthly cooperative summary;
- financial reports;
- worker development reports;
- risk reports;
- annual cooperative report.

Reports should be honest. Bad news reported early is governance. Bad news hidden until collapse is archaeology.

---

# 15. Mission Alignment Review

The cooperative shall undergo Mission Alignment Review.

Review should address mission, worker dignity, financial sustainability, legal compliance, church boundaries, spiritual care, governance, and risk.

---

# 16. Dissolution or Closure

The cooperative may be closed, dissolved, paused, or restructured if:

- mission alignment fails;
- finances are unsustainable;
- legal compliance cannot be maintained;
- worker safety is compromised;
- governance fails;
- leadership is unavailable;
- risks exceed capacity;
- the pilot does not justify continuation.

Closure should be orderly and documented.

---

# 17. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during cooperative development and at least every three years once operations stabilize.
