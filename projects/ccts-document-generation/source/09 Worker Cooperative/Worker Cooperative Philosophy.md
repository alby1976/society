# Worker Cooperative Philosophy

## 1. Purpose

This document explains the theological and ministry philosophy behind the Society's worker cooperative work.

The Society's cooperative vision is rooted in the biblical dignity of work, the call to stewardship, the reality of human need, and the desire to support people in meaningful participation rather than passive dependency.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Work is not merely income production. Work is a field of service before God.

The Society's cooperative ministry should help people work with dignity, learn responsibility, serve others, develop skill, and participate in community.

Work cannot save. Christ saves. But work can become one ordinary place where gratitude, discipline, love of neighbor, repentance, patience, and stewardship are practiced.

---

# 3. Creation and Work

God created humanity for meaningful work before the fall.

Work is therefore not a curse in itself, though work is now affected by sin, frustration, injustice, exploitation, scarcity, pride, and fatigue.

The cooperative should treat work as a created good needing redemption, discipline, and mercy.

---

# 4. Dignity of the Worker

Every worker is made in the image of God.

Workers should not be treated as disposable labour, charity cases, productivity units, or ministry decorations.

The cooperative should pursue:

- fair treatment;
- clear expectations;
- skill development;
- safe conditions;
- honest compensation where applicable;
- participation;
- accountability;
- respect;
- opportunity for growth.

---

# 5. Responsibility and Mercy

Christian mercy should not remove responsibility where responsibility is possible.

The cooperative should support people while also calling them to faithful participation.

Mercy asks what help is needed. Responsibility asks what duty remains. Wisdom asks how both fit together.

---

# 6. Work and Discipleship

The cooperative may support discipleship indirectly through work habits, community, service, prayer, conflict resolution, humility, and stewardship.

However, the cooperative is not a church.

It must not replace preaching, sacraments, discipline, or ordinary pastoral care.

The cooperative should encourage workers toward faithful local churches where appropriate.

---

# 7. Shared Ownership and Participation

A worker cooperative may include shared ownership, democratic participation, or worker voice, depending on legal structure.

Shared participation can help workers grow in responsibility and stewardship.

But participation must be structured. Undefined democracy can become confusion with minutes.

---

# 8. Economic Participation

The cooperative may help people participate economically through:

- employment;
- training;
- ownership;
- profit sharing where lawful;
- contract work;
- apprenticeship;
- microenterprise support;
- service delivery;
- business skill development.

Economic participation should be honest and sustainable.

---

# 9. Avoiding Exploitation

The cooperative must avoid exploiting vulnerable people under the language of mission.

Warning signs include:

- underpaying workers because the cause is good;
- using volunteers for work that should be paid;
- ignoring employment law;
- using spiritual pressure to increase productivity;
- hiding financial problems from workers;
- overpromising ownership;
- making workers dependent on leaders;
- failing to provide safe conditions.

---

# 10. Financial Realism

A cooperative must be financially realistic.

If the cooperative sells goods or services, it must price, plan, invoice, collect, account, pay, insure, and report.

Mission does not cancel arithmetic.

---

# 11. Community

The cooperative may become a community of work.

Healthy cooperative community includes:

- respect;
- shared purpose;
- honest communication;
- conflict resolution;
- accountability;
- mutual encouragement;
- prayer where voluntary;
- worker voice;
- concern for the vulnerable.

---

# 12. Chaplaincy

Chaplaincy may support workers through spiritual care, prayer, listening, and church referral.

Chaplaincy should be available but not coercive.

Workers should not feel that employment, training, membership, or advancement depends on private spiritual disclosure.

---

# 13. Church-Supporting Identity

The cooperative should support the local church by encouraging workers to connect to faithful local churches.

The cooperative should not attempt to become the primary spiritual authority in a worker's life.

The workplace may encourage faithfulness, but the church remains the ordinary place for worship, sacraments, discipline, and pastoral oversight.

---

# 14. Mission Drift

Cooperatives carry mission drift risk.

Risks include:

- becoming merely commercial;
- becoming financially reckless;
- treating workers as projects;
- allowing spiritual manipulation;
- neglecting church connection;
- confusing Society authority with church authority;
- ignoring legal obligations;
- creating insider benefit;
- allowing charismatic leaders to dominate.

Regular review is required.

---

# 15. Review

This philosophy should be reviewed annually during cooperative development and whenever the Society launches or significantly changes a cooperative.
