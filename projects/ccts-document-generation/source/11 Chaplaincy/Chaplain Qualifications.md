# Chaplain Qualifications

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the qualifications required or expected for a chaplain serving the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The chaplain provides spiritual care in sensitive ministry contexts. Qualifications are therefore necessary to protect doctrine, beneficiaries, churches, staff, volunteers, and the Society's mission.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A Society chaplain must be spiritually mature, theologically grounded, accountable, and safe.

Good intentions are not enough. A chaplain handles prayer, grief, vulnerability, conflict, spiritual questions, and sometimes crisis. That requires more than being "good with people" and owning a nice Bible.

---

# 3. Required Commitments

A chaplain must:

- affirm the Society's [[Statement of Faith]];
- support the [[Mission Statement]], [[Vision Statement]], and [[Core Values]];
- understand that the Society is not a church;
- respect faithful local church authority;
- agree not to contradict local church leadership;
- follow confidentiality and safeguarding policies;
- submit required reports;
- work within defined authority;
- participate in review and supervision.

---

# 4. Theological Training

A chaplain should have theological training from a seminary recognized by the [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]], [[Theological Oversight Council Charter|Theological Oversight Council]], or other authority designated by the International Board.

Theological training should include, where possible:

- biblical studies;
- systematic theology;
- pastoral theology;
- Reformed theology;
- ethics;
- church history;
- spiritual care;
- counseling foundations;
- practical ministry;
- ecclesiology.

The Society may define minimum credentials by role, country, and ministry risk.

---

# 5. Seminary Recognition

The seminary or theological institution should be recognized according to [[Seminary Recognition Policy]].

Recognition may be:

- full;
- provisional;
- limited;
- role-specific;
- national-context specific.

Where a qualified person lacks formal seminary credentials due to local context, the Society may require supplemental review, mentoring, training, or supervision.

Exceptions should be documented.

---

# 6. Church Standing

A chaplain should ordinarily be a member in good standing of a faithful local church.

Where applicable, the Society may request:

- church reference;
- pastoral or elder recommendation;
- confirmation of good standing;
- statement regarding suitability for spiritual care.

A chaplain under unresolved church discipline or serious spiritual scandal should not serve.

---

# 7. Character Qualifications

A chaplain should demonstrate:

- humility;
- patience;
- gentleness;
- honesty;
- self-control;
- compassion;
- courage;
- discretion;
- prayerfulness;
- teachability;
- respect for boundaries;
- willingness to refer;
- ability to listen;
- ability to keep confidences appropriately.

A chaplain should not be quarrelsome, manipulative, controlling, secretive, greedy, harsh, or careless with vulnerable people.

---

# 8. Spiritual Care Competence

A chaplain should be able to:

- pray with people wisely;
- listen carefully;
- offer biblical encouragement;
- recognize limits;
- refer to local churches;
- refer to professional help when needed;
- respond to grief and stress;
- handle conflict calmly;
- identify safeguarding concerns;
- support people without creating dependency;
- report appropriately.

---

# 9. Knowledge of Society Boundaries

A chaplain must understand the Society's boundaries.

The chaplain must know that the Society does not:

- administer sacraments;
- exercise church discipline;
- replace pastors;
- replace elders;
- replace Lord's Day worship;
- act as a church.

The chaplain must be able to explain these boundaries clearly.

---

# 10. Confidentiality and Safeguarding

A chaplain must complete required training in:

- confidentiality;
- privacy;
- safeguarding;
- mandatory reporting where applicable;
- crisis escalation;
- recordkeeping;
- boundaries;
- spiritual care reporting.

The chaplain must understand that confidentiality has limits.

---

# 11. Cultural and International Competence

In international contexts, a chaplain should be attentive to:

- language;
- culture;
- immigration stress;
- local church context;
- family systems;
- legal requirements;
- local safeguarding norms;
- local theological education context;
- trauma and displacement where relevant;
- cross-cultural communication.

The chaplain should not assume that one country's ministry habits are universal.

---

# 12. Role-Specific Qualifications

Different chaplaincy contexts may require additional qualifications.

## 12.1 Housing Chaplaincy

May require understanding of housing instability, resident privacy, family stress, community conflict, and referral pathways.

## 12.2 Worker Cooperative Chaplaincy

May require understanding of workplace stress, employment boundaries, worker dignity, conflict, and discipleship in work.

## 12.3 Foundation or Student Support Chaplaincy

May require understanding of students, ministry candidates, grant accountability, and church referral.

## 12.4 Crisis Chaplaincy

May require additional training in crisis response, trauma-informed care, and referral.

---

# 13. Appointment Review

Before appointment, the Society should review:

- doctrinal alignment;
- theological training;
- seminary recognition;
- church standing;
- character references;
- spiritual care experience;
- safeguarding suitability;
- confidentiality understanding;
- conflict of interest;
- role clarity;
- reporting capacity.

---

# 14. Continuing Requirements

A chaplain should participate in ongoing training and review.

Continuing requirements may include:

- annual doctrinal reaffirmation;
- safeguarding training renewal;
- confidentiality training;
- spiritual care supervision;
- theological continuing education;
- reporting review;
- performance review;
- church standing confirmation.

---

# 15. Disqualification or Concern

A chaplain may be disqualified or suspended for:

- rejection of the Statement of Faith;
- serious doctrinal error;
- breach of confidentiality;
- boundary violation;
- abuse or exploitation;
- unresolved church discipline;
- refusal of supervision;
- serious misconduct;
- failure to report;
- acting as a substitute pastor contrary to policy;
- unmanaged conflict of interest.

---

# 16. Review

These qualifications should be reviewed annually with [[Chaplain Policy]], [[Seminary Recognition Policy]], and [[Theological Oversight Council Charter]].
