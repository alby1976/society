# 5-15 Report Retention Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how long 5-15 reports are stored, when they are archived, when they may be deleted, and which reports should be preserved permanently.

The policy balances institutional memory, privacy, legal risk, storage burden, governance needs, and pastoral sensitivity.

## 2. Governing Principle

The Society should preserve what future leaders need and avoid hoarding what creates unnecessary risk.

Not every weekly report deserves permanent preservation. Reports documenting major decisions, crises, risks, milestones, theological concerns, or mission alignment issues may become vital institutional memory.

The goal is wise retention, not digital attic syndrome.

## 3. Default Retention Schedule

| Report Type | Retention Period | Archive Level |
|---|---:|---|
| Volunteer weekly 5-15 reports | 7 years | Operational |
| Staff weekly 5-15 reports | 7 years | Operational |
| Ministry leader weekly reports | 10 years | Leadership |
| Department director reports | 10 years | Leadership |
| Chaplain reports | 10 years minimum; longer if governance-significant | Restricted |
| Housing manager reports | 10 years minimum | Restricted |
| Worker cooperative manager reports | 10 years | Leadership |
| Foundation director reports | 10 years | Leadership |
| National Executive Director reports | Permanent summary; raw reports 10 years unless significant | Executive |
| International Executive Director reports | Permanent summary; raw reports 10 years unless significant | Executive |
| National board summaries | Permanent | Governance |
| International board summaries | Permanent | Governance |
| Quarterly summaries | Permanent | Governance |
| Annual ministry summaries | Permanent | Historical |
| Reports tied to legal, financial, safeguarding, disciplinary, or theological matters | Permanent or legal-review hold | Restricted/Permanent |

## 4. Permanent Preservation Criteria

A 5-15 report or summary should be preserved permanently if it documents major governance decision, mission alignment concern, theological issue, national chapter formation, major church partnership concern, housing acquisition or crisis, worker cooperative launch or restructuring, church support foundation grant controversy, chaplaincy crisis, legal dispute, safeguarding concern, major financial issue, executive transition, founder transition, ministry closure, major donor restriction, serious risk, important answered prayer, or historical milestone.

## 5. Raw Reports vs Summaries

The Society may retain summaries permanently while deleting routine raw weekly reports after the retention period.

```text
Weekly Raw Reports
        |
        v
Monthly Summary
        |
        v
Quarterly Governance Summary
        |
        v
Annual Historical Summary
```

The annual summary may be retained permanently even when the raw reports are later deleted.

## 6. Confidential Reports

Reports containing personal, pastoral, legal, safeguarding, employment, housing, grant, or financial information should be marked with the proper confidentiality level.

Confidential reports require restricted access. Retention does not mean general access.

## 7. Chaplain Reports

Chaplain 5-15 reports require special care.

A chaplain report should not include unnecessary sensitive pastoral details. Retention should preserve ministry activity, general spiritual care themes, referrals made, risks requiring escalation, prayer themes, and decisions needed.

Private pastoral details should be recorded only where necessary and stored under restricted access.

## 8. Housing Reports

Housing reports may include sensitive resident information.

Housing reports should distinguish between operational maintenance information, resident support concerns, lease or payment issues, community concerns, safeguarding or crisis issues, and governance-significant matters.

Resident privacy must be protected.

## 9. Legal Hold

Reports must not be deleted if they are subject to legal hold.

Legal hold may apply when there is lawsuit, investigation, complaint, audit, safeguarding concern, employment dispute, financial irregularity, property dispute, insurance claim, or regulatory inquiry.

Legal hold overrides normal retention periods.

## 10. Annual Retention Review

The Society should conduct an annual retention review.

The review should identify reports eligible for deletion, reports requiring permanent preservation, reports requiring restricted access, reports subject to legal hold, missing summaries, archive gaps, and privacy risks.

## 11. Deletion Process

Deletion should be authorized and documented.

The deletion record should include report category, date range, retention rule applied, approval authority, deletion date, and confirmation that no legal hold applies.

Deletion should be secure and irreversible where appropriate.

## 12. Archive Structure

Recommended archive structure:

```text
Reports Archive/
    Permanent/
        Governance/
        Annual Reports/
        National Chapters/
        Mission Alignment/
        Theology/
    Restricted/
        Chaplaincy/
        Housing/
        Legal/
        Safeguarding/
        Personnel/
    Operational/
        YYYY/
            Monthly/
            5-15/
```

## 13. Metadata

5-15 reports should include retention metadata:

```yaml
retention-period:
archive-level:
confidentiality:
legal-hold: false
permanent-preservation: false
review-date:
```

## 14. National Chapter Compliance

National chapters must follow this policy or an approved local policy consistent with it and local law.

Where local law requires longer retention or earlier deletion, the national chapter must document the requirement.
