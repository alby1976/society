# Foundation Governance Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes governance standards for the Church Support Foundation of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Foundation handles funds, grants, scholarships, donor restrictions, church relationships, theological education support, and ministry capacity-building. These responsibilities require clear authority, careful records, conflict safeguards, financial controls, and mission alignment review.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Foundation governance must protect generosity from becoming control, favoritism, waste, or mission drift.

Money can bless ministry. Money can also distort ministry. Therefore, the Foundation shall give with open hands and governed books.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Foundation boards or committees;
- grant committees;
- Foundation Director;
- finance officers;
- national chapter foundations;
- restricted funds;
- donor-advised recommendations where permitted;
- grant review teams;
- theological or church advisory reviewers;
- affiliated legal entities carrying out Foundation purposes.

---

# 4. Governance Structure

The Foundation shall have a written governance structure.

The structure should define:

- legal form;
- supervising board;
- grant approval authority;
- finance authority;
- donor restriction authority;
- reporting duties;
- conflict of interest process;
- theological review process;
- church advisory role;
- reserved powers;
- delegated powers;
- recordkeeping;
- audit or review process.

---

# 5. Reserved Powers

The International Society may reserve authority over:

- creation of a Foundation entity;
- use of Society identity;
- adoption of grant categories;
- changes to doctrinal grant standards;
- major restricted gifts;
- large grants above threshold;
- grants with international significance;
- grants involving theological education standards;
- dissolution or merger of Foundation entity;
- changes to Foundation mission;
- donor restrictions affecting mission.

Reserved powers shall be documented in [[Reserved Powers Framework]] and [[Authority Matrix]].

---

# 6. Delegated Authority

Delegated authority may be granted to:

- Foundation Director;
- grant committee;
- National Board;
- finance officer;
- emergency grant reviewer;
- theological reviewer;
- church advisory reviewer.

Delegations must define amount limits, grant categories, reporting duties, escalation triggers, and conflict restrictions.

---

# 7. Foundation Board or Committee

A Foundation board or committee may be responsible for:

- reviewing grant strategy;
- approving grant policies;
- reviewing grant recommendations;
- monitoring restricted funds;
- reviewing financial reports;
- managing risks;
- reviewing conflicts of interest;
- ensuring mission alignment;
- reviewing annual reports;
- recommending policy changes.

---

# 8. Grant Committee

A grant committee may review applications and make recommendations or approvals within delegated authority.

Grant committee members should have competence in:

- stewardship;
- church-supporting mission;
- basic finance;
- confidentiality;
- conflict management;
- theological sensitivity;
- grant accountability.

Committee members must disclose conflicts before reviewing applications.

---

# 9. Theological and Church Advisory Review

The Foundation may seek review from:

- [[Theological Oversight Council Charter|Theological Oversight Council]];
- [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter|Church Advisory Fellowship]];
- recognized seminary advisors;
- national church advisory teams;
- qualified external advisors.

Review should occur when grants involve doctrine, church relationships, seminary recognition, ministry training, or theological controversy.

---

# 10. Financial Controls

Foundation finances shall maintain proper controls.

Controls should include:

- fund accounting;
- restricted gift tracking;
- grant approval records;
- dual approval where appropriate;
- payment documentation;
- bank controls;
- reconciliations;
- budget review;
- audit or review where required;
- grant closure records;
- retention policy.

---

# 11. Conflict of Interest

All Foundation decisions require conflict awareness.

Conflicts may involve:

- applicants related to decision-makers;
- churches connected to reviewers;
- donors influencing awards;
- seminary affiliations;
- board members receiving benefits;
- staff recommending relatives;
- vendors connected to projects;
- personal ministry networks.

Conflicts must be disclosed, recorded, and managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 12. Donor Influence Limits

Donors may give to approved funds and may express preferences where lawful and permitted.

Donors must not control grant decisions improperly, require doctrinal compromise, direct private benefit, or bypass governance.

The Foundation must not become a donor's private grant office with a Christian letterhead.

---

# 13. Documentation

Foundation governance records should include:

- committee charters;
- meeting minutes;
- grant decisions;
- conflict disclosures;
- financial reports;
- restricted fund records;
- donor restrictions;
- legal reviews;
- theological reviews;
- church advisory reviews;
- mission alignment reviews;
- annual reports.

---

# 14. Reporting

The Foundation shall report through:

- [[Foundation Director 5-15 Report]];
- grant summaries;
- restricted fund reports;
- financial reports;
- annual foundation report;
- board reports;
- mission alignment review.

Reports should protect applicant, student, donor, church, and recipient confidentiality.

---

# 15. Mission Alignment Review

The Foundation shall undergo regular [[Mission Alignment Review]].

Foundation review should ask:

- Are grants mission-aligned?
- Are churches respected?
- Are funds stewarded well?
- Are conflicts managed?
- Are donor restrictions healthy?
- Are recipients accountable?
- Is the Foundation creating dependency or control?
- Are theological standards preserved?

---

# 16. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during Foundation launch and at least every three years once Foundation operations stabilize.
