# Lease Standards

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes standards for leases, occupancy agreements, long-term lease arrangements, resident agreements, and other housing documents used by the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The purpose is to ensure housing agreements are lawful, clear, fair, mission-aligned, and properly governed.

This document is not legal advice. Every lease or occupancy agreement must be reviewed for local law before use.

## 2. Governing Principle

Housing agreements should tell the truth clearly.

The Society should not rely on vague expectations, informal understandings, handshake arrangements, or "everyone knows what this means." In housing, unclear words eventually become conflict with a key ring.

## 3. Legal Review Required

Every lease or occupancy agreement must be reviewed by qualified local legal counsel or an approved legal review process before use.

Legal review should consider landlord-tenant law, human rights law, charity law, privacy law, property law, long-term lease rules, eviction or termination law, security deposits, maintenance obligations, religious programming, resident selection, dispute resolution, and local language requirements.

## 4. Types of Agreements

The Society may use different agreement types depending on local law and housing model.

Examples include residential lease, occupancy agreement, transitional housing agreement, long-term lease, ground lease, community land trust lease, cooperative occupancy agreement, supported housing agreement, or staff or worker housing agreement.

The correct legal form must be used.

## 5. Required Lease Elements

A housing agreement should identify:

1. parties;
2. property or unit;
3. term;
4. rent or fee;
5. payment date and method;
6. deposit if applicable;
7. utilities;
8. permitted occupants;
9. maintenance responsibilities;
10. repair reporting process;
11. community expectations;
12. privacy terms;
13. access and inspection rules;
14. guest policy;
15. safety rules;
16. spiritual care availability;
17. complaint process;
18. renewal;
19. termination;
20. applicable law;
21. signatures.

## 6. Mission Disclosure

The agreement or accompanying orientation materials should clearly state that the Society is a Christian organization with a Christian mission.

Where appropriate and lawful, documents may explain chaplaincy availability, prayer opportunities, community expectations, church referral options, Society values, and resident support practices.

Mission disclosure should be honest, not coercive.

## 7. Community Expectations

Community expectations may be incorporated into leases or provided as separate policy depending on law.

Where expectations are enforceable lease terms, they must be legally reviewed.

Expectations should be clear and related to legitimate housing, safety, property, community, or program purposes.

## 8. Long-Term Lease Standards

Where the Society uses long-term leases, including very long lease arrangements, the agreement must be carefully reviewed.

Long-term lease standards should address term length, renewal, transfer restrictions, affordability preservation, mission control, maintenance, inheritance or assignment, default, buyout or termination, property stewardship, dispute resolution, and relationship to charity law.

Long-term leases are powerful tools. They are also legal machinery with many sharp edges.

## 9. Rent and Affordability

Lease documents should state rent amount, calculation method where relevant, affordability basis, subsidy terms where applicable, adjustment process, late payment terms, hardship process, and arrears procedure.

The Society should avoid hidden rent expectations.

## 10. Maintenance and Repairs

Agreements should define resident responsibilities, Society responsibilities, emergency repair process, reporting method, response expectations, access for repairs, damage responsibilities, and inspection process.

Maintenance obligations must comply with local law.

## 11. Access to Unit

The agreement should state when and how the Society or property manager may access the unit.

Access must comply with local law and respect resident privacy.

Emergency access rules should be clear.

## 12. Spiritual Care and Religious Activities

Lease documents should not create confusion about spiritual care.

Where spiritual care is offered, documents should clarify:

- participation is voluntary unless lawful program requirements apply;
- chaplaincy is available;
- local church referrals may be offered;
- the Society is not a church;
- residents retain legal rights;
- confidentiality applies.

Any mandatory religious component requires careful legal and theological review.

## 13. Privacy and Records

Agreements should explain how resident information is collected, used, stored, shared, and retained.

Privacy terms should address application data, income data, emergency contacts, support notes, maintenance requests, rent records, reports, referrals, complaints, and legal notices.

## 14. Complaints and Disputes

Agreements should identify a complaint or dispute process.

This may include property manager contact, housing committee review, written complaint, mediation where appropriate, appeal or reconsideration, and legal remedies.

The process should not remove legal rights.

## 15. Termination and Eviction

Termination provisions must comply with local law.

The agreement should identify grounds for termination, notice requirements, non-renewal rules, serious breach process, emergency safety issues, abandonment, death, arrears, and dispute process.

The Society must handle termination with justice, clarity, and compassion.

## 16. Prohibited Lease Terms

The Society should not use terms that are unlawful, misleading, coercive, abusive, discriminatory, inconsistent with mission, impossible to administer, unclear, designed to evade tenant protections, or contrary to charity obligations.

## 17. Translation

Where residents require another language, translation or interpretation should be considered.

Translated leases must be reviewed for legal meaning and consistency.

## 18. Document Storage

Signed agreements should be stored securely.

Lease records should be retained according to records policy and local law.

Access should be limited to authorized persons.

## 19. Review

These standards should be reviewed annually during housing development and whenever local law, housing model, or lease structure changes.
