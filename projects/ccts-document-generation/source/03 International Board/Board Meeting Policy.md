# Board Meeting Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for meetings of the International Board of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Board meetings are where the Board prays, receives reports, deliberates, makes decisions, records actions, exercises oversight, and protects the Society's mission.

This policy should be read with [[International Board Charter]], [[Board Responsibilities]], [[Board Committees]], [[Decision Register]], and [[Authority Matrix]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Board meetings should be prayerful, orderly, documented, and decision-ready.

The goal is not to have long meetings. The goal is to govern faithfully.

A meeting that produces fog, fatigue, and no recorded decision is not governance. It is a group endurance sport.

---

# 3. Meeting Types

## 3.1 Regular Meetings

Regular meetings are scheduled in advance according to the governance calendar.

## 3.2 Special Meetings

Special meetings may be called to address urgent or specific matters.

## 3.3 Annual Meeting

The annual meeting should review major governance, financial, mission, reporting, and strategic matters.

## 3.4 Executive Session

Executive session may be used for confidential matters such as personnel, legal advice, conflicts of interest, investigations, or sensitive chapter concerns.

## 3.5 Emergency Meetings

Emergency meetings may be called when urgent action is needed to protect people, assets, legal standing, mission, doctrine, or reputation.

---

# 4. Meeting Frequency

During the founding stage, the International Board should meet frequently enough to establish governance, review documents, form national chapters, and oversee major decisions.

After stable governance is established, the Board should meet at least quarterly unless law or bylaws require otherwise.

The Board may meet more often during:

- national chapter launch;
- constitutional review;
- executive transition;
- financial crisis;
- theological concern;
- mission alignment review;
- major property decision;
- international expansion;
- safeguarding or legal crisis.

---

# 5. Notice

Meeting notice should include:

- date;
- time;
- location or video link;
- meeting type;
- proposed agenda;
- materials;
- items requiring decision;
- confidential items if applicable;
- expected preparation.

Notice requirements may also be governed by bylaws or local law.

---

# 6. Agenda

A board agenda should normally include:

1. opening prayer;
2. confirmation of quorum;
3. approval of agenda;
4. approval of previous minutes;
5. review of action items;
6. executive or officer report;
7. financial report;
8. risk report;
9. national chapter reports;
10. committee reports;
11. major decision items;
12. policy or document approvals;
13. mission alignment concerns;
14. confidential or executive session items;
15. new action items;
16. closing prayer.

The agenda should distinguish between:

- information items;
- discussion items;
- decision items;
- consent items;
- confidential items.

---

# 7. Board Materials

Board materials should be distributed with enough time for review.

Materials may include:

- agenda;
- minutes;
- reports;
- financial statements;
- proposed resolutions;
- policy drafts;
- risk summaries;
- chapter reports;
- committee reports;
- decision memos;
- legal or theological review summaries.

Board members are expected to review materials before the meeting. Reading the documents during the meeting is how a two-hour meeting becomes a four-hour penance.

---

# 8. Quorum

Quorum shall be defined by bylaws or governing policy.

If quorum is not present, the Board may discuss matters but should not make binding decisions unless emergency or written consent procedures allow it under governing documents and law.

---

# 9. Chair Responsibilities

The meeting chair is responsible to:

- open and guide the meeting;
- keep the Board within the agenda;
- ensure prayer and mission focus;
- clarify motions and decisions;
- manage discussion fairly;
- identify conflicts of interest;
- protect confidential matters;
- summarize decisions;
- ensure action items are clear;
- maintain order without suppressing legitimate concern.

---

# 10. Secretary Responsibilities

The secretary or assigned recorder is responsible to:

- record attendance;
- confirm quorum;
- record motions and decisions;
- record key action items;
- record conflicts disclosed;
- preserve minutes;
- update the [[Decision Register]] when required;
- ensure approved minutes are archived.

Minutes should be accurate, concise, and useful.

---

# 11. Decision-Making

Board decisions should normally be made by motion, resolution, consensus process, or other approved procedure.

Every decision should identify:

- decision made;
- approving authority;
- date;
- vote or approval method;
- responsible person;
- deadline if applicable;
- related documents;
- whether Decision Register entry is required.

Significant decisions should be entered into [[Decision Register]].

---

# 12. Consent Agenda

The Board may use a consent agenda for routine items.

Consent agenda items may include:

- routine minutes approval;
- routine reports received for information;
- minor policy updates;
- committee reports requiring no discussion.

Any board member may request that an item be removed from consent agenda for discussion.

Consent agenda should not be used to bury important decisions.

---

# 13. Executive Session

Executive session may be used for:

- executive evaluation;
- personnel matters;
- legal advice;
- conflicts of interest;
- safeguarding concerns;
- confidential financial matters;
- disciplinary matters;
- sensitive national chapter issues;
- investigations.

The Board should record that executive session occurred and any formal decision made, while protecting confidential discussion.

---

# 14. Conflicts of Interest During Meetings

At the start of each meeting, the chair should ask whether any conflicts exist for agenda items.

A conflicted board member may be required to:

- disclose the conflict;
- abstain from voting;
- leave discussion;
- leave the meeting temporarily;
- refrain from receiving confidential materials.

Conflict handling should be recorded.

---

# 15. Remote Meetings

The Board may meet by video or other electronic means if allowed by law and bylaws.

Remote meetings should still preserve:

- quorum;
- identity of participants;
- ability to hear and participate;
- confidentiality;
- recording of decisions;
- secure sharing of materials.

---

# 16. Written Decisions

The Board may make written decisions between meetings if permitted by bylaws and law.

Written decisions should be:

- circulated to all required board members;
- documented clearly;
- approved according to required threshold;
- entered into minutes or records;
- recorded in the Decision Register when significant.

Written decisions should not be used to avoid necessary discussion on complex matters.

---

# 17. Minutes

Minutes should include:

- organization name;
- meeting type;
- date and time;
- location or platform;
- attendees;
- regrets or absences;
- quorum status;
- approval of agenda;
- approval of prior minutes;
- reports received;
- motions and resolutions;
- decisions;
- conflicts disclosed;
- abstentions if relevant;
- action items;
- executive session notation;
- adjournment time.

Minutes should not attempt to transcribe every comment.

The goal is not courtroom stenography. The goal is reliable governance memory.

---

# 18. Action Items

Each action item should identify:

- task;
- responsible person;
- deadline;
- reporting method;
- related decision;
- follow-up status.

Action items should be reviewed at the next meeting.

---

# 19. Prayer

Board meetings should begin and end with prayer unless exceptional circumstances prevent it.

Major decisions should be accompanied by prayerful dependence, especially decisions involving:

- doctrine;
- national chapters;
- executive leadership;
- major finances;
- housing;
- chaplaincy;
- vulnerable people;
- conflict;
- discipline;
- expansion.

---

# 20. Meeting Records

Board records should be stored securely and retained according to records retention policy.

Board records may include:

- agendas;
- minutes;
- reports;
- resolutions;
- board packets;
- financial statements;
- legal materials;
- theological review materials;
- conflict disclosures;
- decision register entries.

---

# 21. Annual Meeting Cycle

The annual cycle should include:

- approval of annual plan;
- budget approval;
- financial review;
- executive evaluation;
- board self-evaluation;
- review of major risks;
- review of national chapters;
- review of mission alignment;
- review of key governance documents;
- review of reports and institutional memory;
- succession review;
- prayer for the coming year.

---
