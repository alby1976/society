# Worker Cooperative Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the worker cooperative ministry of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The worker cooperative exists to support meaningful work, skill development, worker dignity, shared responsibility, economic participation, community life, and mission-aligned service under the oversight of the Society.

The cooperative is not merely a business experiment. It is a ministry structure that seeks to connect work, discipleship, responsibility, stewardship, and practical service without replacing the local church.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Work is a created good and a field for Christian stewardship.

A worker cooperative connected to the Society must serve people and mission before profit, while still being financially honest and operationally competent.

A cooperative that ignores business realities will fail. A cooperative that forgets people will betray its purpose. Both are bad spreadsheets.

---

# 3. Nature of the Worker Cooperative

The worker cooperative may be:

- a pilot cooperative;
- a legally incorporated worker cooperative;
- a ministry enterprise;
- a training cooperative;
- an employment development project;
- an affiliated organization under Society oversight;
- a national chapter initiative;
- another lawful cooperative or enterprise structure approved by the Society.

The legal form may vary by country, but the mission standards remain under the Society's governance.

---

# 4. Relationship to the Society

Any cooperative created to help fulfill the Society's purpose, vision, and mission shall remain under the oversight of the Society.

Oversight may include:

- doctrinal alignment;
- mission alignment;
- reserved powers;
- board reporting;
- financial reporting;
- governance standards;
- worker protection standards;
- conflict of interest rules;
- chaplaincy boundaries;
- church-supporting identity;
- risk review.

The cooperative may have operational independence, but not mission independence.

---

# 5. Objectives

The worker cooperative may pursue the following objectives:

1. provide meaningful work;
2. develop worker skills;
3. support economic participation;
4. encourage responsibility and dignity;
5. provide practical services;
6. create a pathway for vulnerable persons into stable work;
7. support Society ministry;
8. model Christian stewardship in work;
9. support church-connected community life;
10. generate sustainable revenue where appropriate;
11. prepare future leaders;
12. reduce dependency through responsible opportunity.

---

# 6. What the Cooperative Is Not

The worker cooperative is not:

- a church;
- a replacement for Lord's Day worship;
- a substitute for pastoral care;
- a charity loophole;
- a private benefit scheme;
- a place for exploiting low-cost labour;
- a business detached from mission;
- a therapy program unless properly qualified;
- a way to avoid employment law;
- an unaccountable side project.

---

# 7. Eligible Cooperative Activities

A cooperative may operate in areas such as:

- consulting;
- technology support;
- maintenance;
- cleaning;
- food service;
- property care;
- administration;
- printing or document services;
- training services;
- community services;
- trades support;
- social enterprise activity;
- other lawful activities approved by the Society.

Activities must be reviewed for mission fit, legal compliance, safety, profitability, and worker suitability.

---

# 8. Worker Participation

Worker participation may include:

- member-workers;
- employees;
- trainees;
- volunteers where lawful;
- apprentices;
- contractors;
- supervisors;
- mentors;
- cooperative board members.

The legal status of each person must be clear.

The Society must not blur worker status to avoid employment, tax, safety, or cooperative obligations.

---

# 9. Cooperative Membership

If the cooperative uses formal membership, the membership system must define:

- eligibility;
- probationary period;
- rights;
- responsibilities;
- voting;
- capital contributions if any;
- profit allocation if any;
- discipline or removal;
- exit;
- conflict of interest;
- relationship to Society membership.

Cooperative membership is not the same as Society membership or church membership.

---

# 10. Governance

Cooperative governance must define:

- legal authority;
- board or steering committee;
- worker voting rights;
- Society oversight;
- reserved powers;
- management authority;
- financial authority;
- dispute resolution;
- reporting;
- mission alignment review;
- risk management.

Governance should be simple enough to operate and strong enough to protect the mission.

---

# 11. Financial Stewardship

A cooperative must be financially responsible.

Financial stewardship includes:

- budget;
- pricing;
- payroll or compensation;
- taxes;
- insurance;
- reserves;
- accounting;
- invoicing;
- cash flow;
- worker dividends or surplus allocation if lawful;
- conflict of interest review;
- financial reporting.

A cooperative that cannot pay its bills is not empowering workers; it is moving stress around.

---

# 12. Legal Compliance

The cooperative must comply with applicable law.

Legal review may include:

- cooperative law;
- employment law;
- tax law;
- charity law;
- nonprofit law;
- occupational health and safety;
- insurance;
- contracts;
- privacy;
- payroll;
- worker classification;
- board liability;
- charitable business activity restrictions.

---

# 13. Chaplaincy and Spiritual Care

The Society chaplain may support workers through prayer, encouragement, spiritual care, and church referral.

Chaplaincy must remain voluntary, confidential, and church-supporting.

Managers must not use spiritual care to pressure employment performance or cooperative participation.

---

# 14. Local Church Relationship

The cooperative should encourage connection to faithful local churches where appropriate.

The cooperative must not become a substitute congregation or a workplace church.

The Society/church distinction must remain clear.

---

# 15. Pilot First Principle

Before launching a full foundation or large cooperative structure, the Society may begin with a pilot cooperative.

The pilot should be:

- small;
- legally reviewed;
- mission-aligned;
- financially trackable;
- time-limited;
- evaluated;
- prayerfully governed;
- reported through 5-15 reports;
- reviewed before scaling.

Pilot first. Empire later, preferably never.

---

# 16. Reporting

The cooperative shall report according to the Society's reporting framework.

Reports may include:

- [[Worker Cooperative Manager 5-15 Report]];
- monthly cooperative summary;
- financial report;
- worker development report;
- risk register;
- mission alignment review;
- annual cooperative report.

---

# 17. Mission Alignment Review

The cooperative shall undergo regular Mission Alignment Review.

Review should ask:

- Does the cooperative serve workers and mission?
- Are workers treated justly?
- Is the business sustainable?
- Are church boundaries clear?
- Is spiritual care voluntary?
- Are profits or revenues stewarded rightly?
- Are conflicts of interest managed?
- Are risks reported?
- Is the cooperative drifting into mere business?

---

# 18. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually during pilot and launch stages and at least every three years once cooperative operations are stable.
