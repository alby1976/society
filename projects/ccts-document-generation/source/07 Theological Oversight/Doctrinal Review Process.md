# Doctrinal Review Process

## 1. Purpose

This process establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society reviews doctrinal questions, concerns, documents, teaching materials, ministry proposals, chaplaincy standards, translations, and mission alignment matters.

The purpose is to preserve fidelity to Scripture, the [[Statement of Faith]], and the Society's Reformed theological identity.

This process should be used by the [[Theological Oversight Council Charter|Theological Oversight Council]], International Board, national chapters, and authorized committees.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Doctrinal review should be careful, biblical, confessional, charitable, documented, and proportionate to the issue.

Not every question requires a formal process. Not every concern is a crisis. But real doctrinal risk should not be ignored until it becomes expensive, public, and wearing a helmet.

---

# 3. Matters Subject to Review

Doctrinal review may be required for:

- proposed amendments to the [[Statement of Faith]];
- confessional subscription policies;
- chaplaincy qualifications;
- theological teaching materials;
- devotional materials used by the Society;
- ministry curriculum;
- national chapter theological concerns;
- church partnership concerns;
- grant proposals involving doctrine or teaching;
- housing community devotional practices;
- worker cooperative discipleship materials;
- translation of doctrinal documents;
- seminary recognition;
- public statements on theological matters;
- mission alignment concerns involving doctrine.

---

# 4. Review Triggers

A doctrinal review may be triggered by:

1. request of the International Board;
2. request of a National Board;
3. request of the International Executive Director;
4. request of the Theological Oversight Council;
5. request of the Church Advisory Fellowship;
6. concern submitted by a member;
7. concern submitted by a church partner;
8. concern raised during mission alignment review;
9. proposed policy or document approval;
10. proposed international expansion;
11. concern identified in reporting.

---

# 5. Intake

A doctrinal review request should identify:

- issue or question;
- document, teaching, practice, or ministry involved;
- relevant facts;
- urgency;
- affected chapter or ministry;
- relevant governing documents;
- submitter;
- requested outcome;
- confidentiality concerns;
- conflicts of interest.

Requests should be submitted in writing unless urgency requires immediate verbal escalation.

---

# 6. Preliminary Assessment

The receiving authority should determine:

- whether the issue is theological;
- whether it is material;
- whether formal review is needed;
- whether the matter belongs to another process;
- whether immediate action is needed;
- whether confidentiality applies;
- whether conflicts exist;
- whether legal or pastoral counsel is needed.

Minor questions may be answered informally.

Major concerns should proceed to formal review.

---

# 7. Review Authority

The review may be conducted by:

- Theological Oversight Council;
- Theology and Mission Committee;
- International Board;
- authorized theological reviewer;
- joint review with Church Advisory Fellowship;
- national theological advisory body under international standards.

The International Board retains final governance authority unless a governing document provides otherwise.

---

# 8. Review Standards

A doctrinal review should evaluate consistency with:

1. Scripture;
2. [[Statement of Faith]];
3. adopted confessional standards;
4. [[Mission Statement]];
5. [[Core Values]];
6. church-supporting boundaries;
7. international governance documents;
8. prior decisions in the [[Decision Register]];
9. local church relationships;
10. mission alignment standards.

---

# 9. Evidence and Materials

Reviewers may consider:

- proposed documents;
- teaching notes;
- sermons or talks given under Society authority;
- training materials;
- public statements;
- reports;
- correspondence;
- complaints;
- church statements;
- confessional documents;
- legal or governance documents;
- translation comparisons;
- interviews.

The process should avoid gossip and anonymous accusation unless safety or serious risk requires confidential handling.

---

# 10. Consultation

The review body may consult:

- Church Advisory Fellowship;
- local church leaders;
- subject matter theologians;
- seminary faculty;
- chaplains;
- national chapter leaders;
- legal counsel if relevant;
- affected ministry leaders.

Consultation should be documented.

---

# 11. Confidentiality

Doctrinal reviews may involve sensitive matters.

Confidentiality is required where the review involves:

- personnel;
- pastoral concerns;
- church disputes;
- member discipline;
- national chapter conflict;
- legal issues;
- safeguarding concerns;
- confidential board matters.

Confidentiality must not conceal wrongdoing or prevent lawful reporting.

---

# 12. Review Outcomes

A review may conclude:

- no concern;
- minor clarification needed;
- revision required;
- further review required;
- teaching or practice should stop temporarily;
- policy amendment recommended;
- mission alignment review required;
- matter should be referred to church leadership;
- matter should be escalated to International Board;
- matter is inconsistent with Society doctrine;
- matter requires corrective action.

---

# 13. Review Report

A formal review report should include:

- issue reviewed;
- materials considered;
- review body;
- governing standards applied;
- findings;
- recommendations;
- dissent or minority view if applicable;
- confidentiality level;
- required action;
- deadline;
- responsible authority.

The report should be concise enough to use and complete enough to preserve institutional memory.

---

# 14. Board Action

Where Board action is required, the International Board may:

- accept recommendation;
- request revision;
- refer back for further review;
- require corrective action;
- suspend a practice;
- amend policy;
- initiate mission alignment review;
- reject recommendation with reasons;
- archive the matter.

Material decisions should be entered in the [[Decision Register]].

---

# 15. Corrective Action

Corrective action may include:

- document revision;
- public clarification;
- private correction;
- training;
- suspension of material;
- withdrawal of approval;
- chaplaincy review;
- partnership review;
- national chapter review;
- membership or staff process;
- mission alignment review.

Corrective action should match seriousness.

---

# 16. Appeals or Reconsideration

A person, ministry, or chapter affected by a doctrinal review may request reconsideration if policy allows.

Grounds may include:

- new evidence;
- procedural error;
- misreading of evidence;
- conflict of interest;
- unclear recommendation;
- disproportionate action.

Reconsideration should not become endless theological ping-pong.

---

# 17. Translation Review

Translations of doctrinal documents should be reviewed for faithful meaning, not merely linguistic smoothness.

Translation review should consider:

- theological terminology;
- confessional precision;
- cultural idioms;
- potential ambiguity;
- consistency with original document;
- local church usage.

A translated document should identify whether it is official, provisional, or for reference.

---

# 18. Records

Doctrinal review records should be stored according to records policy.

Records may include:

- request;
- materials reviewed;
- notes;
- consultations;
- report;
- decision;
- corrective action;
- follow-up.

Material doctrinal decisions should be linked in the [[Document Registry]] or [[Decision Register]].

---

# 19. Review

This process should be reviewed annually with [[Theological Oversight Council Charter]] and [[Mission Alignment Review]].
