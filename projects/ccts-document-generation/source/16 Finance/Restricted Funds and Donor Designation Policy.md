# Restricted Funds and Donor Designation Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs restricted funds, donor designations, board-designated funds, grant funds, and purpose-limited money held by the Society.

It applies broadly to Society finances and works alongside the more specific [[Donor Restricted Gift Policy]] for Foundation gifts.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Restricted funds must be used for their approved purpose.

The Society should not treat restricted funds as general money merely because the cash is available.

A restriction is not a suggestion unless it was clearly accepted only as a non-binding preference.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- donor-restricted gifts;
- donor-designated gifts;
- board-designated funds;
- grant funds;
- housing funds;
- Foundation funds;
- national chapter funds;
- emergency funds;
- project funds;
- ministry-specific funds;
- scholarship or student support funds.

---

# 4. Definitions

## 4.1 Restricted Fund

Money limited by donor restriction, legal requirement, grant agreement, board designation, or approved purpose.

## 4.2 Donor Designation

A donor's stated preference or designation for a gift.

## 4.3 Board-Designated Fund

Funds internally set aside by board decision for a particular purpose.

## 4.4 Unrestricted Fund

Funds available for general mission purposes according to approved budget and authority.

---

# 5. Accepting Restrictions

Before accepting a restricted gift or fund, the Society should confirm:

- purpose is mission-aligned;
- restriction is lawful;
- restriction is administratively feasible;
- restriction does not create improper private benefit;
- restriction does not compromise doctrine;
- restriction does not improperly control churches;
- restriction does not bypass governance;
- reporting can be completed.

---

# 6. Refusing Restrictions

The Society should refuse restrictions that:

- contradict mission;
- contradict doctrine;
- create unlawful discrimination;
- create improper donor control;
- benefit insiders improperly;
- are impossible to track;
- impose excessive reporting burden;
- bind future leaders unwisely;
- create legal risk;
- distort ministry priorities.

A large restricted gift can still be a bad gift.

---

# 7. Tracking Restricted Funds

Restricted funds should be tracked separately in financial records.

Tracking should include:

- fund name;
- restriction;
- source;
- date received;
- amount received;
- amount spent;
- remaining balance;
- approved uses;
- reporting obligations;
- responsible owner;
- review date.

---

# 8. Use of Restricted Funds

Before spending restricted funds, the approver should confirm:

- expense matches restriction;
- fund balance is available;
- documentation is present;
- authority is valid;
- conflict review is complete if needed;
- reporting obligations are understood.

---

# 9. Board-Designated Funds

The Board may designate funds for particular purposes.

Board-designated funds should record:

- purpose;
- amount;
- authority;
- restrictions;
- review date;
- release conditions;
- reporting requirements.

The Board may undesignate board-designated funds unless legal, donor, or grant restrictions prevent it.

---

# 10. Donor Preferences

A donor preference may be treated as non-binding only if the Society clearly retains discretion.

Donation language should make this clear before the gift is accepted.

If donor language creates a binding restriction, it must be tracked.

---

# 11. Unused Restricted Funds

Unused restricted funds should be reviewed.

Options may include:

- continued holding for approved purpose;
- approved related use;
- donor communication where appropriate;
- legal review;
- return if required or appropriate;
- board decision;
- release through lawful process.

Unused funds should not quietly disappear into general operations.

---

# 12. Reporting

Restricted fund reports should include:

- opening balance;
- income;
- expenses;
- closing balance;
- purpose;
- concerns;
- upcoming obligations;
- unused balances;
- reporting due dates.

Reports should be reviewed by the appropriate authority.

---

# 13. Conflicts of Interest

Restricted funds may create conflicts.

Examples:

- donor wants to support a relative;
- donor directs funds to a connected church;
- board member benefits from restricted purpose;
- staff member influences fund use;
- grant recipient is related to decision-maker.

Conflicts must be disclosed and managed.

---

# 14. Cross-Border Restricted Funds

Cross-border restricted funds require special review.

Review should consider:

- legal transfer rules;
- tax treatment;
- donor communication;
- recipient eligibility;
- currency;
- reporting;
- local restrictions;
- charity or nonprofit requirements;
- mission alignment.
