# Public Statements and Media Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs public statements, media responses, public announcements, newsletters, social media statements, public presentations, and other communications made on behalf of the Society.

The policy protects mission, truthfulness, confidentiality, legal compliance, church relationships, donors, and people served by the Society.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

No person should publicly speak for the Society without authority.

A sincere public statement can still be unauthorized, inaccurate, harmful, or legally risky.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- board statements;
- executive statements;
- ministry announcements;
- media responses;
- public letters;
- social media posts;
- newsletters;
- website announcements;
- donor appeals;
- church presentations;
- grant announcements;
- housing announcements;
- national chapter statements;
- crisis statements.

---

# 4. Authority to Speak

The Society should define authorized spokespersons.

Authority may vary by topic:

| Topic | Possible Authority |
|---|---|
| International governance | International Board or authorized officer |
| National Chapter matters | National Chapter Board or authorized delegate |
| Theology and doctrine | Authorized theological oversight or board-approved statement |
| Donor and fundraising matters | Authorized finance or development officer |
| Legal matters | Board-authorized spokesperson after legal review |
| Crisis or incident | Crisis communications authority |
| Ministry updates | Authorized ministry leader within approved limits |

---

# 5. Approval Requirements

Public statements should be approved when they involve:

- doctrine;
- legal matters;
- finance;
- donor appeals;
- chapter recognition;
- crisis response;
- safeguarding;
- privacy;
- public controversy;
- church relationships;
- new ministry launch;
- housing;
- grants;
- public claims about impact.

Routine approved updates may use delegated authority.

---

# 6. Statement Review Checklist

Before release, ask:

- Is the statement true?
- Is it authorized?
- Is it mission-aligned?
- Does it preserve Society/church distinction?
- Does it protect confidentiality?
- Does it avoid overpromising?
- Does it avoid implying approval not yet granted?
- Does it respect donor and grant rules?
- Does it need legal review?
- Does it need board review?
- Does it need church advisory or theological review?

---

# 7. Media Contact

Media inquiries should be directed to an authorized spokesperson.

Persons receiving media contact should:

- acknowledge receipt;
- avoid off-the-cuff statements;
- notify the proper authority;
- record the inquiry;
- preserve relevant messages;
- wait for approved response.

Do not say "no comment" without considering whether another response is wiser.

---

# 8. Social Media

Social media posts on official channels must be authorized.

Posts should not include:

- confidential information;
- unapproved photos or stories;
- identifying details of vulnerable persons;
- theological claims not approved;
- political partisanship;
- donor pressure;
- inaccurate chapter status;
- unapproved crisis comments;
- personal opinions presented as Society position.

---

# 9. Personal Accounts

People serving the Society may use personal accounts, but they must not imply they speak for the Society unless authorized.

Personal posts should not disclose confidential information or damage the Society's mission and relationships.

---

# 10. Public Presentations

Public presentations should be reviewed when they involve:

- fundraising;
- church relationships;
- theology;
- national chapter formation;
- housing;
- grants;
- vulnerable persons;
- public partnership;
- legal or financial commitments.

Presenters should use approved language and current documents.

---

# 11. Retractions and Corrections

If a public statement is inaccurate, the Society should correct it promptly and appropriately.

Corrections should identify:

- what was inaccurate;
- corrected information;
- authority approving correction;
- whether affected parties should be notified;
- whether risk register or incident process applies.

---

# 12. Recordkeeping

Public statements should be retained according to [[Records Management Policy]].

Records should include:

- statement;
- date;
- channel;
- approving authority;
- related decision;
- supporting documents;
- correction if any.

---

# 13. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and after any major public statement, media issue, crisis, or public correction.
