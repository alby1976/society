# Customer and Contract Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for customers, contracts, pricing, commitments, service delivery, and customer relationships in a Society-affiliated worker cooperative.

The cooperative must deal honestly, protect workers, preserve mission, and avoid contracts that create legal, financial, safety, or spiritual risk.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The cooperative should keep its word and know what it is promising before promising it.

A contract should not be signed because the work sounds exciting, the customer seems friendly, or the cooperative needs cash. Contracts create obligations. Obligations require capacity.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- customer agreements;
- service contracts;
- purchase orders;
- statements of work;
- informal work arrangements;
- recurring service relationships;
- consulting engagements;
- cooperative sales;
- ministry-related customers;
- related-party customers.

---

# 4. Customer Eligibility

The cooperative may serve customers who are lawful, suitable, and consistent with mission.

Customers may include:

- individuals;
- churches;
- nonprofits;
- businesses;
- Society ministries;
- housing programs;
- worker cooperative clients;
- community partners;
- public entities where lawful.

The cooperative should avoid customers or work that would compromise mission, safety, legal compliance, or worker dignity.

---

# 5. Contract Approval

Contract approval authority must be defined.

The approval process should consider:

- contract value;
- risk;
- worker capacity;
- timeline;
- insurance;
- legal review;
- pricing;
- conflict of interest;
- customer reputation;
- mission fit;
- required skills;
- safety;
- payment terms.

High-risk or high-value contracts require board or delegated senior approval.

---

# 6. Written Agreements

Work should ordinarily be documented in writing.

The agreement should define:

- parties;
- scope;
- deliverables;
- timeline;
- price;
- payment terms;
- change process;
- responsibilities;
- limitations;
- confidentiality;
- liability;
- cancellation;
- dispute process;
- acceptance criteria.

Small jobs may use simplified written confirmation.

---

# 7. Pricing

Pricing should be honest and sustainable.

Pricing should consider:

- labour;
- supervision;
- materials;
- overhead;
- insurance;
- taxes;
- equipment;
- travel;
- training time;
- risk;
- reserve needs;
- market comparison;
- mission subsidy if approved.

Underpricing may feel generous but can quietly exploit workers or bankrupt the cooperative.

---

# 8. Discounts and Subsidized Work

Discounts or subsidized work must be approved.

Discounts may be appropriate for:

- churches;
- low-income clients;
- Society ministries;
- pilot learning;
- charitable purposes;
- hardship cases.

The cooperative should document:

- reason;
- amount;
- funding source;
- approval;
- effect on worker compensation;
- mission purpose.

---

# 9. Related-Party Customers

Related-party customer relationships must be disclosed.

Examples include:

- Society leaders hiring the cooperative;
- board members' businesses;
- churches connected to decision-makers;
- family members;
- donors;
- affiliated organizations;
- staff businesses.

Related-party work must be fair, documented, and approved according to conflict policy.

---

# 10. Scope Control

Scope must be controlled.

Changes should be documented and may require:

- revised price;
- revised timeline;
- worker capacity review;
- customer approval;
- manager approval;
- contract amendment.

Scope creep is unpaid work wearing a polite smile.

---

# 11. Customer Communication

Customer communication should be handled by authorized persons.

The cooperative should communicate:

- expectations;
- delays;
- changes;
- risks;
- missing information;
- completion;
- invoice status;
- disputes.

Communication should be truthful and professional.

---

# 12. Customer Complaints

Customer complaints should be documented and reviewed.

Complaint review should consider:

- contract terms;
- work quality;
- communication;
- timeline;
- worker conduct;
- customer conduct;
- payment;
- remedy;
- learning needed.

Serious complaints should be escalated.

---

# 13. Payment Terms

Payment terms should be clear.

The cooperative should define:

- deposit if any;
- milestones;
- invoice timing;
- due date;
- late payment process;
- collections process;
- cancellation fees where lawful;
- payment methods;
- tax treatment.

The cooperative should not repeatedly accept work from customers who do not pay without review.

---

# 14. Customer Conduct

The cooperative may refuse or end work where customer conduct is unsafe, abusive, unlawful, exploitative, discriminatory, or mission-compromising.

Workers should not be required to endure abusive customers for revenue.

---

# 15. Confidentiality and Data

Contracts may involve confidential customer or Society data.

The cooperative must protect:

- customer information;
- personal information;
- business information;
- Society information;
- worker information;
- passwords or access credentials where applicable.

Data obligations should be clear before work begins.

---

# 16. Liability and Insurance

Before accepting work, the cooperative should review liability and insurance.

This may include:

- general liability;
- professional liability;
- workers compensation;
- auto;
- property;
- cyber or data risk;
- contractual indemnities;
- safety requirements.

The cooperative should not accept risks it cannot understand or insure.

---

# 17. Legal Review

Legal review may be required for:

- high-value contracts;
- unusual terms;
- indemnity;
- data processing;
- government contracts;
- cross-border work;
- employment-related services;
- construction or trades;
- long-term commitments;
- related-party arrangements.

---

# 18. Contract Records

Contract records should include:

- agreement;
- approvals;
- pricing;
- scope changes;
- customer communications;
- invoices;
- payment records;
- complaint records;
- closeout notes.

Records must be retained according to policy.

---

# 19. Reporting

Contract activity should be reported in cooperative reports.

Reports may include:

- active contracts;
- completed work;
- revenue;
- receivables;
- customer complaints;
- discounts;
- risks;
- related-party transactions;
- support needed.

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during cooperative launch and at least every three years once stable.
