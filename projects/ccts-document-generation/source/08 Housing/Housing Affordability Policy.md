# Housing Affordability Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society defines, approves, reviews, and stewards housing affordability.

The Society may provide affordable rental housing, long-term lease housing, stewardship housing, supported housing, or other lawful housing models. Each model must define affordability clearly enough to be governed, audited, reviewed, and explained.

Affordability is not a slogan. It is a policy decision with numbers, tradeoffs, maintenance costs, and people attached.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society shall pursue affordability with mercy and stewardship.

Housing should be affordable enough to serve the intended households and sustainable enough to keep serving future households.

The Society should avoid both extremes:

- charging so much that the housing no longer serves the mission;
- charging so little that the ministry becomes financially unstable and harms residents later.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- rental housing;
- long-term lease housing;
- community land trust housing;
- transitional housing;
- supported housing;
- ministry worker housing;
- worker cooperative housing;
- housing subsidies;
- rent relief;
- donor-supported affordability programs.

Local implementation must comply with applicable law.

---

# 4. Definitions

## 4.1 Affordable Housing

Housing priced according to an approved affordability standard, which may be based on income, market comparison, household need, or program category.

## 4.2 Stewardship Housing

Housing intentionally managed for mission, community stability, resident dignity, and long-term affordability where possible.

## 4.3 Subsidy

A financial reduction, grant, donor-supported contribution, or internal allocation that lowers the resident's housing cost.

## 4.4 Hardship Relief

Temporary or exceptional rent or fee relief for a resident experiencing difficulty.

## 4.5 Sustainability

The ability of the housing ministry to pay operating, maintenance, debt, reserve, insurance, and governance costs over time.

---

# 5. Approved Affordability Models

A housing program may use one or more of the following models.

## 5.1 Below-Market Rent

Rent is set below comparable local market rent.

## 5.2 Income-Based Rent

Rent is calculated as a percentage of household income.

## 5.3 Fixed Affordable Rate

Rent is set at a fixed rate approved by the housing authority and reviewed periodically.

## 5.4 Program-Based Subsidy

A subsidy is tied to a specific program, household category, or ministry purpose.

## 5.5 Donor-Supported Affordability

Restricted or designated gifts help reduce housing cost.

## 5.6 Long-Term Affordability Control

A lease, covenant, trust, or land structure preserves affordability over time.

---

# 6. Affordability Standard

Each housing program shall define its affordability standard in writing.

The standard should address:

- target residents;
- income assumptions;
- rent or lease calculation;
- subsidy method;
- review cycle;
- documentation required;
- hardship process;
- sustainability analysis;
- board approval authority;
- reporting expectations.

No housing program should operate on an undefined affordability promise.

---

# 7. Resident Income Review

Where income-based or needs-based affordability is used, the Society may require income review.

Income review should be:

- lawful;
- necessary;
- respectful;
- documented;
- confidential;
- applied consistently.

Income information must be protected as confidential resident information.

---

# 8. Rent Setting

Rent or housing fees should be approved by the delegated housing authority.

The decision should consider:

- affordability goal;
- household need;
- market comparison;
- property expenses;
- maintenance reserves;
- vacancy risk;
- debt obligations;
- insurance;
- utilities;
- donor restrictions;
- legal requirements;
- long-term sustainability.

---

# 9. Rent Changes

Rent changes must comply with local law and lease terms.

The Society should provide clear notice, reasons where appropriate, and hardship process information.

Rent changes should be reviewed for mission alignment and resident impact.

---

# 10. Hardship Relief

The Society may provide temporary hardship relief where authorized.

Hardship relief should define:

- eligibility;
- required documentation;
- maximum duration;
- approval authority;
- review schedule;
- confidentiality;
- effect on arrears;
- repayment expectations if any;
- donor or subsidy source.

Hardship decisions must be documented.

---

# 11. Subsidy Stewardship

Subsidies must be governed carefully.

The Society should track:

- source of subsidy;
- amount;
- resident or unit supported;
- duration;
- restrictions;
- approval authority;
- reporting obligations;
- renewal or expiry;
- mission purpose.

Subsidies should not create secret favoritism.

---

# 12. Donor Restrictions

Donor-restricted housing funds must be used according to lawful and approved restrictions.

The Society should not accept restrictions that:

- contradict the Society's mission;
- create unlawful discrimination;
- compromise governance;
- give donors improper control;
- are impossible to administer;
- create hidden future liabilities.

---

# 13. Financial Sustainability Review

Before approving an affordability model, the Society should review:

- operating budget;
- reserve needs;
- maintenance costs;
- capital replacement;
- insurance;
- taxes or fees;
- vacancy;
- staffing;
- property management;
- debt;
- projected subsidy gap;
- funding source.

Good intentions do not repair boilers.

---

# 14. Reporting

Housing affordability should be reported through:

- [[Housing Manager 5-15 Report]];
- monthly housing summary;
- annual housing report;
- financial reports;
- mission alignment review.

Reports may include:

- number of units;
- affordability level;
- subsidy amount;
- vacancies;
- arrears summary;
- hardship relief;
- reserve status;
- sustainability concerns.

Resident privacy must be protected.

---

# 15. Mission Alignment Review

Affordability should be reviewed through [[Mission Alignment Review]].

The review should ask:

- Does affordability serve the intended residents?
- Is the program sustainable?
- Are subsidies fair?
- Are decisions documented?
- Are residents treated with dignity?
- Are church and chaplaincy boundaries respected?
- Is affordability being used coercively?
- Are donor restrictions appropriate?

---

# 16. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
