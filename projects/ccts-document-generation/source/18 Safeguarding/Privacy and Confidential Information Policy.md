# Privacy and Confidential Information Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Society collects, uses, stores, shares, protects, and disposes of confidential and personal information.

The Society handles information about people, churches, donors, residents, workers, students, volunteers, staff, chaplaincy care, grants, finances, incidents, and legal matters. This information must be handled carefully.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should collect only what it needs, share only what is authorized, protect what it holds, and retain records only as long as required or justified.

Confidential information is not ministry currency.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- board members;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- contractors where applicable;
- chaplains;
- advisors;
- national chapters;
- ministries;
- housing operations;
- worker cooperative operations;
- Foundation grant processes;
- church advisory relationships;
- public communications;
- records systems.

Local chapters must comply with applicable privacy laws.

---

# 4. Types of Information

Confidential or personal information may include:

- contact information;
- donor information;
- financial information;
- staff and volunteer information;
- resident information;
- worker information;
- grant applicant information;
- student support information;
- spiritual care information;
- church relationship concerns;
- incident reports;
- safeguarding reports;
- legal records;
- board confidential records.

---

# 5. Information Collection

The Society should collect information only when needed for a lawful and mission-aligned purpose.

Collection should be:

- limited;
- explainable;
- authorized;
- relevant;
- accurate where possible;
- protected from unnecessary exposure.

Do not collect sensitive information merely because it might be interesting later.

---

# 6. Use of Information

Information should be used only for the purpose for which it was collected or another lawful and approved purpose.

Examples of approved uses may include:

- governance;
- service coordination;
- housing administration;
- grant review;
- worker support;
- chaplaincy support;
- reporting;
- legal compliance;
- financial reporting;
- safeguarding response;
- risk management;
- donor stewardship.

---

# 7. Access Control

Access should be role-based and need-to-know.

The Society should define:

- who may access each record category;
- who may edit records;
- who may export records;
- who may share records;
- who may delete or archive records;
- who reviews access permissions.

Convenience is not an access-control policy.

---

# 8. Confidentiality Levels

The Society may classify records as:

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Public | Approved for public release |
| Internal | For Society use but not public |
| Restricted | Limited to relevant leaders or roles |
| Confidential | Sensitive and need-to-know |
| Board Confidential | Restricted to board or delegated reviewers |
| Legal Privileged | Handled according to legal counsel direction |

---

# 9. Sharing Information

Information may be shared only when:

- authorized by role;
- required for service;
- required by law;
- required for safety;
- approved by policy;
- consented to where required;
- necessary for governance or risk response.

Sensitive information should not be shared broadly in email, chat, or informal conversation.

---

# 10. Chaplaincy and Spiritual Care Information

Spiritual care information requires special care.

Chaplaincy records should be limited, respectful, and stored according to chaplaincy policy and privacy requirements.

Chaplaincy confidentiality must not be used to hide serious safety, safeguarding, or legal concerns.

---

# 11. Donor and Grant Information

Donor and grant information should be protected.

This includes:

- donor identity;
- donation amounts;
- restricted gift information;
- grant applicant details;
- student support details;
- recipient reporting;
- conflict disclosures.

Do not use donor or applicant information for unauthorized purposes.

---

# 12. Data Storage

Approved storage locations should be defined.

Storage planning should address:

- access permissions;
- encryption where needed;
- backups;
- device security;
- shared drives;
- document vaults;
- paper records;
- national chapter records;
- cross-border access;
- retention periods.

---

# 13. Email and Messaging

Sensitive information should be minimized in email and messaging.

Where possible:

- use approved systems;
- avoid unnecessary attachments;
- avoid forwarding confidential threads;
- confirm recipients;
- use restricted links;
- summarize without excessive detail;
- store official records in the proper archive.

---

# 14. Breach Response

A privacy breach should be escalated under [[Incident Escalation Policy]].

Response may include:

- contain the breach;
- notify appropriate leader;
- preserve records;
- assess risk;
- seek legal advice;
- notify affected persons where required;
- notify regulators where required;
- update risk register;
- corrective action.

---

# 15. Retention and Disposal

Confidential records should be retained according to [[Records Management Policy]] and applicable law.

Disposal should be secure.

Do not keep sensitive information indefinitely merely because storage is cheap.

---

# 16. Training

Persons with access to confidential information should receive privacy and confidentiality training appropriate to role.

Training should be documented for higher-risk roles.

---

# 17. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and after any privacy breach or major change in record systems.
