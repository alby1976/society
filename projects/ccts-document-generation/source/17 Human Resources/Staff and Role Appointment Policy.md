# Staff and Role Appointment Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Society creates, approves, appoints, reviews, changes, and ends staff and significant service roles.

The Society should not create vague roles, invisible authority, or undocumented expectations.

## 2. Governing Principle

A role should be defined before it is filled.

People should not be asked to carry responsibilities that no one has written down, limited, supervised, funded, or reviewed.

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to staff roles, executive roles, ministry leader roles, administrative roles, chaplain roles, housing manager roles, cooperative manager roles, Foundation director roles, committee chair roles, volunteer leadership roles, advisors with continuing responsibilities, and national chapter formation roles.

## 4. Role Creation

Before creating a role, the Society should define:

- purpose;
- mission need;
- duties;
- authority;
- limits;
- reporting relationship;
- compensation or volunteer status;
- budget impact;
- legal classification;
- term;
- confidentiality level;
- qualifications;
- reporting duties;
- review process.

## 5. Approval Authority

Role approval must follow the [[Authority Matrix]].

Higher approval may be required for executive roles, roles with financial authority, roles with legal signing authority, chaplaincy roles, housing roles, Foundation roles, worker cooperative management, national chapter leadership, paid roles, and roles involving vulnerable persons.

## 6. Role Description

Every continuing role should have a role description.

A role description should include title, purpose, supervisor or reporting body, duties, authority, limits, qualifications, expected reports, confidentiality obligations, conflict of interest requirements, review schedule, and term or duration.

## 7. Candidate Review

Before appointment, the Society should review:

- qualifications;
- mission alignment;
- character and conduct;
- competence;
- conflicts of interest;
- legal eligibility;
- workload capacity;
- references where appropriate;
- confidentiality suitability;
- safeguarding suitability where relevant.

## 8. Appointment Record

An appointment record should include:

- person appointed;
- role;
- approving authority;
- date;
- term;
- compensation or volunteer status;
- authority granted;
- reporting requirements;
- conditions;
- review date;
- conflict disclosures;
- required training.

## 9. Orientation

New appointees should receive orientation.

Orientation should cover mission and values, Society/church distinction, role description, authority limits, reporting requirements, confidentiality, conflict of interest, records, safeguarding or privacy where relevant, and leadership health expectations.

## 10. Probation or Provisional Appointment

Some roles may begin provisionally.

A provisional period should define length, duties, limits, supervisor, evaluation criteria, reporting, training, and possible outcomes.

Do not call a role provisional if no one intends to review it.

## 11. Role Changes

Material changes to a role should be approved and documented.

Material changes include expanded authority, added financial authority, change in supervisor, compensation change, ministry responsibility change, confidentiality access change, and role duration change.

## 12. Performance and Role Review

Roles should be reviewed regularly.

Review should ask whether the role is needed, the person is fulfilling it, authority is clear, reporting is happening, workload is sustainable, conflicts are managed, policies are followed, training is needed, and whether the role should continue, change, or end.

## 13. Acting or Interim Roles

Acting or interim roles should be documented.

The record should include reason, person appointed, authority, limits, start date, expected end date, reporting, and review date.

Interim roles should not become permanent by neglect.

## 14. Ending a Role

A role may end through completion, resignation, termination, removal, restructuring, expiration of term, loss of qualification, legal requirement, or mission or workload concern.

Ending a role should include record handoff, access removal, final report, expense reconciliation, confidentiality reminder, and transition plan.

## 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and whenever major role structures change.
