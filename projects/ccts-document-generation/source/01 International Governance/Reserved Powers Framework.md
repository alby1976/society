# Reserved Powers Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines the powers retained by the International Society and not delegated to national chapters, affiliated organizations, officers, ministries, committees, councils, or local bodies unless expressly authorized by the [[International Constitution]] or International Board.

Reserved powers protect the Society's theological identity, mission, constitutional unity, and international continuity.

This document should be read with [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], [[Governance Principles]], [[Delegated Authority Framework]], and [[Authority Matrix]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Reserved powers exist because the Society is international by design.

National chapters need real local authority. They must comply with local law, operate ministries, hire staff, manage budgets, and serve communities. But they must not redefine the Society.

The basic distinction is:

```text
Reserved Powers:
Matters that preserve identity, doctrine, mission, constitutional unity, and international authority.

Delegated Authority:
Matters that allow chapters, officers, ministries, and affiliated organizations to operate faithfully.
```

Both are necessary. Reserved powers without delegation create paralysis. Delegation without reserved powers creates drift.

---

# 3. Authority Source

Reserved powers arise from:

- [[Statement of Faith]]
- [[Mission Statement]]
- [[Vision Statement]]
- [[Core Values]]
- [[International Constitution]]
- [[International Governance Framework]]
- International Board resolutions
- approved international policies

No national chapter, officer, ministry, council, affiliated organization, or local board may exercise a reserved power unless that power has been expressly delegated in writing.

---

# 4. Reserved Power Categories

The following categories are reserved to the International Society.

## 4.1 Doctrine

The International Society reserves authority over:

- adoption or amendment of the [[Statement of Faith]];
- interpretation of the Society's theological identity;
- approval of international confessional standards;
- approval of doctrinal standards for chaplains, theological officers, and teachers;
- final determination of doctrinal disputes involving the Society's identity;
- determination of whether a chapter, ministry, policy, or partnership contradicts adopted doctrine.

National chapters may translate, teach, and apply doctrine locally, but they may not alter it.

## 4.2 Mission, Vision, and Core Values

The International Society reserves authority over:

- amendment of the [[Mission Statement]];
- amendment of the [[Vision Statement]];
- amendment of [[Core Values]];
- final interpretation of the Society's religious purpose;
- determination of whether an activity falls inside or outside the Society's mission.

National chapters may express the mission locally, but they may not redefine it.

## 4.3 Constitutional Authority

The International Society reserves authority over:

- adoption, amendment, interpretation, and repeal of the [[International Constitution]];
- approval of constitutional amendment procedures;
- establishment of constitutional governance standards;
- interpretation of constitutional conflicts between the International Society and national chapters.

National chapter bylaws may not contradict the International Constitution.

## 4.4 International Governance Standards

The International Society reserves authority over:

- international governance framework;
- governance principles;
- board standards;
- mission alignment review standards;
- international reporting standards;
- document registry standards;
- decision register standards;
- definitions of key terms;
- governance lifecycle requirements.

## 4.5 National Chapter Recognition

The International Society reserves authority over:

- recognizing new national chapters;
- suspending national chapters;
- dissolving recognition of national chapters;
- approving restoration of suspended chapters;
- defining requirements for chapter good standing;
- approving chapter launch and transition processes.

A group may not represent itself as a national chapter without formal recognition.

## 4.6 International Name and Identity

The International Society reserves authority over:

- use of the Society's name;
- use of Society marks, logos, branding, and identity;
- official public representation of the Society;
- recognition of authorized international spokespersons;
- protection against unauthorized organizations using Society identity.

## 4.7 Affiliated Organizations

The International Society reserves authority over:

- approval of major international affiliated organizations;
- recognition of national affiliated organizations that use Society name or authority;
- approval of structures that hold significant property or mission assets;
- approval of ownership or control arrangements involving the Society;
- termination or restructuring of affiliated organizations when mission alignment fails.

Examples include housing trusts, church support foundations, worker cooperatives, property-holding entities, and international ministry bodies.

## 4.8 International Board and Senior Offices

The International Society reserves authority over:

- composition and authority of the International Board;
- appointment or confirmation processes for international officers;
- appointment or recognition of the International Executive Director;
- creation of international offices;
- international officer accountability standards;
- approval of international executive succession processes.

## 4.9 Theological Oversight

The International Society reserves authority over:

- establishment of the [[Theological Oversight Council]];
- approval of theological review standards;
- approval of doctrinal review processes;
- final appeal in theological mission alignment concerns;
- standards for seminary recognition where required for Society roles.

## 4.10 Church Advisory Structures

The International Society reserves authority over:

- establishment of the [[Church Advisory Fellowship]];
- approval of international church partnership standards;
- criteria for advisory churches;
- recognition of advisory bodies connected to national chapters;
- rules preserving the advisory rather than ecclesiastical nature of church relationships.

## 4.11 Mission Alignment

The International Society reserves authority over:

- mission alignment review standards;
- final determination of international mission alignment disputes;
- required corrective action for national chapters or affiliated organizations;
- suspension of activities that threaten mission, doctrine, or constitutional identity.

## 4.12 International Expansion

The International Society reserves authority over:

- approval of entry into new countries;
- approval of national chapter formation;
- approval of international expansion standards;
- approval of localization policies;
- approval of translation policies;
- approval of international partnerships affecting multiple countries.

## 4.13 Major Property and Asset Stewardship

The International Society reserves authority over:

- international property-holding frameworks;
- transfer of significant mission assets;
- sale or encumbrance of assets designated as international or foundational;
- acceptance of property gifts that may materially affect mission;
- major asset transfers between chapters or affiliated organizations.

National chapters may manage local assets within delegated authority and local law, but may not transfer mission-critical assets in a way that undermines the Society.

## 4.14 Financial Identity and Major Obligations

The International Society reserves authority over:

- international financial standards;
- approval of international debt or guarantees;
- approval of cross-border financial commitments;
- approval of international reserves or endowments;
- financial standards for affiliated organizations;
- major restricted gifts with international implications.

## 4.15 Legal and Structural Changes

The International Society reserves authority over:

- merger of the International Society;
- dissolution of the International Society;
- restructuring that affects international identity;
- separation of a national chapter from the International Society;
- major constitutional legal reorganization.

## 4.16 Crisis Intervention

The International Society reserves authority to intervene when a chapter, ministry, or affiliated organization faces serious risk involving:

- doctrine;
- mission drift;
- financial misconduct;
- legal crisis;
- abuse or safeguarding concerns;
- governance breakdown;
- unauthorized use of Society identity;
- conflict with local churches;
- major reputational harm;
- material breach of governing documents.

# 5. Reserved Powers and National Law

National chapters must comply with local law.

If local law requires a national chapter board to hold certain legal powers, those powers may be held legally by the national board but must be exercised consistently with the Society's constitutional and mission commitments where lawful.

Where local law conflicts with an international reserved power, the chapter must notify the International Board and seek a lawful governance solution.

No chapter may quietly use local law as a crowbar to pry itself away from the Society's mission.

---

# 6. Reserved Powers and Local Adaptation

Local adaptation is permitted for:

- legal compliance;
- language;
- administrative practice;
- cultural communication;
- local church relationships;
- ministry implementation;
- employment and volunteer management;
- local reporting formats.

Local adaptation is not permitted for:

- doctrine;
- mission;
- constitutional identity;
- church-supporting boundaries;
- chapter recognition;
- reserved powers;
- governance accountability;
- mission alignment standards.

---

# 7. Exercise of Reserved Powers

Reserved powers may be exercised only by the authority designated in governing documents.

Ordinarily, reserved powers are exercised by:

- International Board resolution;
- constitutional amendment process;
- approved international policy;
- formal recognition decision;
- formal suspension or corrective action decision;
- delegated committee action where expressly authorized by the International Board.

Every exercise of a major reserved power should be recorded in the [[Decision Register]].

---

# 8. Emergency Exercise of Reserved Powers

In an urgent situation, the International Board or authorized emergency committee may exercise reserved powers to protect the Society from serious harm.

Emergency action should be limited to what is necessary, documented promptly, reviewed by the full International Board, and recorded in the Decision Register.

Emergency authority must not become normal governance by shortcut. Fire extinguishers are useful; cooking with them is not.

---

# 9. Reserved Powers Review

This framework should be reviewed:

- annually during the founding stage;
- when a new national chapter is recognized;
- when a major affiliated organization is created;
- after a significant governance dispute;
- after a mission alignment crisis;
- before international constitutional amendment.
