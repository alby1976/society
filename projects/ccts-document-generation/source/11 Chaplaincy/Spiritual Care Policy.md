# Spiritual Care Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines spiritual care within the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Spiritual care is the Society's ministry of biblical encouragement, prayer, listening, support, referral, and compassionate presence for people connected to Society ministries. It is offered under the Society's mission and within the boundaries of its church-supporting identity.

This policy should be read with [[Chaplaincy Framework]], [[Chaplain Policy]], [[Chaplain Qualifications]], [[Church Advisory Fellowship Charter]], [[Theological Oversight Council Charter]], and [[5-15 Report Confidentiality Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Spiritual care within the Society should be compassionate, biblical, wise, confidential, accountable, and church-supporting.

The Society may care spiritually for people, but it is not a church. Spiritual care must therefore encourage rather than replace faithful local church life.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to spiritual care offered by:

- Society chaplains;
- ministry leaders;
- staff;
- approved volunteers;
- housing ministry workers;
- worker cooperative leaders;
- foundation personnel;
- national chapter leaders;
- persons authorized to provide prayer or spiritual support.

The depth of care offered must match the person's role, training, authorization, and accountability.

---

# 4. Spiritual Care Activities

Spiritual care may include:

- prayer;
- Scripture reading;
- biblical encouragement;
- listening;
- grief support;
- crisis support;
- spiritual conversation;
- referral to local church;
- referral to chaplain;
- referral to professional help;
- devotional support;
- support during housing, work, family, or ministry stress.

Spiritual care should be offered with humility and without manipulation.

---

# 5. What Spiritual Care Is Not

Society spiritual care is not:

- church discipline;
- sacramental ministry;
- ordained pastoral office unless separately held in a church;
- professional therapy unless provided by a qualified professional in that capacity;
- legal advice;
- medical advice;
- financial advice;
- substitute parenting;
- permanent dependency structure;
- coercive religious pressure.

The Society should not pretend to provide what it is not qualified or authorized to provide.

---

# 6. Relationship to Local Churches

Where a person belongs to a faithful local church, spiritual care should respect that church's pastoral leadership.

The Society may encourage the person to speak with their pastor, elders, or appropriate church leaders.

Where a person is unchurched, the Society may provide temporary spiritual care while encouraging connection to a faithful local church where appropriate.

---

# 7. Spiritual Care for Unchurched Persons

The Society may serve unchurched persons through housing, work, grants, mercy ministry, or other support.

Spiritual care for unchurched persons should be:

- patient;
- voluntary;
- clear about the Society's Christian identity;
- non-coercive;
- connected to local church referral where appropriate;
- respectful of legal and privacy boundaries;
- prayerful.

The Society should not use material need to manipulate religious participation.

---

# 8. Confidentiality

Spiritual care often involves sensitive information.

Those providing spiritual care must protect confidentiality according to policy.

Confidential information may include:

- family stress;
- grief;
- spiritual struggle;
- financial hardship;
- housing stress;
- employment difficulty;
- church conflict;
- health concerns;
- trauma;
- sin or moral failure;
- personal vulnerability.

Information should be shared only with proper authorization, consent, legal requirement, safety concern, safeguarding need, or governance need.

---

# 9. Limits of Confidentiality

No person providing spiritual care may promise absolute confidentiality.

Disclosure may be required or permitted when:

- a person is at risk of serious harm;
- safeguarding concerns arise;
- abuse or exploitation is suspected;
- law requires reporting;
- the person consents;
- immediate safety is at issue;
- legal counsel must be consulted;
- authorized leadership needs limited information to act;
- a serious mission or governance concern exists.

---

# 10. Referrals

Spiritual care providers should refer when the need exceeds their role or competence.

Referral may be made to:

- local church pastor or elders;
- Society chaplain;
- professional counselor;
- medical professional;
- crisis service;
- legal advisor;
- financial counselor;
- safeguarding officer;
- executive leadership;
- emergency services.

Referral is not failure. Referral is often wisdom wearing work boots.

---

# 11. Prayer

Prayer may be offered as part of spiritual care.

Prayer should be:

- voluntary;
- appropriate to the situation;
- consistent with Scripture;
- respectful of confidentiality;
- free from manipulation;
- connected to the Society's mission.

Prayer requests should not disclose unnecessary personal details.

---

# 12. Scripture Use

Scripture may be read, explained, or applied in spiritual care.

Those using Scripture should do so carefully, avoiding:

- proof-texting;
- harsh application;
- speculative claims;
- manipulation;
- replacing pastoral care of a local church;
- simplistic answers to complex suffering.

---

# 13. Documentation

Spiritual care should be documented only as necessary.

Documentation may include:

- date of contact;
- general nature of care;
- referral made;
- follow-up needed;
- risk level;
- escalation;
- prayer theme;
- reviewer or supervisor notified.

Sensitive details should be minimized.

Chaplaincy documentation should follow [[Chaplain 5-15 Report]] and confidentiality policy.

---

# 14. Safeguarding

Spiritual care providers must follow safeguarding requirements.

Concerns involving abuse, exploitation, coercion, serious harm, vulnerable persons, or safety must be escalated promptly according to policy and law.

---

# 15. Boundaries

Spiritual care providers must maintain proper boundaries.

They should avoid:

- dependency;
- secrecy;
- favoritism;
- private relationships that compromise care;
- financial entanglement;
- romantic or sexualized conduct;
- spiritual manipulation;
- acting beyond role or competence;
- unsupervised high-risk care where supervision is required.

---

# 16. Spiritual Care in Housing

Housing-related spiritual care should respect resident privacy and tenancy rights.

Residents should not be coerced into spiritual conversations, prayer, church attendance, or devotional participation as a condition of basic housing care unless specific lawful program terms have been reviewed and approved.

The Society may maintain a clear Christian identity and offer spiritual support without coercion.

---

# 17. Spiritual Care in Worker Cooperative Ministry

Worker cooperative spiritual care should support workers without confusing employment supervision with pastoral control.

Care may include prayer, encouragement, referral, and support during workplace stress.

Supervisors should avoid using spiritual care to pressure workers in employment matters.

---

# 18. Spiritual Care in Foundation Ministry

Foundation-related spiritual care may involve students, ministry workers, churches, or grant recipients.

Care should avoid conflicts of interest, coercion, and misuse of funding relationships.

A person should not feel spiritually controlled because they receive financial support.

---

# 19. Review and Supervision

Spiritual care practices should be reviewed by authorized leaders.

Review may include:

- chaplain supervisor review;
- church advisory counsel;
- theological oversight;
- safeguarding review;
- mission alignment review;
- report review.

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually with [[Chaplain Policy]], [[Chaplain Qualifications]], and [[5-15 Report Confidentiality Policy]].
