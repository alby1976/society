# International Board Charter

## 1. Purpose

This Charter establishes the role, authority, responsibilities, limits, composition, and operating principles of the International Board of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The International Board is the senior governing body responsible for the international Society's doctrine, mission, constitution, reserved powers, international strategy, national chapter recognition, institutional memory, and long-term stewardship.

This Charter should be read with [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], [[Reserved Powers Framework]], [[Delegated Authority Framework]], [[Authority Matrix]], [[Board Responsibilities]], and [[Board Member Qualifications]].

---

# 2. Nature of Board Authority

The International Board governs as steward, not owner.

Board authority is:

- theological, because the Society is bound to Christ and Scripture;
- fiduciary, because the Society manages resources entrusted by God and donors;
- constitutional, because the Board guards the [[International Constitution]];
- missional, because the Board protects the Society from mission drift;
- international, because the Board preserves unity across national chapters.

Board members must remember that governance is service. The Board does not exist to preserve its own importance. It exists to protect the mission and enable faithful ministry.

---

# 3. Core Mandate

The International Board shall:

1. guard the Society's [[Statement of Faith]];
2. preserve the [[Mission Statement]], [[Vision Statement]], and [[Core Values]];
3. maintain and interpret the [[International Constitution]];
4. exercise reserved powers;
5. approve international governance standards;
6. recognize, review, suspend, or restore national chapters;
7. approve major international affiliated organizations;
8. appoint, evaluate, and if necessary remove the International Executive Director;
9. establish and review international councils and advisory bodies;
10. receive and review reports from officers, chapters, councils, ministries, and affiliated organizations;
11. oversee international risk, finance, and accountability;
12. preserve institutional memory through registries, records, reports, and archives;
13. lead with prayer, humility, courage, and discipline.

---

# 4. Board Relationship to Christ and Scripture

The Board is accountable first to God.

The Board shall not approve any action that knowingly contradicts Scripture, denies the Society's Statement of Faith, undermines the local church, or turns the Society into an ecclesiastical substitute.

Board governance should be marked by prayer, truthfulness, patience, order, accountability, and willingness to correct error.

---

# 5. Board Relationship to the Local Church

The Board shall preserve the Society's church-supporting identity.

The Board shall not direct the Society to:

- replace Lord's Day worship;
- administer sacraments;
- exercise church discipline;
- override lawful church officers;
- use chaplaincy as a substitute for church membership;
- create permanent spiritual dependency on the Society;
- treat social service as a substitute for the Gospel.

The Board should encourage all Society ministries to support faithful local churches.

---

# 6. Board Relationship to National Chapters

The Board recognizes that national chapters require real local authority to function. The Board shall not micromanage lawful local operations.

At the same time, the Board must protect international doctrine, mission, governance standards, and reserved powers.

The Board shall ensure that national chapters:

- remain confessionally aligned;
- operate under delegated authority;
- comply with local law;
- report faithfully;
- maintain proper local governance;
- participate in mission alignment review;
- respect the Society's name and identity.

---

# 7. Board Relationship to Officers

The Board governs. Officers implement.

The Board shall appoint or recognize officers according to policy and define their authority through job descriptions, delegations, budgets, performance expectations, and reporting requirements.

The Board should avoid two opposite errors:

- neglecting oversight and allowing officers to govern by habit;
- micromanaging officers so thoroughly that faithful administration becomes impossible.

Both errors are expensive. One is chaos wearing a blazer; the other is bureaucracy with a theological haircut.

---

# 8. Board Relationship to Advisory Bodies

The Board may establish advisory or oversight bodies, including:

- [[Church Advisory Fellowship]];
- [[Theological Oversight Council]];
- [[Stewardship Advisors]];
- board committees;
- task forces;
- national advisory teams;
- external professional advisors.

Each body shall have a charter describing its purpose, authority, limits, reporting obligations, and relationship to the Board.

Advisory bodies advise unless specific governing authority is granted.

---

# 9. Composition

The International Board should include persons who collectively bring:

- theological maturity;
- commitment to the Society's Reformed identity;
- governance wisdom;
- financial stewardship;
- legal and risk awareness;
- international perspective;
- church relationship sensitivity;
- ministry experience;
- humility and courage;
- capacity to preserve institutional memory.

As the Society develops, the Board should seek appropriate representation from multiple national chapters while avoiding tokenism or political balancing that weakens mission fidelity.

---

# 10. Board Member Qualifications

Board members should meet qualifications defined in [[Board Member Qualifications]].

At minimum, a board member should:

- affirm the Society's Statement of Faith according to the required level of subscription;
- support the mission, vision, and core values;
- be a member in good standing of a faithful local church, where reasonably possible;
- demonstrate Christian character;
- understand the Society is not a church;
- avoid conflicts of interest or disclose them promptly;
- be willing to read, pray, deliberate, report, and decide carefully;
- protect confidential information;
- act for the Society's mission rather than personal preference.

---

# 11. Terms of Service

Board terms should be defined by bylaws or board policy.

The Society should avoid both permanent board control and constant turnover.

A healthy board should provide:

- continuity;
- renewal;
- accountability;
- institutional memory;
- succession planning.

Term limits, staggered terms, reappointment rules, and emeritus or advisory roles should be used wisely.

---

# 12. Officers of the Board

The Board may appoint board officers such as:

- Chair;
- Vice-Chair;
- Secretary;
- Treasurer;
- Committee Chairs;
- other officers required by law or policy.

Board officer duties shall be defined by bylaws, policy, or resolution.

Board officers do not possess unlimited authority simply because they hold a title. Officer authority must be defined.

---

# 13. Meetings

The Board shall meet regularly enough to govern faithfully.

Board meetings should include:

- prayer;
- approval of agenda;
- review of prior decisions;
- reports;
- financial review;
- risk review;
- governance decisions;
- mission alignment concerns;
- national chapter updates;
- executive session where needed;
- recording of decisions;
- assignment of follow-up actions.

A separate [[Board Meeting Policy]] shall define meeting procedures.

---

# 14. Decision-Making

Board decisions should be:

- informed;
- prayerful;
- documented;
- consistent with governing documents;
- made by proper authority;
- recorded in minutes;
- entered in the [[Decision Register]] when significant.

The Board should distinguish between:

- discussion;
- advice;
- recommendation;
- decision;
- resolution;
- delegated action.

Blurring those categories is how minutes become fiction.

---

# 15. Committees

The Board may establish committees to support governance.

Common committees may include:

- Governance Committee;
- Finance and Audit Committee;
- Theology and Mission Committee;
- Risk Committee;
- International Expansion Committee;
- Executive Committee;
- Nominating Committee.

Committee authority shall be defined by charter. A committee may not exercise reserved powers unless expressly authorized.

---

# 16. Conflicts of Interest

Board members shall disclose actual, potential, and perceived conflicts of interest.

A board member with a conflict should not improperly influence discussion, decision, contract, grant, hiring, property transaction, or ministry approval.

The Society shall adopt a separate [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

---

# 17. Confidentiality

Board members shall protect confidential information.

Confidential information may include:

- personal information;
- pastoral concerns;
- employment matters;
- legal advice;
- financial information;
- donor information;
- risk reports;
- disciplinary matters;
- national chapter concerns;
- sensitive negotiations.

Confidentiality must not be abused to hide wrongdoing.

---

# 18. Reporting

The Board shall receive reports sufficient to govern well.

Reports may include:

- International Executive Director reports;
- national chapter reports;
- 5-15 summaries;
- financial reports;
- risk reports;
- ministry reports;
- theological oversight reports;
- church advisory reports;
- mission alignment reviews;
- annual reports.

Reports should be concise enough to be used and complete enough to be useful.

---

# 19. Evaluation

The Board should evaluate:

- its own performance;
- board member participation;
- executive performance;
- committee effectiveness;
- national chapter health;
- mission alignment;
- governance document currency;
- reporting quality;
- risk posture.

Board evaluation should lead to correction, training, succession planning, and better governance.

---

# 20. Board Culture

The Board should cultivate a culture marked by:

- prayer;
- theological seriousness;
- careful listening;
- clear decisions;
- charitable disagreement;
- courage;
- transparency;
- confidentiality where appropriate;
- humility;
- accountability;
- readiness to correct course.

A board may have excellent documents and still fail because of poor culture. Documents are bones; culture is the nervous system.

---

# 21. Relationship to Institutional Memory

The Board shall preserve institutional memory by maintaining:

- minutes;
- decision register;
- document registry;
- changelog;
- release plan;
- review schedule;
- annual reports;
- mission alignment reviews;
- archives;
- stewardship advisor records.

The Board should ensure future leaders can understand not only what was decided, but why.

---

# 22. Review

This Charter shall be reviewed annually during the founding period and at least every three years after stable governance is established.

Review shall consider whether the Board's structure, composition, authority, and reporting practices remain adequate for an international Society.
