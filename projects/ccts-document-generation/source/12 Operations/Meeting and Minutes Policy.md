# Meeting and Minutes Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for Society meetings, agendas, minutes, decision records, action items, and follow-up.

Meetings are one of the places where governance becomes visible. Good minutes protect memory, accountability, and continuity.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Meetings should serve mission, governance, prayer, decision-making, accountability, and shared understanding.

A meeting should not exist merely because people are used to meeting. A meeting should produce clarity.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- International Board meetings;
- National Chapter Board meetings;
- committee meetings;
- ministry leadership meetings;
- advisory meetings;
- Foundation meetings;
- housing meetings;
- cooperative meetings;
- governance review meetings;
- Mission Alignment Review meetings;
- annual review meetings.

Informal conversations may still create decisions that need records.

---

# 4. Meeting Types

## 4.1 Governance Meetings

Meetings where boards or committees exercise formal authority.

## 4.2 Operational Meetings

Meetings for coordination, planning, reporting, and follow-up.

## 4.3 Advisory Meetings

Meetings with church, theological, legal, finance, or stewardship advisors.

## 4.4 Review Meetings

Meetings for reports, risk review, Mission Alignment Review, document review, or annual review.

## 4.5 Emergency Meetings

Meetings called to address urgent risk, legal, safety, financial, or mission concerns.

---

# 5. Agendas

Meetings should normally have an agenda.

An agenda may include:

- opening prayer where appropriate;
- attendance;
- conflict declarations;
- approval of previous minutes;
- reports;
- decisions needed;
- risk review;
- financial review;
- ministry updates;
- action items;
- closing prayer where appropriate.

Agendas should distinguish discussion from decision.

---

# 6. Preparation

Participants should receive necessary materials in advance where possible.

Materials may include:

- agenda;
- prior minutes;
- reports;
- financial summaries;
- policy drafts;
- decision proposals;
- risk register;
- mission alignment documents;
- supporting notes.

Leaders should not be expected to govern from memory and surprise documents.

---

# 7. Attendance

Minutes should record attendance.

Attendance record should include:

- members present;
- members absent;
- guests;
- advisors;
- staff present;
- conflicts or recusals;
- quorum if applicable.

---

# 8. Conflict Declarations

Meetings involving decisions should provide opportunity for conflict declarations.

Minutes should record:

- conflict disclosed;
- person affected;
- matter involved;
- management action;
- whether the person left discussion;
- whether the person abstained.

Conflicts should not be hidden in polite silence.

---

# 9. Minutes

Minutes should record:

- organization or committee name;
- meeting date;
- time and location;
- attendance;
- agenda items;
- key reports received;
- decisions made;
- motions where applicable;
- action items;
- responsible persons;
- deadlines;
- next meeting;
- approvals.

Minutes should be clear enough for future leaders to understand what happened.

---

# 10. What Minutes Should Not Include

Minutes should avoid unnecessary:

- personal details;
- confidential pastoral information;
- sensitive resident information;
- sensitive worker information;
- confidential grant applicant details;
- legal advice details;
- gossip;
- speculative comments;
- excessive debate transcript;
- emotional commentary.

Minutes are not a novel.

---

# 11. Decision Records

Significant decisions should be entered in [[Decision Register]].

Minutes may summarize the decision, while the Decision Register preserves searchable decision history.

---

# 12. Action Items

Action items should include:

- action;
- owner;
- due date;
- status;
- related decision;
- follow-up note.

Unassigned action items are usually wishes.

---

# 13. Approval of Minutes

Formal minutes should be approved according to board or committee practice.

Approval should record:

- date approved;
- approving body;
- corrections if any;
- secretary or records officer.

Draft minutes should be marked as draft until approved.

---

# 14. Confidential Sessions

Boards may meet in confidential or executive session where appropriate.

Minutes should record that a confidential session occurred, the general topic where appropriate, and decisions made where recordable.

Sensitive details should be stored separately if necessary.

---

# 15. Emergency Meetings

Emergency meetings should still produce records.

Emergency records should include:

- reason for urgency;
- participants;
- authority;
- decision;
- immediate actions;
- follow-up;
- reporting requirement;
- review date.

---

# 16. Advisor Meetings

Advisory meetings should record:

- advisors present;
- topic;
- advice received;
- decisions required;
- follow-up;
- confidentiality level.

Advice is not the same as authority unless authority has been granted.

---

# 17. Storage

Minutes and meeting records should be stored according to [[Records Management Policy]].

Board minutes and major governance minutes should be retained permanently.

---

# 18. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually with [[Core Operations Manual]] and [[Records Management Policy]].
