# Church Support Foundation Grant Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs grants made by the Church Support Foundation of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The purpose is to ensure that grants are mission-aligned, lawful, accountable, financially responsible, free from improper private benefit, and respectful of local church authority.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A grant is a stewardship decision.

The Foundation should give with generosity, clarity, accountability, and humility. Grant-making should never become a way to buy control, reward insiders, or avoid governance.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- church support grants;
- capacity-building grants;
- ministry training grants;
- theological education grants;
- mercy ministry grants;
- leadership development grants;
- pilot project grants;
- emergency ministry grants;
- national chapter grants;
- restricted fund grants.

Specific grant types may have additional policies.

---

# 4. Grant Eligibility

Eligible recipients may include:

- faithful local churches;
- church-connected ministries;
- ministry trainees;
- theological students;
- national chapters;
- Society-affiliated ministries;
- approved nonprofit or charitable organizations;
- approved individuals where lawful and mission-aligned.

Eligibility must be defined by grant category.

---

# 5. Grant Criteria

Grant applications should be evaluated according to:

- doctrinal alignment;
- mission fit;
- church-supporting purpose;
- need;
- capacity;
- stewardship;
- accountability;
- legal compliance;
- financial reasonableness;
- expected impact;
- risk;
- conflicts of interest;
- sustainability.

---

# 6. Application Process

A grant process may include:

1. inquiry;
2. eligibility screening;
3. application;
4. supporting documents;
5. conflict of interest disclosure;
6. financial review;
7. theological or church advisory review where needed;
8. grant committee review;
9. approval or denial;
10. grant agreement;
11. payment;
12. reporting;
13. follow-up;
14. closure.

---

# 7. Required Application Information

Applications may request:

- applicant name;
- organization or church;
- contact information;
- grant purpose;
- requested amount;
- budget;
- timeline;
- theological or church connection;
- expected outcomes;
- other funding sources;
- reporting capacity;
- conflicts of interest;
- references or endorsements where appropriate.

Only necessary information should be collected.

---

# 8. Approval Authority

Grant approval authority must be defined by amount and risk.

Possible authorities include:

- Foundation Director;
- grant committee;
- National Board;
- International Board;
- designated officers;
- emergency grant authority.

High-risk, high-value, doctrinally sensitive, or related-party grants require higher review.

---

# 9. Grant Agreement

Approved grants should use a written grant agreement.

The agreement should include:

- recipient;
- amount;
- purpose;
- payment schedule;
- permitted uses;
- prohibited uses;
- reporting requirements;
- term;
- conditions;
- confidentiality;
- recovery or suspension rights;
- contact person;
- signatures.

---

# 10. Permitted Uses

Permitted uses should be specific to each grant.

Examples may include:

- training costs;
- approved ministry equipment;
- ministry materials;
- governance support;
- facility support;
- translation;
- program development;
- temporary ministry staffing;
- approved travel;
- approved hardship or mercy support.

---

# 11. Prohibited Uses

Grants must not be used for:

- personal benefit unrelated to grant purpose;
- unlawful activity;
- doctrinally contrary activity;
- political partisan activity;
- undisclosed compensation;
- expenses outside approved budget;
- insider benefit;
- activities contrary to local church authority;
- activities that replace church worship or sacraments;
- speculative investments.

---

# 12. Reporting

Grant recipients should submit reports according to grant terms.

Reports may include:

- funds spent;
- activities completed;
- outcomes;
- challenges;
- prayer requests;
- receipts or financial summary;
- remaining funds;
- changes to plan;
- testimony or narrative where appropriate.

Reporting should be proportionate.

A $500 grant should not require a 40-page report unless something has gone very wrong.

---

# 13. Failure to Use Funds as Approved

If grant funds are not used as approved, the Foundation may:

- request clarification;
- pause further payments;
- require revised budget;
- require repayment where lawful;
- decline future funding;
- report to appropriate authority;
- conduct review;
- seek legal advice.

Responses should be documented and proportionate.

---

# 14. Student or Ministry Candidate Change of Direction

If a student or ministry candidate receives funding and later does not pursue the expected ministry path, the Foundation should respond according to the grant agreement and [[Ministry Student Support Policy]].

The recommended policy is:

- do not assume bad faith;
- review the reason for change;
- confirm whether funds were used for approved purposes;
- require final reporting;
- consider repayment only where the agreement required it or misuse occurred;
- preserve pastoral sensitivity;
- update future grant conditions if needed.

The Foundation should avoid turning vocational uncertainty into punishment.

---

# 15. Conflicts of Interest

All grant decisions require conflict review.

A conflicted person must not participate in discussion or decision unless policy permits limited factual input.

Conflicts should be recorded.

---

# 16. Donor Influence

Donors may designate funds only within approved restricted gift rules.

Donors must not control grant decisions improperly.

The Foundation should not accept gifts that turn donors into shadow grant committees.

---

# 17. Privacy

Applicant and recipient information must be protected.

Sensitive financial, family, church, academic, immigration, medical, pastoral, or personal information should be limited to authorized persons.

---

# 18. Denial

Grant denials should be documented.

Reasons may include:

- ineligibility;
- insufficient funds;
- weak mission fit;
- doctrinal concern;
- insufficient accountability;
- legal concern;
- conflict concern;
- incomplete application;
- better fit for another program.

Denial should be communicated respectfully.

---

# 19. Recordkeeping

Grant files should include:

- application;
- review notes;
- conflict disclosure;
- approval record;
- grant agreement;
- payment record;
- reports;
- correspondence;
- closure record.

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during Foundation launch and at least every three years once grant operations stabilize.
