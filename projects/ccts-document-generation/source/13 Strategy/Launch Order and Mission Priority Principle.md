# Launch Order and Mission Priority Principle

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the Society decides what to launch first, what to delay, what to pause, and what not to launch.

The principle exists to prevent mission drift, overextension, leadership burnout, legal risk, financial disorder, and confusion between good ideas and ready ministries.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should launch ministries in the order that best preserves mission faithfulness, legal readiness, governance capacity, financial stewardship, leadership health, and support for local churches.

The question is not only, "Is this good?"  
The question is also, "Is this the right time, under the right authority, with the right safeguards?"

---

# 3. Mission Priority Test

Before launching a ministry, the Society should ask:

1. Does this directly support the Society's mission?
2. Does it support rather than replace faithful local churches?
3. Is the ministry within the Society's authority?
4. Is there qualified leadership?
5. Are required policies ready?
6. Is legal review complete where needed?
7. Is financial stewardship ready?
8. Is reporting ready?
9. Are risks identified?
10. Is Mission Alignment Review complete?
11. Has the matter been prayed over?
12. Are we launching from obedience rather than pressure?

---

# 4. Launch Readiness Categories

Each proposed ministry should be classified as one of the following:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ready to Launch | Required authority, leadership, legal, financial, reporting, and risk controls are in place |
| Ready for Pilot | Small controlled test is appropriate, but full launch is not yet ready |
| Study Further | More prayer, legal review, financial review, or church counsel is needed |
| Delay | Good idea but not ready |
| Pause | Current activity should stop temporarily until concerns are addressed |
| Do Not Launch | Not mission-aligned or too risky |

---

# 5. Why Worker Cooperative May Precede Foundation

The worker cooperative pilot may launch before the Church Support Foundation because:

- it can begin small;
- it can be time-limited;
- it tests reporting and governance in practical work;
- it develops leadership and worker support;
- it can be evaluated before scaling;
- it may require fewer restricted fund controls than grant-making;
- it can teach the Society how to operate before it distributes funds.

This does not mean the cooperative is less serious. It means it can be piloted in a controlled way.

---

# 6. Why Foundation Should Wait Until Ready

The Church Support Foundation should not begin grant-making until strong controls exist because grants involve:

- donor restrictions;
- recipient accountability;
- private benefit risk;
- church relationship risk;
- theological education standards;
- student support complexity;
- reporting requirements;
- financial tracking;
- conflict of interest risk.

A Foundation without controls can damage mission faster than it can distribute money.

---

# 7. Why Housing Should Wait Until Strong Readiness

Housing should not launch merely because need is obvious.

Housing requires:

- legal structure;
- property law review;
- lease standards;
- resident selection policy;
- human rights review;
- affordability model;
- maintenance reserves;
- insurance;
- crisis and safety processes;
- resident support;
- financial sustainability;
- long-term mission review.

Housing is mercy with keys, pipes, liability, and long-term obligations.

---

# 8. Why Chaplaincy Requires Church Advisory Readiness

Chaplaincy should not expand before church boundaries are clear.

Chaplaincy requires:

- qualified chaplain;
- theological training standard;
- seminary recognition;
- spiritual care policy;
- confidentiality;
- church referral policy;
- crisis care policy;
- reporting system;
- accountability.

Chaplaincy must support the local church, not become a private pastoral system.

---

# 9. Delay Is Not Failure

Delaying a ministry may be faithful stewardship.

Delay may be needed when:

- leadership is not ready;
- legal review is incomplete;
- finances are weak;
- policies are missing;
- reporting is absent;
- risk is high;
- church relationships are unclear;
- leaders are overloaded;
- the ministry depends too much on one person.

A delayed ministry may become stronger. A rushed ministry may become a crisis.

---

# 10. Launch Decision Matrix

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| Mission aligned? | Continue review | Do not launch |
| Church-supporting? | Continue review | Do not launch or revise |
| Legal readiness complete? | Continue review | Delay |
| Leadership qualified? | Continue review | Delay |
| Financial controls ready? | Continue review | Delay |
| Reporting ready? | Continue review | Delay |
| Risk acceptable? | Continue review | Pause or delay |
| Prayer and discernment complete? | Continue review | Pause |
| Board authority clear? | Launch / pilot | Delay |

---

# 11. Launch Approval Record

Every launch decision should record:

- ministry name;
- launch category;
- authority approving;
- date;
- mission rationale;
- readiness summary;
- risks;
- reporting plan;
- review date;
- prayer and discernment note;
- responsible leader.

---

# 12. Pause or Stop Principle

The Society should be willing to pause or stop a ministry when:

- mission alignment fails;
- legal risk becomes unacceptable;
- financial controls fail;
- leaders are overloaded;
- reports are not submitted;
- serious complaints arise;
- church relationships are harmed;
- safety risks are unmanaged;
- the ministry depends on secrecy or personality;
- correction is refused.

Stopping a ministry may protect the mission.
