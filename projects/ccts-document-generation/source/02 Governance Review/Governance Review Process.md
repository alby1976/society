# Governance Review Process

## 1. Purpose

This process establishes how the Society reviews its governance system.

Governance Review evaluates whether the Society's boards, councils, committees, chapters, policies, documents, authority structures, reports, and institutional memory systems are functioning faithfully and effectively.

This process is distinct from [[Mission Alignment Review]], but the two are closely related.

## 2. Governing Principle

Good governance requires maintenance.

Even a well-written governance system can decay through neglect, unclear practice, missing reports, outdated documents, informal habits, or leaders who assume everyone remembers what happened three years ago.

Governance review is the oil change. Ignore it long enough, and the engine gives a sermon no one enjoys.

## 3. Scope

Governance Review may examine International Board performance, national board performance, board committees, councils, advisory bodies, delegated authority, reserved powers, policies, charters, bylaws, reporting, decision-making, records, conflicts of interest, financial controls, risk oversight, document lifecycle, institutional memory, and compliance with review schedules.

## 4. Review Frequency

The International Board should conduct an annual governance review during the founding stage.

After governance is stable, the Society should conduct annual focused review, comprehensive review every three years, and special review after major crisis, expansion, dispute, or restructuring.

National chapters should conduct governance reviews according to international standards and local law.

## 5. Review Authority

Governance Review may be led by the International Board, Governance Committee, external governance advisor, National Board, board chair, secretary, or delegated review team.

The International Board retains responsibility for international governance review.

## 6. Review Questions

A governance review should ask:

1. Are governing documents current?
2. Are decisions being recorded?
3. Are reports being submitted?
4. Are authority lines clear?
5. Are reserved powers respected?
6. Are delegations documented?
7. Are board meetings effective?
8. Are committees functioning within charter?
9. Are conflicts disclosed?
10. Are financial controls operating?
11. Are risks reviewed?
12. Are national chapters accountable?
13. Are advisory bodies useful?
14. Are theological concerns escalated properly?
15. Are records preserved?
16. Are leaders being prepared for succession?
17. Are governance practices consistent with mission?

## 7. Governance Review Checklist

### Documents

Review the Constitution, bylaws, board charter, policies, committee charters, national chapter charters, ministry frameworks, document registry, decision register, and review schedule.

### Board Practice

Review meeting frequency, agenda quality, minutes, attendance, decision clarity, conflict management, executive oversight, financial review, and risk review.

### Authority

Review reserved powers, delegated authority, authority matrix, signing authority, spending authority, committee authority, and officer authority.

### Reporting

Review 5-15 reporting, monthly reports, quarterly reports, annual reports, financial reports, risk reports, and mission alignment reports.

### Institutional Memory

Review archived decisions, changelog, release notes, historical records, founder records, and stewardship advisor input.

## 8. Evidence

Reviewers may examine minutes, policies, reports, board packets, committee records, financial statements, risk register, conflict disclosures, membership records, national chapter reports, decision register, document registry, audit or review reports, and external advisor comments.

## 9. Findings

Findings may include functioning well, minor improvement needed, document update needed, authority unclear, reporting inadequate, governance gap, mission risk, compliance risk, board training needed, committee restructuring needed, national chapter review needed, or urgent correction required.

## 10. Corrective Action Plan

A governance review should produce a corrective action plan where needed.

The plan should identify issue, required action, responsible person or body, deadline, priority, related document, follow-up date, and reporting method.

## 11. Board Self-Evaluation

The annual governance review should include board self-evaluation.

Board self-evaluation may consider theological faithfulness, mission focus, meeting preparation, decision-making, financial oversight, risk oversight, executive oversight, confidentiality, conflicts, prayer, unity, courage, succession, and document review.

Board self-evaluation should be honest without becoming a ritual of vague compliments.

## 12. Committee Review

Each standing committee should be reviewed.

The review should ask whether the committee is still needed, whether its charter is clear, whether it reported properly, whether it exceeded authority, whether it added value, whether membership needs renewal, whether records are complete, and whether conflicts are managed.

## 13. National Chapter Governance Review

National chapter governance review should examine local legal compliance, board function, alignment with international documents, local policies, reports, finances, church relationships, risk, delegated authority, reserved powers, and mission alignment.

## 14. Reporting to the Board

A governance review report should include review date, reviewers, scope, documents reviewed, findings, risks, recommendations, corrective actions, deadlines, and matters requiring board decision.

The report should be entered into institutional memory.

## 15. Escalation

Governance concerns should be escalated when they involve constitutional conflict, reserved powers, board misconduct, financial irregularity, serious reporting failure, national chapter breakdown, legal risk, mission drift, theological concern, or safeguarding risk.

## 16. Review

This process should be reviewed annually with [[Mission Alignment Review]] and [[Document Review Policy]].
