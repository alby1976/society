# Budget and Financial Planning Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how the Society prepares, approves, monitors, revises, and reports budgets.

Budgets help the Society plan responsibly, limit commitments, protect restricted funds, and align spending with mission.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

A budget is a mission plan expressed in numbers.

The Society should not treat a budget as mere paperwork or as an inflexible prophecy. It is a plan for stewardship that must be reviewed honestly.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- international budgets;
- national chapter budgets;
- ministry budgets;
- grant budgets;
- housing budgets;
- cooperative budgets;
- Foundation budgets;
- project budgets;
- event budgets;
- restricted fund budgets;
- annual operating budgets.

---

# 4. Budget Authority

Budget approval authority must follow:

- International Constitution;
- reserved powers;
- delegated authority;
- Authority Matrix;
- national chapter governance;
- board resolutions;
- local law.

A budget does not authorize spending beyond the limits set by policy, law, donor restriction, or board condition.

---

# 5. Budget Preparation

Budget preparation should identify:

- expected income;
- expected expenses;
- restricted funds;
- unrestricted funds;
- reserves;
- staffing or contractor costs;
- ministry costs;
- administrative costs;
- legal and compliance costs;
- technology costs;
- insurance;
- reporting and audit costs;
- contingency amounts;
- cash flow concerns.

---

# 6. Budget Categories

Standard budget categories may include:

- governance;
- administration;
- theology and church advisory;
- chaplaincy;
- housing;
- worker cooperative;
- Church Support Foundation;
- national chapter development;
- reporting and institutional memory;
- communications;
- legal and compliance;
- finance and audit;
- reserves.

---

# 7. Budget Review Questions

Before approving a budget, the approving authority should ask:

- Does this budget support mission?
- Are income assumptions realistic?
- Are expenses complete?
- Are restricted funds separated?
- Are reserves adequate?
- Are legal and compliance costs included?
- Are reporting and audit costs included?
- Are leaders being asked to do unpaid work unsustainably?
- What happens if donations are lower than expected?
- What commitments are being created?

---

# 8. Budget Approval

Budget approval should be documented.

Approval record should include:

- budget name;
- period covered;
- approving authority;
- date approved;
- restrictions;
- spending limits;
- assumptions;
- major risks;
- review date.

---

# 9. Budget Monitoring

Budgets should be monitored regularly.

Monitoring may include:

- monthly reports;
- quarterly board review;
- budget-to-actual comparison;
- cash flow review;
- restricted fund review;
- variance notes;
- forecast updates.

Ignoring a budget after approval is like printing a map and never looking at it.

---

# 10. Budget Variance

Material variances should be explained.

Variance review should ask:

- Why did income differ?
- Why did expenses differ?
- Is the variance temporary?
- Does action need to be taken?
- Does the budget need revision?
- Is restricted fund use affected?
- Is mission risk affected?

---

# 11. Budget Revision

Budgets may be revised when assumptions change.

Budget revisions should be approved when:

- major income changes;
- major expenses change;
- new ministry launches;
- restricted funds change;
- legal obligations arise;
- emergency needs occur;
- board direction changes;
- mission alignment concerns arise.

---

# 12. Ministry Launch Budgets

No major ministry should launch without a budget.

Ministry launch budgets should include:

- startup costs;
- operating costs;
- staffing;
- volunteer support;
- reporting;
- insurance;
- legal review;
- reserves;
- exit or closure costs;
- evaluation costs.

---

# 13. Capital and Property Budgets

Capital budgets require special care.

They should include:

- acquisition costs;
- renovation;
- legal fees;
- inspections;
- insurance;
- debt service;
- maintenance reserves;
- capital replacement;
- contingency;
- property management;
- disposal risk.

---

# 14. Grant Budgets

Grant budgets should identify:

- grant purpose;
- amount;
- recipient;
- payment schedule;
- restricted fund source;
- reporting requirement;
- remaining balance;
- unused fund process.

---

# 15. Budget Reports

Budget reports should be prepared for appropriate review.

Reports may include:

- approved budget;
- actual income;
- actual expenses;
- variance;
- restricted fund balances;
- reserve status;
- forecast;
- decisions needed.

---

# 16. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually before the next budget cycle begins.
