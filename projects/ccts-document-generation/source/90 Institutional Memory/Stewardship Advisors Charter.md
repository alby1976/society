# Stewardship Advisors Charter

## 1. Purpose

This Charter establishes the Stewardship Advisors of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Stewardship Advisors preserve institutional memory, provide historical counsel, mentor future leaders, and help the Society remember its founding principles without giving former leaders permanent governing control.

The Stewardship Advisors serve the Society by remembering what has been learned, why major decisions were made, and what dangers the Society has already identified.

This Charter should be read with [[Founder Advisory Role]], [[Institutional Memory Policy]], [[International Constitution]], [[International Governance Framework]], and [[International Board Charter]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should honor past wisdom without becoming controlled by the past.

Stewardship Advisors help preserve continuity across generations. They are advisors, not a second board, not hidden directors, and not a constitutional veto machine.

The Society needs memory, not nostalgia with voting rights.

---

# 3. Nature of the Role

Stewardship Advisors are appointed advisory persons who may include:

- founders;
- former board members;
- former officers;
- former national chapter leaders;
- experienced ministry leaders;
- retired chaplains;
- former committee chairs;
- persons with significant historical knowledge of the Society.

They serve in an advisory, mentoring, and historical capacity.

They do not govern unless separately appointed to a governing office.

---

# 4. Objectives

The Stewardship Advisors exist to:

1. preserve founding principles;
2. provide historical context;
3. advise on mission drift concerns;
4. mentor board members and leaders;
5. support founder transition;
6. help interpret prior decisions;
7. identify repeated governance problems;
8. support succession planning;
9. advise on institutional memory practices;
10. help future leaders avoid rediscovering old mistakes.

---

# 5. Appointment

Stewardship Advisors may be appointed by the International Board.

The appointment process may include:

- nomination;
- review of service history;
- review of theological and mission alignment;
- conflict of interest disclosure;
- willingness to serve in an advisory role;
- board approval;
- term designation;
- orientation to advisory limits.

Appointment should be recorded in the [[Decision Register]] where significant.

---

# 6. Qualifications

A Stewardship Advisor should:

- support the Society's [[Statement of Faith]];
- support the [[Mission Statement]], [[Vision Statement]], and [[Core Values]];
- understand the Society is not a church;
- have meaningful experience with the Society or related ministries;
- demonstrate wisdom and humility;
- respect board authority;
- protect confidential information;
- avoid using history as a weapon;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- be willing to advise without controlling.

A person who cannot release control should not be appointed as a Stewardship Advisor.

---

# 7. Term

A Stewardship Advisor may be appointed for a renewable term.

The term should be long enough to preserve continuity but not so indefinite that advisory roles become permanent shadow offices.

Possible terms:

- one year;
- three years;
- five years;
- project-specific term;
- emeritus advisory term subject to periodic review.

---

# 8. Responsibilities

Stewardship Advisors may be asked to:

- explain historical decisions;
- participate in board orientation;
- advise on governance changes;
- review mission drift concerns;
- advise during founder transition;
- support national chapter formation;
- contribute to annual institutional memory review;
- assist in documenting history;
- identify lessons learned;
- participate in anniversary or milestone reviews.

---

# 9. Limits of Authority

Stewardship Advisors shall not:

- vote as board members unless separately elected or appointed;
- direct staff;
- override board decisions;
- approve budgets;
- bind the Society contractually;
- represent the Society publicly without authorization;
- access confidential information without need and authorization;
- interfere with national chapter governance;
- use founder status to control decisions.

Their influence should be moral, historical, and advisory, not coercive.

---

# 10. Relationship to Founders

Founders may become Stewardship Advisors, but founder status alone does not automatically create advisory authority.

The Society may honor founders while requiring them to serve under ordinary governance boundaries.

Founders should be invited to contribute institutional memory without preventing future leaders from governing faithfully.

---

# 11. Relationship to the Board

The International Board may consult Stewardship Advisors.

The Board may invite them to:

- selected board discussions;
- orientation sessions;
- annual reviews;
- special consultations;
- historical interviews;
- mission alignment discussions.

The Board is not bound by advisory counsel unless governing documents state otherwise.

If the Board rejects significant historical or mission-related counsel, it should document the reason where appropriate.

---

# 12. Relationship to National Chapters

Stewardship Advisors may advise national chapters if invited or authorized.

They may help new chapters understand:

- Society history;
- mission boundaries;
- theological identity;
- governance lessons;
- founding roadmap;
- church-supporting commitments;
- reporting expectations.

They should not become international inspectors unless given a defined role.

---

# 13. Confidentiality

Stewardship Advisors may receive confidential information only when necessary for their advisory role.

They must protect confidential information concerning:

- board matters;
- personnel;
- legal issues;
- finances;
- pastoral concerns;
- national chapter issues;
- member discipline;
- housing residents;
- grant applicants;
- internal conflicts.

Confidentiality obligations continue after service ends.

---

# 14. Conflicts of Interest

Stewardship Advisors must disclose conflicts of interest.

Conflicts may arise from:

- prior board decisions;
- family relationships;
- financial interests;
- church relationships;
- employment history;
- property interests;
- grant or contract relationships;
- strong personal involvement in disputed decisions.

Advisors should not provide controlling advice on matters where they are materially conflicted.

---

# 15. Records and Institutional Memory

The Society may record interviews, written reflections, timelines, lessons learned, and historical summaries from Stewardship Advisors.

These materials should be stored in the institutional memory archive.

Sensitive materials should be marked with appropriate confidentiality.

---

# 16. Annual Consultation

The International Board should consider an annual consultation with Stewardship Advisors.

Topics may include:

- mission drift risks;
- governance continuity;
- founder transition;
- major lessons learned;
- national chapter concerns;
- document review;
- leadership succession;
- historical milestones;
- prayer concerns.

---

# 17. Removal

A Stewardship Advisor may resign or be removed.

Grounds for removal may include:

- doctrinal opposition;
- mission misalignment;
- breach of confidentiality;
- conflict of interest abuse;
- attempts to control governance;
- serious misconduct;
- persistent divisiveness;
- inability or unwillingness to serve within advisory limits.

Removal should be documented.

---

# 18. Review

This Charter should be reviewed annually during the founding period and at least every three years after the advisory structure is stable.
