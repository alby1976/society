# Incident Escalation Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes how serious incidents are reported, escalated, documented, reviewed, and followed up within the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Incidents require clear response because delay, confusion, secrecy, or improvisation can harm people and mission.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Serious incidents must be reported promptly to the right authority with the right level of detail.

Do not hide emergencies inside routine reports. Do not turn every small concern into a crisis. Discernment and escalation must work together.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to incidents involving:

- safety;
- safeguarding;
- privacy;
- legal notices;
- finances;
- restricted funds;
- grant misuse;
- housing;
- worker cooperative activity;
- chaplaincy;
- church relationships;
- public communications;
- leadership misconduct;
- serious conflict of interest;
- national chapters;
- affiliated organizations.

---

# 4. Incident Categories

Incidents may include:

- injury or serious harm;
- death connected to ministry;
- safeguarding concern;
- abuse or exploitation concern;
- privacy breach;
- financial irregularity;
- grant misuse;
- donor restriction breach;
- legal notice;
- police, regulatory, or government contact;
- serious complaint;
- public controversy;
- unauthorized public statement;
- data loss;
- property damage;
- serious worker safety concern;
- resident safety concern;
- chaplaincy boundary breach;
- church relationship crisis;
- leadership misconduct;
- repeated reporting failure.

---

# 5. Incident Levels

| Level | Description | Escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Minor issue with limited effect | Responsible manager |
| Level 2 | Significant issue requiring leadership review | Executive or ministry director |
| Level 3 | Serious legal, safety, financial, privacy, or mission concern | Executive and board delegate |
| Level 4 | Critical incident, immediate danger, major exposure, public crisis | Emergency response and board leadership |

---

# 6. Immediate Safety

If immediate danger exists, emergency services or appropriate local authorities should be contacted according to law and circumstances.

Internal approval should not delay urgent safety action.

---

# 7. Required Escalation

Escalation is required when an incident involves:

- immediate safety concern;
- safeguarding concern;
- suspected abuse or exploitation;
- legal notice;
- serious privacy breach;
- financial misconduct;
- major restricted fund concern;
- grant misuse;
- serious housing incident;
- serious worker injury;
- public controversy;
- misconduct by senior leader;
- mission drift concern requiring board action.

---

# 8. Incident Report Contents

An incident report should include:

- date and time;
- location;
- incident type;
- persons or roles involved;
- immediate action taken;
- current risk;
- emergency services or authorities contacted;
- documents or evidence;
- confidentiality level;
- person reporting;
- person receiving report;
- follow-up needed;
- next review date.

Only necessary sensitive details should be included.

---

# 9. Confidentiality

Incident information must be handled carefully.

Access should be limited to those with a legitimate need to know.

Information may need to be shared with:

- emergency services;
- legal counsel;
- insurer;
- safeguarding lead;
- privacy lead;
- board officers;
- executive leadership;
- affected persons where appropriate;
- regulators where required.

---

# 10. Legal and Privileged Information

If legal advice may be needed, the matter should be escalated to authorized leadership before broad distribution.

Legal advice and privileged communications should be handled according to legal counsel direction.

Do not casually forward legal advice.

---

# 11. Communication Control

Public or external communication after a serious incident must be authorized.

No person should issue public statements beyond their authority.

Communication should be accurate, compassionate, lawful, and consistent with confidentiality.

---

# 12. Follow-Up

Incident follow-up may include:

- safety action;
- support for affected persons;
- legal review;
- financial review;
- privacy review;
- safeguarding review;
- board report;
- insurance notice;
- policy revision;
- training;
- corrective action;
- Mission Alignment Review;
- risk register update.

---

# 13. Debrief

Significant incidents should be debriefed.

Debrief questions:

- What happened?
- Was anyone harmed?
- Was response timely?
- Was escalation proper?
- Was confidentiality protected?
- Are legal obligations satisfied?
- What corrective action is required?
- What risk remains?
- What should future leaders learn?

---

# 14. Documentation

Incident records should be stored according to [[Records Management Policy]].

Serious incident records should be retained according to legal requirements and risk.

Do not delete incident records to avoid embarrassment.

---

# 15. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and after any Level 3 or Level 4 incident.
