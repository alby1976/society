# Cooperative Operations Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for day-to-day operations in a Society-affiliated worker cooperative.

The cooperative must operate with clear roles, reliable work processes, honest reporting, safety, financial discipline, worker dignity, and mission alignment.

A cooperative that cannot organize ordinary work cannot responsibly carry extraordinary mission.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Operations are part of stewardship.

The Society's cooperative should not confuse informality with kindness. Clear systems protect workers, customers, leaders, and mission.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- pilot cooperatives;
- incorporated worker cooperatives;
- cooperative enterprises;
- ministry businesses;
- cooperative training programs;
- managers;
- workers;
- supervisors;
- mentors;
- volunteers where lawful;
- contractors serving cooperative operations.

---

# 4. Operational Authority

Operational authority must be written.

The cooperative shall identify who may:

- schedule work;
- assign tasks;
- approve purchases;
- communicate with customers;
- sign contracts;
- approve invoices;
- supervise workers;
- handle complaints;
- pause unsafe work;
- escalate risks;
- report to the Society.

Authority limits should be clear enough to prevent both paralysis and freelancing.

---

# 5. Work Planning

The cooperative should maintain a work planning process.

Work planning may include:

- project list;
- task assignments;
- deadlines;
- worker capacity;
- required tools;
- quality standards;
- safety requirements;
- customer obligations;
- reporting deadlines;
- supervisor review.

---

# 6. Scheduling

Scheduling should be fair, clear, and lawful.

Scheduling should consider:

- worker availability;
- skill level;
- training needs;
- customer deadlines;
- safety;
- legal work limits;
- rest;
- accessibility needs;
- supervision requirements.

Workers should not be surprised into obligations that were never clearly communicated.

---

# 7. Task Assignment

Task assignments should identify:

- task owner;
- expected outcome;
- deadline;
- quality standard;
- supervisor;
- required tools or information;
- safety considerations;
- reporting requirement.

Assignments should be understandable and proportionate to worker capacity.

---

# 8. Quality Control

The cooperative shall define quality standards for its work.

Quality control may include:

- checklists;
- supervisor review;
- peer review;
- customer acceptance;
- documentation;
- training;
- corrective work;
- quality metrics.

Poor quality should be addressed as a training, supervision, or process issue before it becomes a reputation issue.

---

# 9. Tools and Equipment

Tools and equipment should be managed responsibly.

The cooperative should track:

- ownership;
- assignment;
- maintenance;
- storage;
- safety requirements;
- replacement;
- loss or damage;
- authorized use.

Workers should receive proper training before using equipment.

---

# 10. Purchasing

Purchasing authority must be defined.

Purchasing rules should address:

- spending limits;
- approved vendors;
- receipts;
- pre-approval;
- emergency purchases;
- reimbursement;
- conflict of interest;
- restricted funds;
- documentation.

---

# 11. Records

Operational records may include:

- work orders;
- customer communications;
- schedules;
- worker assignments;
- training records;
- safety records;
- incident reports;
- invoices;
- receipts;
- meeting notes;
- risk logs;
- 5-15 reports.

Records should be secure and accessible to authorized persons.

---

# 12. Meetings

Operational meetings should be useful and proportionate.

Meetings may include:

- daily check-in;
- weekly operations review;
- worker meeting;
- safety meeting;
- project review;
- manager review;
- board or steering committee report.

Meetings should produce decisions, action items, or shared understanding. Otherwise, they are just chairs arranged around confusion.

---

# 13. Worker Communication

Communication should be clear, respectful, and documented where needed.

The cooperative should define approved communication channels for:

- scheduling;
- urgent issues;
- customer matters;
- safety;
- complaints;
- prayer requests;
- reporting;
- policy updates.

---

# 14. Customer Communication

Customer communication should be handled by authorized persons.

Communication should be honest about:

- services;
- timeline;
- price;
- limits;
- changes;
- delays;
- errors;
- payment terms.

The cooperative should not overpromise to win work.

---

# 15. Financial Operations

Operational finances should follow approved financial controls.

This includes:

- invoicing;
- payment tracking;
- expense approval;
- receipts;
- payroll coordination;
- taxes;
- bank controls;
- reconciliation;
- reporting.

Cash handling, if any, must be controlled and documented.

---

# 16. Privacy and Confidentiality

The cooperative must protect confidential information.

This may include:

- worker records;
- customer data;
- contracts;
- invoices;
- training records;
- spiritual care information;
- conflict records;
- Society information.

Confidentiality must not be casual because the team is small.

---

# 17. Incident and Problem Reporting

Operational problems should be reported promptly.

Problems may include:

- safety incident;
- missed deadline;
- customer complaint;
- worker conflict;
- quality failure;
- financial issue;
- privacy concern;
- equipment loss;
- legal concern;
- mission alignment concern.

Early reporting is not disloyalty. It is stewardship.

---

# 18. Reporting

The cooperative shall report through [[Worker Cooperative Manager 5-15 Report]] and other approved reports.

Reports should include:

- work completed;
- current priorities;
- worker development;
- customer issues;
- finances;
- safety;
- risks;
- support needed;
- prayer requests;
- decisions needed.

---

# 19. Mission Alignment

Operations should be reviewed for mission alignment.

Questions include:

- Are workers treated with dignity?
- Is work lawful and safe?
- Are customers served honestly?
- Is the cooperative sustainable?
- Are spiritual care boundaries clear?
- Is the cooperative drifting into mere business?
- Are reports honest?

---

# 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during cooperative launch and at least every three years once stable.
