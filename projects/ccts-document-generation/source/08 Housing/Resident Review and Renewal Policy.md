# Resident Review and Renewal Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for reviewing resident status, renewing housing agreements, updating affordability information, identifying support needs, and addressing concerns during a resident's time in Society-related housing.

Resident review should support stability, fairness, accountability, and mission alignment.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Resident review should not feel like a surprise inspection of someone's life.

The Society should review what is necessary for good stewardship, legal compliance, resident support, affordability, and community peace. It should avoid unnecessary intrusion.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to annual resident reviews, lease renewals, affordability reviews, program eligibility reviews, support plan reviews, community concern reviews, hardship reviews, non-renewal reviews, and transitional housing progress reviews.

Local law and lease terms govern what may be reviewed and how.

---

# 4. Review Types

## 4.1 Annual Review

A regular review of resident status, agreement terms, support needs, and affordability where applicable.

## 4.2 Renewal Review

A review before renewing a lease, occupancy agreement, or program term.

## 4.3 Affordability Review

A review of income, subsidy, rent calculation, or hardship status under [[Housing Affordability Policy]].

## 4.4 Support Review

A review of resident support needs under [[Resident Support Policy]].

## 4.5 Concern-Based Review

A review triggered by repeated complaints, safety concerns, lease issues, or serious support needs.

---

# 5. Review Authority

The authority responsible for review must be defined.

Possible reviewers include housing manager, property manager, housing committee, National Executive Director, resident support coordinator, finance officer for affordability, chaplain for spiritual care themes only where appropriate, or board delegate.

Conflicts of interest must be disclosed.

---

# 6. Notice

Residents should receive reasonable notice of reviews unless urgent circumstances justify otherwise.

Notice should explain reason for review, information requested, deadline, meeting details if any, resident rights, confidentiality, possible outcomes, and contact person.

---

# 7. Information Reviewed

A review may include lease compliance, rent or fee status, affordability information, household changes, maintenance concerns, community expectations, support needs, complaints, safety concerns, resident goals, eligibility, and renewal readiness.

Only necessary information should be reviewed.

---

# 8. Affordability Review

Where affordability depends on income or need, the Society may request updated information.

The review must be respectful, lawful, and confidential.

Changes may lead to rent adjustment, subsidy continuation, subsidy change, hardship support, referral to other resources, or renewal condition.

---

# 9. Support Needs

Resident review should consider support needs without becoming intrusive.

Support may include maintenance help, accessibility adjustments, conflict resolution, financial counseling referral, church referral, chaplaincy referral, crisis support, language support, and community support.

Residents should be asked rather than presumed.

---

# 10. Church and Spiritual Care

Resident review should not become spiritual interrogation.

The Society may remind residents that chaplaincy and church referral are available.

Residents should not be required to disclose private spiritual matters unless an approved lawful program structure requires specific participation.

---

# 11. Community Concerns

If community concerns arise, review should consider facts, complaints, resident response, pattern of behavior, support needs, safety, policy clarity, mediation, and corrective action.

The Society should listen before deciding. Proverbs 18:17 is still good governance.

---

# 12. Possible Outcomes

Review outcomes may include:

- renewal without change;
- renewal with updated terms;
- rent adjustment;
- subsidy adjustment;
- support plan;
- mediation;
- behavior agreement;
- maintenance plan;
- referral;
- additional review;
- non-renewal;
- termination process where lawful.

Outcomes must comply with law and agreement terms.

---

# 13. Renewal

Renewal decisions should be documented.

A renewal record should include resident name, unit, review date, term, rent or fee, subsidy status, conditions if any, concerns, approvals, and resident acknowledgment.

---

# 14. Non-Renewal

Non-renewal must comply with local law and agreement terms.

Reasons may include program completion, ineligibility, lease non-compliance, serious safety concerns, non-payment, property change, inability to meet program requirements, or mission or legal reasons.

Non-renewal should be handled with clarity, documentation, and compassion.

---

# 15. Documentation

Review records should include notice, information reviewed, meeting notes where applicable, decision, reasons, follow-up, confidentiality level, approvals, and resident communication.

Sensitive details should be minimized.

---

# 16. Complaints or Reconsideration

Residents should have access to the relevant complaint or reconsideration process if they believe review was unfair, unlawful, discriminatory, or inconsistent with policy.

See [[Housing Complaints and Dispute Resolution Policy]].

---

# 17. Reporting

Resident review themes may be reported in housing reports.

Reports may summarize renewals, affordability changes, support needs, non-renewals, risks, complaints, and mission alignment concerns.

Resident privacy must be protected.

---

# 18. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
