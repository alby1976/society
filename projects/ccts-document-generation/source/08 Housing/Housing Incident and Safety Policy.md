# Housing Incident and Safety Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for identifying, responding to, documenting, escalating, and reviewing housing incidents and safety concerns.

Housing ministry involves real people in real places. Safety concerns must be handled promptly, lawfully, compassionately, and with proper accountability.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society shall take reasonable steps to protect residents, staff, volunteers, neighbors, visitors, and property.

Safety is not the enemy of mercy. It is one of mercy's guardrails.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Society housing property;
- residents;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- contractors;
- guests;
- shared spaces;
- housing events;
- housing-related visits;
- property management activities;
- resident support activities.

---

# 4. Incident Categories

Housing incidents may include:

- injury;
- illness requiring emergency response;
- fire or smoke;
- flood or major leak;
- property damage;
- security issue;
- violence or threat;
- harassment;
- abuse or exploitation concern;
- resident conflict;
- missing person concern;
- privacy breach;
- unauthorized entry;
- pest or sanitation emergency;
- dangerous maintenance condition;
- police, ambulance, or fire response;
- death;
- serious complaint;
- spiritual care boundary concern;
- contractor safety issue.

---

# 5. Immediate Response

When an incident occurs, the first response should consider:

1. immediate safety;
2. emergency services where needed;
3. safeguarding requirements;
4. medical attention;
5. protection of vulnerable persons;
6. property protection;
7. notification of appropriate authority;
8. documentation;
9. communication with affected residents;
10. follow-up support.

---

# 6. Emergency Services

If immediate danger exists, emergency services should be contacted according to local law and circumstances.

Society personnel should not delay emergency response while seeking internal permission.

---

# 7. Incident Reporting

Incidents should be reported promptly to the appropriate housing authority.

Incident reports should include:

- date and time;
- location;
- persons involved;
- general description;
- immediate action taken;
- emergency services contacted;
- injuries or property damage;
- witnesses if any;
- follow-up needed;
- confidentiality level;
- reporter name;
- reviewer name.

Sensitive details should be limited to what is necessary.

---

# 8. Escalation Levels

| Level | Description | Escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Low | minor issue, no ongoing risk | housing manager |
| Medium | repeated or significant issue | housing manager and director |
| High | safety, legal, privacy, safeguarding, serious conflict | executive or board delegate |
| Critical | immediate danger, death, severe harm, major legal exposure | emergency response and senior leadership |

---

# 9. Safeguarding

Any incident involving suspected abuse, exploitation, vulnerable persons, children, serious misconduct, coercion, or immediate danger must be escalated according to safeguarding policy and local law.

Spiritual care or internal mediation must not be used to avoid required reporting.

---

# 10. Privacy

Incident information must be protected.

Only necessary information should be shared with:

- emergency responders;
- property management;
- authorized leadership;
- safeguarding lead;
- legal counsel;
- insurer;
- affected residents where appropriate;
- board where required.

---

# 11. Resident Communication

Affected residents should receive timely communication where appropriate.

Communication may include:

- safety instructions;
- repair updates;
- temporary relocation information;
- service interruption notice;
- support resources;
- complaint process;
- contact person.

Communication should not reveal unnecessary private information.

---

# 12. Contractor and Visitor Safety

Contractors and visitors must follow safety requirements.

The Society should ensure that contractors:

- are qualified where required;
- carry insurance where appropriate;
- follow access rules;
- respect residents;
- report hazards;
- comply with safety standards;
- disclose conflicts where relevant.

---

# 13. Property Safety Review

Safety reviews may include:

- fire safety;
- exits;
- alarms;
- lighting;
- locks;
- stairs and railings;
- electrical concerns;
- plumbing hazards;
- heating;
- pests;
- shared spaces;
- exterior areas;
- accessibility;
- environmental concerns.

---

# 14. Incident Debrief

Significant incidents should be debriefed.

Debrief questions:

- What happened?
- Was anyone harmed?
- Was emergency response adequate?
- Were residents informed properly?
- Was policy followed?
- Was documentation completed?
- What follow-up is needed?
- What risk remains?
- Does maintenance need action?
- Does governance need action?

---

# 15. Corrective Action

Corrective action may include:

- repairs;
- policy clarification;
- resident support;
- behavior agreement;
- lease enforcement;
- staff training;
- contractor removal;
- safety upgrades;
- insurance claim;
- legal review;
- board report;
- mission alignment review.

---

# 16. Reporting

Incident and safety themes should be included in housing reports.

Serious incidents should be escalated to appropriate leadership promptly and may require board reporting.

---

# 17. Records

Incident records should be stored securely and retained according to policy and local law.

Access should be limited to authorized persons.

---

# 18. Mission Alignment

Safety failures may become mission alignment concerns.

If housing conditions, community conflict, or leadership inaction place residents at risk, the matter should be reviewed under [[Mission Alignment Review]].

---

# 19. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
