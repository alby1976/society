# Housing Application and Intake Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes a fair, lawful, documented, and mission-aligned process for receiving, reviewing, approving, denying, and onboarding housing applicants.

The Christian Community Transformation Society shall handle housing applications with dignity, clarity, confidentiality, and careful stewardship.

Housing application processes must not be casual, secretive, arbitrary, coercive, or driven by favoritism.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should receive applicants as people made in the image of God, not as paperwork problems.

At the same time, housing is a serious legal, financial, and community commitment. Mercy requires order. Order protects mercy from becoming chaos.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to applications for affordable rental housing, stewardship housing, long-term lease housing, supported housing, transitional housing, worker housing, ministry housing, community land trust housing, and other Society-related housing programs.

Local implementation must comply with local housing, privacy, human rights, charity, tenancy, and property law.

---

# 4. Application Pathway

A standard application pathway may include:

1. inquiry;
2. pre-screening;
3. application submission;
4. privacy consent;
5. eligibility review;
6. affordability review;
7. reference or referral review;
8. interview or intake conversation;
9. conflict of interest review;
10. mission and community expectations disclosure;
11. approval, waitlist, or denial;
12. lease or agreement preparation;
13. resident orientation;
14. move-in planning;
15. intake documentation.

The process may be simplified or expanded depending on program type and local law.

---

# 5. Application Materials

Application materials may request:

- applicant name and contact information;
- household members;
- housing need;
- income or affordability information;
- current housing status;
- accessibility needs;
- emergency contact;
- references or referral;
- preferred location or unit type;
- consent for verification;
- consent for data handling;
- acknowledgment of Christian mission and community expectations.

Only necessary information should be collected.

---

# 6. Privacy and Consent

Applicants must be informed how their information will be collected, used, stored, shared, and retained.

Applicant information may be shared only with authorized persons for lawful purposes.

Sensitive information should not be collected unless necessary.

Privacy consent should be documented.

---

# 7. Eligibility Criteria

Each housing program must define eligibility criteria.

Criteria may include income, household size, housing need, vulnerability, connection to Society ministry, worker cooperative participation, ministry worker status, local residency requirements, ability to comply with lease or program terms, compatibility with housing model, and legal eligibility.

Eligibility criteria must be lawful and mission-aligned.

---

# 8. Affordability Review

Where affordability is part of the program, the Society shall review affordability according to [[Housing Affordability Policy]].

The review may include income information, household size, rent-to-income ratio, subsidy eligibility, hardship factors, sustainability of housing arrangement, and documentation requirements.

Income information must be handled confidentially.

---

# 9. Mission Disclosure

Applicants should be told clearly that the Society is a Christian organization.

Where appropriate, the Society should explain:

- Christian mission;
- community expectations;
- chaplaincy availability;
- church referral options;
- prayer or devotional opportunities;
- the Society/church distinction;
- voluntary nature of spiritual care unless a lawful approved program says otherwise.

Mission disclosure should be transparent, not manipulative.

---

# 10. Non-Coercion

Housing need must not be used to pressure applicants into religious participation, private spiritual disclosure, or artificial spiritual performance.

Applicants may be told about the Society's Christian identity and community expectations, but should not be forced to pretend belief to receive fair consideration unless a lawful, approved faith-based eligibility requirement applies.

---

# 11. Interview or Intake Conversation

An intake conversation may be used to understand the applicant's needs and suitability.

Topics may include housing need, household composition, support needs, affordability, community expectations, maintenance responsibilities, church or community connection where voluntarily discussed, accessibility, safety concerns, and move-in timing.

Interviewers must avoid intrusive, discriminatory, or unnecessary questions.

---

# 12. References and Referrals

References or referrals may come from church leaders, ministry leaders, social service providers, employers, housing providers, community organizations, Society ministries, or worker cooperative leaders.

References should be used carefully and not as a substitute for fair review.

---

# 13. Conflict of Interest Review

All applications must be screened for conflict of interest.

Examples include applicant related to board member, applicant related to staff, applicant referred by donor, applicant connected to decision-maker's church, applicant employed by Society or affiliated entity, or applicant connected to contractor or vendor.

Conflicts must be disclosed, documented, and managed before decision.

---

# 14. Decision Options

Application decisions may include:

- approved;
- approved with conditions;
- waitlisted;
- deferred pending more information;
- referred to another program;
- denied;
- closed by applicant withdrawal.

Decisions should be documented.

---

# 15. Approval

Approval should identify the unit or housing type, rent or fee, subsidy if any, agreement type, start date, conditions, orientation requirements, deposit if any, required documents, and move-in plan.

Approval should not be communicated casually before authority is confirmed.

---

# 16. Denial

If an application is denied, the Society should provide an appropriate explanation where lawful and appropriate.

Reasons may include ineligibility, no vacancy, affordability mismatch, inability to meet program requirements, safety concern, incomplete application, conflict issue, legal restriction, or better fit with another support.

The tone should remain respectful.

---

# 17. Waitlist

If a waitlist is used, the Society should define waitlist criteria, priority categories, communication frequency, removal reasons, privacy rules, and review schedule.

Waitlists should not become hidden favoritism.

---

# 18. Orientation and Move-In

Approved residents should receive orientation before or at move-in.

Orientation should include lease or agreement, rent or fee expectations, maintenance process, community expectations, complaint process, privacy, emergency contacts, spiritual care availability, church referral options, resident support, and safety rules.

---

# 19. Documentation

Application files should include:

- application;
- consent forms;
- eligibility review;
- affordability review;
- references or referrals;
- interview notes;
- conflict review;
- decision record;
- approval or denial communication;
- lease or agreement documents;
- orientation checklist.

Records must be stored securely.

---

# 20. Reporting and Review

Application activity may be reported in housing reports. Reports may summarize inquiries, applications, approvals, denials, waitlist, affordability needs, vacancies, risks, and support needs.

Applicant privacy must be protected.

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
