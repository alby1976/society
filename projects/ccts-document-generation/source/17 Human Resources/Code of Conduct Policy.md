# Code of Conduct Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes conduct expectations for people serving or representing the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The policy protects people, mission, churches, records, finances, trust, and public witness.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

People serving the Society should conduct themselves with integrity, humility, respect, honesty, confidentiality, and accountability.

The Society should not tolerate conduct that harms people, misuses authority, hides conflicts, damages churches, or discredits the mission.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- board members;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- contractors where applicable;
- ministry leaders;
- chaplains;
- advisors;
- committee members;
- national chapter leaders;
- formation team members;
- persons publicly representing the Society.

Contracts or role descriptions may add more specific requirements.

---

# 4. General Conduct Expectations

Persons serving the Society should:

- act honestly;
- treat others with dignity;
- respect authority limits;
- protect confidential information;
- disclose conflicts of interest;
- follow approved policies;
- report serious concerns;
- avoid manipulation or coercion;
- use Society resources responsibly;
- maintain appropriate boundaries;
- respect local churches;
- avoid public misrepresentation.

---

# 5. Respectful Treatment

The Society expects respectful treatment among staff, volunteers, leaders, churches, donors, workers, residents, grant applicants, students, beneficiaries, and partners.

Prohibited conduct includes:

- harassment;
- intimidation;
- threats;
- abusive language;
- discrimination contrary to law;
- retaliation;
- bullying;
- exploitation;
- spiritual manipulation;
- humiliation;
- deliberate exclusion used as punishment.

---

# 6. Authority Boundaries

No person may use a Society role to claim authority they do not have.

This includes unauthorized authority over:

- churches;
- residents;
- workers;
- students;
- donors;
- grant applicants;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- public communications;
- finances;
- legal commitments.

Authority should be delegated, documented, and reviewable.

---

# 7. Confidentiality

Confidential information must be protected.

Persons serving the Society should not disclose, gossip about, forward, copy, publish, or casually discuss confidential information.

Confidentiality applies during and after service.

---

# 8. Conflicts of Interest

Conflicts of interest must be disclosed.

A person should not use Society authority to benefit themselves, family, friends, business interests, church contacts, donors, or connected organizations improperly.

Disclosure is not shame. Hiding conflict is the problem.

---

# 9. Use of Society Property and Funds

Society funds, records, data, tools, property, technology, and name must be used only for approved purposes.

Misuse may include:

- unauthorized spending;
- personal use of funds;
- unauthorized access;
- careless handling of records;
- unauthorized public communications;
- improper use of logo or name;
- use of Society relationships for private gain.

---

# 10. Public Representation

Only authorized persons may speak publicly for the Society.

Public representation includes:

- websites;
- social media;
- newsletters;
- fundraising appeals;
- public statements;
- church presentations;
- grant announcements;
- media contact;
- government communication.

No person should imply that a chapter, ministry, or grant is approved before it is authorized.

---

# 11. Church Relationships

Persons serving the Society must respect local churches.

They must not:

- replace local church authority;
- contradict faithful local church leadership within the Society's role;
- draw members away from churches for Society loyalty;
- use Society resources to control churches;
- present Society gatherings as substitutes for Lord's Day worship.

---

# 12. Spiritual Care Boundaries

Persons offering prayer, encouragement, or spiritual care must stay within their role.

Only appointed chaplains or authorized persons may provide chaplaincy care.

Spiritual care must not be coercive, manipulative, or tied to housing, employment, grants, or volunteer standing.

---

# 13. Reporting Misconduct

Concerns should be reported through the appropriate supervisor, board, incident process, or designated reporting channel.

Serious concerns should be escalated under [[Incident Escalation Policy]].

Do not hide serious misconduct inside informal conversation.

---

# 14. Retaliation Prohibited

Retaliation against a person who raises a concern in good faith is prohibited.

Retaliation may include:

- threats;
- exclusion;
- removal from service without proper reason;
- public shaming;
- spiritual pressure;
- reduced opportunities;
- hostile treatment;
- intimidation.

---

# 15. Corrective Action

Violations may result in:

- clarification;
- training;
- warning;
- role restriction;
- supervision;
- removal from role;
- suspension;
- termination;
- legal action where needed;
- board review;
- Mission Alignment Review.

Corrective action should be proportionate and documented.

---

# 16. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and after any serious conduct concern.
