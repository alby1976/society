# Housing Trust Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes the housing ministry of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

The Housing Trust exists to steward housing, land, leases, property, affordability, community life, and resident support in a manner consistent with the Society's Christian mission, Reformed theological identity, church-supporting purpose, and international governance standards.

Housing is not merely an asset class. For the Society, housing is a ministry of stewardship, stability, mercy, hospitality, and community care.

This framework should be read with [[Housing Philosophy]], [[Housing Governance Policy]], [[Stewardship Housing Policy]], [[Mission Alignment Review]], and [[Church Referral Policy]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society may use housing to advance religion by supporting Christian community, mercy, discipleship, stability, hospitality, and practical care.

Housing ministry must not become merely real estate management with a cross on the brochure.

The Society's housing must remain mission-aligned, law-compliant, financially responsible, and respectful of local church authority.

---

# 3. Nature of the Housing Trust

The Housing Trust may be structured as:

- a trust;
- a nonprofit corporation;
- a charitable housing corporation;
- a community land trust;
- a property-holding entity;
- a national chapter housing ministry;
- an affiliated organization under Society oversight;
- another lawful structure approved by the Society.

The legal form may vary by jurisdiction, but the theological and governance purpose remains the same.

---

# 4. International and National Structure

The Society may establish an international housing framework with national implementation.

```text
International Housing Framework
        |
        +-- Canada Housing Trust
        +-- Hong Kong Housing Ministry
        +-- Future National Housing Ministries
```

Each national housing entity shall comply with local law while remaining accountable to the Society's international doctrine, mission, governance standards, and reserved powers.

---

# 5. Housing Ministry Objectives

The Housing Trust may serve the following objectives:

1. provide affordable or stewardship housing;
2. support Christian community;
3. serve vulnerable persons and families;
4. support workers and ministry participants;
5. stabilize households connected to Society ministries;
6. encourage connection to faithful local churches;
7. preserve long-term affordability where possible;
8. steward land and property for mission;
9. support discipleship in ordinary life;
10. model mercy with responsibility;
11. reduce housing instability that hinders faithful service.

---

# 6. What Housing Ministry Is Not

Housing ministry is not:

- a church;
- a substitute for local church fellowship;
- a tool for coercing religious participation;
- a speculative real estate venture;
- a private benefit scheme;
- a way to enrich insiders;
- a workaround for mission drift;
- a property empire;
- a replacement for professional property management where needed.

Housing must serve the mission. The mission must not become a hostage of housing.

---

# 7. Eligible Housing Models

The Society may consider several housing models.

## 7.1 Affordable Rental Housing

Rental housing offered below or within an approved affordability standard.

## 7.2 Long-Term Lease Housing

Housing offered through long-term leases where lawful and appropriate, possibly including very long lease terms while preserving mission control.

## 7.3 Community Land Trust

A structure in which land is held for long-term community and mission benefit while homes or lease rights may be managed separately.

## 7.4 Stewardship Housing

Housing that includes community expectations, spiritual support, accountability, and connection to Society mission.

## 7.5 Transitional Housing

Temporary or medium-term housing for persons in need, subject to legal and safeguarding review.

## 7.6 Worker Housing

Housing connected to worker cooperative or ministry worker support, subject to conflict of interest and employment law review.

---

# 8. Residents and Beneficiaries

Housing may serve:

- low-income families;
- ministry workers;
- worker cooperative participants;
- students or trainees;
- newcomers or immigrants where lawful;
- vulnerable persons;
- families connected to church partners;
- Society staff or volunteers where conflicts are managed;
- other persons approved under housing policy.

Eligibility must be clearly defined and legally reviewed.

---

# 9. Non-Christian Residents

The Society may house non-Christian residents where consistent with law, charity rules, mission, and approved policy.

Non-Christian residents should be treated with dignity and honesty.

The Society may maintain a clearly Christian identity and offer spiritual care, prayer, and church referral, but must not use housing vulnerability to coerce religious participation.

Christian community expectations must be lawful, transparent, and reviewed carefully.

---

# 10. Church Connection

Housing ministry should encourage appropriate connection to faithful local churches.

This may include:

- church referral;
- chaplaincy support;
- prayer opportunities;
- community care;
- pastoral referral where requested;
- church partner involvement.

Housing residents should not be treated as members of a Society church. There is no Society church.

---

# 11. Chaplaincy in Housing

The Society chaplain may support housing ministry through:

- prayer;
- spiritual care;
- visitation where appropriate;
- crisis support;
- church referral;
- general spiritual care themes;
- support for resident community life;
- support for staff and volunteers.

Chaplaincy must follow confidentiality, boundaries, and church-supporting policies.

---

# 12. Governance

Housing governance must be clear before property is acquired or residents are admitted.

Housing governance should define:

- ownership;
- board oversight;
- management authority;
- resident eligibility;
- lease authority;
- rent or fee setting;
- maintenance responsibility;
- financial controls;
- conflict of interest rules;
- reporting requirements;
- risk management;
- mission alignment review;
- church relationship boundaries;
- privacy and records practices.

---

# 13. Financial Stewardship

Housing must be financially sustainable.

The Housing Trust shall consider:

- acquisition cost;
- debt;
- reserves;
- maintenance;
- insurance;
- taxes;
- utilities;
- property management;
- repairs;
- vacancies;
- affordability;
- subsidies;
- donor restrictions;
- long-term capital planning.

Affordable housing that bankrupts the ministry is not stewardship. It is compassion without a spreadsheet.

---

# 14. Legal Review

Housing ministry must receive legal review appropriate to each jurisdiction.

Legal review may include:

- charity law;
- housing law;
- landlord-tenant law;
- human rights law;
- privacy law;
- property law;
- tax law;
- lease terms;
- long-term lease structures;
- community land trust structure;
- resident selection criteria;
- religious programming;
- risk and liability.

---

# 15. Resident Agreements

Resident agreements should be clear, lawful, and mission-aligned.

Agreements may include:

- rent or lease terms;
- occupancy rules;
- maintenance obligations;
- community expectations;
- privacy terms;
- spiritual care availability;
- complaint process;
- safety rules;
- termination terms;
- legal rights;
- contact information.

Agreements must not overpromise or conceal expectations.

---

# 16. Community Expectations

The Society may define community expectations where lawful and appropriate.

Expectations may address:

- respect for neighbors;
- safety;
- quiet enjoyment;
- care for property;
- participation in community life where voluntary or program-based;
- non-harassment;
- conflict resolution;
- guest policies;
- shared spaces;
- communication with management.

Faith-based expectations must be carefully reviewed to avoid coercion, illegality, or confusion.

---

# 17. Risk Management

Housing risks include:

- legal risk;
- financial risk;
- property damage;
- resident conflict;
- safety concerns;
- abuse or exploitation;
- privacy breaches;
- discrimination claims;
- mission drift;
- church relationship confusion;
- staff burnout;
- maintenance failures;
- debt burden;
- public reputation.

Housing ministry must maintain a risk register.

---

# 18. Reporting

Housing ministry shall report according to the Society's reporting system.

Reports may include:

- [[Housing Manager 5-15 Report]];
- monthly housing summary;
- maintenance report;
- financial report;
- occupancy report;
- incident report;
- risk report;
- annual housing report;
- mission alignment review.

---

# 19. Mission Alignment Review

Housing ministry shall undergo Mission Alignment Review.

Review should ask:

- Does the housing serve the Society's mission?
- Are residents treated with dignity?
- Is the housing financially sustainable?
- Are church relationships healthy?
- Is religious participation handled lawfully and non-coercively?
- Are conflicts of interest managed?
- Is property stewardship adequate?
- Are risks being reported?
- Is the ministry drifting into mere real estate management?

---

# 20. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually during the launch stage and at least every three years once housing governance is stable.
