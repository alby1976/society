# Stewardship Housing Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines stewardship housing for the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Stewardship housing is housing managed not merely for occupancy or income but for faithful use of property, long-term mission, community stability, affordability, resident dignity, and support of local church-connected Christian community.

This policy should be read with [[Housing Trust Framework]], [[Housing Philosophy]], [[Housing Governance Policy]], [[Tenant and Resident Policy]], and [[Mission Alignment Review]].

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society holds housing as a trust before God.

Property must serve mission. Mission must not be swallowed by property. Stewardship housing protects both people and purpose.

Housing stewardship requires clear theology, careful governance, financial prudence, resident dignity, and legal compliance.

---

# 3. Stewardship Objectives

Stewardship housing seeks to:

1. preserve housing for mission use;
2. maintain affordability where possible;
3. support stable households;
4. encourage community life;
5. protect resident dignity;
6. steward property responsibly;
7. maintain financial sustainability;
8. encourage church connection;
9. avoid speculation;
10. preserve long-term institutional memory.

---

# 4. Property as Stewardship

The Society shall treat property as entrusted, not merely owned.

Property decisions should consider:

- mission;
- residents;
- affordability;
- long-term maintenance;
- community impact;
- financial sustainability;
- legal compliance;
- risk;
- donor intent;
- future generations.

Property is not neutral. It creates obligations, power, risks, and opportunities.

---

# 5. Affordability Stewardship

Affordability must be defined and reviewed.

The Society may use:

- below-market rent;
- income-based rent;
- fixed affordability formula;
- subsidy;
- donor-supported affordability;
- long-term lease affordability controls;
- community land trust model.

Affordability should not be improvised case by case without policy.

---

# 6. Long-Term Mission Control

Where possible, the Society should preserve long-term mission control over housing assets.

Tools may include:

- nonprofit ownership;
- charitable trust;
- community land trust;
- long-term lease restrictions;
- resale restrictions;
- mission covenants;
- board approval for transfer;
- reserved powers;
- asset lock provisions.

Legal review is required.

---

# 7. Maintenance Stewardship

The Society shall maintain housing responsibly.

Maintenance planning should include:

- routine maintenance;
- preventive maintenance;
- emergency repairs;
- capital reserve;
- inspections;
- resident reporting;
- vendor oversight;
- safety checks.

Neglecting maintenance is not frugality. It is borrowing from future chaos.

---

# 8. Financial Stewardship

Housing finances must be managed carefully.

The Society should maintain:

- operating budget;
- maintenance reserve;
- capital reserve;
- vacancy reserve where needed;
- insurance;
- rent collection process;
- hardship process;
- financial reporting;
- debt policy.

Housing ministry should not become financially reckless in the name of compassion.

---

# 9. Resident Stewardship

Residents are not merely recipients of help. They are neighbors with dignity and responsibility.

Resident stewardship includes:

- clear expectations;
- fair treatment;
- support where needed;
- accountability;
- privacy;
- opportunity for community;
- spiritual care availability;
- appropriate referral.

---

# 10. Community Stewardship

The Society should cultivate community without coercion.

Community stewardship may include:

- shared expectations;
- hospitality;
- conflict resolution;
- prayer opportunities;
- church referral;
- resident support;
- volunteer involvement;
- community events.

Community should be encouraged, not manufactured by pressure.

---

# 11. Environmental and Neighborhood Stewardship

The Society should care for property in a way that respects neighbors and creation.

This may include:

- safe property upkeep;
- waste management;
- energy stewardship where feasible;
- good neighbor communication;
- responsible parking and noise management;
- compliance with local bylaws.

---

# 12. Donor and Gift Stewardship

Housing gifts must be reviewed carefully.

Before accepting property, funds, or restricted gifts for housing, the Society should consider:

- restrictions;
- legal obligations;
- maintenance burden;
- debt;
- tax consequences;
- mission fit;
- location;
- resident impact;
- long-term sustainability.

A free building can be very expensive if it arrives with hidden problems and a leaking roof.

---

# 13. Debt Stewardship

Housing debt should be approached carefully.

Before taking on debt, the Society should review:

- repayment plan;
- reserve capacity;
- interest rate risk;
- vacancy risk;
- maintenance costs;
- donor restrictions;
- legal covenants;
- mission risk;
- board approval authority;
- effect on other ministries.

Debt must not quietly become the landlord of the mission.

---

# 14. Vendor Stewardship

Vendors and contractors should be selected with integrity.

The Society should consider:

- competence;
- cost;
- conflicts of interest;
- reliability;
- insurance;
- safety;
- resident impact;
- legal compliance;
- mission sensitivity.

Contractor relationships must comply with conflict of interest policy.

---

# 15. Data and Records Stewardship

Housing records should be protected.

Records may include:

- applications;
- leases;
- rent records;
- maintenance requests;
- resident support notes;
- complaints;
- incident reports;
- inspections;
- financial records;
- mission alignment reviews.

Housing records often contain sensitive information and should be handled according to privacy and records retention policies.

---

# 16. Mission Alignment Review

Stewardship housing shall undergo regular [[Mission Alignment Review]].

The review should consider:

- mission use;
- resident dignity;
- affordability;
- church connection;
- financial sustainability;
- property condition;
- legal compliance;
- community health;
- reporting quality;
- risk.

---

# 17. Reporting

Stewardship housing should report through:

- [[Housing Manager 5-15 Report]];
- monthly housing summary;
- annual housing report;
- financial report;
- maintenance report;
- mission alignment review.

---

# 18. Warning Signs

Warning signs of housing mission drift include:

- property management crowding out ministry;
- lack of resident care;
- financial pressure driving harsh decisions;
- affordability disappearing without review;
- church connection disappearing;
- residents treated as problems rather than neighbors;
- reports not submitted;
- maintenance deferred repeatedly;
- legal compliance ignored;
- debt increasing without mission review.

---

# 19. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing development and at least every three years after housing operations stabilize.
