# Tenant and Resident Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for selecting, admitting, supporting, reviewing, and where necessary ending housing arrangements with tenants or residents in Society-related housing.

The Society uses the terms tenant, resident, household, or participant according to local law and the type of housing arrangement. In all cases, persons housed by the Society must be treated with dignity, fairness, clarity, and Christian care.

This policy should be read with [[Housing Trust Framework]], [[Housing Governance Policy]], [[Lease Standards]], [[Community Expectations Policy]], and [[Stewardship Housing Policy]].

## 2. Governing Principle

Housing residents are neighbors, not projects.

The Society's housing ministry should combine mercy, order, accountability, privacy, stability, and mission alignment. It should neither exploit vulnerability nor remove all responsibility.

Residents should know what is being offered, what is expected, what rights they have, what support is available, and what limits apply.

## 3. Legal Status

Every housing arrangement must comply with local law.

Local law may distinguish between tenant, licensee, resident, program participant, leaseholder, long-term leaseholder, cooperative housing member, transitional housing participant, or supported housing resident.

The Society must use lawful terms in each jurisdiction. This policy provides ministry standards, not legal advice. Local legal review is required before implementation.

## 4. Eligibility

Resident eligibility should be defined by written policy for each housing program.

Eligibility may consider income, housing need, household size, connection to Society ministry, worker cooperative participation, ministry worker status, vulnerability, church or community referral, ability to meet lease or program terms, compatibility with community expectations, and local legal requirements.

Eligibility criteria must be lawful, transparent, and mission-aligned.

## 5. Non-Discrimination and Mission Identity

The Society may maintain a clear Christian mission and housing philosophy.

Resident selection and program requirements must also comply with applicable human rights, housing, charity, and tenancy laws.

The Society must not unlawfully discriminate.

The Society may explain its Christian identity, available chaplaincy, church referral practices, and community expectations clearly before admission.

## 6. Non-Christian Residents

The Society may house non-Christian residents where lawful and consistent with approved policy.

Non-Christian residents should be treated with respect, honesty, and dignity.

The Society may offer spiritual care, prayer, referral to local churches, and participation in Christian community activities, but should avoid coercion.

Housing need must not be used as leverage to force religious participation.

## 7. Application Process

A housing application process may include:

1. application form;
2. identity and household information;
3. income or affordability information;
4. housing need assessment;
5. reference or referral;
6. interview;
7. eligibility review;
8. conflict of interest disclosure;
9. privacy consent;
10. explanation of Christian mission and community expectations;
11. approval or denial;
12. lease or agreement execution;
13. move-in orientation.

The process should be documented.

## 8. Selection Authority

Resident selection authority must be defined.

Possible approving bodies include the housing manager, housing committee, National Executive Director, National Board, housing trust board, or delegated selection panel.

No person should approve housing for themselves, family members, close friends, business associates, or church contacts without conflict review.

## 9. Conflicts of Interest

Housing conflicts are serious and must be disclosed.

Conflicts may involve board members seeking housing, staff relatives seeking housing, church partners recommending relatives, donors influencing placement, contractors connected to residents, staff housing arrangements, rent relief decisions, or termination decisions.

Conflicts must be managed under [[Conflict of Interest Policy]].

## 10. Resident Orientation

Before move-in or participation, residents should receive orientation.

Orientation should cover lease or occupancy terms, rent or fees, maintenance requests, emergency contacts, community expectations, complaint process, privacy, spiritual care availability, chaplaincy boundaries, church referral options, safety rules, reporting or meeting requirements if any, and termination or renewal process.

Orientation should be understandable and documented.

## 11. Rent, Fees, and Affordability

Rent or fees should be set according to approved housing policy, local law, affordability goals, and financial sustainability.

The Society should define rent calculation method, subsidy process, payment expectations, late payment process, hardship process, arrears review, rent adjustment process, and documentation.

Rent relief or hardship decisions must be fair and documented.

## 12. Resident Support

Resident support may include orientation, practical help, referrals, chaplaincy, church referral, conflict support, community meetings, maintenance coordination, financial counseling referral, and crisis response referral.

Resident support should not become dependency, control, or unqualified professional service.

## 13. Privacy

Resident information must be protected.

Sensitive information may include income, family status, immigration status, health, pastoral care, rent arrears, conflict, complaints, legal matters, and safety concerns.

Resident information should be shared only with persons who need it for lawful and authorized purposes.

## 14. Community Expectations

Residents should follow [[Community Expectations Policy]].

Expectations should be lawful, clear, reasonable, and tied to safety, property stewardship, neighbor respect, and mission integrity.

Residents should know which expectations are lease obligations, which are program expectations, and which are voluntary community opportunities.

## 15. Spiritual Care

Residents may receive spiritual care through Society chaplaincy or referral to local churches.

Spiritual care must be voluntary unless a specific lawful program requirement has been approved.

The Society should avoid coercion and preserve the Society/church distinction.

## 16. Complaints and Concerns

Residents should have a clear complaint process.

Complaints may involve maintenance, staff conduct, neighbor conflict, privacy, discrimination, community expectations, safety, chaplaincy, rent, lease issues, or termination concerns.

Complaints should be documented and reviewed.

## 17. Resident Review

Resident status may be reviewed according to policy.

Review may include ongoing eligibility, lease compliance, rent status, support needs, community concerns, renewal readiness, mission fit where lawful, and safety or risk concerns.

Resident reviews must respect legal rights.

## 18. Ending Housing Arrangements

Housing arrangements may end through lease expiry, voluntary move-out, transfer, non-renewal, termination for cause, program completion, legal eviction process, safety intervention, death, or property closure.

Any termination must comply with local law, lease terms, policy, and fairness.

The Society should be firm where necessary and humane throughout.

## 19. Reporting

Housing staff shall report according to [[Housing Manager 5-15 Report]] and other reporting policies.

Reports should protect resident confidentiality while identifying risks, support needs, maintenance, and decisions required.

## 20. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually during housing launch and at least every three years once housing operations stabilize.
