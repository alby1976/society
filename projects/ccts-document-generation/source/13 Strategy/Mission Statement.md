# Mission Statement

## Mission

The mission of the Christian Community Transformation Society is:

> To advance the Christian religion by supporting faithful local churches and developing ministries that demonstrate the Gospel through discipleship, mercy, meaningful work, affordable housing, leadership development, and community transformation under the Lordship of Jesus Christ.

## Purpose

The Society exists to strengthen Christian life throughout the week without replacing the local church. It serves churches, workers, families, students, residents, communities, and vulnerable people through organized ministries that remain accountable to Scripture, Reformed theology, and sound governance.

The Society's purpose is not to build a parallel church structure. Its purpose is to help Christian worship bear fruit in daily life.

## Mission Principles

### Christ-Centered

The Society's mission begins with Christ, belongs to Christ, and must be carried out for Christ's glory.

### Church-Supporting

The Society supports faithful local churches. It does not administer sacraments, exercise church discipline, or replace pastors and elders.

### International by Design

The Society is designed as an international society from the beginning. National chapters exist under shared doctrine, mission, governance standards, and reserved powers.

### Practical and Spiritual

The Society serves both practical needs and spiritual formation. Housing, work, education, grants, chaplaincy, and mercy ministries should be integrated with prayer, discipleship, accountability, and church connection.

### Governed for Continuity

The Society must preserve its mission across generations through documents, decisions, reports, archives, advisory structures, and disciplined review.

## What the Society Does

The Society may pursue its mission through church-supporting ministries, affordable and stewardship housing, worker cooperative development, church support foundations, capacity-building grants, chaplaincy, leadership development, prayer gatherings, community service, training and education, international chapter formation, and institutional memory systems.

## What the Society Does Not Do

The Society does not become a church, replace Lord's Day worship, administer sacraments, exercise church discipline, override lawful church leadership, treat social service as a substitute for the Gospel, allow national chapters to redefine doctrine or mission, or permit ministries to drift from founding commitments.

## Relationship to Local Churches

The Society's ministries should encourage beneficiaries, workers, residents, students, and volunteers to participate in faithful local churches.

When the Society serves people who are unchurched, the Society should provide care with the intention of helping them connect to faithful church life where appropriate.

## Relationship to National Chapters

Every national chapter must implement the Society's mission in its local context without altering the mission itself.

Local law, language, and culture may shape implementation, but not the Society's theological identity or core purpose.

## Mission Drift Protection

The Society protects mission through [[Statement of Faith]], [[Definitions of Key Terms]], [[Core Values]], international reserved powers, board oversight, theological review, church advisory counsel, mission alignment reviews, document registry, decision register, reporting framework, and stewardship advisors.

## Review

This Mission Statement should be reviewed annually and whenever the Society launches a national chapter, establishes a major ministry, or revises its constitution.
