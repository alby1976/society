# Hospital and Visitation Ministry Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs visitation ministry conducted by chaplains or authorized representatives of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

Visitation ministry may include hospital visits, care home visits, home visits, workplace visits, housing visits, crisis visits, and visits to persons connected to Society ministries.

The purpose of visitation is to provide prayer, encouragement, spiritual care, compassionate presence, and appropriate referral while respecting privacy, consent, safety, local church authority, and applicable law.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Visitation is a ministry of presence and care, not control.

A Society visitor should come as a servant, not an inspector. The visit should be marked by humility, prayer, listening, discretion, and respect for the person visited.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Society chaplains;
- authorized staff;
- approved volunteers;
- ministry leaders;
- housing ministry workers;
- worker cooperative leaders where appropriate;
- national chapter personnel authorized to conduct visits.

The depth and nature of visitation must match the person's role, training, authorization, and relationship to the person visited.

---

# 4. Types of Visits

## 4.1 Hospital Visits

Visits to persons in hospital or medical facilities.

## 4.2 Care Home or Facility Visits

Visits to persons in long-term care, assisted living, recovery facilities, or similar settings.

## 4.3 Home Visits

Visits to a person's residence. These require particular attention to consent, safety, privacy, and boundaries.

## 4.4 Housing Ministry Visits

Visits connected to Society housing ministry, including resident support, spiritual care, or crisis response.

## 4.5 Workplace or Cooperative Visits

Visits connected to worker cooperative ministry or staff care.

## 4.6 Crisis Visits

Visits in response to grief, emergency, conflict, trauma, displacement, serious illness, or sudden need.

---

# 5. Authorization

A person conducting visitation must be authorized by the Society or appropriate ministry authority.

Authorization may be:

- role-based;
- event-specific;
- case-specific;
- emergency-based;
- delegated by a supervisor.

Unauthorized persons should not represent themselves as Society visitors.

---

# 6. Consent

Visitation should ordinarily occur with the consent of the person visited or an appropriate authorized person.

Consent may be obtained directly, through family, through a ministry leader, through a hospital or facility process, or through prior permission.

Visitors should not pressure someone to receive a visit.

---

# 7. Local Church Coordination

Where the person visited belongs to a faithful local church, the Society should respect that church's pastoral care.

The Society visitor may ask whether the person would like their pastor, elder, or church leader contacted.

The Society should not replace local church visitation.

---

# 8. Visit Preparation

Before a visit, the visitor should consider:

- purpose of visit;
- consent;
- location;
- safety;
- confidentiality;
- relationship to local church;
- whether another person should attend;
- whether the visit involves a vulnerable person;
- whether facility rules apply;
- whether prayer or Scripture would be welcome;
- whether follow-up is likely needed.

---

# 9. Conduct During Visits

Visitors should:

- be respectful;
- listen carefully;
- avoid rushing;
- offer prayer where appropriate;
- avoid intrusive questions;
- avoid theological speculation;
- avoid medical, legal, or financial advice unless qualified and authorized;
- respect family and facility boundaries;
- protect confidentiality;
- keep appropriate physical and emotional boundaries;
- leave when requested.

Visitors should not turn visits into speeches. People in distress rarely need a sermon with a clipboard.

---

# 10. Prayer and Scripture

Visitors may offer prayer or Scripture.

Prayer and Scripture should be:

- voluntary;
- appropriate to the person's condition;
- consistent with the Society's doctrine;
- sensitive to the setting;
- respectful of local church involvement;
- free from manipulation.

---

# 11. Visiting Vulnerable Persons

Visits involving vulnerable persons require special care.

Consider:

- whether two visitors are needed;
- whether family or staff should be present;
- safeguarding requirements;
- privacy;
- capacity and consent;
- local facility rules;
- documentation;
- escalation requirements.

---

# 12. Home Visit Safety

Home visits should be approached carefully.

The Society may require:

- supervisor awareness;
- check-in and check-out;
- two-person visits for higher-risk situations;
- daylight visits where possible;
- avoidance of isolated situations;
- documentation;
- risk review.

A visitor should leave if the situation becomes unsafe.

---

# 13. Confidentiality

Visitors must protect confidential information.

Only necessary information should be recorded or shared.

Confidentiality may yield where safety, safeguarding, law, or proper leadership escalation requires disclosure.

---

# 14. Documentation

After a visit, the visitor should document only what is necessary.

Documentation may include:

- date;
- type of visit;
- general purpose;
- prayer or support offered;
- referral made;
- follow-up needed;
- risk identified;
- escalation made;
- confidentiality level.

Sensitive details should be minimized.

---

# 15. Follow-Up

Follow-up may include:

- additional visit;
- prayer;
- church referral;
- chaplain referral;
- professional referral;
- family contact where authorized;
- supervisor update;
- safeguarding escalation;
- ministry support;
- practical assistance.

---

# 16. Prohibited Conduct

Visitors must not:

- exploit vulnerability;
- pressure religious participation;
- contradict local church leadership;
- make promises beyond authority;
- accept inappropriate gifts;
- enter unsafe settings unnecessarily;
- provide unqualified professional advice;
- misuse confidential information;
- visit against a person's wishes;
- create dependency.

---

# 17. Reporting

Visitation activity should be reported through the appropriate reporting system, often using [[Chaplain 5-15 Report]] or another role-specific report.

Reports should summarize activity without unnecessary personal details.

---
