# Core Operations Manual

## 1. Purpose

This manual defines the basic operational practices of the Christian Community Transformation Society.

It exists to help leaders, staff, volunteers, advisors, national chapters, and affiliated ministries know how ordinary work should be handled, recorded, reported, reviewed, and preserved.

The manual does not replace the International Constitution, bylaws, legal requirements, or board-approved policies. It provides operational order under them.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Operations should serve mission.

The Society should be organized enough to protect people and preserve mission, but not so complicated that ordinary service becomes impossible.

Good operations are the rails that help mission move without falling into the ditch.

---

# 3. Scope

This manual applies to:

- International Society operations;
- National Chapters;
- ministries;
- committees;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- advisors;
- chaplaincy;
- housing;
- worker cooperative activities;
- Church Support Foundation activities;
- reporting and institutional memory systems.

Local chapters may adapt operational details where approved and lawful.

---

# 4. Operational Foundations

The Society's operations should be shaped by:

- doctrine and mission;
- lawful governance;
- delegated authority;
- accurate records;
- timely reporting;
- financial stewardship;
- confidentiality;
- risk awareness;
- church-supporting boundaries;
- institutional memory;
- prayerful dependence.

---

# 5. Authority Before Action

No person should act for the Society without proper authority.

Before acting, a person should know:

- who authorized the action;
- what authority applies;
- spending limits;
- reporting duty;
- confidentiality level;
- whether board approval is needed;
- whether legal, theological, church advisory, or financial review is required.

When in doubt, ask before binding the Society.

---

# 6. Routine Operations

Routine operations may include:

- scheduling meetings;
- preparing agendas;
- writing minutes;
- maintaining document registries;
- submitting reports;
- processing ordinary expenses;
- coordinating volunteers;
- updating records;
- following up on decisions;
- communicating approved information;
- organizing prayer gatherings;
- supporting ministry leaders.

Routine does not mean unimportant. Many failures begin with ordinary tasks ignored.

---

# 7. Decision Records

Significant decisions should be recorded in the Decision Register.

Decision records should include:

- decision title;
- date;
- authority;
- decision made;
- rationale;
- related documents;
- conditions;
- responsible person;
- follow-up date.

Do not rely on memory for decisions that future leaders will need.

---

# 8. Document Records

Governance and ministry documents should be tracked in the Document Registry.

Document records should include:

- document title;
- document ID;
- version;
- status;
- owner;
- category;
- review date;
- related documents;
- dependencies;
- authority level.

Documents without ownership become abandoned furniture in the governance house.

---

# 9. Reporting

Operations should follow [[Reporting Framework]] and [[5-15 Reporting Framework]].

Reports should be:

- timely;
- concise;
- truthful;
- reviewed;
- archived;
- connected to decisions where needed;
- respectful of confidentiality.

Reports should identify progress, concerns, risks, decisions needed, support needed, and prayer requests where appropriate.

---

# 10. Meetings

Meetings should have:

- purpose;
- agenda;
- chair or facilitator;
- attendance record;
- conflict declarations where relevant;
- decisions recorded;
- action items assigned;
- follow-up date;
- minutes or notes.

A meeting without decisions, action, prayer, or shared understanding may not need to be a meeting.

---

# 11. Communication

Operational communication should be clear, respectful, and authorized.

Communication should identify:

- audience;
- purpose;
- confidentiality level;
- decision authority;
- whether public release is permitted;
- whether legal or board review is needed;
- responsible sender.

No one should publicly speak for the Society beyond their authority.

---

# 12. Confidentiality

Operations often involve sensitive information.

Confidential information may involve:

- staff;
- volunteers;
- residents;
- workers;
- grant applicants;
- students;
- donors;
- churches;
- legal matters;
- finances;
- safeguarding;
- spiritual care.

Confidential details should be shared only with persons who need to know and are authorized to receive them.

---

# 13. Records Storage

Records should be stored in approved locations.

The Society should define:

- source of truth;
- archive location;
- backup process;
- access permissions;
- retention period;
- naming conventions;
- version control process;
- document owner.

For the governance vault, source Markdown files are the source of truth. Generated outputs are useful but disposable.

---

# 14. Version Control

Documents should use version numbers and status labels.

Statuses may include:

- Draft;
- Proposed;
- Active;
- Superseded;
- Archived.

Major changes should be recorded in the changelog or document history.

---

# 15. Task Follow-Up

Action items should include:

- action;
- owner;
- due date;
- status;
- related decision;
- follow-up note.

Tasks should not disappear after meetings.

---

# 16. Risk Awareness

Operational risks should be escalated.

Examples:

- missed reports;
- unclear authority;
- financial irregularity;
- privacy concern;
- safeguarding concern;
- legal notice;
- leadership overload;
- conflict of interest;
- public communication error;
- church relationship concern;
- mission drift.

---

# 17. Local Adaptation

National Chapters may adapt operational procedures where lawful and approved.

Local adaptations should be tracked in the appropriate local adaptation register.

Adaptation should not alter doctrine, mission, reserved powers, reporting obligations, or confidentiality standards without proper approval.

---

# 18. Annual Review

The Core Operations Manual should be reviewed annually.

Review should ask:

- Are operations supporting mission?
- Are reports being submitted?
- Are decisions recorded?
- Are documents current?
- Are records accessible?
- Are leaders overloaded?
- Are risks being escalated?
- Are local adaptations documented?

---

# 19. Review

This manual should be reviewed annually and whenever major operational systems change.
