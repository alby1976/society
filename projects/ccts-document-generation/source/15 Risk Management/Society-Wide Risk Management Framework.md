# Society-Wide Risk Management Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework establishes how the Christian Community Transformation Society identifies, records, reviews, escalates, mitigates, and learns from risk.

Risk management protects mission, doctrine, people, churches, leaders, finances, records, ministries, national chapters, affiliated organizations, and public trust.

Risk management is not fearfulness. It is disciplined stewardship.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

The Society should name risks early, report them honestly, act on them proportionately, and preserve lessons for future leaders.

A risk ignored does not become spiritual. It becomes a surprise with invoices.

---

# 3. Scope

This framework applies to:

- International Board;
- National Chapters;
- committees;
- staff;
- volunteers;
- chaplaincy;
- housing;
- worker cooperative ministries;
- Church Support Foundation;
- church advisory relationships;
- theological oversight;
- reporting systems;
- legal and financial administration;
- public communications;
- affiliated organizations.

---

# 4. Risk Categories

Society-wide risks may include:

- doctrine and mission drift;
- church relationship confusion;
- governance failure;
- leadership overload;
- legal noncompliance;
- financial weakness;
- restricted fund misuse;
- safeguarding concern;
- privacy breach;
- chaplaincy boundary concern;
- housing risk;
- cooperative worker risk;
- Foundation grant risk;
- national chapter risk;
- international expansion risk;
- public communications risk;
- records and institutional memory failure;
- conflict of interest;
- donor influence;
- reputational harm;
- operational overextension.

---

# 5. Risk Ownership

Every significant risk should have an owner.

A risk owner is responsible to:

- understand the risk;
- update the risk record;
- identify mitigation;
- escalate when needed;
- report progress;
- request support;
- recommend closure when appropriate.

Unowned risks are usually unmanaged risks.

---

# 6. Risk Rating

Risks should be rated by likelihood and impact.

## 6.1 Likelihood

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Low | Unlikely |
| Medium | Possible |
| High | Likely |
| Urgent | Already happening or imminent |

## 6.2 Impact

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Low | Minor inconvenience or limited effect |
| Medium | Significant operational, financial, or ministry effect |
| High | Serious mission, legal, financial, safety, doctrine, or church effect |
| Critical | Severe harm, major legal exposure, public crisis, mission failure, or suspension risk |

## 6.3 Overall Risk

Overall risk may be rated:

- Low;
- Medium;
- High;
- Critical.

---

# 7. Risk Register System

The Society shall maintain a [[Society-Wide Risk Register]].

Specialized risk registers may also exist for:

- national chapters;
- housing;
- worker cooperatives;
- Church Support Foundation;
- chaplaincy;
- finance;
- safeguarding;
- privacy;
- public communications.

Specialized registers should feed major risks into the Society-wide register.

---

# 8. Risk Review Rhythm

Risk should be reviewed regularly.

Recommended rhythm:

| Context | Review Frequency |
|---|---|
| International Board | Quarterly |
| National Chapters during launch | Monthly or quarterly |
| National Chapters once stable | Quarterly |
| Housing ministries | Quarterly |
| Worker cooperative pilots | Monthly |
| Foundation grant activity | Quarterly |
| Serious active risk | As required |
| Annual review | Annually |

---

# 9. Escalation

Risks must be escalated when:

- risk is High or Critical;
- doctrine or mission is threatened;
- legal advice may be needed;
- safety or safeguarding is involved;
- privacy breach occurs;
- financial controls fail;
- donor restriction may compromise mission;
- public communications may cause harm;
- chapter recognition is at risk;
- board decision is required;
- corrective action is not working.

---

# 10. Mitigation

Risk mitigation may include:

- policy revision;
- training;
- legal review;
- financial control improvement;
- leadership support;
- ministry pause;
- reporting increase;
- conflict of interest remedy;
- communication correction;
- restricted authority;
- Mission Alignment Review;
- external review;
- board decision;
- suspension or closure where needed.

---

# 11. Reporting

Risk reporting should be included in:

- 5-15 reports;
- board summaries;
- ministry reports;
- annual reports;
- Mission Alignment Reviews;
- incident reports;
- risk register updates.

Reports should be concise and should not bury serious risk in polite language.

---

# 12. Relationship to Mission Alignment Review

Risk management and [[Mission Alignment Review]] are connected.

Mission drift is a risk. Repeated unresolved risks may become mission alignment concerns.

A ministry or chapter may be operationally active but missionally unsafe.

---

# 13. Confidentiality

Risk records may contain sensitive information.

Access should be limited according to confidentiality level.

Risk records should protect:

- personal information;
- resident information;
- worker information;
- grant applicant information;
- church concerns;
- donor records;
- legal advice;
- safeguarding information;
- personnel matters.

---

# 14. Corrective Action

When a risk requires action, it should be connected to [[Corrective Action Policy]].

Corrective action should define:

- issue;
- responsible person;
- required action;
- due date;
- reporting requirement;
- review date;
- escalation if incomplete.

---

# 15. Learning and Institutional Memory

Closed risks should not disappear without learning.

The Society should preserve lessons from:

- serious incidents;
- near misses;
- failed launches;
- ministry pauses;
- chapter restrictions;
- legal concerns;
- financial weaknesses;
- governance failures;
- successful mitigations.

Institutional memory is one way the Society loves future leaders.

---

# 16. Review

This framework should be reviewed annually and after any major incident, mission alignment failure, chapter suspension, serious legal matter, or major ministry restructuring.
