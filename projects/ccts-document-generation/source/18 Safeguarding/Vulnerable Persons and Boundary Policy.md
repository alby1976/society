# Vulnerable Persons and Boundary Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes boundary expectations when serving people who may be vulnerable because of age, disability, poverty, housing instability, crisis, dependency, employment need, spiritual distress, grant need, or other circumstances.

The Society must never use vulnerability as an opportunity for control.

---

# 2. Governing Principle

Power must be named before it is misused.

When the Society provides housing, work, grants, care, referrals, prayer, or support, power differences may exist. Clear boundaries help protect people and mission.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- staff;
- volunteers;
- chaplains;
- housing personnel;
- cooperative leaders;
- Foundation reviewers;
- board members;
- ministry leaders;
- advisors;
- national chapter leaders;
- persons representing the Society.

---

# 4. Vulnerability Factors

A person may be vulnerable due to:

- housing need;
- financial need;
- employment dependence;
- disability;
- age;
- grief;
- family crisis;
- immigration or language barriers;
- spiritual distress;
- social isolation;
- grant or scholarship need;
- power imbalance with a leader;
- dependence on Society services.

This list is not exhaustive.

---

# 5. Boundary Areas

Boundaries may be needed in:

- one-on-one meetings;
- transportation;
- home visits;
- housing access;
- spiritual care;
- gifts and money;
- communication;
- social media;
- employment supervision;
- grant review;
- physical contact;
- prayer ministry;
- confidentiality;
- personal relationships;
- after-hours contact.

---

# 6. One-on-One Meetings

One-on-one meetings should be appropriate to role and setting.

Where risk is higher, use safeguards such as:

- meeting in visible or appropriate locations;
- notifying a supervisor;
- documenting purpose;
- avoiding unnecessary secrecy;
- setting time limits;
- using approved communication channels;
- avoiding dependency.

---

# 7. Transportation

Transportation should be approved and appropriate.

Consider:

- role authority;
- safety;
- insurance;
- consent where required;
- documentation;
- whether two adults or another safeguard is needed;
- avoiding unnecessary repeated private transportation.

---

# 8. Housing Boundaries

Housing ministry creates significant power imbalance.

Housing personnel must not use housing access, lease status, rent, support, or resident vulnerability to pressure spiritual participation, personal loyalty, unpaid labour, donations, or private benefit.

Housing access and inspections should follow policy, law, and documented procedure.

---

# 9. Cooperative Work Boundaries

Worker cooperative leaders must avoid exploitation.

Workers should not be pressured through spiritual language, housing need, immigration concerns, financial distress, or relationship dependency.

Work expectations, compensation, safety, reporting, and authority should be clear.

---

# 10. Grant and Student Support Boundaries

Foundation personnel must not use grants, scholarships, or student support to create personal loyalty, improper influence, or spiritual coercion.

Grant decisions should follow policy, documented criteria, conflict rules, and reporting requirements.

---

# 11. Chaplaincy Boundaries

Chaplains must follow chaplaincy policy.

Chaplaincy should not replace local church authority, create dependency, or use spiritual care to control housing, work, grants, or organizational standing.

---

# 12. Gifts and Money

People serving the Society should not request or accept inappropriate gifts, loans, favors, personal payments, or financial arrangements from vulnerable persons.

Small culturally appropriate gifts may require disclosure depending on policy and role.

---

# 13. Communication Boundaries

Communication should be appropriate, documented where needed, and role-related.

Avoid:

- secretive communication;
- excessive personal messaging;
- late-night non-urgent contact;
- pressure;
- emotional dependency;
- inappropriate personal disclosure;
- social media boundary confusion.

---

# 14. Reporting Boundary Concerns

Boundary concerns should be reported early.

A concern does not require proof of misconduct before reporting.

Supervisors should respond proportionately through clarification, support, training, restriction, or escalation.

---

# 15. Corrective Action

Boundary violations may lead to:

- coaching;
- training;
- written warning;
- changed supervision;
- restricted duties;
- removal from role;
- incident escalation;
- safeguarding response;
- legal review where needed.

---

# 16. Review

This policy should be reviewed annually and after any significant boundary concern.
